1665 · Probability

Pascal's arithmetical triangle

The triangle and induction were used to compute fair divisions.

Also known as: Khayyam's triangle

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1010–1303

    Triangle of binomial coefficients in China and Persia

    precursor

    Jia Xian (1010-1070); al-Karaji (953-1029); Yang Hui (1238-1298)

    The array of binomial coefficients was described long before Pascal in Indian, Persian and Chinese sources, so Pascal's contribution is a systematic treatment and use, not the table itself.

    Evidence (1)
    • The triangle is traced to Pingala/Halayudha, al-Karaji, Omar Khayyam, Jia Xian and Yang Hui. Wikipedia: Pascal's triangle
  2. 1544

    Stifel prints the triangle as figurate numbers

    precursor

    Michael Stifel, Arithmetica integra, Nuremberg, 1544

    Stifel prints the table of binomial coefficients in Europe, as a table of figurate numbers.

    Evidence (2)
    • Arithmetica integra printed 1544. Open Library, Arithmetica integra
    • Stifel described the triangle in 1544 as a table of figurate numbers; Tartaglia published six rows in 1556. English Wikipedia, Pascal's triangle
  3. 1654

    Pascal composes the treatise

    precursor

    Blaise Pascal, treatise on the arithmetical triangle, composed 1654, not printed until 1665

    Pascal wrote his treatise in 1654 alongside his letters with Fermat but it remained in manuscript for years.

    Evidence (1)
    • Page images of the treatise are catalogued as 1654 in a Cambridge digital facsimile link on the Wikipedia page; print date is 1665. Wikipedia: Pascal's triangle
  4. 1665

    Traite du triangle arithmetique printed

    first statement

    Blaise Pascal, Traite du triangle arithmetique, avec quelques autres petits traitez, Paris, 1665 (posthumous)

    Pascal's systematic treatise on the triangle, with properties of the coefficients and proofs, appeared in print three years after his death.

    Evidence (2)
    • Archive.org record dates the Traite to 1665. archive.org item bub_gb_UqgUAAAAQAAJ
    • MacTutor calls it the most important work on the triangle and not the first to study it. MacTutor: Blaise Pascal
  5. 1665

    Using the triangle to divide stakes

    first application

    Pascal, Traite du triangle arithmetique, 1665 (usage section)

    Pascal applied identities of the triangle, including early explicit inductive proofs, to give a closed binomial formula for dividing the stakes.

    Evidence (1)
    • The problem of points was a main motivating example in the 1665 treatise; identities of the triangle with inductive proofs gave the formula. Wikipedia: Problem of points
  6. 1708

    Montmort calls it 'Mr. Pascal's table for combinations'

    naming

    Pierre Remond de Montmort, Essay d'analyse sur les jeux de hazard, Paris, 1708

    Montmort attaches Pascal's name to the triangle; de Moivre followed in 1730.

    Dating or attribution note

    Wikipedia's account of the naming was read; the passage in Montmort's book itself was not checked.

    Evidence (2)
    • Triangle named for Pascal by Montmort (1708) and de Moivre (1730). English Wikipedia, Pascal's triangle
    • Essay d'analyse sur les jeux de hazard, 1708. Open Library
  7. 1987

    Edwards' monograph on the arithmetical triangle

    consolidation

    A. W. F. Edwards, Pascal's Arithmetical Triangle, Griffin, 1987

    A modern historical monograph tracing the triangle across cultures up to Pascal and after.

    Evidence (2)
    • Pascal's Arithmetical Triangle, 1987. Open Library
    • Wikipedia cites Edwards for the European history of the triangle. English Wikipedia, Pascal's triangle
