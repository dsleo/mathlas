1953 · Combinatorics

Roth's theorem: Fourier-analytic method for 3-term progressions

A circle-method style Fourier argument with a density-increment iteration shows dense integer sets contain three-term progressions.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1953

    On certain sets of integers

    first statement

    Roth, On certain sets of integers, Journal of the London Mathematical Society 28 (1953) 104-109

    Roth's public statement that dense sets of integers contain three-term progressions, by a circle-method argument.

    Evidence (2)
    • Title, journal, volume, pages and year verified Crossref
    • Case k=3 established in 1953 via an adaptation of the Hardy-Littlewood circle method Wikipedia: Szemeredi's theorem
  2. 1953

    Circle method applied to progressions (same paper)

    first application

    Roth, On certain sets of integers, JLMS 28 (1953) 104-109 (as above)

    The same paper applies the circle method to the progression problem, the first purposeful use on the target.

    Evidence (1)
    • Same publication; circle-method argument Wikipedia: Szemeredi's theorem
  3. 1975

    Szemeredi's theorem for all k

    key development

    Szemeredi, On sets of integers containing k elements in arithmetic progression, Acta Arithmetica 27 (1975) 199-245

    Extends the 3-term result to all k, using a combinatorial argument.

    Evidence (2)
    • Title, journal, volume, pages and year verified Crossref
    • Szemeredi proved the general case in 1975 Wikipedia: Szemeredi's theorem
  4. 2001

    New proof combining Fourier analysis and combinatorics

    key development

    Gowers, A new proof of Szemeredi's theorem, GAFA 11 (2001) 465-588

    Combines Fourier analysis with combinatorial arguments in a new proof, which later work built on.

    Evidence (2)
    • Title, journal, volume, pages and year verified Crossref
    • Gowers's 2001 proof uses Fourier analysis and combinatorics Wikipedia: Szemeredi's theorem
  5. 2006

    Tao and Vu's textbook 'Additive combinatorics'

    consolidation

    T. Tao, V. H. Vu, Additive Combinatorics, Cambridge Studies in Advanced Mathematics 105, Cambridge University Press, 2006

    A textbook on additive combinatorics that includes the three-term progression results, of which Roth's theorem is the first.

    Dating or attribution note

    Textbook consolidation of the wider theory, not the first systematic treatment of Roth's method. Chapter coverage of 3-term progressions was not read. The 1966 Halberstam-Roth 'Sequences' book was checked and does not name the progression result in its title.

    Evidence (1)
