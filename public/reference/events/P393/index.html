1878 · Algebra

Geometric (Clifford) algebra

Clifford fused Hamilton's quaternions and Grassmann's exterior algebra into a single geometric algebra.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1844

    Grassmann's extension theory

    precursor

    Grassmann, Die lineale Ausdehnungslehre, 1844

    Grassmann created exterior algebra and briefly noted a second product combining its elements, one root of the geometric product.

    Evidence (2)
    • Archive.org record: Die Lineale Ausdehnungslehre, 1844. Internet Archive
    • States the geometric product was first briefly mentioned by Grassmann. Wikipedia: Geometric algebra
  2. 1853

    Hamilton's systematic quaternion lectures

    precursor

    Hamilton, Lectures on Quaternions, 1853

    Hamilton's quaternion algebra, the other ingredient fused by Clifford, was set out systematically in this book.

    Evidence (2)
    • Archive.org record: Lectures on quaternions, 1853. Internet Archive
    • Says Hamilton discovered the quaternions. MacTutor biography of Hamilton
  3. 1871

    Clifford's biquaternions

    precursor

    Clifford, Preliminary sketch of biquaternions, Proc. London Math. Soc. s1-4 (issue 1, 1871) 381-395

    Clifford generalised quaternions to biquaternions for motions in non-Euclidean spaces, an earlier step towards his algebras.

    Evidence (3)
    • Dates the biquaternion paper to 1873. MacTutor biography of Clifford
    • Crossref record for the paper (volume-level date 1871). Crossref
    • Crossref: 'Preliminary Sketch of Biquaternions', Proc. London Math. Soc. s1-4, issue 1, pp. 381-395, print date 1871. Crossref 10.1112/plms/s1-4.1.381
  4. 1878

    Clifford unifies Grassmann and Hamilton

    first statement

    Clifford, Applications of Grassmann's Extensive Algebra, American Journal of Mathematics 1, 350-358, 1878

    Clifford defined the algebra now named after him as a union of Grassmann's exterior algebra and Hamilton's quaternions.

    Evidence (3)
    • Record: Am. J. Math. 1 (1878) 350, Clifford. Crossref
    • Says he introduced Clifford algebras in this 1878 paper. MacTutor biography of Clifford
    • Says Clifford joined Grassmann's exterior algebra to Hamilton's quaternions in 1878. MacTutor biography of Grassmann
  5. 1878

    'Geometric algebras' as Clifford's own term

    naming

    Same paper (Am. J. Math. 1, 1878)

    Clifford himself called his algebras geometric algebras, the name later revived.

    Dating or attribution note

    Same paper as the first statement; the naming claim rests on Wikipedia only and the paper text was not read.

    Evidence (1)
    • Notes that Clifford himself chose to call them geometric algebras. Wikipedia: Geometric algebra
  6. 1960

    Hestenes revives geometric algebra for physics

    key development

    Hestenes, Space-Time Algebra (1960s)

    In the 1960s Hestenes advocated the term and framework for relativistic physics, ending a long neglect.

    Evidence (2)
    • Says the term was repopularised in the 1960s by Hestenes. Wikipedia: Geometric algebra
    • Crossref lists a 2015 Springer reissue of Space-Time Algebra. Crossref
  7. 1984

    Hestenes-Sobczyk textbook

    consolidation

    Hestenes and Sobczyk, Clifford Algebra to Geometric Calculus, Reidel, 1984

    A systematic monograph presenting geometric algebra and calculus as a unified language.

    Evidence (1)
    • Archive.org record: 1984. Internet Archive
