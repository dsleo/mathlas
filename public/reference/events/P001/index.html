820 · Algebra

Algebra as a discipline (al-Khwarizmi, al-Jabr)

Problem-solving with unknowns is organised around a small set of normal-form equations with systematic geometric justification, giving algebra its own objects and rules.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1,800 BCE–1,600 BCE

    Old Babylonian algebraic procedures

    precursor

    Old Babylonian tablets

    Quadratic-type problems were already solved by explicit procedures long before the Baghdad school.

    Evidence (1)
    • Hoyrup 2002 chapters on Old Babylonian algebra Hoyrup, Old Babylonian Algebra (2002)
  2. 250

    Diophantus: algebraic solution with symbolic abbreviations

    precursor

    Diophantus, Arithmetica

    Arithmetica solves numerical problems with a named unknown and simplification to standard form, a Greek precursor concerned with number theory.

    Evidence (1)
    • Wikipedia Arithmetica (in Diophantus): three-stage method like medieval Arabic algebra; no classification into six types Wikipedia, Diophantus
  3. 628

    Brahmagupta: first clear quadratic formula in Brahmasphutasiddhanta

    precursor

    Brahmagupta, Brahmasphutasiddhanta, 628

    An Indian text gives rules for quadratic equations a century before al-Jabr, in verse and with negative numbers.

    Evidence (2)
    • Wikipedia Brahmagupta: first clear description of quadratic formula; Brahmasphutasiddhanta dated 628 Wikipedia, Brahmagupta
    • Wikipedia Al-Jabr: Islamic mathematicians unlike Hindus did not treat negative numbers Wikipedia, Al-Jabr
  4. 820–825

    Al-Jabr: first book to teach algebra for its own sake

    first statement

    Al-Khwarizmi, Kitab al-mukhtasar fi hisab al-jabr wa al-muqabala, Baghdad c. 820

    Al-Khwarizmi classifies equations into six normal forms with their solutions and geometric proofs, treating algebra as an independent subject.

    Evidence (3)
    • Wikipedia Al-Jabr: written in Baghdad around 820; first text to teach elementary algebra for its own sake; six types of quadratic equations Wikipedia, Al-Jabr
    • MacTutor Al-Khwarizmi: algebra treatise in Baghdad (born about 780, died about 850) MacTutor, Al-Khwarizmi
    • Gandz 1936 on the sources of al-Khwarizmi algebra (Osiris) Gandz, Osiris 1 (1936)
  5. 820–825

    Title gives the word algebra

    naming

    Al-Jabr; Latin Liber algebrae et almucabala

    The Arabic term al-jabr in the title, restoring a subtracted term to the other side, is the origin of the word algebra.

    Evidence (2)
    • Wikipedia Al-Jabr: title is the ultimate etymology of the word algebra; al-jabr and al-muqabala defined Wikipedia, Al-Jabr
    • Gandz 1926, The origin of the term algebra (Amer. Math. Monthly) Gandz (1926)
  6. 1145

    Robert of Chester translates Al-Jabr into Latin

    key development

    Robert of Chester, Liber algebrae, 1145

    The Latin translation makes algebra the standard university text in Europe until the sixteenth century.

    Evidence (2)
    • Wikipedia Al-Jabr: translated into Latin by Robert of Chester in 1145; principal textbook until the sixteenth century Wikipedia, Al-Jabr
    • Wikipedia Al-Khwarizmi: Al-Jabr translated by Robert of Chester in 1145 Wikipedia, Al-Khwarizmi
