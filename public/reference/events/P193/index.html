1593 · Algebra

Viete: trigonometry applied to algebra (van Roomen's problem)

A degree-45 equation posed by van Roomen was solved by recognising a trigonometric identity.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1591

    Viete's In artem analyticam isagoge

    precursor

    François Viète, In artem analyticam isagoge, Tours 1591

    Viete introduces letters for unknowns and parameters, the symbolic algebra that his answer to van Roomen relies on.

    Evidence (2)
    • MacTutor places the Isagoge among Viete's main algebra publications of the early 1590s. MacTutor, biography of Viete
    • Internet Archive record: Francisci Vietae In artem analyticem isagoge, Tours, Iamettius Mettayer, 1591. Internet Archive item bub_gb_BWTyywN39KEC
  2. 1593

    Van Roomen poses a degree-45 equation

    precursor

    Adriaan van Roomen, Ideae mathematicae pars prima, 1593

    Public challenge to mathematicians.

    Evidence (3)
    • Roomen proposed the problem in 1593. MacTutor, Viete biography
    • 1593 book scan. Internet Archive bub_gb_qinevzxnHFoC
    • The scan prints the problem with the coefficients 45, 3795, 95634 ... (OCR garbled but the numbers and the heading are legible). Van Roomen, Ideae mathematicae pars prima, Antwerp 1593 (archive.org full text, read)
  3. 1593

    Viete's theorems on multiple angles in Variorum responsorum liber VIII

    first statement

    Viete, Variorum de rebus mathematicis responsorum liber VIII, Tours: Mettayer, 1593, chapter X (Progressio linearum rectarum quae circumferentiis circuli subtenduntur)

    Viete states, without proof, how the sides of right triangles with an angle that is a multiple (double, triple, quadruple, quintuple) of a given acute angle are polynomials in the sides of the first triangle, the algebra of angle multiplication.

    Dating or attribution note

    Read in the Internet Archive scan (chapter X, theorem I and the rules for the double to quintuple angle). Viete says there that he has condensed the analytic part of his Canon mathematicus (first issued in 1579) into the 'mystery of angular sections'; the 1579 Canon was not read, so an earlier statement is not excluded. Alexander Anderson (1615 and later) says Viete had published only bare theorems, in book VIII and in the reply to van Roomen, and supplied the proofs himself. The statement applies the idea to triangles, not yet to a polynomial equation: that is the 1595 reply.

    Evidence (3)
    • Chapter X, theorem I: for right triangles whose acute angles stand in a ratio of numbers, the hypotenuse, base and perpendicular of the multiple-angle triangle are given as powers and combinations of those of the first, with explicit rules for the double, triple, quadruple and quintuple angle. Viete, Variorum de rebus mathematicis responsorum liber VIII (1593), archive.org full text, read
    • Internet Archive record: Francisci Vietae Variorum de rebus mathematicis responsorum liber 8, Tours, Mettayer, 1593; the preface is dated May 1593. Internet Archive item bub_gb_SM2Az-oKgoEC
    • Anderson: apart from bare theorems without demonstration in book VIII of the Variorum and in the reply to Adriaan van Roomen, Viete published nothing in this genre; Anderson supplied the proofs. Viete, Opera mathematica, ed. van Schooten (1646), Anderson's preface text (archive.org full text, read)
  4. 1595

    Trigonometry used on an algebraic equation

    first application

    Viete, Ad problema quod omnibus mathematicis totius orbis construendum proposuit Adrianus Romanus, Responsum, Paris: Mettayer, 1595

    Viete recognises van Roomen's degree-45 equation as a relation between chords of multiple arcs, solves it by the angular-section theorems, and gives the root as a chord.

    Evidence (4)
    • MacTutor: Viete solved the problem by realising there was an underlying trigonometric relation; his answer was published in 1595. MacTutor, Viete biography (re-read in review)
    • Reference list gives the title 'Ad problema quod omnibus mathematicis totius orbis construendum proposuit Adrianus Romanus Francisci Vietae Responsum' (Paris: Iametium Mettayer, 1595). Oliveira and Haddad, Adriaan van Roomen e sua correspondencia, Rev. Bras. Hist. Mat. 18 (2020)
    • The reply, reprinted in the Opera, quotes van Roomen's problem word for word, then states that the construction is taught by the Analysis of angular sections and lists theorems on right triangles with added angles. Viete, Opera mathematica, ed. van Schooten (1646), pp. 305-313 (archive.org full text, read)
    • MacTutor notes Viete knew formulas for sin nx. MacTutor, Trigonometric functions
  5. 1615

    Ad angularium sectionum analyticen theoremata, proofs by Anderson

    key development

    Viete, Ad angularium sectionum analyticen theoremata katholikotera, ed. and with demonstrations by Alexander Anderson, Paris: Olivier de Varennes, 1615

    Anderson publishes the angular-section theorems found by Viete 'without any demonstration', now proved, and refers readers to Viete's reply to van Roomen for examples.

    Evidence (2)
    • Title page: Ad angularium sectionum analyticen theoremata katholikotera, a Francisco Vieta primum excogitata, at absque vlla demonstratione ad nos transmissa, iam tandem demonstrationibus confirmata, opera et studio Alexandri Andersoni Scoti, Paris, 1615; the text refers to 'Vietae responsum ad problema Romani' for examples. Internet Archive item bub_gb_q58_zsoFgzIC (full text, read)
    • MacTutor lists Viete's posthumous work on angular sections edited by Anderson (the page gives no date). MacTutor, Viete biography
  6. 1646

    Collected Opera mathematica of Viete

    consolidation

    Francois Viete, Francisci Vietae Opera mathematica, 1646

    Collected edition of Viete's mathematical works, which brings together the trigonometric-algebra material of the event.

    Evidence (2)
    • Open Library record of the 1646 Opera mathematica of Viete. Open Library work record
    • Internet Archive record: Francisci Vietae Opera mathematica, in unum volumen congesta, ac recognita opera atque studio Francisci a Schooten, Elzevir, 1646. Internet Archive item bub_gb_25iHrtJpy9oC
