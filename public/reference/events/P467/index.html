1954 · Dynamics

KAM theory: stability of Hamiltonian systems

Kolmogorov, Arnold and Moser show that most quasi-periodic motions of perturbed integrable Hamiltonian systems survive, using an iterative scheme that beats small divisors.

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1953

    Kolmogorov's first dynamical-systems papers

    precursor

    Kolmogorov, two four-page papers on dynamical systems and Hamiltonian dynamics, 1953-54

    MacTutor dates the start of KAM theory to two short Kolmogorov papers of 1953 and 1954; the 1953 paper is treated as a precursor.

    Dating or attribution note

    Individual 1953 paper not checked in a bibliographic record.

    Evidence (1)
    • MacTutor says two papers by Kolmogorov appeared in 1953 and 1954, marking the beginning of KAM theory MacTutor, Andrey Kolmogorov biography
  2. 1954

    Kolmogorov's persistence of conditionally periodic motions

    first statement

    Kolmogorov, On conservation of conditionally periodic motions under small perturbation of the Hamiltonian, Doklady Akademii Nauk SSSR, 1954

    Kolmogorov states that most quasi-periodic motions of a nearly integrable Hamiltonian system survive small perturbation; the 1954 ICM address presents the programme.

    Evidence (2)
    • Reprint record in Selected Works of A. N. Kolmogorov (1991) for the 1954 paper Crossref
    • Wikipedia cites the 1954 Doklady paper as the original breakthrough; MacTutor dates the ICM address to 1954 Wikipedia KAM theorem; MacTutor Kolmogorov
  3. 1962

    Moser's invariant curves for area-preserving maps

    key development

    Moser, On invariant curves of area-preserving mappings of an annulus, Nachrichten der Akademie der Wissenschaften in Göttingen, 1962

    Moser proves persistence of invariant curves for twist maps, turning Kolmogorov's programme into a rigorous method usable on general Hamiltonian systems.

    Evidence (2)
    • MacTutor describes the 1962 Göttingen paper and the Moser twist theorem MacTutor, Jürgen Moser biography
    • Wikipedia: Moser proved the smooth twist-map case in 1962 Wikipedia, KAM theorem
  4. 1963

    Arnold's proof for analytic Hamiltonian systems

    first application

    Arnold, Small denominators and problems of stability of motion in classical and celestial mechanics, Russian Mathematical Surveys, 1963

    Arnold applies the iteration to analytic Hamiltonian systems, including celestial mechanics, the application that gives the theorem its name.

    Evidence (2)
    • Article record, Russian Math. Surveys 1963 Crossref
    • MacTutor says Arnold's doctorate on small denominators and stability was awarded in 1963 MacTutor, Vladimir Arnold biography
  5. 1973

    Stable and Random Motions in Dynamical Systems

    consolidation

    Moser, Stable and Random Motions in Dynamical Systems, Princeton University Press, 1973

    Moser's monograph gives a systematic account of stability and KAM-type results for dynamical systems.

    Evidence (1)
    • OpenLibrary record, Princeton University Press 1973 OpenLibrary search
