Ibn al-Haytham and Ibn Sinan: quadrature

Sums of powers were used for areas and volumes, and Archimedean parabola quadrature was generalised.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 250 BCE

    Archimedes' Quadrature of the Parabola

    precursor

    Archimedes, Quadrature of the Parabola

    Found the area of a parabola segment by exhaustion, the Greek model for later Arabic work.

    Evidence (2)
    • In the Quadrature of the Parabola Archimedes finds the area of a segment cut off by any chord. MacTutor, Archimedes biography
    • Overview of the work and its exhaustion proof. English Wikipedia, Quadrature of the Parabola
  2. 870–901

    Thabit ibn Qurra: parabola and paraboloid by integral sums

    first statement

    Thabit ibn Qurra, treatises on the measurement of the parabola and paraboloid

    Computed parabola and paraboloid measures using upper and lower sums with unequal subdivisions, apparently without Archimedes' own treatises.

    Evidence (1)
    • Thabit effectively integrated sqrt(x) using upper and lower integral sums and exhaustion, dividing the interval into unequal parts. MacTutor, Thabit biography
  3. 925–946

    Ibrahim ibn Sinan: simpler parabola quadrature

    key development

    Ibrahim ibn Sinan, Quadrature of the parabola (10th century)

    He gave a shorter proof of the parabola's quadrature, generalising Archimedes without having his text.

    Evidence (2)
    • Ibrahim ibn Sinan advanced the quadrature of the parabola and integration theory, generalising Archimedes' work. English Wikipedia, Ibrahim ibn Sinan
    • Ibrahim ibn Sinan lived c. 908-946 according to the Biographical Encyclopedia of Astronomers entry Van Brummelen entry, Biographical Encyclopedia of Astronomers
  4. 1000–1039

    Ibn al-Haytham: sum of fourth powers and paraboloid volume

    first application

    Ibn al-Haytham, On the measurement of the paraboloid (early 11th century); studied by Rashed in Les mathematiques infinitesimales, vol. 2 (1993)

    Derived a formula for the sum of fourth powers and used sums of powers to compute the volume of a paraboloid.

    Evidence (3)
    • Alhazen derived the sum of fourth powers, used it for the paraboloid volume, and could have generalised to higher powers. English Wikipedia, Ibn al-Haytham
    • Modern edition and study of Ibn al-Haytham's infinitesimal mathematics, volume 2, first published 1993. Open Library: Rashed, Les mathematiques infinitesimales du IXe au XIe siecle
    • MacTutor lists Rashed, 'Ibn al-Haytham and the measurement of the paraboloid', J. Hist. Arabic Sci. 5 (1981). MacTutor, Ibn al-Haytham
  5. 1993

    Rashed's Les mathematiques infinitesimales

    consolidation

    Roshdi Rashed, Les mathematiques infinitesimales du IXe au XIe siecle, vol. 2, 1993

    Monograph on Arabic infinitesimal mathematics of the ninth to eleventh centuries, covering the quadrature work discussed in this event.

    Evidence (1)
    • Open Library record of Rashed's 1993 monograph. Open Library work record
