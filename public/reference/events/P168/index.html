Graunt: life tables

Bills of mortality were analysed to build the first life table; Halley refined it.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1662

    Graunt's Observations on the Bills of Mortality

    first statement

    John Graunt, Natural and Political Observations ... upon the Bills of Mortality, London, 1662

    Graunt reworked London burial records into an estimate of survival to each age, usually called the first life table.

    Dating or attribution note

    Wikipedia says copies were presented to the Royal Society on 5 February 1661, probably Old Style, i.e. 1662. Whether Graunt's 'table' is a true life table is a modern characterisation.

    Evidence (2)
    • Archive.org lists an edition dated 1662. archive.org item natural-and-political-observations-mentioned-in-a-following-index-and-made-upon-
    • He is credited with the first life table; bills of mortality did not give age at death. Wikipedia: John Graunt
  2. 1667

    Huygens brothers interpolate Graunt's table

    precursor

    Christiaan and Lodewijk Huygens, correspondence, 1667 (unpublished)

    The brothers distinguished mean from median lifetime and fitted a smooth curve to Graunt's table, but the exchange stayed private.

    Evidence (1)
    • Huygens correspondence of 1667 discussed Graunt's tables; unpublished. Wikipedia: History of probability
  3. 1690

    Petty's Political Arithmetick published

    naming

    William Petty, Political Arithmetick, London 1690 (written 1676)

    Petty's book gives the name political arithmetic to quantitative study of population and state, the Graunt-Petty tradition.

    Evidence (2)
    • Internet Archive item titled Political Arithmetick (1676-1690). Internet Archive political-arithmetick
    • Wikipedia describes Graunt and Petty as joint developers of early statistical and census methods. English Wikipedia, John Graunt
  4. 1693

    Halley's Breslau mortality table

    key development

    Edmond Halley, An estimate of the degrees of the mortality of mankind, Phil. Trans. 17(196), 596-610, 1693

    Halley used actual age-at-death records for Breslau to give a better table and priced annuities.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref DOI 10.1098/rstl.1693.0007 gives title, Philosophical Transactions vol. 17, 1693. Crossref
    • MacTutor: mortality tables for Breslau published 1693, among earliest to relate mortality and age. MacTutor: Edmond Halley
  5. 1725

    De Moivre's Annuities upon Lives

    consolidation

    Abraham de Moivre, Annuities upon Lives, London 1725

    The first mathematical treatise on life annuities builds a formula-based theory on mortality tables of Graunt and Halley.

    Evidence (1)
    • Internet Archive scan of the 1725 edition of Annuities upon Lives. Internet Archive bim_eighteenth-century_annuities-upon-lives-or_moivre-abraham-de_1725
