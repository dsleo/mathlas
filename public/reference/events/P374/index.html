1888 · Logic & foundations

Peano arithmetic axioms

Dedekind and Peano gave axioms characterizing the natural numbers, Peano in formal logical notation.

Also known as: Dedekind-Peano axioms

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1860

    Grassmann: arithmetic from successor and induction

    precursor

    Grassmann, Lehrbuch der Arithmetik (early 1860s)

    Grassmann showed in the 1860s that many arithmetic facts follow from the successor operation and induction.

    Evidence (1)
    • Wikipedia credits Grassmann's 1860s work with showing arithmetic derives from successor and induction. Wikipedia: Peano axioms
  2. 1881

    Peirce's axiomatization of natural-number arithmetic

    precursor

    Peirce, On the Logic of Number, Amer. J. Math. 4, 1881

    Peirce gave an early axiomatization of the natural numbers.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref: American Journal of Mathematics 4, 1881. Crossref
    • Wikipedia says Peirce axiomatized natural-number arithmetic in 1881. Wikipedia: Peano axioms
  3. 1888

    Dedekind: Was sind und was sollen die Zahlen

    first statement

    Dedekind, Was sind und was sollen die Zahlen?, Vieweg, 1888

    Dedekind characterized the natural numbers by a simply infinite system with a successor map and induction.

    Evidence (3)
    • Wikipedia says Dedekind proposed an axiomatization of natural-number arithmetic in 1888. Wikipedia: Peano axioms
    • MacTutor cites Was sind und was sollen die Zahlen? as 1888. MacTutor Biographies/Dedekind
    • zbMATH 20.0049.05: Dedekind, 'Was sind und was sollen die Zahlen?', Braunschweig 1888. zbMATH Open, document 20.0049.05
  4. 1889

    Peano: Arithmetices principia, nova methodo exposita

    key development

    Peano, Arithmetices principia, nova methodo exposita, Turin, 1889

    Peano restated the axioms in a formal logical notation, the version that carries his name.

    Evidence (4)
    • zbMATH record dated 1889. zbMATH Open
    • Internet Archive record dated 1889. Internet Archive
    • MacTutor calls the pamphlet a landmark in logic and foundations. MacTutor Biographies/Peano
    • zbMATH 21.0051.02: Peano, 'Arithmetices principia, nova methodo exposita', Torino 1889. zbMATH Open, document 21.0051.02
  5. 1922

    Ingraham titles a paper on Peano's postulates

    naming

    Ingraham, M. H., A modification of Peano's postulates for positive integers, 1922

    Earliest zbMATH title found using the Peano postulates name.

    Evidence (1)
    • zbMATH record: title "A modification of Peano's postulates for positive integers", Ingraham, 1922 zbMATH Open doc 2600416
  6. 1930

    Landau publishes a full development of the number systems from axioms

    consolidation

    Landau, E., Grundlagen der Analysis, 1930

    Monograph developing the natural numbers and larger systems from axioms in the Peano style.

    Dating or attribution note

    Chosen as a monograph on axiomatic number systems; Peano's own 1889 treatise is already in the record as a first statement, and Peano 1895 Formulaire not checked.

    Evidence (1)
    • Open Library lists the book with first publication year 1930 Open Library works OL11273905W
  7. 1931

    Goedel's incompleteness theorems for an arithmetic system

    key development

    Goedel, Ueber formal unentscheidbare Saetze der Principia Mathematica und verwandter Systeme I, Monatsh. Math. Phys. 38, 1931

    Goedel proved that a formal arithmetic system of this kind is incomplete, making Peano-style axioms the central object of metamathematics.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref: Monatshefte fuer Mathematik und Physik 38, 1931. Crossref
    • Wikipedia says the axioms were used in metamathematical research on consistency and completeness. Wikipedia: Peano axioms
  8. 1936

    Gentzen's consistency proof for arithmetic

    key development

    Gentzen, Die Widerspruchsfreiheit der reinen Zahlentheorie, Math. Ann. 112, 1936

    Gentzen proved the consistency of first-order arithmetic using transfinite induction.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref: Mathematische Annalen 112, 1936. Crossref
