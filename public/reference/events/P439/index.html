1882 · Geometry

Transcendence of pi and the impossibility of squaring the circle

Lindemann's proof that pi is transcendental implied no ruler-and-compass squaring of the circle, resolving a geometric problem by an algebraic argument.

Also known as: quadrature of the circle impossibility

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1837

    Constructible lengths shown to be algebraic

    precursor

    Wantzel, Recherches sur les moyens de reconnaitre si un probleme de geometrie peut se resoudre avec la regle et le compas, J. Math. Pures Appl. 2, 1837

    Wantzel showed that lengths obtainable by ruler and compass must be algebraic, so transcendence of pi would rule out squaring the circle.

    Evidence (1)
    • Narrates Wantzel's 1837 result that constructible lengths are algebraic, so a transcendental pi would make the construction impossible. Wikipedia: Squaring the circle
  2. 1873

    Hermite proves e is transcendental

    precursor

    Hermite, Sur la fonction exponentielle, Comptes Rendus 77, 1873

    Hermite published the first proof that e is transcendental, introducing the method that Lindemann adapted nine years later.

    Evidence (2)
    • States that Hermite published the first proof of the transcendence of e in 1873 and that Lindemann used similar methods in 1882. MacTutor biography of Hermite
    • Crossref lists the memoir 'Sur la fonction exponentielle' in Hermite's Oeuvres (a 2009 reprint; the original 1873 Comptes Rendus run is not in Crossref). Crossref
  3. 1882

    Lindemann proves pi is transcendental

    first statement

    Lindemann, Ueber die Zahl pi, Mathematische Annalen 20, 213-225, 1882

    Lindemann extended Hermite's method to show pi is transcendental, which together with algebraicity of constructible lengths ends the squaring problem.

    Evidence (4)
    • Record: Mathematische Annalen 20 (1882) 213-225, Lindemann, 'Ueber die Zahl pi'. Crossref
    • Credits Lindemann as first to prove pi transcendental, answering the classical squaring problem. MacTutor biography of Lindemann
    • Dates the proof of impossibility to 1882 via the Lindemann-Weierstrass theorem. Wikipedia: Squaring the circle
    • Digitized original 1882 paper on pi DigiZeitschriften
  4. 1885

    Weierstrass gives the full theorem

    key development

    Weierstrass, Zu Lindemann's Abhandlung 'Ueber die Ludolph'sche Zahl', Sitzungsber. Berlin, 1885

    Weierstrass supplied a complete proof of the general statement now called the Lindemann-Weierstrass theorem, filling gaps in Lindemann's argument.

    Evidence (2)
    • Says Lindemann allowed algebraic numbers into Hermite's work in 1882 and Weierstrass shortly afterwards obtained the full result. Wikipedia: Lindemann%E2%80%93Weierstrass theorem
    • Crossref lists the paper in 'Pi: A Source Book' (reprint; original year not given by the record). Crossref
  5. 1893

    Hilbert's short proof

    key development

    Hilbert, Ueber die Transcendenz der Zahlen e und pi, Math. Ann. 43, 216-219, 1893

    Hilbert gave a much shorter proof of the transcendence of e and pi that became the textbook version.

    Evidence (1)
    • Record: Mathematische Annalen 43 (1893) 216-219. Crossref
  6. 1913

    Hobson's 'Squaring the circle: a history of the problem'

    consolidation

    E. W. Hobson, Squaring the circle: a history of the problem, Cambridge University Press, 1913

    A book-length history titled after the problem; the earliest book with that title found in zbMATH.

    Dating or attribution note

    Historical rather than a proof treatment. It names the problem but its coverage of Lindemann's 1882 proof was not read, so it is a weak consolidation. Weber's 1897 survey is a candidate too, but its scope on pi was not read.

    Evidence (1)
    • zbMATH record 'Squaring the circle. A history of the problem', Hobson, Cambridge University Press (1913), Zbl 44.0050.02 and 45.1215.12 zbMATH Open API
