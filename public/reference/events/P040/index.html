1956 · Number theory

Modularity of elliptic curves and Fermat's Last Theorem

A counterexample to Fermat would give an elliptic curve contradicting modularity, and modularity of semistable curves is proved with Galois deformation theory and Hecke algebras.

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1955

    Taniyama's problem list distributed at the Tokyo-Nikko symposium

    precursor

    Y. Taniyama, Problems of number theory (mimeographed), International Symposium on Algebraic Number Theory, Tokyo and Nikko, September 1955

    Taniyama hands out a mimeographed list of 36 problems, including the question linking elliptic curves and automorphic forms.

    Dating or attribution note

    Mimeographed and circulated to symposium participants, so a precursor under schema rule 3; the first print found is Sugaku 1956. Not read.

    Evidence (2)
    • Taniyama distributed mimeographs of 'Problems of Number Theory' to participants of the 1955 symposium in Tokyo and Nikko. Wikipedia, Taniyama's problems
    • MacTutor: Taniyama posed two problems at the 1955 Tokyo-Nikko symposium on algebraic number theory. MacTutor Biography: Yutaka Taniyama
  2. 1956

    Taniyama's problem on elliptic curves and modular forms

    first statement

    Y. Taniyama, Problem 12, Sugaku 7 (1956) 269 (in Japanese); the list of 36 problems was distributed in English at the 1955 Tokyo-Nikko symposium

    Taniyama states a preliminary version of the modularity conjecture as one of 36 unsolved problems; later refined with Shimura.

    Evidence (2)
    • Wikipedia: Taniyama stated a preliminary version at the 1955 symposium as problem twelve of thirty-six Wikipedia, Modularity theorem
    • Wikipedia: Taniyama, Problem 12, Sugaku 7 (1956) 269. Wikipedia, Taniyama's problems
  3. 1967

    Weil rediscovers the conjecture and gives evidence

    key development

    A. Weil, Ueber die Bestimmung Dirichletscher Reihen durch Funktionalgleichungen, Math. Ann. 168 (1967) 149-156

    Weil shows the conjecture would follow from functional equations of twisted L-series and that the conductor is the level, the first serious evidence for it.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref: Ueber die Bestimmung Dirichletscher Reihen durch Funktionalgleichungen, Math. Ann. 168, 149-156 (1967). Crossref API
    • Wikipedia: Weil rediscovered the conjecture in 1967 and gave the first serious evidence. Wikipedia, Modularity theorem
  4. 1986

    Frey's link between a Fermat counterexample and modularity

    first application

    Frey, Links between stable elliptic curves and certain diophantine equations, Annales Universitatis Saraviensis, 1986

    Frey shows that a counterexample to Fermat would give a semistable elliptic curve that cannot be modular, making modularity a route to FLT.

    Evidence (3)
    • zbMATH record, Ann. Univ. Sarav. Ser. Math. 1 (1986), 40 p. zbMATH Open
    • Wikipedia: Frey suggested in 1986 that the conjecture implies FLT Wikipedia, Modularity theorem
    • zbMATH Zbl 0586.10010: Frey, Links between stable elliptic curves and certain diophantine equations, Ann. Univ. Sarav. Ser. Math. 1 (1986), 40 p. zbMATH Open API
  5. 1987

    Serre's epsilon conjecture

    key development

    Serre, Sur les représentations modulaires de degré 2 de Gal(Q̄/Q), Duke Mathematical Journal, 1987

    Serre identifies the missing link in Frey's argument, the epsilon conjecture, which made the FLT route workable.

    Evidence (2)
    • Article record, Duke Math. J. 1987 Crossref
    • Wikipedia: Serre identified the missing link in 1987 Wikipedia, Modularity theorem
  6. 1990

    Ribet's level-lowering theorem

    key development

    Ribet, On modular representations of Gal(Q̄/Q) arising from modular forms, Inventiones Mathematicae, 1990

    Ribet proves the epsilon conjecture (level lowering), completing the link from Frey's curve to a modular form.

    Evidence (2)
    • Article record, Invent. Math. 1990 Crossref
    • Wikipedia: Ribet completed the proof of the epsilon conjecture two years later Wikipedia, Modularity theorem
  7. 1995

    Wiles's proof of modularity for semistable curves

    key development

    Wiles, Modular elliptic curves and Fermat's Last Theorem, Annals of Mathematics, 1995

    Wiles proves modularity for semistable elliptic curves, enough to deduce FLT; the first version had a gap, repaired with Taylor.

    Evidence (2)
    • Article record, Annals 1995 Crossref
    • MacTutor: Wiles announced a proof in June 1993, and a subtle error was found when written up MacTutor, Andrew Wiles biography
  8. 1995

    Taylor-Wiles repair of the Hecke algebra argument

    key development

    Taylor and Wiles, Ring-theoretic properties of certain Hecke algebras, Annals of Mathematics, 1995

    Taylor and Wiles supply the Hecke-algebra step that repairs the gap in the 1993 argument, completing the 1995 proof.

    Evidence (1)
    • Article record, Annals 1995 Crossref
