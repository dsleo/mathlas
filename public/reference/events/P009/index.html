1846 · Number theory

Ideals and algebraic number theory

Failure of unique factorisation in rings of integers is repaired by introducing ideal elements, later recast as ideals, which become the central object.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1844

    Kummer shows unique factorisation can fail

    precursor

    E. E. Kummer, De numeris complexis qui unitatis radicibus et numeris integris realibus constant, Breslau, 1844

    Kummer publishes an example showing that factorisation into primes need not be unique in rings of cyclotomic integers.

    Dating or attribution note

    Printed as a Breslau Gratulationsschrift in 1844 (reprinted in Liouville's J. Math. 12, 1847). Wikipedia (Ideal number) says this is where the failure of unique factorisation in cyclotomic fields was first published; the scan itself was not read.

    Evidence (3)
    • Topic page says Kummer pointed to his 1844 example of non-uniqueness. MacTutor: Ring theory
    • Archive catalogue lists De numeris complexis (1844). Internet Archive scan
    • Wikipedia: Kummer first published the failure of unique factorization in cyclotomic fields in 1844 (Breslau Gratulationsschrift), reprinted 1847 in Liouville's journal. Wikipedia: Ideal number
  2. 1845

    Kummer explains ideal numbers to Kronecker in a letter

    precursor

    E. E. Kummer, letter to L. Kronecker, 18 October 1845 (private; original lost, partly printed by Hensel)

    Kummer first sets out his theory of ideal prime factors of complex numbers in a private letter, before any public statement.

    Evidence (1)
    • Dates the explanation of the new theory of ideal numbers to the letter to Kronecker of 18 October 1845. Lemmermeyer, Jacobi and Kummer's Ideal Numbers, Abh. Math. Semin. Univ. Hamb. 79 (2009) 165-187
  3. 1846

    Kummer, Zur Theorie der complexen Zahlen (Berlin Academy report)

    first statement

    E. E. Kummer, Zur Theorie der complexen Zahlen, Monatsber. Berlin Akad. 1846 (pp. 87-96); reprinted J. reine angew. Math. 35 (1847) 319-326

    Kummer announces, without proofs, that unique factorisation of cyclotomic integers is restored by introducing ideal complex numbers.

    Evidence (3)
    • Title, journal, volume 35, pages 319-326 confirmed, 1847 (the reprint). Crossref record 10.1515/crll.1847.35.319
    • Lists the academy report (Monatsber. Berlin, pp. 87-96) and its Crelle reprint 35 (1847) 319-326; quotes the passage where Kummer calls the new divisors ideale complexe Zahlen. Lemmermeyer, Jacobi and Kummer's Ideal Numbers (arXiv:1108.6066)
    • Topic page says he restored uniqueness in 1846 with ideal complex numbers. MacTutor: Ring theory
  4. 1846

    The term ideal coined by Kummer

    naming

    E. E. Kummer, Zur Theorie der complexen Zahlen, Monatsber. Berlin Akad. 1846 (pp. 87-96); reprinted J. reine angew. Math. 35 (1847) 319-326

    Kummer names his new elements ideal (complex) numbers.

    Evidence (3)
    • Article says the term ideal was invented by Kummer. English Wikipedia: Ernst Kummer
    • Topic page speaks of ideal complex numbers introduced in 1846. MacTutor: Ring theory
    • Quotes Kummer: imaginary divisors which I call ideale complexe Zahlen. Lemmermeyer, Jacobi and Kummer's Ideal Numbers (arXiv:1108.6066)
  5. 1847

    Kummer applies ideal numbers to Fermat's Last Theorem

    first application

    E. E. Kummer, Beweis des Fermat'schen Satzes der Unmöglichkeit von x^λ + y^λ = z^λ für eine unendliche Anzahl Primzahlen λ, Monatsber. Berlin Akad. 1847, 132-141, 305-319

    Kummer uses unique factorisation into ideal primes to prove Fermat's Last Theorem for regular prime exponents, a case that includes most exponents below 100.

    Evidence (3)
    • Topic page says he proved the theorem for all n below 100 except 37, 59, 67 and 74 in 1847. MacTutor: Ring theory
    • Biography calls his work fundamental for Fermat's Last Theorem and notes the 1857 Paris grand prize. MacTutor: Kummer biography
    • Kummer found a proof of FLT for a class of exponents in late March 1847 and sent it to Dirichlet on 11 April 1847; he had not mentioned FLT as a motive before. Lemmermeyer, Jacobi and Kummer's Ideal Numbers (arXiv:1108.6066)
  6. 1871

    Dedekind defines ideals and prime ideals

    consolidation

    R. Dedekind, Supplement X to Dirichlet, Vorlesungen ueber Zahlentheorie, 2nd ed., Braunschweig, 1871

    Dedekind recasts Kummer's ideal numbers as subrings absorbing multiplication, with prime ideals replacing primes.

    Evidence (3)
    • Topic page credits Dedekind with defining ideals and introducing prime ideals in 1871. MacTutor: Ring theory
    • Biography says ideals were introduced in supplements to later editions of Dirichlet's lectures. MacTutor: Dedekind biography
    • Avigad's translation of Dedekind's 1871 version of the theory of ideals (Supplement X, 2nd ed. 1871). J. Avigad, Dedekind's 1871 version of the theory of ideals
  7. 1897

    Hilbert, Zahlbericht on algebraic number fields

    consolidation

    D. Hilbert, Die Theorie der algebraischen Zahlkörper, Jahresbericht der Deutschen Mathematiker-Vereinigung 4 (1897) 175-546

    Hilbert's report organises the theory of algebraic number fields, with Dedekind's ideals as the basic tool, into the standard account for decades.

    Evidence (1)
    • 2017 edition article on Hilbert's Zahlbericht; the report appeared in 1897. Crossref record, Lemmermeyer, David Hilbert: Die Theorie der algebraischen Zahlkörper, Jahresbericht der DMV 120 (2017)
  8. 1921

    Noether, Idealtheorie in Ringbereichen

    key development

    E. Noether, Idealtheorie in Ringbereichen, Math. Ann. 83 (1921) 24-66

    Noether unifies ideal theory of polynomial rings and number rings under abstract commutative rings.

    Evidence (2)
    • Title, journal, volume 83, pages 24-66, 1921 confirmed. Crossref record 10.1007/bf01464225
    • Topic page says around 1921 she brought rings of polynomials and numbers under one abstract theory. MacTutor: Ring theory
