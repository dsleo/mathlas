1936 · Logic & foundations

Computability theory (Church-Turing)

Turing defined an abstract machine, computable numbers and a universal machine, answering the Entscheidungsproblem negatively.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1933

    Gödel and Herbrand formalise general recursive functions

    precursor

    Gödel, with Herbrand, gave a formal class of general recursive functions, an early candidate for computability.

    Dating or attribution note

    Lecture-based precursor; no public paper was checked, so this is a note, not a priority claim.

    Evidence (1)
    • Gödel with Herbrand formalised general recursive functions in 1933 Wikipedia, Church-Turing thesis (history section)
  2. 1936

    Church's lambda-definability thesis

    first statement

    Church, An Unsolvable Problem of Elementary Number Theory, American Journal of Mathematics 58 (1936) 345-363

    Church proposed that effectively calculable functions are exactly the lambda-definable (general recursive) functions.

    Evidence (2)
    • Church, An Unsolvable Problem of Elementary Number Theory, Amer. J. Math. 58 (1936) 345-363 Crossref DOI 10.2307/2371045
    • Church formalised effective calculability by general recursive functions in the 1935-36 papers Wikipedia, Church-Turing thesis
  3. 1936

    Post's finite combinatory processes

    first statement

    Post, Finite combinatory processes - formulation I, Journal of Symbolic Logic 1 (1936) 103-105

    Post gave an independent formulation of finite combinatory processes as a model of mechanical procedure.

    Evidence (1)
    • Post, Finite combinatory processes - formulation I, J. Symbolic Logic 1 (1936) 103-105 Crossref DOI 10.2307/2269031
  4. 1937

    Turing machines applied to the Entscheidungsproblem

    first application

    Turing, On Computable Numbers, with an Application to the Entscheidungsproblem, Proceedings of the London Mathematical Society (2) 42 (1937) 230-265

    Turing defined an abstract machine and a universal machine and used them to show the Entscheidungsproblem has no solution.

    Evidence (2)
    • Turing, On Computable Numbers, with an Application to the Entscheidungsproblem, Proc. London Math. Soc. (2) 42 (1937) 230-265 Crossref DOI 10.1112/plms/s2-42.1.230 (issue dated 1937; read 1936)
    • Turing's 1936 paper established a universal Turing machine and his application to computing MacTutor biography of Alan Turing
  5. 1952

    Kleene's Introduction to Metamathematics

    consolidation

    Kleene, Introduction to Metamathematics, Van Nostrand (1952)

    A textbook that treats recursive functions and the Church-Turing material in one systematic exposition.

    Evidence (2)
    • Kleene, Introduction to Metamathematics (1952) Open Library search record (first publish year 1952)
    • Church, Kleene and Turing are the three named sources of the thesis Wikipedia, Church-Turing thesis
