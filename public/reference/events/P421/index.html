1910 · Topology

Knot problems become group-theoretic: knot groups (Dehn)

Dehn took the fundamental group of a knot complement as the key invariant, treated knot questions as word problems, and used this to separate the two trefoils.

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1895

    Poincare introduces the fundamental group

    precursor

    Henri Poincare, Analysis situs, J. Ecole Polytech., 1895

    Defines the group of loops of a space, the object Dehn applies to knot complements.

    Evidence (2)
    • Biography of Poincare covering Analysis situs. MacTutor, Poincare biography
    • Dehn's 1914 paper was written not long after the fundamental group's discovery. MacTutor, Dehn biography
  2. 1910

    Dehn's topology of three-space

    first statement

    Dehn, M., Über die Topologie des dreidimensionalen Raumes, Mathematische Annalen (1910)

    Dehn's 1910 Mathematische Annalen paper is the earliest record found for the topological programme in which the knot group is used.

    Dating or attribution note

    The use of the knot group in this 1910 paper comes from the input summary and has not been checked against the text.

    Evidence (2)
    • Paper exists with this title and year Crossref
    • Knot group as key invariant Wikipedia: Knot group (definition and invariance)
  3. 1914

    Trefoils separated by the knot group

    first application

    Dehn, M., Die beiden Kleeblattschlingen, Mathematische Annalen (1914)

    Dehn applies the knot group to show that the two trefoil knots are inequivalent.

    Dating or attribution note

    The separation of the trefoils is taken from the input summary and the 1914 title; the proof itself was not read.

    Evidence (2)
    • Paper exists with this title and year Crossref
    • Knot group distinguishes knots; trefoil group identified with braid group B3 Wikipedia: Knot group, Examples
  4. 1927

    Reidemeister's elementary foundation of knot theory

    key development

    Kurt Reidemeister, Elementare Begruendung der Knotentheorie, Abh. Math. Sem. Hamburg 5, 1927

    Shows knot equivalence reduces to three diagram moves, making group presentations computable from diagrams.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref record, Abh. Math. Sem. Univ. Hamburg 5, pp. 24-32. Crossref 10.1007/bf02952507
    • zbMATH lists it as 1926 (Hamburg volume 5). zbMATH 2587523
  5. 1928

    Alexander's invariants of knots and links

    key development

    J. W. Alexander, Topological invariants of knots and links, Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. 30, 1928

    Builds knot invariants from the knot group's presentation, giving the Alexander polynomial.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref record, Trans. AMS 30 (1928), pp. 275-306. Crossref 10.1090/s0002-9947-1928-1501429-1
  6. 1932

    Reidemeister's Knotentheorie

    consolidation

    Reidemeister, K., Knotentheorie (1932)

    Reidemeister's 1932 monograph gives a systematic treatment of knot theory, including knot groups.

    Evidence (1)
    • Monograph with this title and year zbMATH Open
  7. 1957

    Papakyriakopoulos proves Dehn's lemma

    key development

    C. D. Papakyriakopoulos, On Dehn's lemma and the asphericity of knots, Ann. of Math. 66, 1957

    Fills the gap in Dehn's 1910 argument and shows knot complements are aspherical, so the knot group carries the knot's topology.

    Dating or attribution note

    The consequence that a knot with group Z is trivial is the standard reading and was not checked in the paper.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref record, Annals of Mathematics 66 (1957). Crossref 10.2307/1970113
