1956 · Algorithms & complexity

Formal language theory (Chomsky hierarchy and automata)

Grammar types were classified by automata.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1956

    Kleene, Representation of events in nerve nets and finite automata

    precursor

    S. C. Kleene, Automata Studies (1956)

    Independent automata-side work: regular events and finite automata.

    Evidence (1)
    • Automata Studies chapter Crossref
  2. 1956

    Chomsky, Three models for the description of language

    first statement

    N. Chomsky, IRE Trans. Inf. Theory 2 (1956) 113-124

    Compares finite-state, phrase-structure and transformational models of language.

    Evidence (1)
    • IRE Transactions Crossref / zbMATH 3251419
  3. 1958

    Chomsky and Miller, Finite state languages

    first application

    N. Chomsky, G. A. Miller, Inform. and Control 1 (1958) 91-112

    Grammar classes matched with finite-state automata, the first use of automata to classify languages.

    Evidence (1)
    • The article appeared in Information and Control 1(2), May 1958, pp. 91–112. Elsevier, Information and Control article
  4. 1959

    Chomsky, On certain formal properties of grammars

    key development

    N. Chomsky, Inform. and Control 2 (1959) 137-167

    The hierarchy of grammar types 0-3 linked to machine classes.

    Dating or attribution note

    A and B. B uses this paper as the earliest indexed title pairing formal with grammars (naming candidate, institutional 1959, low).

    Evidence (1)
    • Information and Control 2 Crossref
  5. 1959

    Rabin and Scott, Finite automata and their decision problems

    key development

    M. O. Rabin, D. Scott, IBM J. Res. Dev. 3 (1959)

    Founding paper on nondeterministic finite automata.

    Evidence (1)
    • IBM J. Res. Dev. 3 Crossref
  6. 1962

    Earliest indexed title with 'formal languages' (Burks and Wright)

    naming

    Burks and Wright, 1962 (zbMATH 0109.35301)

    Earliest zbMATH title containing formal languages.

    Evidence (1)
    • Title search py:1955-1961 0 hits; 1962 first zbMATH Open
  7. 1963

    Chomsky and Schutzenberger, The algebraic theory of context-free languages

    key development

    N. Chomsky, M.-P. Schutzenberger, Computer Programming and Formal Systems (1963)

    Algebraic side of context-free languages.

    Evidence (1)
    • Chapter Crossref
  8. 1966

    Ginsburg, The mathematical theory of context-free languages

    consolidation

    S. Ginsburg, McGraw-Hill (1966)

    First monograph on a major sub-area; the first textbook with formal languages in the title follows in 1968-69 (Engeler 1968, Hopcroft and Ullman 1969).

    Evidence (2)
    • Monograph zbMATH Zbl 0184.28401
    • Hopcroft and Ullman, Formal languages and their relation to automata (1969) zbMATH 0196.01701
