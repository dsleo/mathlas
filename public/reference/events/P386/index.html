1892 · Analysis

Heaviside operational calculus

Heaviside treated differentiation as an algebraic operator to solve telegraph and circuit equations, later justified through Laplace transforms.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1855

    Operator methods for differential equations (Carmichael, Boole)

    precursor

    Carmichael 1855; Boole, Treatise on Differential Equations, 1859

    British and Irish mathematicians wrote treatises applying operator symbols to differential equations decades before Heaviside.

    Evidence (1)
    • Wikipedia's history lists treatises by Carmichael (1855) and Boole (1859) on operator methods. Wikipedia: Operational calculus
  2. 1880–1887

    Heaviside's operators for telegraph and circuit equations

    precursor

    Heaviside, articles in The Electrician, 1880s

    Heaviside replaced d/dx by an algebraic variable p to turn circuit differential equations into algebraic ones.

    Evidence (1)
    • MacTutor says his operational calculus was developed between 1880 and 1887 to solve circuit differential equations. MacTutor Biographies/Heaviside
  3. 1892

    Heaviside's Electrical Papers

    first statement

    Heaviside, Electrical Papers, vol. 1, 1892

    The collected papers, whose first volume appeared in 1892, put his operator methods into book form.

    Dating or attribution note

    That operator methods appear in these volumes is taken from MacTutor's dating; contents were not read.

    Evidence (1)
    • Internet Archive record of Electrical Papers, 1892. Internet Archive
  4. 1893

    Heaviside: On operators in physical mathematics, Part I

    key development

    Heaviside, Proc. R. Soc. 52, 1893

    An explicit public exposition of the operator method in the Royal Society's Proceedings.

    Evidence (3)
    • Crossref: Proc. Roy. Soc. London 52, 1893. Crossref
    • zbMATH lists 'On operators in physical mathematics', 1893. zbMATH Open
    • Wikipedia says the method was fully developed by Heaviside in 1893. Wikipedia: Operational calculus
  5. 1917

    Bromwich ties Heaviside's calculus to contour integrals

    key development

    Bromwich, Normal coordinates in dynamical systems, Proc. London Math. Soc. (2) 15, 1917

    Bromwich put the operator rules on a rigorous footing using contour integrals, which links them to the Laplace transform.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref: Proc. London Math. Soc. s2-15, 1917. Crossref
    • MacTutor: he put Heaviside's calculus on a rigorous basis, treating operators as contour integrals. MacTutor Biographies/Bromwich
  6. 1926

    Wiener's operational calculus

    key development

    Wiener, The operational calculus, Math. Ann. 95, 1926

    Wiener analysed the logical status of the calculus and justified it with Fourier-transform methods.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref: Math. Annalen 95, 1926. Crossref
    • Wikipedia credits Wiener's 1926 review with laying foundations and a Fourier-transform justification. Wikipedia: Operational calculus
  7. 1927

    Jeffreys' Operational Methods in Mathematical Physics

    consolidation

    Jeffreys, Operational Methods in Mathematical Physics, Cambridge Tract, 1927

    A textbook-level exposition of Heaviside's operators via Laplace-transform methods.

    Evidence (2)
    • zbMATH record, 1927. zbMATH Open
    • Wikipedia cites Jeffreys' book for a detailed exposition. Wikipedia: Operational calculus
  8. 1950

    Mikusinski's algebraic operational calculus

    key development

    Mikusinski, Sur les fondements du calcul operatoire, Studia Math. 11, 1950

    Mikusinski built the calculus algebraically as a field of convolution quotients, with no appeal to transforms.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref: Studia Mathematica 11, 1950. Crossref
    • Wikipedia credits Mikusinski with an algebraic approach in the 1930s. Wikipedia: Operational calculus
