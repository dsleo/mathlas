Symplectic topology

The geometry of phase space is studied by rigidity phenomena, using complex-geometric techniques (holomorphic curves) and Morse-theoretic ones (Floer homology).

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1939

    Weyl names the symplectic groups

    precursor

    Weyl, The Classical Groups: Their Invariants and Representations, Princeton (1939)

    Weyl adopts the term symplectic for the classical groups, the origin of the name of the field.

    Evidence (2)
    • Wikipedia Symplectic geometry: the term adopted by Weyl, citing the 1939 book Wikipedia: Symplectic geometry
    • Mathematical Gazette 1940 review of the book (record checked) Crossref
  2. 1985

    Pseudo-holomorphic curves in symplectic manifolds

    first statement

    Gromov, Pseudo holomorphic curves in symplectic manifolds, Invent. Math. 82 (1985)

    Introduces pseudo-holomorphic curves as a tool for symplectic manifolds, the modern start of symplectic topology.

    Dating or attribution note

    Taken as the first public statement of the modern field; earlier results (Darboux, Arnold conjecture) are not included and not verified here.

    Evidence (4)
    • Record title, journal and year checked Crossref
    • Wikipedia Symplectic geometry: Gromov's almost complex structures and pseudoholomorphic curves led to symplectic topology Wikipedia: Symplectic geometry
    • Journal, pages and year verified (Crossref) Crossref
    • Wikipedia: pseudoholomorphic curves introduced by Gromov in 1985 Wikipedia: Pseudoholomorphic curve
  3. 1985

    Non-squeezing theorem

    first application

    Gromov, Pseudo holomorphic curves in symplectic manifolds, Invent. Math. 82 (1985) 307-347

    Non-squeezing theorem: a symplectic ball cannot be squeezed into a thinner cylinder; the first rigidity result from the method.

    Evidence (2)
    • Journal and year verified (Crossref) Crossref
    • Wikipedia: the non-squeezing theorem was first proven by Gromov in 1985 Wikipedia: Non-squeezing theorem
  4. 1986

    'First steps in symplectic topology'

    naming

    Arnold, First steps in symplectic topology, Uspekhi Mat. Nauk 41 (1986) no. 6, 3-18; Russian Math. Surveys 41 (1986) 1-21

    Title uses the term symplectic topology, a survey of the new area.

    Evidence (2)
    • Title, venue and year verified (zbMATH, two records) zbMATH
    • Wikipedia cites this 1986 survey in the symplectic geometry article Wikipedia: Symplectic geometry
  5. 1988

    Floer homology for Lagrangian intersections

    key development

    Floer, Morse theory for Lagrangian intersections, J. Differential Geom. 28 (1988)

    Floer's construction gives a homology theory for Lagrangian intersections, opening the Arnold-type rigidity problems.

    Evidence (1)
    • Record title, journal and year checked Crossref
  6. 1995

    Monograph: Introduction to Symplectic Topology

    consolidation

    McDuff, Salamon, Introduction to Symplectic Topology, Oxford (1995)

    First textbook treatment of symplectic topology, based on the holomorphic curve methods.
