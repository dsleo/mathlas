1956 · Algorithms & complexity

Kleene's theorem: regular events and finite automata

Finite automata and regular events were shown equivalent.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1943

    McCulloch-Pitts nerve nets

    precursor

    McCulloch and Pitts, A logical calculus of the ideas immanent in nervous activity, 1943

    The nerve-net model whose events Kleene later represented by finite automata.

    Evidence (1)
    • Paper dated 1943 (reprint record cites the original year) Crossref reprint record
  2. 1951

    Kleene's RAND memorandum on nerve nets

    precursor

    Kleene spent summer 1951 at RAND and found the characterisation; the memorandum is an unpublished precursor.

    Evidence (1)
    • Summer 1951 at RAND; characterisation of finite automata found there MacTutor biography of Kleene
  3. 1956

    Representation of events in nerve nets and finite automata

    first statement

    Kleene, Representation of Events in Nerve Nets and Finite Automata, in Automata Studies (AM-34), Princeton, 1956

    Kleene's public statement that regular events are exactly the events represented by finite automata.

    Evidence (1)
    • Title, volume and year verified Crossref
  4. 1956

    Nerve-net representation (same publication)

    first application

    Kleene, Automata Studies (AM-34), 1956 (as above)

    The same paper applies the characterisation to the nerve-net problem posed by McCulloch and Pitts.

    Evidence (1)
    • Same publication; paper addresses nerve nets Crossref title
  5. 1959

    Finite automata and their decision problems

    key development

    Rabin and Scott, Finite automata and their decision problems, IBM Journal of Research and Development 3 (1959) 114-125

    Introduces nondeterministic finite automata and decision problems, extending the automata side of the correspondence.

    Dating or attribution note

    Role judgment; the link to Kleene's theorem is not checked against the paper text.

    Evidence (1)
    • Title, journal, volume, pages and year verified Crossref
  6. 1969

    Hopcroft and Ullman's textbook 'Formal languages and their relation to automata'

    consolidation

    J. E. Hopcroft, J. D. Ullman, Formal Languages and their Relation to Automata, Reading, MA: Addison-Wesley, 1969

    A textbook that presents regular languages (regular events) and finite automata together, as a standard systematic treatment.

    Dating or attribution note

    Textbook role from the record. The regular-events chapter was not read. Salomaa's 'Theory of automata' (1969, Zbl 0193.32901) is a contemporary textbook candidate and was not chosen.

    Evidence (1)
    • zbMATH record 'Formal languages and their relation to automata', Hopcroft and Ullman, Addison-Wesley 1969, Zbl 0196.01701 zbMATH Open API
