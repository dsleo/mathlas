1918 · Differential geometry

Weyl's gauge theory (local scale invariance)

Weyl proposed local scale invariance.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1918

    Weyl proposes local scale invariance

    first statement

    Weyl, 'Gravitation und Elektrizität', Sitzungsberichte der Preussischen Akademie (1918)

    Weyl proposes that local rescaling of the metric is a symmetry, aiming to derive electromagnetism from geometry.

    Evidence (2)
    • The concept and the name of gauge theory derive from Weyl's 1918 work Wikipedia: Gauge theory
    • Raum-Zeit-Materie first appeared in 1918, with gauge ideas in later editions MacTutor: Weyl biography
  2. 1918

    Scale geometry applied to electromagnetism

    first application

    Weyl, 'Gravitation und Elektrizität' (1918), same work

    Weyl applies the local scale symmetry to electromagnetic fields, the first physical target problem; same work as the first_statement.

    Evidence (1)
    • Weyl's 1918 attempt to derive electromagnetism from the scale geometry Wikipedia: Gauge theory
  3. 1918

    Name 'gauge' (Eich-) from Weyl's 1918 work

    naming

    Weyl (1918), same work as first_statement

    The term gauge (Eichung) for the rescaling symmetry is attributed to Weyl's 1918 work; same work as the first_statement.

    Evidence (1)
    • Name derives from Weyl's 1918 work Wikipedia: Gauge theory
  4. 1929

    Weyl's electron and gravitation paper (phase gauge)

    key development

    Weyl, 'Elektron und Gravitation I', Zeitschrift für Physik 56 (1929)

    Weyl reinterprets the rescaling as a phase symmetry tied to the electron, the gauge principle of later quantum electrodynamics.

    Evidence (1)
    • 1929 Zeitschrift für Physik record Crossref record
  5. 1954

    Yang-Mills non-abelian gauge invariance

    key development

    Yang & Mills, 'Conservation of Isotopic Spin and Isotopic Gauge Invariance', Physical Review 96 (1954)

    Yang and Mills generalise gauge invariance to non-abelian symmetry groups.

    Evidence (1)
    • 1954 Physical Review record Crossref record
