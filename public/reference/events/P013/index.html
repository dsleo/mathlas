Functional analysis

Integral equations and the calculus of variations are treated as linear algebra in infinite-dimensional normed spaces with topological structure.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1887

    Volterra introduces functions that depend on other functions

    first statement

    V. Volterra, Sopra le funzioni che dipendono da altre funzioni, Rend. Accad. Lincei (4) 3 (1887), notes I-III

    Volterra's note defined quantities depending on a whole function, the notion later called a functional.

    Evidence (3)
    • zbMATH lists a 1887 Volterra paper on functions depending on other functions in the Lincei Rendiconti. zbMATH Open
    • Wikipedia credits Volterra with the general concept of a functional in 1887. Wikipedia: Functional analysis
    • Volterra's Opere matematiche (Polish Academy digitisation) lists 'Sopra le funzioni che dipendono da altre funzioni', 1887, as three notes (Nota I-III); a Springer reference cites Rendiconti Lincei III (1887), second semester, fascicles 4, 6, 7. Opere matematiche di Vito Volterra, rcin.org.pl
  2. 1889

    Volterra's first memoir on functions of lines

    key development

    V. Volterra, Sur une generalisation de la theorie des fonctions d'une variable imaginaire, Acta Math. 12 (1889) 233-286

    Volterra develops a systematic calculus for quantities depending on a whole curve (functions of lines), the basis of his functional calculus.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref: Acta Mathematica 12, pp. 233-286, 1889, 'Sur une generalisation de la theorie des fonctions d'une variable imaginaire'. Crossref 10.1007/BF02391880
    • Frechet 1906 (read): 'MM. Volterra (xv) et Arzela (v) paraissent avoir ete les premiers a etudier systematiquement les fonctions dont la valeur depend de la position et de la forme d'une ligne variable', with (xv) this Acta memoir of 1889. Frechet, Sur quelques points du calcul fonctionnel, Rend. Circ. Mat. Palermo 22 (1906), introduction and bibliography
  3. 1890

    Volterra's functional calculus applied to Hamilton-Jacobi theory

    first application

    V. Volterra, Sopra una estensione della teoria Jacobi-Hamilton del calcolo delle variazioni, Rend. Accad. Lincei (4) 6 (1890), 1st semester, pp. 127-138

    Volterra extended the Jacobi-Hamilton theory of the calculus of variations to multiple integrals, one of the first uses of his functional calculus on a target problem.

    Evidence (2)
    • MacTutor states that in 1890 he showed his functional calculus extends the Hamilton-Jacobi theory to other physics problems. MacTutor Biographies/Volterra
    • The reprint's introduction says no attempt had been made to extend the Jacobi-Hamilton theory to variations of multiple integrals; it is dated 1890. Opere matematiche di Vito Volterra, vol. on 1890 notes (rcin.org.pl)
  4. 1903

    Fredholm's theory of a class of functional equations

    key development

    Fredholm, Sur une classe d'equations fonctionnelles, Acta Math. 27, 1903

    Fredholm solved integral equations by the determinant method, giving the model for treating them as infinite linear systems.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref: Acta Mathematica 27, pp. 365-390, 1903. Crossref
    • MacTutor remembers Fredholm for integral equations and spectral theory. MacTutor Biographies/Fredholm
  5. 1903

    Hadamard on linear functional operations

    key development

    J. Hadamard, Sur les operations fonctionnelles, C. R. Acad. Sci. Paris 136 (1903), 351-354

    Hadamard shows that a continuous linear operation taking a function on an interval to a number is a limit of integrals of the function against a sequence of kernels, an early representation theorem for what are now called continuous linear functionals.

    Evidence (2)
    • Read: 'Sur les operations fonctionnelles', Note de M. Hadamard presentee par M. Jordan, C. R. 136, pp. 351-354; defines linear functional operations on f(x) on (a,b), cites Volterra, Pincherle and Bourlet, and gives the representation by limits of integrals against F_n(x - u). Comptes rendus hebdomadaires de l'Academie des sciences, t. 136, 1er semestre 1903, no. 6 (9 February 1903)
    • Miller: the noun 'functional' is generally attributed to Hadamard via Frechet 1906 p. 38, but the 1903 note uses the word only in the phrase operation fonctionnelle, so Frechet may be responsible for the noun himself. MacTutor, Earliest Uses of Some of the Words of Mathematics (F)
  6. 1904

    Hilbert's first communication on linear integral equations

    key development

    D. Hilbert, Grundzuege einer allgemeinen Theorie der linearen Integralgleichungen (Erste Mitteilung), Nachr. Ges. Wiss. Goettingen, Math.-Phys. Kl. 1904, 49-91

    Hilbert began his series on integral equations, extending Fredholm's theory to eigenfunction expansions and, in later communications, to infinitely many variables.

    Dating or attribution note

    Citation confirmed twice: EuDML, and Hilbert's own 1912 preface (read in the Internet Archive scan of Grundzuege einer allgemeinen Theorie der linearen Integralgleichungen), which lists 'Erste Mitteilung (Gott. Nachr. 1904, S. 49-91)' and the five further communications to 1910. The introduction (read) states the aim, a general theory of linear integral equations with symmetric kernels leading to expansions of an arbitrary function in eigenfunctions. The 1904 text itself was not read separately; the fourth communication (1906) and the 1912 book are under the person.

    Evidence (3)
    • EuDML record of the first communication, Goettingen Nachrichten 1904, pp. 49-91. EuDML doc 58572
    • Read: Hilbert's preface to the 1912 book lists the six communications, the first being Gott. Nachr. 1904, pp. 49-91, and the introduction states the eigenfunction-expansion programme. D. Hilbert, Grundzuege einer allgemeinen Theorie der linearen Integralgleichungen, Teubner 1912 (Internet Archive grundzgeeinera00hilbuoft)
    • Miller: the study of function spaces grew out of Hilbert's work on integral equations in the first decade of the century. MacTutor, Earliest Uses (F, H)
  7. 1906

    Frechet's thesis on the functional calculus and abstract spaces

    key development

    Frechet, Sur quelques points du calcul fonctionnel, Rend. Circ. Mat. Palermo 22, 1906

    Frechet's thesis introduced metric spaces and an axiomatic setting for analysis on spaces of functions, built on ideas of Hadamard and Volterra.

    Evidence (3)
    • Crossref: Rendiconti del Circolo Matematico di Palermo 22, pp. 1-72, 1906. Crossref
    • MacTutor describes the 1906 thesis as introducing metric spaces from the ideas of Hadamard and Volterra. MacTutor Biographies/Frechet
    • Read: section 59, 'Les operations portant sur des fonctions continues sont specialement appelees des fonctionnelles par M. Hadamard (xi)', and the linear functional U(f) = integral of m(x) f(x) dx given as an example. Frechet, Rend. Circ. Mat. Palermo 22 (1906)
  8. 1910

    Hadamard's Lecons sur le calcul des variations

    key development

    Hadamard, Lecons sur le calcul des variations, 1910

    Hadamard's book recast the calculus of variations around the functional, spreading the noun and the Volterra-Hadamard viewpoint.

    Evidence (5)
    • Internet Archive catalogue record for the 1910 first volume, collected by Frechet. Internet Archive
    • MacTutor dates Hadamard's introduction of the word functional to this 1910 book (Miller's Earliest Uses traces it to his 1903 note instead). MacTutor Biographies/Hadamard
    • Wikipedia dates the noun usage to Hadamard's 1910 book (earlier attribution to 1903 is in the Miller entry). Wikipedia: Functional analysis
    • Open Library lists Hadamard, Lecons sur le calcul des variations, 1910. Open Library
    • Read: preface and section 232 define 'fonctionnelle' and develop continuity and derivative of functionals. J. Hadamard, Lecons sur le calcul des variations, t. I (1910), Internet Archive leonssurlecalc00hadauoft
  9. 1910

    Riesz on systems of integrable functions

    key development

    F. Riesz, Untersuchungen ueber Systeme integrierbarer Funktionen, Math. Ann. 69 (1910), 449-497

    Riesz studied the spaces of p-th power integrable functions and their norms, a main example of the abstract spaces of the field.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref record: Untersuchungen ueber Systeme integrierbarer Funktionen, Mathematische Annalen 69, pp. 449-497, 1910. Crossref 10.1007/BF01457637
  10. 1922

    Banach's thesis on operations in abstract sets

    key development

    Banach, Sur les operations dans les ensembles abstraits et leur application aux equations integrales, Fund. Math. 3, 1922

    Banach defined complete normed spaces and proved the contraction principle, launching the Polish school of linear operator theory.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref: Fundamenta Mathematicae 3, pp. 133-181, 1922. Crossref
    • MacTutor says this thesis is sometimes said to mark the birth of functional analysis. MacTutor Biographies/Banach
  11. 1922

    Levy's 'Lecons d'analyse fonctionnelle'

    naming

    Levy, Lecons d'analyse fonctionnelle, Gauthier-Villars, 1922

    The earliest title found that uses the phrase for the field, a course reflecting Hadamard's school of nonlinear functionals.

    Evidence (3)
    • zbMATH record for Levy's Lecons d'analyse fonctionnelle, 1922. zbMATH Open
    • Open Library lists Levy, Lecons d'analyse fonctionnelle, 1922. Open Library
    • Miller: the term functional analysis appears in the title of Paul Levy's Lecons de l'analyse fonctionelle (1922); the English term was established in the 1930s. MacTutor, Earliest Uses of Some of the Words of Mathematics (F)
  12. 1932

    Banach's treatise on linear operators

    consolidation

    Banach, Theorie des operations lineaires, Warsaw, 1932

    The first monograph presenting linear operators on Banach spaces as a unified theory.

    Evidence (3)
    • zbMATH record for Banach, Theorie des operations lineaires, 1932. zbMATH Open
    • Internet Archive scan listed under 1932. Internet Archive
    • Open Library lists Banach, Theorie des operations lineaires, 1932. Open Library
