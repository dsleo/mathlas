Functional analysis

Integral equations and the calculus of variations are treated as linear algebra in infinite-dimensional normed spaces with topological structure.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1887

    Volterra introduces functions that depend on other functions

    first statement

    Volterra, Sopra le funzioni che dipendono da altre funzioni, Rend. Accad. Lincei (4), 1887

    Volterra's note defined quantities depending on a whole function, the notion later called a functional.

    Evidence (2)
    • zbMATH lists a 1887 Volterra paper on functions depending on other functions in the Lincei Rendiconti. zbMATH Open
    • Wikipedia credits Volterra with the general concept of a functional in 1887. Wikipedia: Functional analysis
  2. 1890

    Volterra's functional calculus applied to Hamilton-Jacobi theory

    first application

    Volterra, functional calculus papers, c. 1890

    Volterra used functionals to extend the Hamilton-Jacobi integration theory of dynamics to other problems of mathematical physics.

    Evidence (1)
    • MacTutor states that in 1890 he showed his functional calculus extends the Hamilton-Jacobi theory to other physics problems. MacTutor Biographies/Volterra
  3. 1903

    Fredholm's theory of a class of functional equations

    key development

    Fredholm, Sur une classe d'equations fonctionnelles, Acta Math. 27, 1903

    Fredholm solved integral equations by the determinant method, giving the model for treating them as infinite linear systems.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref: Acta Mathematica 27, pp. 365-390, 1903. Crossref
    • MacTutor remembers Fredholm for integral equations and spectral theory. MacTutor Biographies/Fredholm
  4. 1906

    Frechet's thesis on the functional calculus and abstract spaces

    key development

    Frechet, Sur quelques points du calcul fonctionnel, Rend. Circ. Mat. Palermo 22, 1906

    Frechet's thesis introduced metric spaces and an axiomatic setting for analysis on spaces of functions, built on ideas of Hadamard and Volterra.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref: Rendiconti del Circolo Matematico di Palermo 22, pp. 1-72, 1906. Crossref
    • MacTutor describes the 1906 thesis as introducing metric spaces from the ideas of Hadamard and Volterra. MacTutor Biographies/Frechet
  5. 1910

    Hadamard's lectures introduce the noun 'functional'

    naming

    Hadamard, Lecons sur le calcul des variations, 1910

    Hadamard's book fixed the word 'functional' for a function of a function, replacing Volterra's own terminology.

    Evidence (3)
    • Internet Archive catalogue record for the 1910 first volume, collected by Frechet. Internet Archive
    • MacTutor says he introduced the word functional in this 1910 book. MacTutor Biographies/Hadamard
    • Wikipedia dates the noun usage to Hadamard's 1910 book. Wikipedia: Functional analysis
  6. 1922

    Banach's thesis on operations in abstract sets

    key development

    Banach, Sur les operations dans les ensembles abstraits et leur application aux equations integrales, Fund. Math. 3, 1922

    Banach defined complete normed spaces and proved the contraction principle, launching the Polish school of linear operator theory.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref: Fundamenta Mathematicae 3, pp. 133-181, 1922. Crossref
    • MacTutor says this thesis is sometimes said to mark the birth of functional analysis. MacTutor Biographies/Banach
  7. 1922

    Levy's 'Lecons d'analyse fonctionnelle'

    naming

    Levy, Lecons d'analyse fonctionnelle, Gauthier-Villars, 1922

    The earliest title found that uses the phrase for the field, a course reflecting Hadamard's school of nonlinear functionals.

    Evidence (1)
    • zbMATH record for Levy's Lecons d'analyse fonctionnelle, 1922. zbMATH Open
  8. 1932

    Banach's treatise on linear operators

    consolidation

    Banach, Theorie des operations lineaires, Warsaw, 1932

    The first monograph presenting linear operators on Banach spaces as a unified theory.

    Evidence (2)
    • zbMATH record for Banach, Theorie des operations lineaires, 1932. zbMATH Open
    • Internet Archive scan listed under 1932. Internet Archive
