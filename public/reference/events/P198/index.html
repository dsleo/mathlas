1533 · Astronomy & trigonometry

Regiomontanus: trigonometry separated from astronomy in Europe

De triangulis omnimodis presented trigonometry as a branch in its own right.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1462

    Epitome of the Almagest

    precursor

    Peurbach and Regiomontanus, Epitome of Ptolemy's Almagest, completed 1462 (printed Venice, 1496)

    Regiomontanus promised a separate treatise on trigonometry while completing the Epitome.

    Dating or attribution note

    Completed 1462 per Museo Galileo and the EBSCO research starter (end of 1462), as well as MacTutor; first printed Venice 1496. Dating now rests on three secondary sources, not MacTutor alone. A precursor, not a statement of trigonometry: the Epitome is astronomy and Regiomontanus found no systematic account of triangles to cite.

    Evidence (3)
    • Epitome completed 1462; he promised a systematic account of trigonometry, then wrote De triangulis. MacTutor, Regiomontanus biography
    • Epitome of the Almagest completed in 1462 in Italy and printed in Venice in 1496. Museo Galileo, Johannes Regiomontanus
    • Regiomontanus finished the Epitome manuscript at the end of 1462; first printed in 1496; De triangulis composed 1464, published 1533. EBSCO Research Starters, Epitome of Ptolemy's Almagest
  2. 1464

    De triangulis omnimodis completed in manuscript

    precursor

    Regiomontanus, De triangulis omnimodis libri quinque, completed 1464 (manuscript; printed 1533)

    The first systematic Latin account of plane and spherical triangles, structured like Euclid's Elements, completed in 1464 and unprinted until 1533.

    Evidence (3)
    • Systematic account of solving triangles, dated 1464. MacTutor, Regiomontanus biography
    • In 1464, he completed De triangulis omnimodis. English Wikipedia, Regiomontanus
    • De triangulis omnimodis completed in 1464 in Italy, printed in Nuremberg in 1533. Museo Galileo, Johannes Regiomontanus
  3. 1533

    First printed: De triangulis omnimodis, Nuremberg

    first statement

    Regiomontanus, De triangulis omnimodis libri quinque, ed. Johann Schoener, Nuremberg, Johann Petreius, 1533

    The five books on triangles reach print, edited by Schoener and bound with Nicholas of Cusa's De quadratura circuli.

    Evidence (3)
    • Scan of the 1533 edition: title page 'anno Christi M.D.XXXIII', Nuremberg, Petreius; edited by Schoener. Internet Archive item ARes352131
    • Published 1533. MacTutor, Trigonometric functions
    • Catalogue record: Nuremberg, Johann Petreius, 1533, edited by Johann Schoener, with Nicholas of Cusa's De quadratura circuli. Internet Archive metadata for ARes352131
  4. 1595

    'Trigonometria' coined

    naming

    Pitiscus, Trigonometria, 1595

    The word 'trigonometry' enters a title.

    Evidence (2)
    • The word first appears as a book title in 1595. MacTutor, Trigonometric functions
    • Pitiscus coined the word. MacTutor, Pitiscus biography
