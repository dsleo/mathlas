1687 · Mathematical physics

Newton's Principia

Central-force motion, universal gravitation and orbit theory were derived by geometric mathematics.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1673

    Centrifugal force and the inverse-square law discussed

    precursor

    Huygens' centrifugal force law, Hooke, Halley, Wren, 1673 onward

    Huygens' law of centrifugal force in uniform circular motion led Huygens, Hooke, Halley and Wren to formulate an inverse-square law.

    Evidence (1)
    • Huygens derived the law of centrifugal force; with Hooke, Halley and Wren this led to the inverse-square law. MacTutor: Christiaan Huygens
  2. 1684

    De motu corporum in gyrum

    precursor

    Isaac Newton, De motu, 1684

    After Halley asked about orbits under inverse-square force, Newton wrote a short tract proving the converse of what he claimed; Halley then persuaded him to write the full treatise.

    Evidence (1)
    • In 1684 Halley asked Newton about the orbit; De Motu proved the converse; Halley persuaded him to write a full treatment. MacTutor: Isaac Newton
  3. 1687

    Principia published

    first statement

    Isaac Newton, Philosophiae naturalis principia mathematica, London, 1687

    Gave the laws of motion, universal gravitation and orbit theory derived by geometrical methods.

    Evidence (3)
    • Archive.org records list the 1687 London edition printed by Streater. archive.org item philosophiaenatu00newt_0
    • Newton published the Principia in 1687. MacTutor: Isaac Newton
    • First published in 1687; imprimatur 5 July 1686. Wikipedia: Principia
  4. 1687

    Inverse-square force applied to orbits

    first application

    Principia, 1687

    Newton showed that a central inverse-square force corresponds to Kepler's laws and applied the theory to orbiting bodies, projectiles, pendulums and free fall.

    Evidence (1)
    • The results were applied to orbiting bodies, projectiles, pendulums and free-fall near the earth. MacTutor: Isaac Newton
  5. 1702

    Keill's Introductio ad veram physicam

    consolidation

    John Keill, Introductio ad veram physicam, Oxford, 1702

    Oxford lecture textbook expounding Newtonian natural philosophy, an early systematic presentation of Newton's system.

    Evidence (2)
    • Open Library record of the 1702 Introductio ad veram physicam. Open Library work record
    • Keill's principal publications include the 1702 Oxford Introductio ad Veram Physicam. Wikipedia, John Keill
  6. 1713

    Second edition

    key development

    Principia, 2nd edition, Cambridge, 1713, ed. Cotes

    Corrections and additions, including a sketched proof that inverse-square force gives conics.

    Evidence (2)
    • Archive.org records list the 1713 edition. archive.org item principiamathem00newtgoog
    • The proof is sketched in the second and third editions, not the first. MacTutor: Isaac Newton
