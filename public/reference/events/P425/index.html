Computational group theory: computers enter group theory

From Todd-Coxeter coset enumeration to the 1967 Oxford conference, Sims's permutation methods and the Cayley and GAP systems, machine computation became a standard working method of group theory.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1911

    Dehn's word, conjugacy and isomorphism problems

    precursor

    Dehn asked for algorithms for the word, conjugacy and isomorphism problems of finitely presented groups.

    Evidence (1)
    • Dehn's 1911 formulation of the word, conjugacy and isomorphism problems begins the prehistory of CGT MacTutor, History of Computational Group Theory
  2. 1936

    Todd-Coxeter coset enumeration

    first statement

    Todd and Coxeter, A practical method for enumerating cosets of a finite abstract group, Proceedings of the Edinburgh Mathematical Society (2) 5 (1936) 26-34

    Todd and Coxeter gave a systematic method for enumerating cosets of a subgroup in a finitely presented group; it became a basic computational procedure.

    Evidence (2)
    • Todd, Coxeter, A practical method for enumerating cosets of a finite abstract group, Proc. Edinburgh Math. Soc. (2) 5 (1936) 26-34 Crossref DOI 10.1017/s0013091500008221
    • Todd and Coxeter published their coset enumeration procedure in 1936 MacTutor, History of Computational Group Theory and Todd biography
  3. 1945

    Turing's 1945 remark on enumerating groups

    precursor

    Turing, Proposed Electronic Calculator, NPL report (1945) (as quoted by MacTutor)

    Turing's 1945 design proposal for an electronic computer anticipated machine enumeration of groups of order 720.

    Evidence (1)
    • Turing's 1945 proposal quoted in MacTutor history of CGT (NPL report) MacTutor, History of Computational Group Theory
  4. 1951

    Newman's talk on automatic computers and groups

    precursor

    Newman, The influence of automatic computers on mathematical methods, talk at the Manchester University Computer Inaugural Conference (1951)

    Newman proposed probabilistic computer methods to gain insight into the groups of order 256.

    Dating or attribution note

    A talk; the proceedings were not checked. Precursor only.

    Evidence (1)
    • Newman's 1951 Manchester talk proposing probabilistic methods for groups of order 256 MacTutor, History of Computational Group Theory
  5. 1953

    First machine implementations: Todd-Coxeter on EDSAC II and symmetric-group characters

    first application

    Early computer implementations of coset enumeration and of character calculations for symmetric groups.

    Evidence (1)
    • Haselgrove's partial Todd-Coxeter on EDSAC II and Comet's character programs on BARK, about 1953 MacTutor, History of Computational Group Theory
  6. 1970

    Oxford 1967 methods: Sims permutation methods and Knuth-Bendix

    key development

    Leech (ed.), Computational Problems in Abstract Algebra, Pergamon Press (1970)

    Methods presented at the 1967 Oxford conference, including Sims's permutation-group methods, were published in the 1970 proceedings.

    Evidence (2)
    • Leech (ed.), Computational Problems in Abstract Algebra, Pergamon (1970); chapter 'Coset enumeration' pp. 21-35 Crossref DOI 10.1016/b978-0-08-012975-4.50006-0
    • The 1967 Oxford conference presented Sims's permutation methods and the Knuth-Bendix method MacTutor, History of Computational Group Theory
  7. 1973

    Sims's permutation methods and the existence of Lyons' group

    key development

    Sims used permutation-group methods to prove the existence of Lyons' sporadic group, showing computation mattered to group theorists.

    Dating or attribution note

    Bibliographic record for the 1973 result was not checked.

    Evidence (1)
    • Sims proved the existence of Lyons' sporadic group in 1973 by permutation methods MacTutor, History of Computational Group Theory
  8. 1994

    Sims's monograph Computation with Finitely Presented Groups

    consolidation

    Sims, Computation with Finitely Presented Groups, Cambridge University Press (1994)

    A systematic monograph on computing with finitely presented groups, presenting the methods as a unified subject.

    Evidence (2)
    • Sims, Computation with Finitely Presented Groups, Encyclopedia Math. Appl. 48, Cambridge University Press (1994) Crossref DOI 10.1017/CBO9780511574702
    • Wikipedia lists Sims (1994) as a standard reference and mentions an older Neubuser survey Wikipedia, Computational group theory
