1945 · Algebra

Category theory

Natural isomorphisms noticed in homology computations are formalised as functors and natural transformations, and the resulting structure becomes a general language.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1942

    Natural isomorphisms in group theory

    precursor

    S. Eilenberg and S. Mac Lane, Natural isomorphisms in group theory, PNAS 28 (1942)

    Naturality appears before the full category framework.

    Evidence (1)
    • Eilenberg and Mac Lane, Natural isomorphisms in group theory, PNAS 28 (1942) Crossref record
  2. 1945

    General theory of natural equivalences

    first statement

    S. Eilenberg and S. Mac Lane, General theory of natural equivalences, Trans. AMS 58 (1945)

    The published paper introduces categories and functors as a general theory.

    Evidence (1)
    • Eilenberg and Mac Lane, General theory of natural equivalences, Trans. AMS 58 (1945) Crossref record
  3. 1945

    Categories organize natural equivalences

    first application

    S. Eilenberg and S. Mac Lane, General theory of natural equivalences, Trans. AMS 58 (1945)

    The formalism is used to express natural equivalence across algebraic constructions.

    Evidence (1)
    • The paper introduces categories and functors to study natural equivalences. AMS original article record
  4. 1948

    Categories in a paper title

    naming

    S. Mac Lane, Groups, categories and duality, PNAS 34 (1948)

    A published title explicitly names categories.

    Evidence (1)
    • Mac Lane, Groups, categories and duality, PNAS 34 (1948) Crossref
