1010 · Algebra

Al-Karaji polynomial algebra

Algebra was freed from geometric operations, with powers and product rules defined for x and 1/x and polynomial arithmetic.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 820

    Al-Khwarizmi's Algebra

    precursor

    Al-Khwarizmi, Kitab al-jabr wa'l-muqabala (c. 820)

    Gave the six standard equation types with solutions by rules and by geometric completion of the square, with no polynomial calculus.

    Evidence (1)
    • Shows how to solve six standard types of equation using algebraic and geometric methods, e.g. completing the square. MacTutor, Al-Khwarizmi biography
  2. 900

    Abu Kamil's Book on Algebra

    precursor

    Abu Kamil, Book on algebra (c. 900)

    Extended al-Khwarizmi's methods and applied algebra to geometric problems.

    Evidence (1)
    • Abu Kamil was a successor of al-Khwarizmi who applied algebraic methods to geometric problems; his Book on algebra survives. MacTutor, Abu Kamil biography
  3. 1010–1015

    Al-Karaji, Al-Fakhri: monomials and polynomial arithmetic

    first statement

    Al-Karaji, Al-Fakhri fi'l-jabr wa'l-muqabala

    Defined the powers x^n and 1/x^n as monomials and gave multiplication of polynomials, freeing algebra from geometric operations.

    Evidence (4)
    • What al-Karaji achieved in Al-Fakhri was first to define the monomials x, x^2, x^3, ... and 1/x, 1/x^2, ... MacTutor, Al-Karaji biography
    • His work gave rules for adding, subtracting and multiplying polynomials, though division was only by monomials. English Wikipedia, Al-Karaji
    • Britannica (as found by search, page itself not opened): al-Karaji's service in Baghdad about 1010-1015, during which he wrote his main works including al-Fakhri. Encyclopaedia Britannica, al-Karaji
    • MacTutor: he gave rules for adding, subtracting and multiplying sums of monomials, division only by a monomial; Rashed argues he freed algebra from geometric operations, Crossley was less impressed. MacTutor, Al-Karaji biography (re-read in review)
  4. 1149

    Al-Samaw'al, al-Bahir fi'l-jabr

    key development

    Al-Samaw'al, al-Bahir fi'l-jabr (written at 19)

    Treated polynomial arithmetic more fully, including division and root extraction, and defined powers with negative exponents.

    Evidence (2)
    • Al-Samawal extended arithmetic to polynomials; in Book 1 he defines x, x^2, ..., x^-1, x^-2 and the study resembles polynomial rings. MacTutor, Al-Samawal biography
    • MacTutor: in the first book of al-Bahir he defines powers and describes addition, subtraction, multiplication and division of polynomials; the binomial theorem with Pascal-triangle coefficients is attributed to al-Karaji. MacTutor, Al-Samawal biography (re-read in review)
  5. 1853

    Woepcke's extract of al-Karaji's al-Fakhri

    consolidation

    Franz Woepcke, Extrait du Fakhri, Paris, 1853

    French extract of al-Karaji's al-Fakhri, the printed edition through which European historians first learned of his polynomial arithmetic.

    Evidence (4)
    • Open Library record of Woepcke's 1853 Extrait du Fakhri. Open Library work record
    • Woepcke was the first historian to realise the importance of al-Karaji's work. Wikipedia, Al-Karaji
    • Internet Archive record: Woepcke, Extrait du Fakhri: traite d'algebre, Imprimerie imperiale, 1853. Internet Archive item extraitdufakhrt00karagoog
    • MacTutor: Woepcke was the first historian to recognise the importance of al-Karaji's work and found that much of Diophantus' first and third books is included in al-Fakhri. MacTutor, Al-Karaji biography (re-read in review)
