1888 · Group & representation theory

Killing-Cartan classification

Killing and Cartan decomposed semisimple Lie algebras into simple ones and classified them into four families plus exceptional cases.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1884

    Killing's Programmschrift introduces Lie algebras

    precursor

    Killing, Programmschrift, Lyceum Hosianum, Braunsberg, 1884

    In a school programme Killing independently introduced Lie algebras while studying space forms; the classification aim came later.

    Evidence (1)
    • MacTutor says he introduced Lie algebras in this 1884 Programmschrift but was not yet aiming at a classification. MacTutor Biographies/Killing
  2. 1888–1890

    Killing: Die Zusammensetzung der stetigen endlichen Transformationsgruppen

    first statement

    Killing, Math. Ann. 31, 33, 34, 36 (four parts), 1888-1890

    Killing classified the complex simple Lie algebras into four families and exceptional cases, introducing Cartan subalgebras, the Cartan matrix and root systems.

    Evidence (4)
    • Crossref: Part I, Math. Annalen 31, 1888. Crossref
    • Crossref: Part IV, Math. Annalen 36, 1890. Crossref
    • MacTutor describes the classification in these four parts, 1888-1890. MacTutor Biographies/Killing
    • Wikipedia: Killing essentially classified the complex simple Lie algebras in 1888-1890, with unproven but true conjectures. Wikipedia: Wilhelm Killing
  3. 1894

    Cartan's thesis rigorously completes the classification

    key development

    Cartan, Sur la structure des groupes de transformations finis et continus, Paris thesis, 1894

    Cartan rewrote Killing's results rigorously, corrected gaps and completed the list, including the exceptional algebras.

    Evidence (3)
    • zbMATH record: Cartan's 1894 thesis. zbMATH Open
    • MacTutor: Cartan set out to complete Killing's classification in his 1894 thesis. MacTutor Biographies/Cartan
    • Wikipedia: Cartan's 1894 dissertation was essentially a rigorous rewriting of Killing's paper. Wikipedia: Wilhelm Killing
  4. 1913

    Cartan classifies irreducible representations using the roots

    first application

    Cartan, Les groupes projectifs qui ne laissent invariante aucune multiplicite plane, Bull. Soc. Math. France 41, 1913

    Cartan used the root and weight structure of Killing's classification to describe the irreducible representations of simple groups.

    Dating or attribution note

    That this paper is the first use of the classification for representations rests on general knowledge, not a page read here; other earlier uses were not searched.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref: Bulletin de la Societe Mathematique de France, 1913. Crossref
  5. 1933

    van der Waerden's algebraic reproof of the classification

    key development

    van der Waerden, Die Klassifikation der einfachen Lieschen Gruppen, Math. Z. 37, 1933

    A reworked, algebraically cleaner derivation of the classification.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref: Mathematische Zeitschrift 37, 1933. Crossref
  6. 1951

    The term 'Killing form' appears

    naming

    Borel, report for Seminaire Bourbaki, 1951 (per Borel 2001)

    The name 'Killing form' for the trace form of the adjoint representation first appeared in a 1951 Bourbaki seminar report, a misnomer since Cartan had used the form earlier.

    Evidence (1)
    • Wikipedia, citing Borel's historical survey (2001), dates the first use of the term to 1951. Wikipedia: Killing form
  7. 1962

    Jacobson's Lie Algebras

    consolidation

    Jacobson, Lie algebras, Interscience, 1962

    A textbook giving the classification of simple Lie algebras in the algebraic setting.

    Evidence (1)
    • zbMATH record: Jacobson, Lie algebras, 1962. zbMATH Open
