1931 · Statistics & decision theory

Subjective probability and decision

Keynes, Ramsey and de Finetti defined probability as degree of belief, reviving Bayesian ideas against frequentism.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1921

    Keynes' treatise on probability as degree of rational belief

    precursor

    J. M. Keynes, A Treatise on Probability, Macmillan, 1921

    Keynes treated probability as a logical relation of partial belief, an earlier idea that is not yet the subjective (personalist) position.

    Evidence (2)
    • Open Library lists A Treatise on Probability with first publication 1921 Open Library search
    • MacTutor Keynes biography describes the Treatise on Probability MacTutor Keynes biography
  2. 1926

    Ramsey's manuscript 'Truth and Probability' (written 1926)

    precursor

    Ramsey developed a betting-based account of degrees of belief in a manuscript written in 1926 and published later.

    Evidence (1)
    • MacTutor Ramsey biography dates the work to 1926 MacTutor Ramsey biography
  3. 1931

    de Finetti's subjective meaning of probability

    first statement

    B. de Finetti, 'Sul significato soggettivo della probabilita', Fundamenta Mathematicae 17, 1931

    de Finetti defined probability as a subjective degree of belief operationalised by coherent betting.

    Evidence (1)
    • de Finetti 1931 Fundamenta Mathematicae paper with this title exists Crossref DOI 10.4064/fm-17-1-298-329
  4. 1931

    Ramsey's 'Truth and Probability' published

    key development

    F. P. Ramsey, 'Truth and Probability', in The Foundations of Mathematics and other Logical Essays, 1931

    Ramsey's published essay gave the betting account and a decision-theoretic framing of degrees of belief.

    Evidence (2)
    • Open Library lists The Foundations of Mathematics and other logical essays with first publication 1931 Open Library search
    • Reviews of the volume appeared in 1932 (Neville, Mathematical Gazette; Bernstein, BAMS) Crossref DOI 10.1090/s0002-9904-1932-05470-2
  5. 1954

    Savage's Foundations of Statistics

    consolidation

    L. J. Savage, The Foundations of Statistics, Wiley, 1954

    Savage gave the systematic axiomatic treatment that joined subjective probability and expected-utility decision theory.

    Evidence (2)
    • Naval Research Logistics Quarterly review of the 1954 book Crossref DOI 10.1002/nav.3800010316
    • Open Library lists The Foundations of Statistics with first publication 1954 (Wiley) Open Library search
