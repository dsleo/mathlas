Kirchhoff's circuit laws and the graph-theoretic treatment of networks

Kirchhoff's circuit laws.

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1827

    Ohm's galvanische Kette

    precursor

    G. S. Ohm, Die galvanische Kette, mathematisch bearbeitet, Berlin 1827

    Ohm's law for single conductors supplies the current-voltage relation that Kirchhoff's network laws extend.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref record for the 1827 book Die galvanische Kette. Crossref, DOI 10.5479/sil.354716.39088005838644
    • Internet Archive scan of the 1827 book. Internet Archive 10707551bsb
  2. 1845

    Kirchhoff's paper on current through a plane

    first statement

    Kirchhoff, Ueber den Durchgang eines elektrischen Stromes durch eine Ebene, insbesondere durch eine kreisförmige, Annalen der Physik (Poggendorff) 64, 1845

    History pages date the announcement of the current and voltage laws to 1845, the year of this paper by Kirchhoff, then a student at Königsberg.

    Evidence (3)
    • Crossref confirms a Kirchhoff paper of 1845 in Annalen der Physik (Crossref lists it as vol. 140, pp. 497-514) Crossref 10.1002/andp.18451400402
    • MacTutor says Kirchhoff announced his laws for multi-loop circuits in 1845 while studying with Neumann MacTutor biography of Kirchhoff
    • Wikipedia states the laws were first described in 1845 by Kirchhoff, generalising Ohm English Wikipedia, Kirchhoff's circuit laws
  3. 1847

    Kirchhoff solves linear current distributions in networks

    key development

    Kirchhoff, Ueber die Auflösung der Gleichungen, auf welche man bei der Untersuchung der linearen Vertheilung galvanischer Ströme geführt wird, Annalen der Physik (Poggendorff) 72, 497-508, 1847

    Kirchhoff treated the system of equations for currents in a network of linear conductors in general; later graph theory reads this paper as an early use of the branch structure of a network (loops, trees, cyclomatic number).

    Evidence (3)
    • Crossref record for the 1847 paper, title concerning the solution of the equations for linear distribution of galvanic currents (Crossref lists vol. 148, pp. 497-508) Crossref 10.1002/andp.18471481202
    • Wikipedia calls Kirchhoff's 1845 work the first example of modern algebra used in graph theory English Wikipedia, Graph theory
    • Wikipedia notes that the cyclomatic number was named by Kirchhoff before Betti's paper English Wikipedia, Betti number
  4. 1873

    Maxwell's Treatise treats networks

    consolidation

    J. C. Maxwell, A Treatise on Electricity and Magnetism, Oxford 1873

    Maxwell's Treatise includes a systematic account of conduction in linear networks with loop currents, building on Kirchhoff.

    Dating or attribution note

    That the Treatise discusses network loops is recalled from standard history; not read in this pass.

    Evidence (2)
    • zbMATH (Jahrbuch) record of the 1873 Treatise. zbMATH record 05.0556.01
    • MacTutor biography of Maxwell. MacTutor, biography of Maxwell
  5. 1932

    'Note on network theory' (earliest zbMATH title with the phrase in the 1845-1950 window)

    naming

    J. G. Brainerd, 'Note on network theory', journal article, pages 1660-1664, zbMATH record 3006808, 1932

    The earliest title found in the searched window that uses the phrase 'network theory' for the field that Kirchhoff's circuit laws started.

    Evidence (1)
    • zbMATH record gives title 'Note on network theory', author J. G. Brainerd, year 1932, journal article, pages 1660-1664 zbMATH Open API, document 3006808
