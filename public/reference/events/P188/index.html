Potential theory in celestial mechanics and physics

The potential function and its equation were introduced for attracting masses, then used in electrostatics.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1773

    Lagrange on the attraction of elliptic spheroids

    precursor

    J.-L. Lagrange, Sur l'attraction des spheroides elliptiques, Nouveaux Memoires de l'Academie de Berlin, annee 1773 (volume printed later)

    Lagrange expresses attraction through a single function whose derivatives give the force components, the idea later called the potential.

    Evidence (2)
    • Read: memoir 'Sur l'attraction des spheroides elliptiques', Nouveaux Memoires Berlin, annee 1773; Problem I gives the attraction of a body on a point as three integrated components, and the memoir solves the ellipsoid case analytically against Maclaurin's synthetic method. Oeuvres de Lagrange, t. III, Internet Archive oeuvresdelagrang03lagr
    • MacTutor biography of Lagrange, covering his work on gravitational attraction. MacTutor, biography of Lagrange
  2. 1785

    Laplace: the function V for attracting spheroids and its partial differential equation

    first statement

    P.-S. Laplace, Theorie des attractions des spheroides et de la figure des planetes, Memoires de l'Academie royale des Sciences de Paris, annee 1782; 1785 (Oeuvres completes, t. X)

    Laplace defines V as the sum of the masses over their distances to the attracted point, shows that its derivatives give the attractions, and derives the second-order partial differential equation it satisfies, the base of his theory of the figure of the planets.

    Evidence (4)
    • Read: heading 'Memoires de l'Academie royale des Sciences de Paris, annee 1782; 1785'; section II: V, the sum of the molecules divided by their distances, gives the attractions by differentiation, and satisfies a second-order partial differential equation, called the fundamental equation of his researches on the figure of the planets. Oeuvres completes de Laplace, t. X, Internet Archive uvrescompltesde11fragoog (pp. 341ff)
    • Read: Poisson writes that this equation (sum of the second derivatives of V equal to zero outside the masses) is the one 'dont M. Laplace a fait la base de ses belles recherches sur l'attraction des spheroides'. S.-D. Poisson, Remarques sur une equation..., Nouveau Bulletin des sciences 3 (1813)
    • MacTutor says Laplace introduced the potential function in analysis MacTutor, Laplace
    • Wikipedia dates Laplace's study of his equation to 1786, later than the memoir printed in 1785. English Wikipedia, Laplace's equation
  3. 1813

    Poisson extends the equation inside attracting masses

    key development

    S.-D. Poisson, Remarques sur une equation qui se presente dans la theorie des attractions des spheroides, Nouveau Bulletin des sciences, Soc. philomathique 3 (1813), pp. 388-392

    Poisson shows that the sum of the second derivatives of V is not zero when the attracted point lies in the mass but equals minus four pi times the density there.

    Dating or attribution note

    Paper identified and read in the Internet Archive scan of Tome III (in the issue run of 1813; the note carries the page heads (589)-(591) misread, i.e. 389-391, and the usual citation is pp. 388-392). Reclassified from first_application: it is a result extending Laplace's equation, not an application to a target problem; the electrostatic memoirs of 1811-12 that Green credits to Poisson were not read, and may be the first application. The exact month was not read from the issue heading.

    Evidence (2)
    • Read: the note states that V satisfies Laplace's equation outside the attracting body and, by splitting the body and using a homogeneous sphere, that the sum of second derivatives equals -4 pi rho (rho the density at the attracted point) for a body of any form. Internet Archive, Nouveau Bulletin des Sciences, Tome III (nouveaubulletind31812soci), 'Remarques sur une equation...'
    • MacTutor says that in 1813 Poisson studied the potential inside attracting masses. MacTutor, Poisson
  4. 1823

    Poisson's memoir on magnetism in motion

    key development

    Poisson published the inhomogeneous equation for the potential in the context of magnetism, extending Laplace's equation.

    Evidence (1)
    • Wikipedia says Poisson published the equation in 1823 in a memoir on the theory of magnetism in motion English Wikipedia, Poisson's equation
  5. 1828

    Green coins 'potential' in his Essay

    naming

    G. Green, An Essay on the Application of Mathematical Analysis to the Theories of Electricity and Magnetism, Nottingham, 1828

    Green named the sum of masses divided by distance the potential and developed its general properties for electricity and magnetism.

    Evidence (3)
    • Internet Archive item for Green's 1828 Essay on the application of mathematical analysis to the theories of electricity and magnetism archive.org essayonapplicati00gree
    • MacTutor says Green coined the term potential in the Essay published in March 1828 MacTutor, Green
    • Read: the introduction says Poisson presented two memoirs about 1812 on the distribution of electricity on conducting spheres, founded on the consideration of 'what have, in this Essay, been termed potential functions'. G. Green, An Essay on the Application of Mathematical Analysis to the Theories of Electricity and Magnetism
  6. 1840

    Gauss's general theorems on inverse-square forces

    key development

    C. F. Gauss, Allgemeine Lehrsätze in Beziehung auf die im verkehrten Verhältnisse des Quadrats der Entfernung wirkenden Anziehungs- und Abstossungs-Kräfte, 1840

    Gauss gives a systematic treatment of potentials with the divergence-type theorems, and uses the word potential independently of Green.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref record of the paper as reprinted in Gauss' Werke (1877); original date 1840 taken from the title series, not the record. Crossref, DOI 10.1007/978-3-642-49319-5_11
    • MacTutor biography of Gauss for his work on potential theory. MacTutor, biography of Gauss
  7. 1992

    Potential Analysis begins

    institution

    A journal named for the field starts publication; the first volume is 1992.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref lists Potential Analysis volume 1 articles issued in 1992 Crossref journal 0926-2601
