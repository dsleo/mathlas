1786 · Differential equations

Potential theory in celestial mechanics and physics

The potential function and its equation were introduced for attracting masses, then used in electrostatics.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1773

    Lagrange on attraction of spheroids

    precursor

    J.-L. Lagrange, Sur l'attraction des sphéroïdes elliptiques, Mém. Acad. Berlin (1773)

    Lagrange expresses attraction through a single function whose derivatives give the force components, the idea later called the potential.

    Evidence (1)
    • MacTutor biography of Lagrange, covering his work on gravitational attraction. MacTutor, biography of Lagrange
  2. 1786

    Laplace's potential function and equation for attracting spheroids

    first statement

    Laplace introduced the potential function and studied its equation in work on the attraction of spheroids, here dated by a range because sources differ.

    Evidence (3)
    • MacTutor says Laplace introduced the potential function in analysis MacTutor, Laplace
    • Wikipedia says Laplace first studied the properties of his equation in 1786 English Wikipedia, Laplace's equation
    • Wikipedia: Laplace first studied the equation's properties in 1786, not 1782 Wikipedia, Laplace's equation
  3. 1813

    Poisson's potential inside attracting masses

    first application

    Poisson studied the potential in the interior of attracting masses, a result applied to electrostatics.

    Evidence (1)
    • MacTutor says that in 1813 Poisson studied the potential inside attracting masses with application to electrostatics MacTutor, Poisson
  4. 1823

    Poisson's equation published

    key development

    Poisson published the inhomogeneous equation for the potential in the context of magnetism, extending Laplace's equation.

    Evidence (1)
    • Wikipedia says Poisson published the equation in 1823 in a memoir on the theory of magnetism in motion English Wikipedia, Poisson's equation
  5. 1828

    Green coins 'potential' in his Essay

    naming

    G. Green, An Essay on the Application of Mathematical Analysis to the Theories of Electricity and Magnetism, Nottingham, 1828

    Green named the sum of masses divided by distance the potential and developed its general properties for electricity and magnetism.

    Evidence (2)
    • Internet Archive item for Green's 1828 Essay on the application of mathematical analysis to the theories of electricity and magnetism archive.org essayonapplicati00gree
    • MacTutor says Green coined the term potential in the Essay published in March 1828 MacTutor, Green
  6. 1840

    Gauss's general theorems on inverse-square forces

    key development

    C. F. Gauss, Allgemeine Lehrsätze in Beziehung auf die im verkehrten Verhältnisse des Quadrats der Entfernung wirkenden Anziehungs- und Abstossungs-Kräfte, 1840

    Gauss gives a systematic treatment of potentials with the divergence-type theorems, and uses the word potential independently of Green.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref record of the paper as reprinted in Gauss' Werke (1877); original date 1840 taken from the title series, not the record. Crossref, DOI 10.1007/978-3-642-49319-5_11
    • MacTutor biography of Gauss for his work on potential theory. MacTutor, biography of Gauss
  7. 1992

    Potential Analysis begins

    institution

    A journal named for the field starts publication; the first volume is 1992.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref lists Potential Analysis volume 1 articles issued in 1992 Crossref journal 0926-2601
