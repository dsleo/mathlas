Potential theory in celestial mechanics and physics

The potential function and its equation were introduced for attracting masses, then used in electrostatics.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1773

    Lagrange on the attraction of elliptic spheroids

    precursor

    J.-L. Lagrange, Sur l'attraction des spheroides elliptiques, Nouveaux Memoires de l'Academie de Berlin, annee 1773 (volume printed later)

    Lagrange expresses attraction through a single function whose derivatives give the force components, the idea later called the potential.

    Evidence (2)
    • Read: memoir 'Sur l'attraction des spheroides elliptiques', Nouveaux Memoires Berlin, annee 1773; Problem I gives the attraction of a body on a point as three integrated components, and the memoir solves the ellipsoid case analytically against Maclaurin's synthetic method. Oeuvres de Lagrange, t. III, Internet Archive oeuvresdelagrang03lagr
    • MacTutor biography of Lagrange, covering his work on gravitational attraction. MacTutor, biography of Lagrange
  2. 1777–1779

    Lagrange: forces of attracting bodies as partial derivatives of one function

    first statement

    J.-L. Lagrange, Remarques generales sur le mouvement de plusieurs corps qui s'attirent mutuellement en raison inverse des carres des distances, Nouveaux Memoires de l'Academie de Berlin, annee 1777 (Oeuvres, t. 4, pp. 401-421)

    For a system of attracting bodies Lagrange puts Omega equal to the sum of the products of masses divided by their distances and shows that the force on each body is the partial derivative of Omega divided by its mass, the function later called the potential.

    Evidence (3)
    • Primary text read: Lagrange, Remarques generales sur le mouvement de plusieurs corps qui s'attirent mutuellement (Berlin, annee 1777), no. 1-3: the function Omega and the forces as its partial derivatives divided by the masses. Oeuvres de Lagrange, t. 4 (Internet Archive oeuvresdelagrang04lagr; OCR text read)
    • Editor's note to Gauss' Allgemeine Lehrsaetze: that the components of attraction of a system of points are partial derivatives of one function was first noted by Lagrange (Memoires de Berlin 1777, p. 155); Laplace used it for continuous masses in 1782 (printed 1785); the name 'Potential' is Gauss', Green said 'potential function'. Gauss, Allgemeine Lehrsaetze, ed. A. Wangerin, Ostwalds Klassiker 2 (Leipzig 1889), Anmerkungen zu Art. 2 u. 3
    • A short biography of Lagrange (search excerpt; page not opened) says the 1773 work on the Moon's secular motion contains the earliest idea of the potential and that the theory was elaborated in a paper sent to Berlin in 1777; the 1773 Moon memoir (Oeuvres t. 6, OCR) was searched for partial derivatives of an attraction function and none was found, so the excerpt is not relied on beyond the 1777 date. W. W. Rouse Ball, Lagrange (A Short Account of the History of Mathematics, Trinity College Dublin copy)
  3. 1785

    Laplace: the function V for attracting spheroids and its partial differential equation

    key development

    P.-S. Laplace, Theorie des attractions des spheroides et de la figure des planetes, Memoires de l'Academie royale des Sciences de Paris, annee 1782; 1785 (Oeuvres completes, t. X)

    Laplace defines V as the sum of the molecules of a body divided by their distances from the attracted point, shows that its derivatives give the attractions, and derives the second-order partial differential equation it satisfies; the rectangular-coordinate form of the equation appears in his Saturn-ring memoir of 1787.

    Evidence (6)
    • Read: heading 'Memoires de l'Academie royale des Sciences de Paris, annee 1782; 1785'; section II: V, the sum of the molecules divided by their distances, gives the attractions by differentiation, and satisfies a second-order partial differential equation, called the fundamental equation of his researches on the figure of the planets. Oeuvres completes de Laplace, t. X, Internet Archive uvrescompltesde11fragoog (pp. 341ff)
    • Read: Poisson writes that this equation (sum of the second derivatives of V equal to zero outside the masses) is the one 'dont M. Laplace a fait la base de ses belles recherches sur l'attraction des spheroides'. S.-D. Poisson, Remarques sur une equation..., Nouveau Bulletin des sciences 3 (1813)
    • Primary text read: Memoire sur la theorie de l'anneau de Saturne (annee 1787; 1789), section II: V and the attractions as derivatives of V, and the equation d2V/dx2 + d2V/dy2 + d2V/dz2 = 0 outside the mass in rectangular coordinates. Oeuvres completes de Laplace, t. 11 (Internet Archive oeuvrescomplte11lapluoft; OCR text read)
    • Todhunter: the equation first occurs in the 1782 memoir in polar form (Art. 851) and in rectangular coordinates in the Saturn-ring memoir (Art. 866); Legendre credits Laplace with the function V (Art. 789). I. Todhunter, A History of the Mathematical Theories of Attraction and the Figure of the Earth, vol. 2 (1873)
    • MacTutor says Laplace introduced the potential function in analysis MacTutor, Laplace
    • Wikipedia dates Laplace's study of his equation to 1786, later than the memoir printed in 1785. English Wikipedia, Laplace's equation
  4. 1812

    Poisson applies the potential to electricity on conducting spheres

    first application

    S.-D. Poisson, Memoire sur la distribution de l'electricite a la surface des corps conducteurs, Memoires de l'Institut de France, annee 1811 (two parts, printed 1812-13)

    Poisson uses the potential function and its equation to find the distribution of electricity on conducting spheres and the jump of the normal attraction across a charged surface.

    Evidence (2)
    • Primary text read: Green's introduction credits two Poisson memoirs of about 1812 on the electric distribution on spheres as founded on potential functions, and cites Mem. de l'Institut 1811. G. Green, An Essay on the Application of Mathematical Analysis to the Theories of Electricity and Magnetism (Nottingham 1828), preface (Internet Archive essayonapplicati00gree; read)
    • Editor's note: the theorem on the difference of the normal attraction components of a surface on either side was first found by Poisson (Memoire sur la distribution de l'electricite, Memoires de l'Institut t. XII, 1811). Gauss, Allgemeine Lehrsaetze, ed. A. Wangerin (1889), Anmerkungen zu Art. 13
  5. 1813

    Poisson extends the equation inside attracting masses

    key development

    S.-D. Poisson, Remarques sur une equation qui se presente dans la theorie des attractions des spheroides, Nouveau Bulletin des sciences, Soc. philomathique 3 (1813), pp. 388-392

    Poisson shows that the sum of the second derivatives of V is not zero when the attracted point lies in the mass but equals minus four pi times the density there.

    Evidence (3)
    • Read: the note states that V satisfies Laplace's equation outside the attracting body and, by splitting the body and using a homogeneous sphere, that the sum of second derivatives equals -4 pi rho (rho the density at the attracted point) for a body of any form. Internet Archive, Nouveau Bulletin des Sciences, Tome III (nouveaubulletind31812soci), 'Remarques sur une equation...'
    • MacTutor says that in 1813 Poisson studied the potential inside attracting masses. MacTutor, Poisson
    • Editor's note: Poisson first remarked that Laplace's equation holds only for points outside the mass (Bulletin de la Societe philomathique, t. 3, p. 388) and replaced it by the equation named after him, without a rigorous proof; Gauss gave one. Gauss, Allgemeine Lehrsaetze, ed. A. Wangerin (1889), Anmerkungen zu Art. 8-11
  6. 1828

    Green coins 'potential' in his Essay

    naming

    G. Green, An Essay on the Application of Mathematical Analysis to the Theories of Electricity and Magnetism, Nottingham, 1828

    Green named the sum of masses divided by distance the potential and developed its general properties for electricity and magnetism.

    Evidence (3)
    • Internet Archive item for Green's 1828 Essay on the application of mathematical analysis to the theories of electricity and magnetism archive.org essayonapplicati00gree
    • MacTutor says Green coined the term potential in the Essay published in March 1828 MacTutor, Green
    • Read: the introduction says Poisson presented two memoirs about 1812 on the distribution of electricity on conducting spheres, founded on the consideration of 'what have, in this Essay, been termed potential functions'. Title page read: Nottingham, printed for the author by T. Wheelhouse, 1828. G. Green, An Essay on the Application of Mathematical Analysis to the Theories of Electricity and Magnetism
  7. 1840

    Gauss's general theorems on inverse-square forces

    key development

    C. F. Gauss, Allgemeine Lehrsaetze in Beziehung auf die im verkehrten Verhaeltnisse des Quadrats der Entfernung wirkenden Anziehungs- und Abstossungs-Kraefte, Resultate aus den Beobachtungen des magnetischen Vereins im Jahre 1839, Leipzig: Weidmann, 1840

    Gauss defines V as the sum of the masses divided by their distances, gives it the name Potential, and proves the general theorems on volume and surface potentials, including Poisson's equation, systematically and rigorously.

    Evidence (4)
    • Primary text read: article 3, V as the sum of masses over distances and the name 'Potential'; the force components as partial derivatives; the theorems that follow. Gauss, Allgemeine Lehrsaetze (1840), Internet Archive bub_gb_WII5AAAAcAAJ (original volume, OCR text read) and allgemeinelehrs01gausgoog (Wangerin's reprint)
    • Editor's notes: the paper as the foundation of potential theory; first rigorous proof of the Laplace-Poisson equation; Green independent and unnoticed until 1846-50. Gauss, Allgemeine Lehrsaetze, ed. A. Wangerin, Ostwalds Klassiker 2 (1889), Anmerkungen
    • Crossref record of the paper as reprinted in Gauss' Werke (1877); original date 1840 taken from the title series, not the record. Crossref, DOI 10.1007/978-3-642-49319-5_11
    • MacTutor biography of Gauss for his work on potential theory. MacTutor, biography of Gauss
  8. 1992

    Potential Analysis begins

    institution

    Potential Analysis, vol. 1 (Kluwer, 1992)

    A journal named for the field starts publication; the first volume is 1992.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref lists Potential Analysis volume 1 articles issued in 1992 Crossref journal 0926-2601
