Computer algebra and Groebner bases

Groebner bases and CAD give algorithms for polynomial systems.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1964

    Hironaka's standard bases for power series

    precursor

    Hironaka, standard bases for power series (1964)

    Hironaka introduces standard bases for power series ideals, an analogous concept developed independently.

    Evidence (1)
    • Hironaka developed an analogous concept for power series in 1964, naming them standard bases Wikipedia: Groebner basis
  2. 1970

    Buchberger's algorithmic criterion

    first statement

    Buchberger, Ein algorithmisches Kriterium fur die Losbarkeit eines algebraischen Gleichungssystems, Aequationes Math. 4 (1970)

    Buchberger publishes the algorithm for Groebner bases, the first public statement in a bibliographic record.

    Evidence (2)
    • Buchberger 1970, Aequationes Math., DOI 10.1007/bf01844169 Crossref
    • Groebner bases were introduced by Buchberger in his 1965 PhD thesis, which included the algorithm Wikipedia: Groebner basis
  3. 1971

    MACSYMA as a general computer algebra system

    first application

    Martin, Fateman, The MACSYMA system, Proc. Second ACM Symposium on Symbolic and Algebraic Manipulation (1971)

    MACSYMA is the first general-purpose computer algebra system, the tool that puts symbolic polynomial manipulation on a computer.

    Evidence (2)
    • The MACSYMA system, 1971, DOI 10.1145/800204.806267 Crossref
    • MACSYMA was announced at the SIGSAM symposium in 1971 as the first general-purpose computer algebra system Wikipedia: Computer algebra
  4. 1975

    Collins: cylindrical algebraic decomposition

    key development

    G. E. Collins, 'Quantifier elimination for real closed fields by cylindrical algebraic decomposition', Automata Theory and Formal Languages, LNCS 33, Springer, 1975, 134-183

    Collins gives the first practical algorithm for quantifier elimination over the reals, a core method of computer algebra.

    Evidence (1)
    • Collins, LNCS 33 (1975) 134-183, DOI 10.1007/3-540-07407-4_17 Crossref
  5. 1985

    Journal of Symbolic Computation founded

    institution

    Journal of Symbolic Computation, founded 1985 by Bruno Buchberger

    The journal founded in 1985 gives the field a venue, the institutional marker for computer algebra.

    Evidence (1)
    • The Journal of Symbolic Computation was founded in 1985 by Buchberger Wikipedia: Computer algebra
  6. 1999

    F4 algorithm for Groebner bases

    key development

    Faugere, A new efficient algorithm for computing Groebner bases (F4), J. Pure Appl. Algebra 139 (1999)

    F4 uses linear algebra on matrices for Groebner computation and is much faster than Buchberger's algorithm.

    Evidence (2)
    • Faugere 1999, JPAA, DOI 10.1016/s0022-4049(99)00005-5 Crossref
    • When F4 is available it is generally much more efficient than Buchberger's algorithm Wikipedia: Groebner basis
