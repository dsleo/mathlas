1954 · Geometry

Nash hard implicit function theorem and isometric embedding

Nash solved the isometric embedding problem with a smoothing iteration that overcomes loss of derivatives, a method later extended by Moser.

Also known as: Nash-Moser theorem

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1954

    Nash's C1 isometric embeddings

    first statement

    Nash, C1 isometric imbeddings, Annals of Mathematics, 1954

    Nash shows that every Riemannian manifold admits a C1 isometric embedding in Euclidean space, a surprising result against the expected smoothness.

    Evidence (2)
    • Article record, Annals 1954 Crossref
    • Chern's review quoted by MacTutor describes the 1954 C1 result as surprising MacTutor, John Nash biography
  2. 1955

    Kuiper's companion C1 embedding result

    key development

    Kuiper, On C1-isometric imbeddings I, Indagationes Mathematicae, 1955

    Kuiper extends Nash's C1 result, giving the Nash-Kuiper pair of theorems.

    Evidence (1)
    • Article record, Indag. Math. 1955 Crossref
  3. 1956

    Nash's smooth isometric embedding

    first application

    Nash, The imbedding problem for Riemannian manifolds, Annals of Mathematics, 1956

    Nash proves the smooth isometric embedding theorem with a smoothing iteration that overcomes loss of derivatives, the application that motivates the Nash-Moser method.

    Evidence (2)
    • Article record, Annals 1956 Crossref
    • MacTutor says the 1956 paper extends the C1 work and contains the main smooth result MacTutor, John Nash biography
  4. 1961

    Moser's Nash-Moser iteration technique

    key development

    Moser, A new technique for the construction of solutions of nonlinear differential equations, PNAS, 1961

    Moser generalises Nash's smoothing iteration to a general implicit-function setting, the Nash-Moser method.

    Evidence (2)
    • Article record, PNAS 47 (1961) Crossref
    • Wikipedia: Nash's method generalised, with Moser's 1966 work applying it to KAM Wikipedia, Nash-Moser theorem
  5. 1982

    Hamilton's survey: the inverse function theorem of Nash and Moser

    consolidation

    Hamilton, The inverse function theorem of Nash and Moser, Bulletin of the AMS, 1982

    Hamilton's long survey is the systematic account of the Nash-Moser theorem and its range of applications.

    Evidence (1)
    • Article record, Bull. AMS 1982 Crossref
