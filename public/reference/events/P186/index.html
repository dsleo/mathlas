≈ 2,000 BCE · Algebra

Old Babylonian quadratic and cubic problem-solving

Questions about rectangle areas and excavation volumes were turned into procedures for solving quadratic and some cubic equations, without symbolic notation.

≈ marks an approximate map placement; a dated first statement is not established for this event.

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 2,000 BCE–1,600 BCE

    Old Babylonian tablet procedures

    precursor

    Old Babylonian mathematical tablets (individual dates unverified)

    Tablet problems use area and volume procedures later described as quadratic or cubic.

    Evidence (1)
    • MacTutor describes Old Babylonian tablets with rectangle-area problems leading to quadratic equations and a standard solving method. MacTutor, Babylonian mathematics
  2. 1935

    Neugebauer publishes cuneiform mathematical texts

    key development

    O. Neugebauer, Mathematische Keilschrift-Texte (1935)

    A modern edition makes the relevant tablets accessible as research texts.

    Evidence (1)
    • Neugebauer, Mathematische Keilschrift-Texte, 1935: standard edition of the mathematical tablets. Crossref/zbMATH
  3. 1937

    Gandz names Babylonian quadratic equations

    naming

    S. Gandz, The origin and development of the quadratic equations in Babylonian, Greek and early Arabic algebra (1937)

    The modern article title explicitly names the studied procedure.

    Evidence (1)
    • Gandz, The origin and development of the quadratic equations in Babylonian, Greek and early Arabic algebra (1937): earliest indexed title naming Babylonian quadratic equations. zbMATH Open
