1956 · Optimization & control

Network flows and combinatorial optimisation

Max-flow min-cut ties graph theory to LP duality (Ford-Fulkerson, Edmonds).

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1927

    Menger's theorem on disjoint paths

    precursor

    Menger, Zur allgemeinen Kurventheorie, Fundamenta Mathematicae 10 (1927) 96-115

    Menger's theorem on disjoint paths in graphs, a special case of max-flow min-cut.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref: Fundamenta Mathematicae 10 (1927) 96-115, author Menger Crossref DOI 10.4064/fm-10-1-96-115
    • Max-flow min-cut is a special case of LP duality and can be used to derive Menger's theorem Wikipedia, Max-flow min-cut theorem
  2. 1941

    Hitchcock's transportation problem

    precursor

    Hitchcock, The distribution of a product from several sources to numerous localities, Journal of Mathematics and Physics, 1941

    Hitchcock's transport problem is a linear network-flow precursor, not the max-flow result.

    Evidence (1)
    • Article record, J. Math. Phys. 1941 Crossref
  3. 1956

    Max-flow min-cut theorem

    first statement

    Ford and Fulkerson, Maximal flow through a network, Canadian Journal of Mathematics, 1956

    Ford and Fulkerson prove that maximum flow equals minimum cut in a network, the public statement of the duality.

    Evidence (3)
    • Article record, Canad. J. Math. 1956 Crossref
    • Max-flow min-cut theorem described as the equality of maximum flow and minimum cut Wikipedia, Max-flow min-cut theorem
    • Wikipedia: Ford and Fulkerson (1956) 'Maximal flow through a network', Canadian J. Math. 8; the problem was posed to them in spring 1955 by T. E. Harris Wikipedia, Max-flow min-cut theorem (history)
  4. 1962

    Flows in Networks monograph

    consolidation

    Ford and Fulkerson, Flows in Networks, Princeton University Press, 1962

    First systematic monograph on network flow theory.

    Evidence (1)
    • OpenLibrary records list Princeton University Press 1962 OpenLibrary search
  5. 1965

    Edmonds on matching and polynomial combinatorial algorithms

    key development

    Edmonds, Paths, trees, and flowers, Canadian Journal of Mathematics, 1965

    Edmonds gave a polynomial-time algorithm for maximum matching and proposed polynomial running time as the criterion for a good algorithm.

    Evidence (2)
    • Article record, Canad. J. Math. 1965 Crossref
    • In 1965 Edmonds suggested to consider a good algorithm to be one with running time bounded by a polynomial in the input size Wikipedia, Computational complexity theory
  6. 1972

    Polynomial-time max-flow algorithms

    key development

    Edmonds and Karp, Theoretical improvements in algorithmic efficiency for network flow problems, Journal of the ACM, 1972

    Edmonds and Karp give polynomial-time bounds for augmenting-path max-flow methods.

    Evidence (1)
    • Article record, JACM 1972 Crossref
  7. 1975

    'Combinatorial optimization' in a title (Edmonds)

    naming

    Edmonds, Some well-solved problems in combinatorial optimization, in Combinatorial Programming: Methods and Applications (1975) 285-301

    A paper whose title names the combinatorial optimisation field, a year before the Lawler monograph; the term was in use earlier in other forms.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref: Combinatorial Programming: Methods and Applications (1975) 285-301, author Edmonds Crossref DOI 10.1007/978-94-011-7557-9_15
  8. 1976

    Lawler's Combinatorial Optimization: Networks and Matroids

    consolidation

    Lawler, Combinatorial Optimization: Networks and Matroids, 1976

    A textbook-monograph on networks and matroids that brought combinatorial optimisation together as a subject.

    Evidence (2)
    • zbMATH 0413.90040 record, 1976 zbMATH Open
    • Combinatorial optimization is a subfield of optimization in the Wikipedia article Wikipedia, Combinatorial optimization
