1920 · Logic & foundations

Many-valued logic

Lukasiewicz and Post independently defined logics with more than two truth values.

Also known as: multi-valued logic

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1920

    Łukasiewicz publishes three-valued logic

    first statement

    J. Łukasiewicz, O logice trójwartościowej, Ruch Filozoficzny 5 (1920)

    A public note formulates the three-valued system.

    Evidence (1)
    • Łukasiewicz's O logice trójwartościowej was printed in Ruch Filozoficzny in 1920. Digitized original journal issue
  2. 1920

    Three values applied to future contingents

    first application

    J. Łukasiewicz, O logice trójwartościowej, Ruch Filozoficzny 5 (1920)

    The extra truth value addresses future-contingent propositions.

    Evidence (1)
    • The publication develops a third truth value to address future contingents. Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy
  3. 1920

    Three-valued logic named in the title

    naming

    J. Łukasiewicz, O logice trójwartościowej, Ruch Filozoficzny 5 (1920)

    The title explicitly names three-valued logic, a narrower term than all many-valued logics.

    Evidence (1)
    • The article title names three-valued logic. Digitized original journal issue
  4. 1921

    Post generalizes to finitely many values

    key development

    E. Post, Introduction to a general theory of elementary propositions, Amer. J. Math. 43 (1921)

    Finite many-valued truth-table systems extend the initial example.

    Evidence (1)
    • Post 1921 generalised truth tables to any finite number of values and proved functional completeness results Crossref record; content per SEP Many-Valued Logic and MacTutor Post
  5. 1952

    Many-valued logics monograph

    consolidation

    J. B. Rosser and A. R. Turquette, Many-valued Logics (1952)

    A book-length treatment consolidates the named wider field.

    Evidence (1)
    • Rosser and Turquette, Many-valued logics, North-Holland (1952): first monograph zbMATH 0047.01503
