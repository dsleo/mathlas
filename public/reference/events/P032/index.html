1980 · Geometry

Noncommutative geometry

Geometric invariants (cohomology, index theory) are extended to operator algebras viewed as spaces of noncommutative functions.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1980

    Differential geometry on C*-algebras

    first statement

    Connes, A., C*-algèbres et géométrie différentielle, C. R. Acad. Sci. Paris Sér. A 290, 1980

    Connes' first note proposes studying C*-algebras with tools of differential geometry, the program that became noncommutative geometry.

    Evidence (1)
    • Connes's C*-algèbres et géométrie différentielle is dated 1980 in the IHES archive. IHES archives, Connes M/80/04
  2. 1981

    Thom isomorphism for crossed products

    first application

    Connes, A., An analogue of the Thom isomorphism for crossed products of a C*-algebra by an action of R, Advances in Mathematics 39, 1981

    Connes applies the new algebraic tools to a topological problem for crossed products, an early purposeful use.

    Evidence (1)
    • Article in Advances in Mathematics, 1981 Crossref record
  3. 1985

    Non-commutative differential geometry

    key development

    Connes, A., Non-commutative differential geometry, Publ. Math. IHÉS 62, 1985

    The IHÉS paper develops the differential calculus and cyclic tools that gave the subject its main content.

    Evidence (1)
    • Article in Publ. Math. IHÉS, 1985 (DOI 10.1007/bf02698807) Crossref record
  4. 1994

    Monograph Noncommutative Geometry

    consolidation

    Connes, A., Noncommutative Geometry, Academic Press, 1994

    Connes' book is the first systematic monograph on the subject.

    Evidence (2)
    • Book published by Academic Press, 1994 Open Library record
    • Connes published a major text on the topic in 1994 MacTutor: Alain Connes
