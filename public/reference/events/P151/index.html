1690 · Differential equations

Differential equations from calculus and mechanics

Newton solved fluxional equations by series; Leibniz separated variables; Bernoullis solved named equations.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1684

    Leibniz publishes differential notation

    precursor

    G. W. Leibniz, Nova methodus pro maximis et minimis, Acta Eruditorum (1684)

    Differential calculus and terminology precede the explicit isochrone equation.

    Evidence (1)
    • Leibniz used 'aequationes differentiales' in Acta Eruditorum, October 1684 MacTutor, Earliest Known Uses (D)
  2. 1690

    Bernoulli states an isochrone differential equation

    first statement

    J. Bernoulli, Acta Eruditorum isochrone paper (1690)

    A published mechanics problem is expressed by a differential equation.

    Evidence (1)
    • MacTutor: Jacob Bernoulli's May 1690 Acta Eruditorum paper reduced the isochrone problem to a first-order nonlinear differential equation and solved it by separating variables MacTutor, Jacob Bernoulli
  3. 1690

    Bernoulli solves the isochrone problem

    first application

    J. Bernoulli, Acta Eruditorum isochrone paper (1690)

    The equation is purposefully used to find the curve.

    Evidence (1)
    • Jacob Bernoulli (Acta Eruditorum, May 1690) reduced the isochrone to a first-order nonlinear ODE MacTutor, Jacob Bernoulli biography
  4. 1707

    Manfredi's treatise on differential equations

    consolidation

    G. Manfredi, De constructione aequationum differentialium primi gradus (1707)

    A titled book-length treatment consolidates first-order equations.

    Evidence (1)
    • Manfredi, De constructione aequationum differentialium primi gradus, 1707 archive.org record
