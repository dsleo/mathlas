1808 · Functional & harmonic analysis

Fourier series: from heat flow to analysis

The heat equation leads to arbitrary functions represented as trigonometric series, forcing a redefinition of function and integral.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1753

    Trigonometric series in the vibrating-string dispute (Euler, d'Alembert, D. Bernoulli)

    precursor

    Memoires de l'Academie de Berlin, volumes for 1750 and 1753 (d'Alembert, Euler, D. Bernoulli); Lagrange's later memoir

    Earlier work on wave motion used trigonometric sums, providing the toolkit Fourier generalised.

    Evidence (2)
    • Wikipedia names Euler, d'Alembert and Daniel Bernoulli as having done preliminary investigations before Fourier. English Wikipedia, Fourier series (History)
    • Riemann's history section (read) names Euler, d'Alembert, Daniel Bernoulli and Lagrange as the earlier disputants on representing arbitrary functions of the vibrating string by trigonometric series, with Lagrange claiming a proof of the possibility. B. Riemann, Abh. Gott. 13 (1868), section 1
  2. 1807

    Fourier's memoir on heat propagation in solid bodies

    precursor

    J. Fourier, memoire sur la propagation de la chaleur dans les corps solides, read to the Paris Institute 21 December 1807

    Fourier used sums of sines and cosines to solve the heat equation for a solid, the purpose that created the theory.

    Dating or attribution note

    Memoir read (communicated) to the Academy on 21 December 1807; not printed. Darboux (Oeuvres de Fourier, vol. 1, Avant-propos, read) says the memoir did not reach him and is known only through the four-page extract of 1808, adding that it was read and deposited but probably withdrawn by Fourier in 1810; Fourier's own Discours preliminaire (read) calls it a manuscript handed to the Institut at the end of 1807 and cites the extract. The manuscript was later located in the Academy archives and edited by Grattan-Guinness and Ravetz (1972), which was not read. Precursor because unprinted; its content became public through the 1808 extract.

    Evidence (4)
    • Primary text read: Darboux's Avant-propos says Fourier's heat researches were communicated to the Academy on 21 December 1807, that the memoir has not come down to the editors and is known only by a four-page extract in the Bulletin de la Societe philomathique (1808). Oeuvres de Fourier, ed. G. Darboux, t. 1 (Gauthier-Villars, 1888), Avant-propos
    • MacTutor: work began around 1804; the 1807 memoir was read on 21 December 1807, with Lagrange and Laplace objecting to its generality and rigour. MacTutor, Fourier biography
    • Wikipedia says the memoir introduced Fourier series for the heat equation. English Wikipedia, Fourier series
    • Riemann's 1867 history of the question (read in the Abhandlungen volume): Fourier, in his first heat work submitted to the Academy (21 Dec. 1807), first stated that a quite arbitrary (graphically given) function can be expressed by a trigonometric series, and Lagrange opposed it. B. Riemann, Ueber die Darstellbarkeit einer Function durch eine trigonometrische Reihe, Abh. Gott. 13 (1868), section 'Geschichte der Frage'
  3. 1808

    Poisson's extract of Fourier's heat memoir states the arbitrary-function series

    first statement

    Extract signed P. (S.-D. Poisson) of Fourier's Memoire sur la propagation de la chaleur dans les corps solides, Nouveau Bulletin des sciences, Soc. philomathique 1 (issue of March 1808), pp. 112-116

    A five-page account (pp. 112-116) of the 1807 memoir expands the temperature of a plate in cosine terms and fixes the coefficients by integrating the given function times cosines, so an arbitrary function on an interval is represented by a trigonometric series for the heat problem.

    Evidence (4)
    • Full text of Nouveau Bulletin des sciences, Tome I (1807-08): the extract 'Memoire sur la propagation de la Chaleur dans les corps solides; par M. Fourier', signed P., gives the series sum of cosines, the integral for the coefficients and the validity interval; it is followed by the issue headed April 1808. Internet Archive, Nouveau Bulletin des Sciences, Tome 1 (nouveaubulletind11807soci), djvu text
    • Primary text read: Darboux's note to the reprinted memoir of 1807 says the Bulletin article, signed P., was written by Poisson, an editor of the Bulletin des Sciences for mathematics, and is the first writing to make Fourier's theory known. Oeuvres de Fourier, ed. G. Darboux, t. 2 (Gauthier-Villars, 1890), note at p. 215 (Internet Archive scan, OCR text read)
    • Primary text read: Fourier's Discours preliminaire refers to the 1807 manuscript and its extract in the Bulletin des Sciences (Societe philomathique, 1808, pp. 112-116). Oeuvres de Fourier, t. 1 (1888), Discours preliminaire (Internet Archive scan)
    • Riemann cites 'Bulletin d. sc. Tom. I. p. 115. Les coefficients a, a', a''... etant ainsi determines' as the place where Fourier's statement is found. B. Riemann, Abh. Gott. 13 (1868), footnote to the history section
  4. 1811–1812

    Paris Institute prize for the theory of heat (essay of 1811, crowned January 1812)

    precursor

    Fourier's prize essay, sent 28 September 1811, crowned 6 January 1812; printed in Mem. Acad. Sci. Inst. France 4 (1819-20, publ. 1824) and 5 (1821-22, publ. 1826)

    The Institute's prize on heat propagation was awarded to Fourier, though the report criticised rigour.

    Evidence (2)
    • Primary text read: Fourier presented a memoir on 28 September 1811, crowned on 6 January 1812; its integral printing is later than the 1822 book. Oeuvres de Fourier, t. 2 (1890), Darboux's note on chapter IX (Internet Archive scan, OCR text read)
    • MacTutor: the 1811 prize subject was heat propagation in solids and Fourier's essay won, with reservations. MacTutor, Fourier biography
  5. 1822

    Theorie analytique de la chaleur (systematic treatment)

    consolidation

    Joseph Fourier, Theorie analytique de la chaleur, Paris, 1822

    The book gives the full printed theory: heat equation solved by trigonometric series and integrals for arbitrary functions, with the motivating physical problems; it is Fourier's systematic treatment.

    Evidence (3)
    • Library record and title page (Firmin Didot, Paris) of the 1822 first edition, read in the scan. Internet Archive item thorieanalytiq00four
    • MacTutor: the Academy published his prize essay as the Analytical Theory of Heat in 1822. MacTutor, Fourier biography
    • Wikipedia dates the treatise to 1822. English Wikipedia, Fourier series
  6. 1829

    Dirichlet's convergence theorem

    key development

    P. G. L. Dirichlet, Sur la convergence des series trigonometriques qui servent a representer une fonction arbitraire entre des limites donnees, J. reine angew. Math. 4 (1829) 157-169

    Dirichlet gave the first rigorous sufficient conditions for a Fourier series to converge to its function.

    Evidence (4)
    • Crossref record, Crelle's Journal 1829, pages 157-169. Crossref DOI 10.1515/crll.1829.4.157
    • Primary text read: arXiv 0806.1294 is a transcription of the Crelle 1829 paper; its introduction says the convergence property was stated in Fourier's first memoir on heat, that Cauchy's attempt of 1823 is defective, and the paper proves the result for functions with finitely many maxima, minima and discontinuities. Riemann's 1868 account (read) states the same result. arXiv 0806.1294 (read); Riemann 1868, history section (read)
    • Wikipedia notes Dirichlet and Riemann later put Fourier's results on precise footing. English Wikipedia, Fourier series
    • MacTutor: Dirichlet's work on convergence is published in Crelle's Journal (dated 1828 there); Cauchy's proof was found defective. MacTutor, Dirichlet biography
  7. 1868

    Riemann's habilitation on trigonometric series (printed posthumously)

    key development

    B. Riemann, Ueber die Darstellbarkeit einer Function durch eine trigonometrische Reihe, Abh. K. Ges. Wiss. Goettingen 13 (1866-67) 87-132, ed. Dedekind; written 1853-54

    Riemann studied which functions are integrable and the converse question of what a trigonometric series says about its function.

    Evidence (3)
    • MacTutor: after thirty months of work the dissertation defined integrability (Riemann integral) and asked the reverse question for trigonometric series. MacTutor, Riemann biography
    • Read in the scan: contents list and text give the paper at p. 87 of volume 13 (title page 1868, 'von den Jahren 1866 und 1867'), 'Aus dem Nachlass mitgetheilt durch R. Dedekind', preface dated July 1867; it covers the history of the question, the definition of the integral and the representability theorems. Internet Archive, Abh. Konigl. Ges. Wiss. Gottingen vol. 13 (1866-67), djvu text
    • Dedekind edited the work after Riemann's death; it appeared in the Abhandlungen of the Goettingen Society, vol. 13, pp. 87-132. Sunada, Generalized Riemann sums (arXiv 1701.04699)
