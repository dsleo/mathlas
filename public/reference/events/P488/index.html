1960 · Topology

Kervaire invariant one problem (solved by equivariant homotopy)

Equivariant stable homotopy and a slice spectral sequence show that framed manifolds with Kervaire invariant one exist only in dimensions up to 126.

Also known as: Arf-Kervaire problem

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1950

    Pontryagin's Arf invariant of framed surfaces

    precursor

    Pontryagin uses the Arf invariant of framed surfaces to compute a stable homotopy group, the seed of the Kervaire invariant.

    Evidence (1)
    • Pontryagin's 1950 use of the Arf invariant Wikipedia, Kervaire invariant
  2. 1960

    Kervaire's invariant and the manifold without smooth structure

    first statement

    Michel Kervaire, A manifold which does not admit any differentiable structure, Comment. Math. Helv. 34 (1960)

    Kervaire defines the invariant and uses it to build a PL manifold with no smooth structure, in dimension 10.

    Evidence (2)
    • Kervaire 1960 Comment. Math. Helv. record Crossref
    • Invariant used to construct the Kervaire manifold in dimension 10 Wikipedia, Kervaire invariant
  3. 1963

    Kervaire-Milnor: invariant nonzero in dimensions 6 and 14

    first application

    Michel Kervaire and John Milnor, Groups of homotopy spheres I, Annals of Math. 77 (1963)

    The invariant is computed to be nonzero in dimensions 6 and 14, one step in the exotic-sphere computation.

    Evidence (2)
    • Kervaire-Milnor 1963 Annals record Crossref
    • Nonzero in dimensions 6 and 14 Wikipedia, Kervaire invariant
  4. 1965–1966

    Brown and Peterson's 'The Kervaire invariant of (8k+2)-manifolds'

    naming

    E. H. Brown Jr. and F. P. Peterson, The Kervaire invariant of (8k+2)-manifolds, 1965-1966

    The earliest zbMATH-indexed title naming the Kervaire invariant.

    Evidence (1)
    • zbMATH records: Brown and Peterson, 'The Kervaire invariant of (8k+2)-manifolds', with two entries dated 1965 and 1966. zbMATH Open, API ti:"Kervaire invariant" search
  5. 1969

    Browder: reduction to stable homotopy

    key development

    William Browder, The Kervaire invariant of framed manifolds and its generalization, Annals of Math. 90 (1969)

    Shows the invariant vanishes outside dimensions 2^k-2 and reduces the problem to stable homotopy.

    Evidence (2)
    • Browder 1969 Annals record Crossref
    • Browder's dimension restriction Wikipedia, Kervaire invariant
  6. 1984

    Barratt-Jones-Mahowald: nonzero in dimension 62

    key development

    M. G. Barratt, J. D. S. Jones, M. E. Mahowald, Relations amongst Toda brackets and the Kervaire invariant in dimension 62, J. London Math. Soc. 30 (1984) 533-550

    Establishes a nonzero Kervaire invariant element in dimension 62.

    Evidence (1)
    • BJM 1984 JLMS record Crossref
  7. 2016

    Hill-Hopkins-Ravenel: no elements in dimensions 2^k-2 for k at least 8

    key development

    Michael A. Hill, Michael J. Hopkins, Douglas C. Ravenel, On the nonexistence of elements of Kervaire invariant one, Annals of Math. 184 (2016) 1-262

    Uses equivariant stable homotopy and a slice spectral sequence to show the invariant is zero in dimensions 2^k-2 for k at least 8.

    Evidence (2)
    • HHR 2016 Annals record Crossref
    • Preprint arXiv:0908.3724 posted 2009-08-26 (first public statement) arXiv
  8. 2024

    Lin-Wang-Xu: existence in dimension 126

    key development

    Weinan Lin, Guozhen Wang, Zhouli Xu, On the Last Kervaire Invariant Problem, arXiv:2412.10879 (2024)

    Preprint proving a smooth framed manifold with Kervaire invariant one exists in dimension 126, completing the list of dimensions.

    Evidence (2)
    • arXiv record, posted 2024-12-14 arXiv
    • Dimension 126 settled by 2024 preprint Wikipedia, Kervaire invariant
