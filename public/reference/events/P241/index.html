Rota combinatorial theory

Moebius functions of posets unified inversion.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1936

    Hall's Mobius function for groups

    precursor

    Hall, The Eulerian functions of a group, Quart. J. Math. 7 (1936) 134-151

    Hall's Eulerian functions on the subgroup lattice are an early Mobius-type inversion, the background to Rota's unification.

    Evidence (1)
    • Hall 1936, Quart. J. Math., DOI 10.1093/qmath/os-7.1.134 Crossref
  2. 1964

    Rota's Mobius functions of posets

    first statement

    Rota, On the foundations of combinatorial theory I. Theory of Mobius functions, Z. Wahrscheinlichkeitstheorie verw. Geb. 2 (1964) 340-368

    Rota defines Mobius functions on partially ordered sets and unifies inversion across combinatorics.

    Evidence (2)
    • Rota 1964, DOI 10.1007/bf00531932 Crossref
    • Rota founded combinatorial theory on Mobius functions (Foundations I) Wikipedia: Gian-Carlo Rota
  3. 1976

    Binomial posets and permutation enumeration

    key development

    Stanley, Binomial posets, Mobius inversion, and permutation enumeration, JCTA 20 (1976)

    Stanley extends Rota's Mobius framework to binomial posets, a main development for enumeration.

    Evidence (1)
    • Stanley 1976, J. Combin. Theory Ser. A, DOI 10.1016/0097-3165(76)90028-5 Crossref
  4. 1979

    Aigner's Combinatorial Theory

    consolidation

    Aigner, Combinatorial Theory, Grundlehren der math. Wissenschaften 234, Springer (1979)

    A systematic monograph treating Mobius inversion and order-theoretic combinatorics.

    Evidence (1)
    • Aigner 1979, Springer, DOI 10.1007/978-1-4615-6666-3 Crossref
