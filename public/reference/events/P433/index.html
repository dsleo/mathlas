1908 · Algebra

Non-commutative ring theory: Wedderburn structure theory and chain conditions

Quaternions and matrix algebras supplied examples; Wedderburn then classified finite-dimensional algebras into radical and semisimple parts and Artin extended this with chain conditions.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1843

    Quaternions launch non-commutative algebra

    precursor

    Hamilton, quaternions, 1843-44

    The four-dimensional quaternion algebra gives the first prominent non-commutative number system and the starting point for the later theory.

    Evidence (2)
    • Topic page says quaternions launched non-commutative ring theory; Cayley's matrices and Peirce's 1870 ring-axiom remark followed. MacTutor: Ring theory
    • Hamilton's 1844 Phil. Mag. paper, first part, is on record. Crossref record 10.1080/14786444408644923
  2. 1905

    Wedderburn: finite division rings are commutative

    precursor

    J. H. M. Wedderburn, A theorem on finite algebras, Trans. AMS 6 (1905) 349-352

    Wedderburn proves that a finite division algebra is a field; MacTutor notes the first proof had a gap and that Dickson found a correct proof.

    Evidence (3)
    • Title, journal, volume 6 and pages 349-352 confirmed, 1905. Crossref record 10.1090/s0002-9947-1905-1500717-7
    • Topic page dates the finite-division-ring result to 1905. MacTutor: Ring theory
    • Biography discusses the three proofs and Dickson's role. MacTutor: Wedderburn biography
  3. 1908

    Wedderburn, On hypercomplex numbers

    first statement

    J. H. M. Wedderburn, On hypercomplex numbers, Proc. London Math. Soc. (2) 6 (1908) 77-118

    Wedderburn splits a finite-dimensional algebra into a radical and a semisimple part and shows semisimple algebras are sums of matrix algebras over division rings.

    Evidence (3)
    • Title, journal, volume s2-6 and pages 77-118 confirmed, dated 1908. Crossref record 10.1112/plms/s2-6.1.77
    • Topic page dates the radical/semisimple idea to 1908. MacTutor: Ring theory
    • Biography calls it his most famous paper and gives 1907 as its date. MacTutor: Wedderburn biography
  4. 1927

    Artin extends the theory with chain conditions

    key development

    E. Artin, Zur Theorie der hyperkomplexen Zahlen, Abh. Math. Sem. Hamburg 5 (1927) 251-260

    Artin extends Wedderburn's structure theory to rings satisfying both ascending and descending chain conditions.

    Dating or attribution note

    A companion paper, Zur Arithmetik hyperkomplexer Zahlen (10.1007/bf02952527), follows in the same volume; the exact content of each was not read.

    Evidence (2)
    • Title, journal, volume 5, pages 251-260, December 1927 confirmed. Crossref record 10.1007/bf02952526
    • Topic page dates the chain-condition extension to Artin 1927. MacTutor: Ring theory
  5. 1930

    van der Waerden's Moderne Algebra, vol. 1 (textbook of Artin-Noether algebra)

    consolidation

    B. L. van der Waerden, Moderne Algebra, Bd. I, Berlin: Springer, 1930 (Grundlehren 23)

    A textbook of the Artin-Noether style of algebra, which is the first book-length presentation found in this search that carries the chain-condition approach to rings.

    Dating or attribution note

    Chosen as the textbook consolidation. Chapter content on Artinian rings and the Wedderburn-Artin theorem was not read; the claim rests on the title and series. Dickson's 'Algebras and their arithmetics' (1923) is an earlier monograph, but it predates Artin's 1927 chain-condition work, so it is not used here.

    Evidence (1)
    • zbMATH record 'Moderne Algebra. Bd. I', van der Waerden, Springer 1930 (Grundlehren series) zbMATH Open API
  6. 1939

    Hopkins: descending chain condition suffices

    key development

    C. Hopkins, Rings with minimal condition for left ideals, Ann. of Math. 40 (1939) 712

    Hopkins shows only the descending chain condition is needed.

    Evidence (2)
    • Title, Annals of Mathematics vol. 40, July 1939 confirmed. Crossref record 10.2307/1968951
    • Topic page credits Hopkins 1939 with this reduction. MacTutor: Ring theory
  7. 1945

    Jacobson radical for arbitrary rings

    key development

    N. Jacobson, The radical and semi-simplicity for arbitrary rings, Amer. J. Math. 67 (1945) 300

    Jacobson extends Wedderburn-Artin type results to rings without chain conditions via the Jacobson radical.

    Evidence (2)
    • Title, journal, volume 67, April 1945 confirmed. Crossref record 10.2307/2371731
    • Topic page dates the breakthrough to 1945 and links it to Perlis' 1942 ideas. MacTutor: Ring theory
