1827 · Complex analysis

Abel: elliptic functions as inverses of elliptic integrals

Inverting elliptic integrals gave doubly periodic functions.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1811–1819

    Legendre's work on elliptic integrals

    precursor

    A.-M. Legendre, Exercices de calcul integral, volumes 1811, 1817, 1819

    Legendre set out the theory of elliptic integrals that Abel and Jacobi then inverted.

    Evidence (2)
    • MacTutor: Legendre's main work on elliptic functions is in the three volumes of the Exercices of 1811, 1817 and 1819. MacTutor, Legendre biography
    • MacTutor topic page: elliptic integrals studied since Wallis (1655); terminology changed over time. MacTutor, Elliptic functions and integrals
  2. 1827

    Abel's Recherches sur les fonctions elliptiques, part one

    first statement

    N. H. Abel, Recherches sur les fonctions elliptiques, J. reine angew. Math. 2 (1827) 101-181

    Abel inverted elliptic integrals to obtain doubly periodic elliptic functions.

    Evidence (3)
    • Crossref record, Crelle's Journal 1827, pages 101-181. Crossref DOI 10.1515/crll.1827.2.101
    • MacTutor: Abel turned the theory of elliptic integrals into that of elliptic functions by using inverse functions. MacTutor, Abel biography
    • Wikipedia: Abel discovered elliptic functions by taking the inverse of the elliptic integral. English Wikipedia, Elliptic function
  3. 1828

    Recherches, continuation

    key development

    N. H. Abel, Recherches sur les fonctions elliptiques (suite), J. reine angew. Math. 3 (1828) 160-190

    The second part of the memoir carried the theory further.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref record, Crelle's Journal 1828, pages 160-190, marked as the continuation. Crossref DOI 10.1515/crll.1828.3.160
  4. 1828

    Legendre's praise of Abel and Jacobi

    recognition

    letter of A.-M. Legendre to Jacobi, 9 February 1828

    Legendre saluted two young mathematicians who were advancing the branch of analysis he had long studied.

    Evidence (1)
    • MacTutor quotes the 9 February 1828 letter placing both young men among the best analysts of the era. MacTutor, Jacobi biography
  5. 1829

    Abel's Precis d'une theorie des fonctions elliptiques

    key development

    N. H. Abel, Precis d'une theorie des fonctions elliptiques, J. reine angew. Math. 4 (1829) 236-277 and 309-348

    A fuller presentation of the theory appeared in two parts in the following year.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref records for both parts, Crelle's Journal 1829. Crossref DOIs 10.1515/crll.1829.4.236 and 10.1515/crll.1829.4.309
  6. 1829

    Jacobi proposes the name elliptic functions

    naming

    C. G. J. Jacobi, Fundamenta nova theoriae functionum ellipticarum, 1829

    Jacobi's 1829 suggestion that the inverse functions of elliptic integrals be called elliptic functions, the name still in use.

    Evidence (2)
    • Following a suggestion of Jacobi in 1829, the inverse functions are now called elliptic functions. Wikipedia, Elliptic function
    • Open Library record of the 1829 Fundamenta nova theoriae functionum ellipticarum. Open Library work record
