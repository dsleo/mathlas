1933 · Logic & foundations

Tarski's semantic truth definition and model theory

A semantic definition of truth in formal languages gave the basis for the study of models of theories.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1915

    Lowenheim's downward result

    precursor

    Lowenheim, 'Uber Moglichkeiten im Relativkalkul', Mathematische Annalen 76 (1915)

    Lowenheim proved a special case of the downward Lowenheim-Skolem theorem, the first significant result later counted as model theory.

    Evidence (2)
    • First significant model-theoretic result, dated 1915 Wikipedia: Model theory (history section)
    • Paper record: Math. Ann. 1915 Crossref
  2. 1929–1931

    Tarski's private results before 1933

    precursor

    Tarski's undefinability results, obtained 1929-1931 and presented to Polish audiences (unpublished in that form)

    Tarski had most results of his later monograph by 1931 and talked about them before they were printed; this is a precursor, not a public first statement.

    Evidence (1)
    • Results obtained between 1929 and 1931 and spoken about before publication Wikipedia: Tarski's undefinability theorem (history)
  3. 1933

    Tarski's semantic definition of truth

    first statement

    Tarski, 'Der Wahrheitsbegriff in den formalisierten Sprachen' (German version of the Polish 1933 monograph), Studia Philosophica vol. 1 (1935-36)

    Tarski gave a recursive semantic definition of truth for formal languages and showed truth cannot be defined inside those languages.

    Evidence (2)
    • 1933 monograph on the concept of truth; paper discussed as a 1933 publication MacTutor: Tarski biography
    • German version reprinted in Logik-Texte; its publication year is itself the subject of Milne (2024) Crossref
  4. 1951

    Decision method for elementary algebra and geometry (model-theoretic method applied to the decision problem)

    first application

    Alfred Tarski, A Decision Method for Elementary Algebra and Geometry, 2nd ed., University of California Press, 1951 (first circulated as a RAND report in 1948)

    Tarski uses semantic and model-theoretic methods to show that the first-order theory of the real numbers under addition and multiplication is decidable.

    Evidence (2)
    • Tarski showed the first-order theory of real numbers under addition and multiplication is decidable, in the book A Decision Method for Elementary Algebra and Geometry (listed 1948). MacTutor biography of Alfred Tarski, works list
    • Bibliographic record: Tarski, A decision method for elementary algebra and geometry, 2nd ed., 1951. zbMATH Open record 3068536
  5. 1954

    'Theory of models' coined

    naming

    Tarski, 'Contributions to the Theory of Models I', Indagationes Mathematicae 16 (1954)

    Tarski coined the term 'theory of models' in a 1954 series of papers, the name the discipline took.

    Evidence (2)
    • Paper record: Indagationes Mathematicae 1954, Contributions to the Theory of Models I and II Crossref
    • Term 'theory of models' coined by Tarski in 1954 Wikipedia: Model theory (history section)
  6. 1973

    Chang and Keisler's Model Theory

    consolidation

    Chang and Keisler, Model Theory (North-Holland, 1973)

    A systematic first-order monograph that served as the standard textbook for the subject.

    Evidence (1)
    • Open Library record for 'Model theory', first published 1973 under C. C. Chang Open Library search
