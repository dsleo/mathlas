Combinatorial game theory (impartial and partisan games)

Games assigned number values.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1901

    Bouton's solution of Nim

    precursor

    Bouton, 'Nim, a game with a complete mathematical theory', Annals of Mathematics (1901)

    Bouton gave a complete theory of Nim, the base case that the Sprague-Grundy theorem later generalised.

    Evidence (2)
    • Paper record: Annals of Mathematics 1901 Crossref
    • Nim as the basis of impartial games in the Sprague-Grundy theorem Wikipedia: Combinatorial game theory (history)
  2. 1930–1939

    Sprague-Grundy theorem (impartial games as nim-heaps)

    first statement

    Sprague, 'Uber mathematische Kampfspiele', Tohoku Mathematical Journal (1935-36); Grundy, 'Mathematics and games', Eureka (1939)

    Each impartial game is equivalent to a nim-heap value, so impartial games are unified by one number system.

    Evidence (1)
    • Sprague-Grundy theorem placed in the 1930s Wikipedia: Combinatorial game theory (history)
  3. 1976

    Conway's On Numbers and Games

    key development

    Conway, On Numbers and Games (Academic Press, 1976)

    Extended the theory to partisan games, where the two players are not restricted to the same moves, and introduced surreal numbers as their values.

    Evidence (3)
    • Open Library record: 'On numbers and games' by Conway, first published 1976 Open Library search
    • ONAG described as the first work published on partisan combinatorial games Wikipedia: Combinatorial game theory (history)
    • Conway's publication career and games work MacTutor: Conway biography
  4. 1982

    Winning Ways for your Mathematical Plays

    consolidation

    Berlekamp, Conway and Guy, Winning Ways for your Mathematical Plays (Academic Press, 1982)

    The two-volume reference that presented the theory with applications and became the standard handbook of the field.

    Evidence (2)
    • Open Library record: 'Winning Ways for your mathematical plays', first published 1982 Open Library search
    • Winning Ways presents the partisan theory Wikipedia: Combinatorial game theory (history)
