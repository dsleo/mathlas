Quaternions: non-commutative algebra from the geometry of 3-space

Hamilton's decade-long search for a multiplication on three-dimensional space ended in the four-dimensional non-commutative quaternions, the first prominent non-commutative number system, taken up in physics by Tait.

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1833

    Hamilton treats complex numbers as ordered pairs

    precursor

    W. R. Hamilton, algebraic couples, read to the Royal Irish Academy 4 November 1833; printed Trans. Royal Irish Academy 17 (1837)

    Hamilton presents complex numbers as a two-dimensional system over the reals, then spends about ten years seeking a three-dimensional analogue.

    Evidence (1)
    • Topic page says he presented this to the Irish Academy in 1833 and then tried to define a product on 3-space. MacTutor: Abstract linear spaces
  2. 1843

    Discovery of the quaternion rules, 16 October 1843

    precursor

    Hamilton, private discovery and letters, October 1843

    Hamilton realises that the product on triples fails but a four-dimensional non-commutative system works, and records i^2=j^2=k^2=ijk=-1.

    Evidence (2)
    • Biography dates the discovery to 16 October 1843 and calls quaternions the first non-commutative algebra studied. MacTutor: Hamilton biography
    • Article gives the same date and notes earlier four-parameter work by Euler (1748) and Rodrigues (1840) that did not treat it as an algebra. English Wikipedia: Quaternion
  3. 1844

    Hamilton, On quaternions, Philosophical Magazine

    first statement

    W. R. Hamilton, On quaternions; or on a new system of imaginaries in algebra, Phil. Mag. (3) 25 (1844) 10-13 and later parts

    Hamilton publishes the first part of a series presenting the quaternion system in print.

    Evidence (3)
    • Crossref gives vol. 25, pp. 10-13, dated July 1844, for the first part; later parts have DOIs 10.1080/14786444408644984 and 10.1080/14786444408645047. Crossref record 10.1080/14786444408644923
    • Article says quaternions were introduced by Hamilton in 1843 (announcement) with the series following. English Wikipedia: Quaternion
    • Transcription of the Proc. R. Irish Acad. 2 (1844) 424-434 paper, communicated 13 November 1843, which calls the new quantities quaternions Wilkins, Trinity College Dublin History of Mathematics archive
  4. 1844

    The word quaternion in a title

    naming

    W. R. Hamilton, On quaternions..., Phil. Mag. 25 (1844)

    The new system is named quaternions in the title of the Philosophical Magazine series.

    Evidence (2)
    • Title of the first part includes the term quaternions. Crossref record 10.1080/14786444408644923
    • Title of the Proc. R. Irish Acad. 2 (1844) paper contains 'a theory of Quaternions' Wilkins, Trinity College Dublin History of Mathematics archive
  5. 1853

    Lectures on Quaternions

    consolidation

    W. R. Hamilton, Lectures on Quaternions, Dublin: Hodges and Smith, 1853

    A first book-length systematic account, though Hamilton himself found it hard to learn from.

    Evidence (2)
    • Archive catalogue lists the 1853 Dublin book. Internet Archive scan
    • Biography says it was published in 1853 and soon judged a poor book to learn from. MacTutor: Hamilton biography
  6. 1867

    Tait's Elementary Treatise on Quaternions

    key development

    P. G. Tait, An Elementary Treatise on Quaternions, Oxford, 1867 (later editions 1873, 1890)

    Tait becomes the main proponent, teaching quaternion methods for geometry and physics.

    Evidence (3)
    • Archive catalogue gives 1867 for the first edition scan. Internet Archive catalogue
    • Article says Tait became the chief proponent and that physics and geometry topics were written in quaternion form. English Wikipedia: Quaternion
    • Topic page refers to Tait's work on quaternions published in 1873 and a rivalry with vector methods. MacTutor: Abstract linear spaces
