1696 · Optimization & control

Calculus of variations from the brachistochrone

The brachistochrone challenge and the Bernoullis' isoperimetric problems led Euler to a general variational method and Lagrange to an analytic version with the delta symbol.

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1687

    Newton's minimal resistance problem in the Principia

    precursor

    Newton, Philosophiae naturalis principia mathematica, 1687

    Newton formulated and solved the solid of least resistance, an extremum problem over curves, but not as part of a general method.

    Evidence (2)
    • Wikipedia, Calculus of variations: Newton's minimal resistance problem, formulated 1685 and published in the Principia 1687, was the first correctly solved problem of the field. English Wikipedia, Calculus of variations
    • Internet Archive holds the 1687 first edition. Internet Archive
  2. 1696

    Johann Bernoulli poses the brachistochrone problem

    first statement

    Johann Bernoulli, Problema novum..., Acta Eruditorum, June 1696

    Bernoulli publicly challenged mathematicians to find the curve of quickest descent, the problem that opened the field.

    Evidence (1)
    • MacTutor: the problem was posed by Johann Bernoulli in Acta Eruditorum in June 1696. MacTutor, The brachistochrone problem
  3. 1697

    Solutions to the brachistochrone published in Acta Eruditorum

    first application

    Acta Eruditorum, May 1697 (Leibniz p. 205, Johann Bernoulli pp. 206-211, Jakob Bernoulli pp. 211-214, Newton p. 223)

    Five solutions were obtained and four appeared in print in May 1697; Jakob Bernoulli's approach already treated the curve as an unknown to be varied.

    Evidence (1)
    • MacTutor lists the May 1697 Acta Eruditorum contents with page numbers of each solution. MacTutor, The brachistochrone problem
  4. 1744

    Euler's Methodus inveniendi

    key development

    Euler, Methodus inveniendi lineas curvas maximi minimive proprietate gaudentes, Lausanne and Geneva 1744 (written 1743), E65

    Euler turned a set of special cases into a general method, gave a first-order necessary condition (the Euler differential equation) and treated about a hundred problems.

    Evidence (2)
    • Euler Archive E65: quarto book published 1744, written 1743; summary describes the general procedure and 100 problems. Euler Archive
    • MacTutor Euler biography credits the Methodus with beginning the proper study of the subject. MacTutor, Euler
  5. 1762

    Lagrange's analytic method with the delta symbol

    key development

    Lagrange, Essai d'une nouvelle methode pour determiner les maxima et les minima des formules integrales indefinies, Miscellanea Taurinensia 2 (1760-61, published 1762)

    Lagrange replaced Euler's partly geometric reasoning by a purely analytic one based on variations of the function; Euler adopted it.

    Evidence (2)
    • Wikipedia (citing Thiele and Goldstine): Euler dropped his geometric approach for Lagrange's analytic one after seeing his 1755 work, and waited until Lagrange published in 1762. English Wikipedia, Calculus of variations
    • MacTutor Lagrange: results sent to Euler in August 1755 contributed to the new subject. MacTutor, Lagrange
  6. 1766

    Euler's Elementa calculi variationum

    naming

    Euler, Elementa calculi variationum, Novi Comm. Acad. Sci. Petropol. 10 (1766; read 1756), E296

    Euler gave the subject its name in the title of a treatise written in 1756 and printed ten years later.

    Evidence (2)
    • Euler Archive E296: published 1766, written 1756; read to the Berlin Academy on 16 September 1756. Euler Archive
    • MacTutor Lagrange: the name 'calculus of variations' was given by Euler in 1766. MacTutor, Lagrange
