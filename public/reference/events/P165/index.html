1609 · Astronomy & trigonometry

Kepler's laws of planetary motion

Planetary orbits became ellipses with area and period laws, replacing circle-based models.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1596

    Mysterium cosmographicum: polyhedra model of the orbits

    precursor

    Kepler's first cosmology tried to explain planetary distances by nested regular solids, the circle-based scheme his later laws replaced.

    Evidence (1)
    • Describes the Tubingen 1596 Mysterium cosmographicum and its nested-solids model. MacTutor biography of Kepler
  2. 1609

    Astronomia nova: elliptical Mars orbit and equal-area law

    first statement

    Kepler, Astronomia nova, Heidelberg, 1609

    The book published the ellipse with the Sun at a focus and the equal-areas-in-equal-times rule, derived from Mars observations; these became the first and second laws.

    Dating or attribution note

    This single book is also the first purposeful application of conic geometry to planetary orbits, so no separate first_application is recorded. The 1609 book treats Mars; extension to all planets came later (Epitome, not checked).

    Evidence (3)
    • Record of the 1609 Astronomia nova edition. Internet Archive record: Astronomia nova aitiologetos, 1609
    • Open Library lists a 1609 Astronomia nova. Open Library search: Astronomia nova entries
    • States that Mars' orbit is an ellipse with the Sun at a focus and that the area law is in the 1609 book. MacTutor biography of Kepler
  3. 1618–1621

    Kepler's Epitome of Copernican astronomy

    consolidation

    Johannes Kepler, Epitome astronomiae Copernicanae, 1618-1621

    Kepler's systematic textbook of Copernican astronomy, published in parts, in which the laws of planetary motion appear.

    Evidence (2)
    • Open Library record of the 1618 Epitome astronomiae Copernicanae. Open Library work record
    • The three laws were published between 1609 and 1621 in Astronomia nova, Harmonices mundi and the Epitome. Wikipedia, Kepler's laws of planetary motion
  4. 1619

    Harmonices mundi: third law (period squared ~ radius cubed)

    key development

    Kepler, Harmonices mundi libri V, Linz, 1619

    Book V of the Harmony of the World states the relation between the squares of periods and the cubes of mean orbital radii.

    Evidence (3)
    • Record of the 1619 Harmonices mundi. Internet Archive record: Harmonices mundi libri V, 1619
    • Open Library lists a 1619 Harmonices mundi. Open Library search: Harmonices mundi entries
    • The Harmony of the World contains the third law, discovered while it was in press. MacTutor biography of Kepler
  5. 1687

    Principia: orbits from an inverse-square force

    key development

    Newton, Philosophiae naturalis principia mathematica, 1687

    Newton derived Kepler's laws from universal gravitation, turning the empirical laws into consequences of dynamics and starting celestial mechanics proper.

    Evidence (2)
    • Open Library lists the 1687 Principia. Open Library search: Principia entries
    • Discusses the inverse-square force and conic orbits in Book 1 of the Principia, 1687. MacTutor biography of Newton
