1829 · Algebraic geometry

Abel's theorem and abelian integrals

A theorem on sums of integrals of algebraic functions introduced genus, lost until 1952.

Also known as: Abel's addition theorem

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1826

    Abel's Paris memoir submitted

    precursor

    N. H. Abel, memoir on a general property of a class of transcendental functions, submitted to the Paris Academy

    Abel left a masterpiece with the Paris Academy, where it went unread and then was lost.

    Evidence (1)
    • MacTutor: the Paris memoir treated sums of integrals of an algebraic function and introduced the genus; Cauchy and Legendre were referees but it seemed lost. MacTutor, Abel biography
  2. 1829

    Abel's short statement of the general theorem

    first statement

    N. H. Abel, Demonstration d'une propriete generale d'une certaine classe de fonctions transcendantes, J. reine angew. Math. 4 (1829) 200-201

    A two-page note restated the main theorem of the Paris memoir without introduction.

    Dating or attribution note

    Candidate not promoted: Remarques sur quelques proprietes generales d'une certaine sorte de fonctions transcendantes, Crelle 3 (1828) 313-323 (DOI 10.1515/crll.1828.3.313), may state results earlier; its content was not read. Wikipedia instead says the theory originated with the 1841 paper.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref record, Crelle's Journal 1829, pages 200-201. Crossref DOI 10.1515/crll.1829.4.200
    • MacTutor: after the Paris memoir seemed lost, Abel wrote the main result in a two-page paper. MacTutor, Abel biography
  3. 1832

    Jacobi on Abelian transcendents

    key development

    C. G. J. Jacobi, Considerationes generales de transcendentibus Abelianis, J. reine angew. Math. 9 (1832) 394-403

    Jacobi discussed the problem of inverting abelian integrals, extending Abel's circle of ideas.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref record, Crelle's Journal 1832, pages 394-403. Crossref DOI 10.1515/crll.1832.9.394
  4. 1841

    Paris memoir printed

    key development

    N. H. Abel, memoire on a general property of a very extensive class of transcendental functions, printed 1841

    The memoir found by Cauchy in 1830 was finally printed, after which the theory of abelian integrals took shape.

    Evidence (2)
    • MacTutor: found by Cauchy in 1830, printed in 1841, vanished again until found in Florence in 1952. MacTutor, Abel biography
    • Wikipedia: the theory of abelian integrals originated with a paper by Abel published in 1841. English Wikipedia, Abelian integral
  5. 1857

    Riemann's theory of abelian functions

    key development

    B. Riemann, Theorie der Abel'schen Functionen, J. reine angew. Math. 54 (1857) 115-155

    Riemann rebuilt the theory on Riemann surfaces, giving geometric meaning to the genus.

    Dating or attribution note

    Role of genus in Riemann's paper from memory; not verified in a source read.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref record, Crelle's Journal 1857, pages 115-155. Crossref DOI 10.1515/crll.1857.54.115
  6. 1875

    Weber's 'New proof of Abel's theorem'

    naming

    H. Weber, New proof of Abel's theorem, 1875

    The earliest zbMATH-indexed title that names the theorem after Abel.

    Evidence (1)
    • zbMATH record: Weber, 'New proof of Abel's theorem', year 1875. zbMATH Open, API ti:"Abel's theorem" search
  7. 1897

    Baker's monograph on Abel's theorem

    consolidation

    H. F. Baker, Abel's Theorem and the Allied Theory, Cambridge University Press, 1897

    A monograph devoted to Abel's theorem and the allied theory of theta functions, the first book-length treatment with the theorem in its title among the Open Library records checked.

    Evidence (1)
    • Open Library records: Abel's theorem and the allied theory, first publication year 1897, University Press (Cambridge). Open Library search API
  8. Date unknown

    Euler's relation for elliptic integrals

    precursor

    L. Euler, addition relation for elliptic integrals

    Abel's theorem generalises an addition relation of Euler for elliptic integrals.

    Evidence (1)
    • MacTutor calls Abel's theorem a vast generalisation of Euler's relation for elliptic integrals. MacTutor, Abel biography
