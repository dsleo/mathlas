De Moivre's formula: trigonometry and complex powers

Powers of cos x + i sin x tie multiple-angle trigonometry to complex arithmetic.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1707

    De Moivre's cubic and higher-degree root formula

    precursor

    De Moivre, Aequationum quarundam potestatis tertiae, quintae, septimae, nonae... resolutio analytica, Phil. Trans. 25 (309), pp. 2368-2371, 1707

    A closely related relation between multiples of an angle appears in an earlier paper on solving equations by series.

    Dating or attribution note

    Identification of the 1707 paper with MacTutor's remark follows Wikipedia's citation; the content was not read.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref: Phil. Trans. 25, pp. 2368-2371, dated 1707. Crossref DOI 10.1098/rstl.1706.0037
    • MacTutor: a closely related formula appeared in an earlier paper of 1707. MacTutor, De Moivre
  2. 1714

    Cotes's geometric logarithm statement

    precursor

    Cotes, Logometria, Phil. Trans. 29, pp. 5-45, 1714

    An equivalent logarithmic statement for the angle, which implies the power rule, appeared eight years earlier.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref: Logometria, Phil. Trans. 29, 1714. Crossref DOI 10.1098/rstl.1714.0002
    • Wikipedia, De Moivre's formula: Cotes's 1714 argument, corrected for a misplaced factor, gives ix = ln(cos x + i sin x). English Wikipedia, De Moivre's formula
  3. 1722

    De Moivre's formula appears in a Philosophical Transactions paper

    first statement

    De Moivre, paper of 1722 (candidate: De sectione anguli, Phil. Trans. 32, pp. 228-230)

    MacTutor says the formula for (cos x + i sin x)^n first appears in this exact form in a paper of 1722.

    Evidence (1)
    • MacTutor: the formula appears in this form in a paper that De Moivre published in 1722. MacTutor, De Moivre
  4. 1730

    Miscellanea analytica states the multiple-angle lemma

    key development

    De Moivre, Miscellanea analytica de seriebus et quadraturis, London: Tonson and Watts, 1730

    Wikipedia cites the first page of this book for De Moivre's explicit lemma on cosines of an angle and its multiples.

    Evidence (2)
    • Internet Archive holds the 1730 London edition. Internet Archive
    • Wikipedia, De Moivre's formula: in 1730 De Moivre explicitly considered cos of an angle and its integer multiple (p. 1). English Wikipedia, De Moivre's formula
  5. 1748

    Euler's exponential form

    key development

    Euler, Introductio in analysin infinitorum, vol. 1, 1748, E101

    Euler's formula exp(ix) = cos x + i sin x contains the power rule as an exponent law and replaced De Moivre's trigonometric form in practice.

    Evidence (2)
    • Euler Archive E101: published 1748. Euler Archive
    • MacTutor: Euler gave exp(ix) = cos x + i sin x in 1748. MacTutor, Trigonometric functions
