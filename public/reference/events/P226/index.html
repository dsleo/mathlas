1965 · Algorithms & complexity

Algorithmic information theory (randomness via program length)

Randomness was defined through shortest program length.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1960

    Solomonoff's 'A Preliminary Report on a General Theory of Inductive Inference'

    precursor

    Ray Solomonoff, A preliminary report on a general theory of inductive inference, Zator Report V-131 (1960)

    Solomonoff's first circulated report of algorithmic probability; a technical report, not a journal publication.

    Evidence (1)
    • Solomonoff's 1960 report named in the history section Wikipedia, Algorithmic information theory
  2. 1964

    Solomonoff: algorithmic probability applied to inductive inference

    precursor

    Ray Solomonoff, A formal theory of inductive inference, Part I, Information and Control 7 (1964) 1-22

    Applies algorithmic (universal) probability to the problem of induction.

    Dating or attribution note

    The 'first application' claim rests on the field history; the record is verified. Reclassified from first_application to precursor: it predates this event's first statement (1965) and belongs to the wider field.

    Evidence (2)
    • Solomonoff 1964 Part I record Crossref
    • Algorithmic probability as the basis of the field, founded by Solomonoff Wikipedia, Algorithmic information theory
  3. 1965

    Kolmogorov's 'Three approaches to the quantitative definition of information'

    first statement

    A. N. Kolmogorov, Three approaches to the quantitative definition of information, Problems of Information Transmission 1 (1965)

    Kolmogorov defines the complexity of a finite object as the length of its shortest program, so randomness is incompressibility.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref record of the 1968 reprint (same title) in Int. J. Comput. Math. Crossref
    • Kolmogorov's biography (1965 Lenin Prize context only) MacTutor, Kolmogorov biography
  4. 1966

    Chaitin: program length for finite binary sequences

    key development

    Gregory J. Chaitin, On the length of programs for computing finite binary sequences, J. ACM 13 (1966)

    Chaitin defines the minimal program length of a finite binary string as a complexity measure.

    Evidence (1)
    • Chaitin 1966 JACM record Crossref
  5. 1966

    Martin-Lof: definition of random sequences

    key development

    Per Martin-Lof, The definition of random sequences, Information and Control 9 (1966)

    Gives a constructive test-based definition of random infinite sequences (Martin-Lof randomness).

    Evidence (2)
    • Martin-Lof 1966 record Crossref
    • Martin-Lof randomness named after him Wikipedia, Algorithmic information theory
  6. 1975

    Chaitin: program size formally identical to information theory

    key development

    Gregory J. Chaitin, A theory of program size formally identical to information theory, J. ACM 22 (1975) 329-340

    Chaitin shows program-size complexity obeys information-theoretic relations, uniting randomness with information theory.

    Evidence (2)
    • Chaitin 1975 JACM record Crossref
    • Chaitin 1975 cited as the source of the entropy analogy Wikipedia, Algorithmic information theory
  7. 1987

    Chaitin's 'Algorithmic Information Theory' (book)

    naming

    Gregory J. Chaitin, Algorithmic Information Theory, Cambridge University Press (1987)

    The book title fixes the name of the field.

    Evidence (1)
    • Chaitin 1987 book record (title 'Algorithmic information theory') Open Library
  8. 1993

    Li and Vitanyi, Introduction to Kolmogorov Complexity

    consolidation

    Ming Li and Paul Vitanyi, An Introduction to Kolmogorov Complexity and Its Applications, Springer (1st ed. 1993)

    The standard systematic textbook of the field.

    Evidence (2)
    • First edition 1993 record Open Library
    • 2nd edition (1997) record Crossref
