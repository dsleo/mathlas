1965 · Algebraic geometry

Geometric invariant theory and moduli

Nineteenth-century invariant theory is recast as a quotient construction for group actions on varieties, giving moduli spaces of curves and bundles.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1845–1846

    Cayley's invariant theory papers

    precursor

    Cayley, papers on invariant theory (1845-1846)

    Cayley's 1845 and 1846 papers lay the foundations of invariant theory, the classical background.

    Evidence (1)
    • Cayley published two papers in 1845 and 1846 laying the foundations for invariant theory MacTutor: Arthur Cayley
  2. 1868

    Gordan's finite basis for binary forms

    precursor

    Gordan, Beweis, dass jede Covariante und Invariante einer binaren Form eine ganze Function ... ist, J. reine angew. Math. 69 (1868) 323-354

    Gordan proves that the covariants and invariants of binary forms have a finite basis, the classical finiteness result.

    Dating or attribution note

    Finiteness for binary forms; the page range is as usually cited and was not verified. Reclassified from key_development to precursor: it predates this event's first statement (1965) and belongs to the wider field.

    Evidence (2)
    • Gordan 1868, Crelle, DOI 10.1515/crll.1868.69.323 Crossref
    • Gordan is the leading expert on invariant theory; Hilbert's 1888 basis theorem was submitted for his opinion MacTutor: Paul Gordan
  3. 1890

    Hilbert's basis theorem

    precursor

    Hilbert, Ueber die Theorie der algebraischen Formen, Math. Ann. 36 (1890)

    Hilbert proves the finite basis theorem for invariants of general forms, a non-constructive finiteness result.

    Evidence (2)
    • Hilbert 1890, Math. Ann., DOI 10.1007/bf01208503 Crossref
    • In 1888 Hilbert proved the finite basis theorem, with an existence proof only MacTutor: Paul Gordan
  4. 1893

    Hilbert's orbit separation criterion

    precursor

    Hilbert, Ueber die vollen Invariantensysteme, Math. Ann. 42 (1893)

    Hilbert formulates and proves a criterion for orbits that cannot be separated, the quotient-theoretic insight behind GIT.

    Dating or attribution note

    The criterion is from the Wikipedia summary; the paper's text was not checked. Reclassified from key_development to precursor: it predates this event's first statement (1965) and belongs to the wider field.

    Evidence (2)
    • Hilbert 1893 criterion for orbits that are not separated Wikipedia: Geometric invariant theory
    • Hilbert 1893, Math. Ann., DOI 10.1007/bf01444162 Crossref
  5. 1965

    Mumford's quotient construction

    first statement

    Mumford, Geometric Invariant Theory, Springer, Ergebnisse 34 (1965)

    Mumford constructs quotients of varieties by group actions, giving moduli spaces of curves and bundles.

    Evidence (2)
    • Mumford, Geometric Invariant Theory, Springer 1965, DOI 10.1007/978-3-662-00095-3 Crossref
    • Geometric invariant theory was founded and developed by Mumford in a monograph first published in 1965 Wikipedia: Geometric invariant theory
  6. 1965

    'Geometric invariant theory' in the monograph title

    naming

    Mumford, Geometric Invariant Theory (1965) (as above)

    The monograph's title gives the theory its name.

    Evidence (1)
    • Title 'Geometric Invariant Theory', Springer 1965 Crossref
  7. 1969

    Deligne-Mumford: irreducibility of the moduli of curves via stable curves

    first application

    Deligne, P.; Mumford, D., The irreducibility of the space of curves of given genus, Publ. Math. IHES 36, 1969, 75-109

    Mumford's quotient construction is used to build the moduli space of stable curves as a quotient of a Hilbert-scheme open set, applied to the irreducibility problem.

    Evidence (2)
    • Deligne and Mumford's 1969 paper proves irreducibility of the moduli of curves; the moduli space is a quotient of a Hilbert-scheme subset by PGL, and the GIT article states that quotients as moduli were the motivation of Mumford's theory Wikipedia, Moduli of algebraic curves and Geometric invariant theory (raw wikitext)
    • Bibliographic record: 'The irreducibility of the space of curves of given genus', Publ. Math. IHES 36 (1969) pp. 75-109 zbMATH Open search
