1909 · Number theory

Normal numbers: measure and probability applied to digit expansions (Borel)

Borel defined normal numbers by digit frequencies, proved almost every real is normal, and Sierpinski, Champernowne, Mahler, and Copeland and Erdos later gave explicit examples.

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1909

    Borel's almost-every real is normal

    first statement

    Borel, E., Les probabilites denombrables et leurs applications arithmetiques, Rendiconti del Circolo Matematico di Palermo (1909)

    Borel's 1909 paper defines normal numbers and shows that almost every real is normal.

    Evidence (1)
    • Paper with this title, journal and year Crossref
  2. 1909

    Probability applied to digit frequencies

    first application

    Borel, E., Les probabilites denombrables et leurs applications arithmetiques (1909)

    Borel applies measure and probability to digit expansions, the purposeful use of the idea on the target problem.

    Evidence (1)
    • Same paper, purposeful use of probability on digit expansions Crossref
  3. 1933

    Champernowne's explicit normal decimal

    key development

    Champernowne, D. G., The construction of decimals normal in the scale of ten, Journal of the London Mathematical Society (1933)

    Champernowne gives the first explicit example of a number normal in base ten.

    Dating or attribution note

    Sierpinski's earlier explicit example (1917) was not verified and remains open.

    Evidence (1)
    • Paper with this title, journal and year Crossref
  4. 1946

    Copeland and Erdos on normal numbers

    key development

    Copeland, A. H. and Erdos, P., Note on normal numbers, Bulletin of the American Mathematical Society (1946)

    Copeland and Erdos show that a concatenation of primes gives a normal number, an extension of the theory.

    Evidence (1)
    • Note with this title, journal and year Crossref
  5. 1974

    Kuipers and Niederreiter, 'Uniform distribution of sequences'

    consolidation

    L. Kuipers, H. Niederreiter, Uniform Distribution of Sequences, Pure and Applied Mathematics, New York: Wiley-Interscience, 1974

    A monograph on uniform distribution mod 1, within which normal numbers in the sense of Borel are the basic case.

    Dating or attribution note

    Chosen as a monograph consolidation of the uniform-distribution theory that contains normal numbers. The normal-number chapter was not read. No title with 'normal numbers' earlier than the 1939-1951 articles was found in the search.
