1635 · Analysis

Method of indivisibles (Cavalieri, Torricelli, Wallis)

Cavalieri, Torricelli and Wallis computed areas by summing indivisible elements arithmetically.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1615

    Kepler's barrel stereometry

    precursor

    Kepler, Nova stereometria doliorum vinariorum, 1615

    Kepler's infinitesimal summations were a model Cavalieri drew on.

    Evidence (2)
    • Record of the 1615 edition. Internet Archive record: Nova stereometria, 1615
    • Cavalieri's theory incorporates Kepler's infinitesimals. MacTutor biography of Cavalieri
  2. 1635

    Cavalieri's Geometria indivisibilibus

    first statement

    Cavalieri, Geometria indivisibilibus continuorum nova quadam ratione promota, Bologna, 1635

    Cavalieri presented areas and volumes as sums of indivisible chords or sections, giving quick rules for many figures.

    Evidence (2)
    • Presented in 1635 (manuscript finished December 1627). MacTutor biography of Cavalieri
    • Torricelli biography confirms 1635 publication. MacTutor biography of Torricelli
  3. 1644

    Torricelli's Opera geometrica

    key development

    Torricelli, Opera geometrica, Florence, 1644

    Torricelli adopted the method, proved it with extra rigour and applied it to new solids.

    Dating or attribution note

    Specific new results of Torricelli (e.g. infinite solid) not checked here.

    Evidence (2)
    • Record of the 1644 Opera geometrica. Internet Archive record: Opera geometrica, 1644
    • Torricelli studied Cavalieri's method, was first suspicious, then used it. MacTutor biography of Torricelli
  4. 1647

    Cavalieri's Exercitationes geometricae sex

    consolidation

    Cavalieri, Exercitationes geometricae sex, 1647

    Cavalieri's improved exposition answering critics became the 17th-century source text.

    Evidence (1)
    • Exercitationes geometricae sex (1647) became the main source for 17th-century mathematicians. MacTutor biography of Cavalieri
  5. 1656

    Wallis's Arithmetica infinitorum

    key development

    Wallis, Arithmetica infinitorum, Oxford, 1656

    Wallis replaced geometric indivisibles by arithmetic summation, giving the product for pi/2 and integrals of powers.

    Evidence (1)
    • Arithmetica infinitorum 1656 with the pi product; Wallis studied Cavalieri, Roberval and Torricelli. MacTutor biography of Wallis
  6. 1693

    Roberval's Traité des indivisibles printed

    key development

    Roberval, Traité des indivisibles, in Divers ouvrages, 1693

    Roberval's independent treatise (he claimed an Archimedean basis) computed integrals of powers and sine; printed long after composition.

    Evidence (1)
    • Published in the 1693 Divers ouvrages; Roberval claimed it did not depend on Cavalieri. MacTutor biography of Roberval
