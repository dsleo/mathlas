250 · Algebra

Diophantus and the Arithmetica (symbolic algebra and Diophantine problems)

Arithmetica introduced symbols for unknowns and sought rational solutions to polynomial equations.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1,800 BCE–1,600 BCE

    Old Babylonian procedures for solving quadratic-type problems

    precursor

    Old Babylonian mathematical tablets (Susa, Nippur, etc.)

    Problem-solving that modern historians call algebra already existed in Mesopotamia long before Diophantus, so the Arithmetica is not the start of the technique itself.

    Evidence (2)
    • Hoyrup 2002 chapters characterise Old Babylonian "algebra" as a body of procedures Hoyrup, Old Babylonian Algebra: a global characterization (2002)
    • Wikipedia Diophantus: he did not invent the method of algebra, which existed before him Wikipedia, Arithmetica section of Diophantus
  2. 250–350

    Arithmetica written (13 books, 290 problems)

    first statement

    Diophantus, Arithmetica; Greek, Alexandria; composition date uncertain

    Diophantus collects numerical problems solved by equations in one unknown and introduces abbreviations for the unknown, its powers and equality.

    Evidence (2)
    • MacTutor Diophantus: wrote after Hypsicles (150 BC) and before Theon of Alexandria (350 AD); symbolism with abbreviations for unknown and powers MacTutor, Diophantus
    • Wikipedia Diophantus: flourished c. 250 CE, possible span 170 BCE-350 CE; 290 problems, six books in Greek, four in Arabic Wikipedia, Diophantus
  3. 1463

    Regiomontanus announces the Greek Diophantus to Latin Europe

    key development

    Regiomontanus, remark of 1463 that no one had yet translated the thirteen books

    Regiomontanus reports the Greek text and says it is untranslated, reopening Diophantus to European mathematicians.

    Evidence (1)
    • MacTutor Diophantus: Europe learnt of the Arithmetica via Regiomontanus in 1463; Bombelli translated much of it in 1570 without publishing MacTutor, Diophantus
  4. 1621

    Bachet's Greek-Latin edition of the Arithmetica

    consolidation

    Bachet, Diophanti Alexandrini Arithmeticorum libri sex, Paris 1621

    Bachet prints the Greek text with a Latin translation and commentary, giving the first widely used scholarly edition.

    Evidence (2)
    • Open Library lists the 1621 edition Diophanti Alexandrini Arithmeticorum libri sex Open Library
    • Wikipedia Diophantus: the 1621 Bachet edition gained fame through Fermat marginal Last Theorem note Wikipedia, Diophantus
  5. 1670

    Fermat's marginal notes published with Bachet's edition

    key development

    Diophanti Alexandrini Arithmeticorum libri sex ... cum observationibus P. de Fermat, Toulouse 1670 (ed. Samuel de Fermat)

    Samuel Fermat publishes his father's observations on Diophantus, which launch modern Diophantine analysis (e.g. the Last Theorem note).

    Evidence (2)
    • MacTutor Fermat: marginal notes became known when Samuel published an edition of Bachet with his father notes in 1670 MacTutor, Fermat
    • Open Library lists the 1670 edition with Fermat as contributor Open Library
  6. 1885

    Heath's study of Diophantus in English

    consolidation

    T. L. Heath, Diophantus of Alexandria: a study in the history of Greek algebra, Cambridge 1885 (2nd ed. 1910)

    Heath provides the standard English monograph and translation, consolidating Diophantus for the modern reader.

    Evidence (1)
    • Open Library lists Heath, Diophantus of Alexandria, first published 1885 (also 1910) Open Library
  7. 1982

    Arabic Books IV-VII identified and edited

    key development

    Sesiano, Books IV to VII of Diophantus Arithmetica in the Arabic translation attributed to Qusta ibn Luqa, Springer 1982

    A manuscript found in 1968 preserves four further books in Arabic, changing the picture of what the Arithmetica contained.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref records Sesiano 1982, Books IV to VII of Diophantus Arithmetica Sesiano 1982 (Springer)
    • MacTutor Diophantus: Sezgin found Arabic manuscript of Books IV-VII in 1968; Rashed argued it is a translation by Qusta ibn Luqa (d. 912) MacTutor, Diophantus
