2006 · Number theory

Periodic torus orbits, subconvexity and homogeneous dynamics

Equidistribution of torus orbits on homogeneous spaces and subconvex bounds for L-functions are combined to prove equidistribution theorems for arithmetic objects.

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 2006

    Periodic torus orbit distribution studied

    first statement

    M. Einsiedler, E. Lindenstrauss, P. Michel and A. Venkatesh, The distribution of periodic torus orbits on homogeneous spaces, arXiv:math/0607815 (2006)

    The preprint studies equidistribution of periodic torus orbits using orbit separation and measure rigidity.

    Evidence (1)
    • ArXiv records the 2006 public preprint and its methods. Original bibliographic record
  2. 2007

    Cubic-field analogue of Duke’s theorem

    first application

    M. Einsiedler et al., Distribution of periodic torus orbits and Duke’s theorem for cubic fields, arXiv:0708.1113 (2007)

    The follow-up applies subconvexity and measure classification to equidistribution for cubic fields.

    Evidence (1)
    • The 2007 abstract identifies the cubic-field result and techniques. Original bibliographic record
  3. 2009

    Distribution of periodic torus orbits on homogeneous spaces

    key development

    Einsiedler, M.; Lindenstrauss, E., Distribution of periodic torus orbits on homogeneous spaces, Duke Math. J. 148 (2009) 119-174, DOI 10.1215/00127094-2009-023

    The periodic torus-orbit equidistribution theorem, a general dynamical form of Duke-type results.

    Evidence (1)
    • zBMATH record: Duke Math. J. 148 (2009) 119-174 zBMATH Open record (search API)
  4. 2019

    Joint equidistribution of CM points

    key development

    Khayutin, I., Joint equidistribution of CM points, Ann. of Math. (2) 189 (2019) 145-276, arXiv 1710.04557

    Extends torus-orbit equidistribution to joint CM points, a later step of the same subconvexity programme.

    Dating or attribution note

    Uses the homogeneous-dynamics method; credit to Einsiedler–Lindenstrauss lineage is inferred from the method, not stated in the record.

    Evidence (1)
    • zBMATH record: Annals 189 (2019) 145-276 zBMATH Open record (search API)
