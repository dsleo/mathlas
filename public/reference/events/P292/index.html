1976 · Group & representation theory

Bass-Serre theory

Group actions on trees decompose groups as amalgams and HNN extensions.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1968

    Splittings of groups with infinitely many ends

    precursor

    John R. Stallings, On torsion-free groups with infinitely many ends, Annals of Mathematics, 1968

    Stallings shows that groups with infinitely many ends split as amalgams, an early form of the tree-based decomposition.

    Evidence (1)
    • Annals of Mathematics paper with this title, 1968 Crossref
  2. 1976

    Group actions on trees

    first statement

    Hyman Bass, Some remarks on group actions on trees, Communications in Algebra, 1976

    Bass treats groups acting on trees and their decomposition as graphs of groups.

    Dating or attribution note

    Serre's monograph is the systematic formalisation; the claim that this is the first public statement is not verified against other sources.

    Evidence (1)
    • Communications in Algebra paper with this title, 1976 Crossref
  3. 1977

    Serre's monograph on trees and amalgams

    consolidation

    Jean-Pierre Serre, Arbres, amalgames, SL2, Asterisque 46, Societe Mathematique de France, 1977

    Serre's monograph sets out the structure theory of groups acting on trees.

    Evidence (2)
    • Open Library: 'Arbres, amalgames, SL2' by Serre, 1977 (Societe Mathematique de France) Open Library
    • Wikipedia: Bass-Serre theory formalised in Serre's 1977 monograph Trees Wikipedia
  4. 1977

    Structure of SL2 via trees

    first application

    Jean-Pierre Serre, Arbres, amalgames, SL2 (as above)

    Serre's motivating application is the structure of SL2 over fields and local fields, via trees.

    Dating or attribution note

    Same monograph as consolidation. The first application is taken from Wikipedia; the specific SL2 result was not checked in the text.

    Evidence (1)
    • Wikipedia: Serre's original motivation concerned algebraic groups with Bruhat-Tits trees Wikipedia
