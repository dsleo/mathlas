1959 · Graph theory

Random graph theory

Graphs chosen at random were studied for thresholds of properties.

Also known as: probabilistic graph theory

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1959

    Gilbert's random graph model G(n,p)

    first statement

    Gilbert, Random graphs, Ann. Math. Statist. 30 (1959) 1141-1144

    Gilbert defines the random graph in which each edge occurs independently with a fixed probability.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref record: Random Graphs, Annals of Mathematical Statistics, 1959 Crossref
    • Wikipedia Random graph: the model is most commonly studied as proposed by Edgar Gilbert Wikipedia Random graph
  2. 1959

    'On random graphs I' names the subject

    naming

    Erdos and Renyi, On random graphs I, Publ. Math. Debrecen 6 (1959) 290-297

    The first use of 'random graphs' in a title by Erdos and Renyi, the model that later carries their name.

    Evidence (1)
    • zbMATH record: On random graphs. I, Publicationes Mathematicae Debrecen 6, 1959 zbMATH
  3. 1960

    Evolution of random graphs: thresholds and giant component

    key development

    Erdos and Renyi, On the evolution of random graphs, Publ. Math. Inst. Hungar. Acad. Sci. 5 (1960) 17-61

    Studies the threshold behaviour of properties (connectivity, giant component) as the number of edges grows, the work that made the topic central.

    Evidence (2)
    • zbMATH record: On the evolution of random graphs, 1960 zbMATH
    • MacTutor Renyi: the most important joint work on random graphs is On the evolution of random graphs (1960) MacTutor Renyi biography
  4. 1985

    Monograph Random Graphs

    consolidation

    Bollobas, Random Graphs, Academic Press (1985)

    The first book-length treatment of random graph theory.

    Evidence (1)
    • Open Library record: Random graphs, Bollobas, Academic Press, 1985 Open Library
