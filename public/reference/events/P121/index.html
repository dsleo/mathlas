Islamic spherical trigonometry

Islamic scholars used all six functions and proved spherical sine laws and identities.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 100

    Menelaus' Sphaerica

    precursor

    Menelaus of Alexandria, Sphaerica (three books), c. 100 CE; Arabic versions edited by Rashed and Papadopoulos, 2017

    The earliest surviving work on spherical triangles, with the rule of six quantities, which later Arabic authors rebuilt into a trigonometry of the sphere.

    Evidence (2)
    • Menelaus' surviving work on spherics is the earliest known work on spherical trigonometry (about 100 AD). MacTutor, Trigonometric functions
    • The Arabic versions of the Spherics (including the al-Mahani/al-Harawi version) were edited and translated in 2017. Rashed, R. and Papadopoulos, A., Menelaus' Spherics, De Gruyter 2017 (Crossref record)
  2. 970–1000

    Spherical law of sines in the circle of Abu'l-Wafa and Abu Nasr Mansur

    first statement

    No single dated work located; reported through al-Biruni's letters and al-Tusi's Shakl al-qatta'

    The sine theorem for spherical triangles was found in the late tenth century; Abu'l-Wafa and Abu Nasr Mansur each claimed it and later authors credit al-Khujandi.

    Evidence (2)
    • Both Abu'l-Wafa and Abu Nasr Mansur claim the sine theorem; al-Tusi credits al-Khujandi with the proof for spherical triangles. MacTutor, Al-Khujandi biography
    • A letter of al-Biruni contains proofs of the plane and spherical sine theorem, attributed to his teacher Abu Nasr Mansur. MacTutor, Al-Biruni biography
  3. 980

    Abu'l-Wafa: tangent function and sine/tangent tables

    key development

    Abu'l-Wafa al-Buzjani, Theories of the Moon and Kitab al-Kamil (simplified Almagest)

    Abu'l-Wafa is credited with the first use of the tangent and with sine and tangent tables at quarter-degree steps, giving the full set of functions needed for spherical work.

    Evidence (2)
    • Best known for the first use of the tan function and tables of sines and tangents at 15 minute steps; knew sin 2x = 2 sin x cos x by 980. MacTutor, Abul-Wafa biography
    • By 980 Abu'l-Wafa knew the double-angle identity. MacTutor, Trigonometric functions
  4. 1060–1079

    Al-Jayyani, Book of Unknown Arcs of a Sphere

    consolidation

    Ibn Mu'adh al-Jayyani, Kitab matahil al-qisi al-maghula 'ala al-kura (11th century)

    A stand-alone treatise on spherical trigonometry in al-Andalus, with right-triangle rules, a general law of sines and the polar triangle.

    Evidence (2)
    • Described as the first treatise on spherical trigonometry; contains formulae for right triangles, the general law of sines and solution by the polar triangle. MacTutor, Al-Jayyani biography
    • al-Jayyani's computational work in 11th-century al-Andalus is studied by Hogendijk (2005). zbMATH 2247744, Hogendijk, Centaurus 47 (2005)
  5. 1260–1274

    Nasir al-Din al-Tusi, Treatise on the Quadrilateral

    consolidation

    Nasir al-Din al-Tusi, Kitab shakl al-qatta' (Book on the complete quadrilateral), 13th century

    The first extant exposition of plane and spherical trigonometry as a discipline apart from astronomy, listing all six right-triangle cases.

    Evidence (2)
    • In the Treatise on the Quadrilateral al-Tusi gave the first extant exposition of the whole system of plane and spherical trigonometry, as a discipline in its own right. MacTutor, Al-Tusi Nasir biography
    • al-Tusi was the first to list the six distinct cases of a right spherical triangle. English Wikipedia, Spherical trigonometry
  6. 1464

    Regiomontanus, De triangulis omnimodis

    key development

    Regiomontanus, De triangulis omnimodis (written 1464; printed 1533)

    A systematic Latin account of solving triangles with books on spherical triangles, which carried the Islamic material into European use.

    Evidence (2)
    • De triangulis omnimodis (1464) is a systematic account of solving triangles; Books III-V treat spherical trigonometry. MacTutor, Regiomontanus biography
    • One of Regiomontanus' sources is argued to be al-Jayyani's Book of Unknown Arcs. MacTutor, Al-Jayyani biography
