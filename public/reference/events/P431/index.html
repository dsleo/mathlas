Axioms for linear (vector) spaces: Moebius, Grassmann, Peano

Directed quantities and weighted points led Grassmann to an abstract calculus of extension, and Peano in 1888 gave the first axioms for a real linear space, soon extended to infinite-dimensional spaces.

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1827

    Moebius' barycentric calculus with directed quantities

    precursor

    A. F. Moebius, Der barycentrische Calcul, Leipzig, 1827

    Moebius places weights at points and works with directed quantities, the idea Grassmann names as his main motivation.

    Evidence (2)
    • Archive catalogue lists the 1827 Leipzig book. Internet Archive scan
    • Topic page says barycentric weights were an early appearance of vectors and Grassmann's main motivation. MacTutor: Abstract linear spaces
  2. 1844

    Grassmann's Die lineale Ausdehnungslehre

    first statement

    H. Grassmann, Die lineale Ausdehnungslehre, ein neuer Zweig der Mathematik, Leipzig, 1844

    Grassmann builds an abstract calculus of undefined quantities with addition, scalar multiplication and a product, containing independence, dimension and the scalar product.

    Evidence (3)
    • Archive catalogue lists an 1844 edition of the book. Internet Archive scan
    • Topic page lists linear independence, dimension and the scalar product as present in the 1844 work. MacTutor: Abstract linear spaces
    • Biography says the manuscript was finished in autumn 1843 and published the next year. MacTutor: Grassmann biography
  3. 1862

    Second, rigorous Ausdehnungslehre

    consolidation

    H. Grassmann, Die Ausdehnungslehre: vollstaendig und in strenger Form bearbeitet, 1862

    Grassmann rewrites the theory in a more readable and formal version with a long programmatic introduction.

    Evidence (1)
    • Topic page dates the more readable second version to 1862 and says the introduction defends its formal method. MacTutor: Abstract linear spaces
  4. 1867

    Hankel recognises Grassmann's work

    key development

    H. Hankel, Theorie der complexen Zahlensysteme, 1867

    Hankel is the first to see the importance of Grassmann's ideas, building abstractly defined number systems on them.

    Evidence (1)
    • Topic page says Hankel was first to see the importance of Grassmann's work and cites his 1867 paper. MacTutor: Abstract linear spaces
  5. 1888

    Peano's axioms for a real linear space

    key development

    G. Peano, Calcolo geometrico secondo l'Ausdehnungslehre di H. Grassmann, Turin, 1888

    In chapter IX Peano gives axioms for a linear space over the reals, allowing infinite dimension and crediting Leibniz, Moebius, Grassmann and Hamilton.

    Evidence (4)
    • Topic page says Peano's 1888 book gives the first axiomatic definition of a real linear space. MacTutor: Abstract linear spaces
    • Article says Peano gave the modern definition in 1888 under the name linear systems and allowed infinite dimension. English Wikipedia: Vector space
    • Biography says the 1888 book sets out Grassmann's ideas with great clarity. MacTutor: Peano biography
    • zbMATH 20.0689.04: Peano, 'Calcolo geometrico secondo l'Ausdehnungslehre di H. Grassmann', Torino 1888. zbMATH Open, document 20.0689.04
  6. 1897

    Pincherle adopts Peano's axioms for infinite dimensions

    key development

    S. Pincherle, work of 1897 on infinite-dimensional spaces (exact title not checked)

    Pincherle takes up Peano's axioms and makes first inroads into infinite-dimensional vector space theory.

    Evidence (1)
    • Article says Pincherle adopted Peano's axioms in 1897 and made initial inroads into infinite-dimensional spaces. English Wikipedia: Vector space
