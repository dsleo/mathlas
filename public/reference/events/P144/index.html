1828 · Mathematical physics

Hamiltonian optics and mechanics

A function from ray optics was used for dynamics.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1828

    Theory of Systems of Rays

    first statement

    W. R. Hamilton, Theory of Systems of Rays, Trans. Royal Irish Academy 15 (1828)

    Hamilton introduced the characteristic function of optics in a memoir presented while still an undergraduate.

    Evidence (2)
    • MacTutor: in his final undergraduate year he presented Theory of Systems of Rays, introducing the characteristic function for optics. MacTutor, Hamilton biography
    • Volume 15 of the Transactions is dated 1828; the memoir's presence there is from memory. Internet Archive item transactionsofro015oya
  2. 1832

    Third supplement and conical refraction

    key development

    W. R. Hamilton, Third Supplement to an Essay on the Theory of Systems of Rays, 1832

    Applying the characteristic function to Fresnel's wave surface, Hamilton predicted conical refraction, confirmed by Lloyd.

    Evidence (1)
    • MacTutor: the 1832 third supplement treats the characteristic function in optics and predicted conical refraction, verified by Lloyd. MacTutor, Hamilton biography
  3. 1834

    On a General Method in Dynamics

    first application

    W. R. Hamilton, On a general method in dynamics, Phil. Trans. Roy. Soc. 1834, 247-308

    Hamilton carried the optical characteristic function over to the dynamics of particle systems.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref record, Philosophical Transactions 1834, pages 247-308. Crossref DOI 10.1098/rstl.1834.0017
    • MacTutor: first statement of the characteristic function applied to dynamics, used just as in optics. MacTutor, Hamilton biography
  4. 1835

    Second essay on a general method in dynamics

    key development

    W. R. Hamilton, Second essay on a general method in dynamics, Phil. Trans. 1835, 95-144

    The second essay completed the dynamical theory with the principal function.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref record, Philosophical Transactions 1835, pages 95-144. Crossref DOI 10.1098/rstl.1835.0009
  5. 1837

    Jacobi's integration theory for first-order equations

    key development

    C. G. J. Jacobi, Ueber die Reduction der Integration der partiellen Differentialgleichungen erster Ordnung, J. reine angew. Math. 17 (1837) 97-162

    Jacobi's work on first-order partial differential equations reshaped Hamilton's dynamics into what is now called Hamilton-Jacobi theory.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref record, Crelle's Journal 1837, pages 97-162. Crossref DOI 10.1515/crll.1837.17.97
  6. 1866

    Jacobi lectures on dynamics published posthumously

    consolidation

    Jacobi, C. G. J., Vorlesungen uber Dynamik, 1866 (ed. Clebsch)

    Lecture course presenting the Hamilton-Jacobi theory of dynamics, published after Jacobi's death.

    Dating or attribution note

    Posthumous course; the Hamilton characteristic-function treatment in it is not checked page by page.

    Evidence (1)
    • Open Library lists the lectures with first publication year 1866 Open Library works OL2571607W
