Computational geometry

Shamos names the field and gives algorithms for geometric problems.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1972

    Graham's convex hull algorithm

    precursor

    Graham, R. L., An efficient algorithm for determining the convex hull of a finite planar set, Information Processing Letters, 1972

    An early efficient geometric algorithm, predating the field's name, that is a precursor rather than the public start.

    Evidence (1)
    • Graham's convex hull paper appeared in Information Processing Letters in 1972 Crossref record
  2. 1975

    Shamos and Hoey, closest-point algorithms

    first statement

    Shamos, M. I., Hoey, D., Closest-point problems, 16th Annual Symposium on Foundations of Computer Science, 1975

    The earliest verified algorithmic paper of the computational-geometry type on geometric problems (closest pairs).

    Evidence (3)
    • Closest-point problems by Shamos and Hoey published at FOCS 1975 Crossref record
    • Preparata and Shamos date the first use of the term in this sense to 1975 Wikipedia, Computational geometry (citing Preparata and Shamos 1985)
    • Open Library: Minsky and Papert, Perceptrons: An Introduction to Computational Geometry (MIT Press 1969), a 1969 title using the phrase in another sense Open Library, Perceptrons
  3. 1975

    Closest-point problems solved algorithmically

    first application

    Shamos, M. I., Hoey, D., Closest-point problems, 16th Annual Symposium on Foundations of Computer Science, 1975

    The same 1975 paper applies the algorithmic approach to the closest-point target problem.

    Evidence (1)
    • Closest-point problems published at FOCS 1975 Crossref record
  4. 1976

    Geometric intersection problems

    key development

    Shamos, M. I., Hoey, D., Geometric intersection problems, 17th Annual Symposium on Foundations of Computer Science, 1976

    A second algorithmic paper extends the approach to intersection problems, consolidating the subject's core problem types.

    Dating or attribution note

    Importance as key development is an editorial judgement; the paper's content was not read.

    Evidence (1)
    • Geometric intersection problems published at FOCS 1976 Crossref record
  5. 1985

    Preparata and Shamos textbook

    consolidation

    Preparata, F. P., Shamos, M. I., Computational Geometry: An Introduction, Springer, 1985

    The first book-length systematic treatment of the subject, cited in the Wikipedia article as the groundlaying text.

    Evidence (1)
    • Computational Geometry: An Introduction by Preparata and Shamos, Springer, 1985 Crossref record for the book
