1969 · Logic & foundations

Classification theory in model theory (Morley, Shelah)

Categoricity in uncountable cardinals leads to stability and a dividing-line classification of first-order theories.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1954

    Los's question on categoricity in uncountable cardinals

    precursor

    Jerzy Los noticed the categoricity question that Morley answered; it is a precursor, not a publication.

    Evidence (1)
    • Wikipedia says that in 1954 Jerzy Los noticed the link between categoricity and the number of models Wikipedia, Categorical theory (History)
  2. 1965

    Morley's categoricity theorem

    precursor

    Morley, M., Categoricity in power, Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. 114 (1965) 514-538

    Proves that a countable theory categorical in one uncountable cardinal is categorical in all uncountable cardinals; the theorem that drove the classification programme.

    Evidence (2)
    • Paper in Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. (1965), DOI 10.1090/s0002-9947-1965-0175782-0, pages 514-538 Crossref; zbMATH 0151.01101
    • Morley's theorem is the source of stability theory Wikipedia, Stable theory (lead)
  3. 1969

    Shelah defines stable theories

    first statement

    Shelah, S., Stable theories, Israel J. Math. 7 (1969) 187-202

    Introduces stability as a dividing line between theories with a classifiable and theories with too many models.

    Evidence (3)
    • Paper in Israel J. Math. (1969), DOI 10.1007/bf02787611, pages 187-202 Crossref; zbMATH 0193.30002
    • Wikipedia dates stable theories to Shelah's classification theory programme Wikipedia, Stable theory (lead; redirect target)
    • zbMATH: Shelah, 'Stable theories', Israel J. Math. 7 (1969) 187-202 zbMATH Open 3307522
  4. 1970

    Stability in power applied to counting models

    first application

    Shelah, S., Finite diagrams stable in power, Annals of Mathematical Logic 2 (1970) 69-118

    Applies the stability notion to count non-isomorphic models of a theory of a given power, the target problem of the classification programme.

    Evidence (1)
    • Paper in Annals of Mathematical Logic 2 (1970), DOI 10.1016/0003-4843(70)90007-0, pages 69-118 Crossref; zbMATH 0204.31104
  5. 1971

    Superstability and the finite cover property

    key development

    Shelah, S., Stability, the f.c.p., and superstability; model theoretic properties of formulas in first order theory, Annals of Mathematical Logic 3 (1971)

    Separates stable from unstable theories with the finite cover property and introduces superstability, sharpening the dividing line.

    Evidence (1)
    • Paper in Annals of Mathematical Logic 3 (1971), DOI 10.1016/0003-4843(71)90015-5 Crossref
  6. 1978

    Title 'Classification theory' in a monograph

    naming

    Shelah, S., Classification Theory and the Number of Non-Isomorphic Models, North-Holland (1978)

    The name 'classification theory' is fixed by this monograph title.

    Evidence (1)
    • Monograph title and 1978 year (zbMATH 0388.03009) zbMATH
  7. 1978

    Shelah's monograph on classification theory

    consolidation

    Shelah, S., Classification Theory and the Number of Non-Isomorphic Models, North-Holland (1978)

    The first systematic book-length treatment of the classification programme.

    Evidence (1)
    • Monograph title and 1978 year zbMATH 0388.03009; MacTutor, Shelah biography
