1666 · Logic & foundations

Leibniz combinatorial logic

Leibniz proposed a calculus of reasoning by symbolic manipulation, largely unpublished.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1666

    Dissertatio de arte combinatoria

    first statement

    Gottfried Leibniz, Dissertatio de arte combinatoria, Leipzig, 1666

    The habilitation dissertation proposes reducing reasoning to combinations of basic elements, the seed of the symbolic-logic programme.

    Evidence (3)
    • Archive.org record dates the Dissertatio to 1666. archive.org item ita-bnc-mag-00000844-001
    • It was published in 1666 and aims to reduce reasoning to combinations of basic elements. MacTutor: Gottfried Leibniz
    • It was his first book; a few copies were printed. Wikipedia: Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz
  2. 1879

    Frege's Begriffsschrift pursues a calculus ratiocinator

    key development

    Gottlob Frege, Begriffsschrift, Halle, 1879

    Frege presents his concept-script as a formal calculus of reasoning in Leibniz's spirit, starting modern formal logic.

    Dating or attribution note

    Included as the first modern formal system that explicitly claims the Leibnizian aim; the book itself was not read.

    Evidence (1)
    • Says Frege intended his concept-script to be a calculus ratiocinator and that formal logic began with it. Wikipedia, Calculus ratiocinator
  3. 1901

    Couturat's La logique de Leibniz

    consolidation

    Louis Couturat, La logique de Leibniz d'apres des documents inedits, Paris, 1901

    Couturat drew on unpublished Hanover manuscripts to present Leibniz' logic systematically, followed by the 1903 edition of fragments.

    Evidence (1)
    • Archive.org records date Couturat's La logique de Leibniz to 1901 and the Opuscules et fragments inedits to 1903. archive.org item lalogiquedeleib00coutgoog
  4. 1903

    Couturat publishes Leibniz's unpublished logical fragments

    key development

    Louis Couturat, Opuscules et fragments inedits de Leibniz, Paris, 1903

    Prints Leibniz's unpublished logical manuscripts, making his calculus of reasoning accessible.

    Evidence (1)
    • Opuscules et fragments inedits de Leibniz, 1903. Internet Archive record opusculesetfrag00bibgoog
  5. 1918

    C. I. Lewis' Survey of Symbolic Logic discusses Leibniz

    key development

    C. I. Lewis, A Survey of Symbolic Logic, 1918

    English survey that gives Leibniz's logical algebra a place in the history of symbolic logic and prints two Leibniz fragments.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref lists an appendix of two Leibniz fragments in the 1918 book. Crossref 10.1525/9780520398252
  6. Date unknown

    Logical calculi in unpublished manuscripts

    precursor

    Leibniz, logical manuscripts, c.1680s (unpublished in his lifetime)

    The calculus ratiocinator and algebraic logic exist only in notes that were published long after his death, so their influence came late.

    Evidence (1)
    • The calculus ratiocinator is a concept in Leibniz' writings; the analytic view treats it as an anticipation of an algebra of logic. Wikipedia: Calculus ratiocinator
