1908 · Algebra

Noncommutative algebras, representation theory and number theory

Noether, Brauer and Hasse linked hypercomplex systems to commutative algebra and arithmetic via modules and cohomological ideas.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1906

    Wedderburn's theorem on hypercomplex numbers

    precursor

    J. H. Maclagan-Wedderburn, 'On a Theorem in Hypercomplex Numbers', Proc. Roy. Soc. Edinburgh, 1906

    Wedderburn's 1906 theorem on hypercomplex numbers was the first step toward the semisimple classification.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref lists the 1906 Proc. Roy. Soc. Edinburgh paper with this title Crossref DOI 10.1017/s037016460002441x
  2. 1908

    Wedderburn's structure theorem for semisimple algebras

    first statement

    J. H. Maclagan-Wedderburn, 'On Hypercomplex Numbers', Proc. London Math. Soc. (2) 6, 1908

    Wedderburn showed that every semisimple algebra is a direct sum of matrix algebras over division algebras.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref lists Wedderburn 1908 PLMS paper Crossref DOI 10.1112/plms/s2-6.1.77
    • MacTutor Wedderburn biography describes the semisimple-algebra result of this paper MacTutor Wedderburn biography
  3. 1927

    Artin's chain-condition theory of hypercomplex numbers

    key development

    E. Artin, 'Zur Theorie der hyperkomplexen Zahlen', Abh. Math. Sem. Hamburg 5, 1927

    Artin extended the Wedderburn theory to rings satisfying chain conditions, giving the Wedderburn-Artin theorem in its usual form.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref lists Artin 1927 Hamburg paper with this title Crossref DOI 10.1007/bf02952526
    • MacTutor Ring theory topic: Artin extended the theory to chain conditions in 1927 MacTutor Ring theory topic
  4. 1929

    Brauer's classification of division algebras (Brauer group)

    first application

    R. Brauer, 'Ueber Systeme hyperkomplexer Zahlen', Math. Z. 30, 1929

    Brauer classified central simple algebras up to equivalence, starting the Brauer group, the purposeful use on the division-algebra classification problem.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref lists Brauer 1929 Math. Z. paper with this title Crossref DOI 10.1007/bf01187754
    • Wikipedia: the Brauer group arose from attempts to classify division algebras Wikipedia: Brauer group (raw wikitext)
  5. 1929

    Noether's hypercomplex numbers and representation theory

    key development

    E. Noether, 'Hyperkomplexe Groessen und Darstellungstheorie', Math. Z. 30, 1929

    Noether linked hypercomplex systems to modules and representation theory, bringing the algebra into commutative and arithmetic methods.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref lists Noether 1929 Math. Z. paper with this title Crossref DOI 10.1007/bf01187794
    • MacTutor: Noether's results became widely known through van der Waerden's 1930 book MacTutor Ring theory topic
  6. 1932

    Albert-Brauer-Hasse-Noether main theorem

    key development

    R. Brauer, 'Beweis eines Hauptsatzes in der Theorie der Algebren', J. reine angew. Math. 167, 1932 (with Hasse and Noether)

    The main theorem of the theory of central simple algebras over number fields tied the algebras to local arithmetic.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref lists Brauer 1932 Crelle paper with the Hauptsatz title Crossref DOI 10.1515/crll.1932.167.399
  7. 1935

    Deuring's Algebren monograph

    consolidation

    M. Deuring, Algebren, Ergebnisse der Mathematik, Springer, 1935

    Deuring's monograph was the first systematic book on the theory of algebras of this kind.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref lists Deuring 1935 Algebren (Ergebnisse) volume Crossref DOI 10.1007/978-3-642-91479-9
