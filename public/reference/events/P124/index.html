1801 · Number theory

Gauss's Disquisitiones Arithmeticae

Congruence notation, quadratic forms and cyclotomy were unified.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1798

    Legendre's Essai sur la théorie des nombres

    precursor

    A.-M. Legendre, Essai sur la theorie des nombres, Paris, 1798

    An earlier systematic number-theory treatise appeared in 1798, the same year Gauss finished writing.

    Evidence (2)
    • MacTutor says the first edition of Legendre's Théorie des nombres was 1798 MacTutor, Legendre
    • Essai sur la theorie des nombres, 1798. Internet Archive record essaisurlathor00lege
  2. 1798

    Gauss completes the manuscript

    precursor

    Gauss, Disquisitiones Arithmeticae (manuscript finished 1798, printed 1801)

    The book was written in 1798 when Gauss was 21 and printed in 1801, so the manuscript is private until then.

    Evidence (1)
    • Wikipedia says it was written in 1798 and published in 1801 English Wikipedia, Disquisitiones Arithmeticae
  3. 1801

    Disquisitiones Arithmeticae published in Leipzig

    first statement

    C. F. Gauss, Disquisitiones Arithmeticae, Leipzig (Fleischer), 1801

    The book presents congruences, quadratic forms and cyclotomy under one scheme and proves quadratic reciprocity.

    Evidence (2)
    • Internet Archive item for the 1801 Disquisitiones arithmeticae archive.org disquisitionesa00gaus
    • MacTutor says Gauss published the book in the summer of 1801 with seven sections MacTutor, Gauss
  4. 1801

    First systematic treatment of number theory

    consolidation

    Gauss, Disquisitiones Arithmeticae, Leipzig, 1801

    Gauss brought together results of Fermat, Euler, Lagrange and Legendre in one rigorous framework.

    Evidence (1)
    • Wikipedia says the book made number theory rigorous and systematic and brought earlier results together English Wikipedia, Disquisitiones Arithmeticae
  5. 1847

    Kummer factorises cyclotomic integers

    key development

    E. E. Kummer, Ueber die Zerlegung der aus Wurzeln der Einheit gebildeten complexen Zahlen, Crelle 35, 1847

    Extends Gauss' cyclotomy to unique factorisation with ideal numbers, an early step of algebraic number theory.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref record, Crelle 35 (1847), pp. 327-367. Crossref 10.1515/crll.1847.35.327
    • The Disquisitiones was a starting point for Kummer, Dirichlet and Dedekind. Wikipedia, Disquisitiones Arithmeticae
  6. 1863

    Dirichlet-Dedekind Vorlesungen ueber Zahlentheorie

    consolidation

    P. G. Lejeune Dirichlet, Vorlesungen ueber Zahlentheorie, ed. R. Dedekind, 1863

    Textbook that recasts the Disquisitiones in simpler form and carries it into algebraic number theory.

    Dating or attribution note

    Textbook role inferred from the title and history pages.

    Evidence (2)
    • Vorlesungen ueber Zahlentheorie, ed. Dedekind, 1863. Internet Archive record 10081672bsb
    • Dirichlet was among the 19th-century mathematicians for whom the book was the starting point. Wikipedia, Disquisitiones Arithmeticae
