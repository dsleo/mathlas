Transcendental numbers from continued fractions (Liouville)

Liouville used rational approximation by continued fractions to produce the first provably transcendental numbers, then gave an explicit example without them in 1851.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1844

    Liouville's first transcendental numbers

    first statement

    J. Liouville, note presented 13 May 1844, Comptes rendus 18

    Liouville uses the way a root of an algebraic equation is approximated by continued-fraction convergents to build numbers that cannot satisfy any integer polynomial.

    Dating or attribution note

    Indexed in the volume table as a Remarques note; the pages (883-885 and a follow-up) were not read in full.

    Evidence (3)
    • Scanned Comptes rendus vol. 18: the 13 May 1844 meeting opens with Liouville's communication on classes of numbers beyond algebraic irrationals, starting from continued fractions. Internet Archive scan of Comptes rendus 18
    • Biography says he proved a transcendental number exists in 1844 with an infinite class built from continued fractions. MacTutor: Liouville biography
    • Article says Liouville proved his approximation bound in 1844 and exhibited examples. English Wikipedia: Liouville number
  2. 1851

    Explicit constant without continued fractions

    key development

    J. Liouville, Sur des classes tres-etendues de quantites dont la valeur n'est ni algebrique..., J. Math. Pures Appl. 16 (1851) 133-142

    Liouville drops continued fractions and gives explicit decimals, such as the digit-1-at-factorial-positions number.

    Evidence (2)
    • Numdam record: journal, tome 16, 1851, pages 133-142. Numdam
    • Biography says the 1851 results remove the dependence on continued fractions and give the constant. MacTutor: Liouville biography
  3. 1873

    Hermite proves e transcendental

    key development

    C. Hermite, Sur la fonction exponentielle, Comptes rendus 77 (1873)

    The first number not built to be transcendental is shown to be so.

    Evidence (2)
    • Scanned Comptes rendus vol. 77 (1873) with Hermite's Sur la fonction exponentielle. Internet Archive scan
    • Article says e was the first such number. English Wikipedia: Transcendental number
  4. 1874

    Cantor: algebraic numbers are countable

    key development

    G. Cantor, Ueber eine Eigenschaft des Inbegriffs aller reellen algebraischen Zahlen, J. reine angew. Math. 77 (1874) 258-262

    Cantor shows transcendental numbers exist in abundance by a counting argument, with a second construction method.

    Evidence (2)
    • Title, journal, volume 77, pages 258-262, 1874 confirmed. Crossref record 10.1515/crll.1874.77.258
    • Article says Cantor proved algebraic numbers countable in 1874 and gave a new construction. English Wikipedia: Transcendental number
  5. 1957

    Schneider's monograph 'Einfuehrung in die transzendenten Zahlen'

    consolidation

    T. Schneider, Einfuehrung in die transzendenten Zahlen, Die Grundlehren der Math. Wiss. 81, Berlin-Goettingen-Heidelberg: Springer, 1957

    A monograph that introduces transcendental numbers as a subject; it is the first book-length treatment with that title found here.

    Dating or attribution note

    Chosen because the title names the subject of Liouville's construction. Its treatment of Liouville's continued-fraction criterion was not read. Perron's 'Irrationalzahlen' (1921) is in the catalogue but its title does not name transcendence, so it is not used.

    Evidence (1)
    • zbMATH record 'Einfuehrung in die transzendenten Zahlen', Schneider, Grundlehren 81 (1957) zbMATH Open API
