1797 · Analysis

Lagrange's algebraic foundation for the calculus

A programme grounded calculus on power series without limits or infinitesimals.

Also known as: Lagrange's power series calculus

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1734

    Berkeley's The Analyst

    precursor

    Berkeley attacked the use of infinitesimals and fluxions, which the later search for algebraic foundations responds to.

    Evidence (1)
    • Internet Archive item: The Analyst, 1734 archive.org theanalystoradis00berkuoft
  2. 1755

    Euler's Institutiones calculi differentialis

    precursor

    Leonhard Euler, Institutiones calculi differentialis, St Petersburg, 1755

    Euler's formal differential calculus, which Lagrange's programme aimed to put on an algebraic basis.

    Evidence (1)
    • Institutiones calculi differentialis, 1755. Open Library
  3. 1797

    Théorie des fonctions analytiques

    first statement

    J.-L. Lagrange, Théorie des fonctions analytiques, Paris, 1797

    Lagrange set out to give calculus principles freed from infinitesimals, vanishing quantities, limits or fluxions, reducing them to the algebra of finite quantities.

    Dating or attribution note

    An earlier Berlin memoir (c. 1772, on a new kind of calculus) is a candidate for the first public statement and was not verified, so 1797 is a floor.

    Evidence (2)
    • Internet Archive item for the 1797 Théorie des fonctions analytiques archive.org theoriedesfoncti00lagr
    • MacTutor quotes Lagrange's aim and says the work appeared in 1797 without enough attention to convergence MacTutor, Lagrange
  4. 1797

    Textbook-length treatment

    consolidation

    Lagrange, Théorie des fonctions analytiques, 1797

    The 1797 book is a systematic account of differential calculus on power series.

    Evidence (1)
    • MacTutor describes it as the first theory of functions of a real variable MacTutor, Lagrange
  5. 1800

    Lagrange's Lecons sur le calcul des fonctions

    key development

    Lagrange, Lecons sur le calcul des fonctions (1800)

    Second work on the programme, a lecture course restating the function-based calculus.

    Evidence (2)
    • MacTutor dates the Lecons to 1800 and calls it his second work on this topic. MacTutor, Lagrange
    • Lecons sur le calcul des fonctions, early editions 1804 and 1806 listed. Open Library
  6. 1821

    Cauchy's Cours d'analyse replaces the power-series foundation

    key development

    Cauchy, Cours d'analyse de l'Ecole royale polytechnique, Paris, 1821

    Cauchy builds calculus on limits and continuity, which displaced Lagrange's algebraic approach.

    Dating or attribution note

    Displacement of the programme is the standard account; Grabiner's page was not read in full.

    Evidence (2)
    • Cours d'analyse, 1821. Open Library
    • Study of Cauchy's rigorous calculus and its relation to earlier approaches. Grabiner, Who Gave You the Epsilon?, Amer. Math. Monthly 1983
