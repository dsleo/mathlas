1000 · Algebra

Al-Karaji: binomial coefficients and early induction

A table of binomial coefficients and stepwise arguments for sums of integers and cubes anticipate induction.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 250 BCE

    Pingala's Meruprastara

    precursor

    Pingala, Chandahshastra

    A triangular array counting poetic metres, later read as the binomial coefficient triangle.

    Evidence (1)
    • Pingala (3rd or 2nd century BCE) arranged counts in a form later read as Pascal's triangle. English Wikipedia, Pascal's triangle
  2. 1000

    Al-Karaji: binomial coefficients and stepwise argument

    first statement

    Al-Karaji, Al-Fakhri and Al-Badi' (c. 1000); quoted by al-Samaw'al

    Built the triangle of binomial coefficients and gave arguments that move from case n to n+1, an early form of induction.

    Evidence (2)
    • Al-Karaji used a form of induction in his work on the binomial theorem and the Pascal triangle; (a+b)^3 in Al-Fakhri, (a+b)^4 in Al-Badi'. MacTutor, Al-Karaji biography
    • Rashed's 1972 study identifies mathematical induction in al-Karaji and al-Samaw'al. zbMATH 3410980, Rashed, Arch. Hist. Exact Sci. 9 (1972) 1-21
  3. 1149

    Al-Samaw'al: al-Bahir fi'l-jabr

    key development

    Al-Samaw'al, al-Bahir fi'l-jabr (written at 19)

    Preserved al-Karaji's triangle and used the same recursive reasoning for sums and the binomial theorem.

    Evidence (2)
    • al-Bahir written at nineteen; records lost works of al-Karaji; gives the binomial theorem with Pascal triangle coefficients and an early form of induction. MacTutor, Al-Samawal biography
    • al-Samaw'al extended results to n=12. English Wikipedia, Al-Samawal
  4. 1575

    Maurolico: early printed explicit induction proof

    key development

    Francesco Maurolico, Arithmeticorum libri duo (written 1557, printed 1575)

    Used an induction step to prove that the first n odd numbers sum to n squared.

    Evidence (3)
    • Arithmeticorum libri duo, written in 1557 and published with Opuscula Mathematica, contains a proof by induction claimed to be the first genuine one. MacTutor, Maurolico biography
    • Maurolico used the technique in 1575. English Wikipedia, Mathematical induction
    • Wikipedia: earliest rigorous use of induction is by Gersonides (1288-1344); Maurolico (1575) is cited as a separate case Wikipedia, Mathematical induction
  5. 1665

    Pascal, Traite du triangle arithmetique

    consolidation

    Blaise Pascal, Traite du triangle arithmetique (written 1654; published 1665)

    The first systematic treatise on the triangle, with an explicit induction principle.

    Evidence (3)
    • Open Library record, first publication 1665. Open Library: Pascal, Traite du triangle arithmetique
    • Pascal was not the first to study the triangle, but his Treatise was the most important work on it. MacTutor, Pascal biography
    • Pascal gave the first explicit formulation of the induction principle in the Traite (1665). English Wikipedia, Mathematical induction
