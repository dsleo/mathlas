1764 · Statistics & decision theory

Bayes' inverse probability

Bayes derived a posterior for the binomial.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1718

    De Moivre's Doctrine of Chances (direct probability)

    precursor

    Abraham de Moivre, The Doctrine of Chances, London, 1718

    Treats probabilities of outcomes given a known chance, the forward problem Bayes inverted.

    Evidence (2)
    • First edition dated 1718. Internet Archive record b30412390
    • Biography of the author. MacTutor, De Moivre biography
  2. 1764

    Bayes' Essay communicated by Price

    first statement

    T. Bayes, An Essay towards solving a Problem in the Doctrine of Chances, Phil. Trans. 53 (1763), pp. 370-418, issued 1764

    The posthumous essay derives the probability of an unknown binomial chance from observed successes and failures, with Price's introduction.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref record: Bayes, An essay towards solving a problem in the doctrine of chances, Philosophical Transactions vol. 53, pp. 370-418, dated 1763 Crossref 10.1098/rstl.1763.0053
    • MacTutor says Bayes set out his theory in the Essay published in Phil. Trans. in 1764, sent in by Richard Price MacTutor, Bayes
  3. 1764

    Price's second paper on the essay

    key development

    Richard Price, A demonstration of the second rule in the essay towards the solution of a problem in the doctrine of chances, Phil. Trans. 54, 1764

    Price follows up Bayes' essay with a demonstration of its second rule and approximations.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref record, Phil. Trans. 54 (1764), pp. 296-325. Crossref 10.1098/rstl.1764.0050
  4. 1774

    Laplace's memoir on the probability of causes

    key development

    Laplace, Mémoire sur la probabilité des causes par les événemens, Mém. Acad. Roy. Sci. (Savants étrangers) 6, 1774

    Laplace independently derived the inverse-probability rule for causes of events and developed it much further.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref records: Stigler's study of Laplace's 1774 memoir and its translation, Statistical Science 1 (1986), 359-378 Crossref 10.1214/ss/1177013620
    • MacTutor lists Stigler's 'Laplace's 1774 memoir on inverse probability' among its sources and says Laplace accepted Bayes' conclusions in 1781 MacTutor, Laplace / Bayes
  5. 1785

    Condorcet applies the probability of causes to voting

    key development

    Condorcet, Essai sur l application de l analyse a la probabilite des decisions rendues a la pluralite des voix, Paris, 1785

    Uses reasoning from observed votes back to the reliability of the voters.

    Evidence (2)
    • Essai sur l'application de l'analyse a la probabilite des decisions, 1785. Internet Archive record essaisurlapplica00cond
    • Biography of Condorcet. MacTutor, Condorcet biography
  6. 1812

    Laplace's Théorie analytique des probabilités

    consolidation

    P.-S. Laplace, Théorie analytique des probabilités, Paris, 1812

    Book two contains Laplace's definition of probability, Bayes' rule and a treatment of inverse probability, making it the standard reference.

    Evidence (2)
    • Internet Archive item for the 1812 edition of Théorie analytique des probabilités archive.org theorieanalytiqu0000mlec
    • MacTutor says the second book contains Laplace's definition, Bayes rule and inverse probability MacTutor, Laplace
  7. 1939

    Jeffreys' Theory of Probability revives Bayesian inference

    key development

    Harold Jeffreys, Theory of Probability, Oxford, 1939

    Builds statistical inference on inverse probability, reviving the Bayes-Laplace approach in the twentieth century.

    Evidence (1)
    • First edition 1939, Clarendon Press. Open Library record OL642021W
