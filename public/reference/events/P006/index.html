1846 · Algebra

Group theory from the solution of polynomial equations

Permutations of roots, first a tool for solving equations, become an object studied for its own structure and are then axiomatised independently of permutations.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1772

    Lagrange studies permutations of roots

    precursor

    J.-L. Lagrange, Réflexions sur la résolution algébrique des équations (1770–72)

    Permutations are used to analyse solvability before groups become independent objects.

    Evidence (1)
    • The 1770-volume first sections of Lagrange's Réflexions were printed in 1772. EMS Press historical bibliography
  2. 1846

    Galois's group memoir appears in print

    first statement

    É. Galois, Mémoire sur les conditions de résolubilité des équations par radicaux, published by Liouville (1846)

    The public memoir gives a group criterion for solvability by radicals.

    Evidence (1)
    • Liouville printed Galois' memoirs in his journal in 1846; group-based solvability theory outlined there MacTutor, Galois biography
  3. 1846

    Galois criterion applied to polynomial solvability

    first application

    É. Galois, Mémoire sur les conditions de résolubilité des équations par radicaux, published by Liouville (1846)

    The printed criterion targets the solvability of polynomial equations.

    Evidence (1)
    • Galois' printed memoirs give a group-theoretic criterion for solvability by radicals MacTutor, Galois biography
  4. 1854

    Cayley abstracts group multiplication

    key development

    A. Cayley, On the theory of groups, as depending on the symbolic equation θⁿ=1 (1854)

    A group is treated as an abstract multiplication structure.

    Evidence (1)
    • Cayley's paper 'On the theory of groups, as depending on the symbolic equation theta^n = 1', Phil. Mag. 1854 Crossref record
  5. 1854

    Theory of groups appears in Cayley's title

    naming

    A. Cayley, On the theory of groups, as depending on the symbolic equation θⁿ=1 (1854)

    The published title supplies a named-field anchor.

    Evidence (1)
    • Cayley's paper 'On the theory of groups, as depending on the symbolic equation theta^n = 1', Phil. Mag. 1854 Crossref record
