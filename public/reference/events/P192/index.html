1700 · Algebra

Leibniz: determinants

Systems of linear equations were handled by an indexed notation for determinants, left unpublished.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1678

    Leibniz begins experimenting with notation for coefficient systems

    precursor

    Leibniz, unpublished manuscripts, from 1678

    Over some fifty years he tried more than fifty ways of writing coefficient systems.

    Evidence (1)
    • More than 50 notations tried over 50 years beginning in 1678; mostly unpublished. MacTutor history topic: Matrices and determinants
  2. 1683

    Seki's determinants in Japan

    precursor

    Seki Takakazu, Method of solving the dissimulated problems, 1683

    Independent earlier appearance in Japan, with determinants up to 5x5 used on equations (not yet on linear systems).

    Evidence (1)
    • In 1683 Seki wrote a work with matrix methods; determinants of orders up to 5. MacTutor history topic: Matrices and determinants
  3. 1693

    Leibniz' letter to de l'Hopital

    precursor

    Leibniz, letter to de l'Hopital, 1693 (private)

    Leibniz explained that three equations in two unknowns have a solution when a determinant condition holds, using a double-index notation.

    Evidence (1)
    • 1693 letter gives the vanishing-determinant condition using two-character indices. MacTutor history topic: Matrices and determinants
  4. 1700

    Leibniz' first printed results on coefficient systems

    first statement

    Leibniz, publications of 1700 and 1710

    MacTutor says only two printed papers (1700 and 1710) by Leibniz contain results on coefficient systems.

    Evidence (1)
    • Only two publications (1700 and 1710) contain results on coefficient systems. MacTutor history topic: Matrices and determinants
  5. 1748

    Maclaurin's Treatise of Algebra

    key development

    Colin Maclaurin, A Treatise of Algebra, London, 1748 (posthumous)

    Contains the first published results on determinants per MacTutor, proving Cramer's rule for 2x2 and 3x3.

    Evidence (2)
    • Archive.org records list the 1748 edition. archive.org item atreatisealgebr00lawsgoog
    • Maclaurin's treatise, published 1748, has the first published results on determinants. MacTutor history topic: Matrices and determinants
  6. 1750

    Cramer's general rule

    key development

    Gabriel Cramer, Introduction a l'analyse des lignes courbes algebriques, Geneva, 1750

    Gave the rule for n unknowns in an appendix, without proof.

    Evidence (2)
    • Archive.org record dates the book to 1750. archive.org item bub_gb_HzcVAAAAQAAJ
    • Cramer gave the rule for n x n systems in the 1750 book. MacTutor history topic: Matrices and determinants
  7. 1841

    Jacobi uses the determinant term in a title

    naming

    Jacobi, C. G. J., De determinantibus functionalibus, 1841

    The earliest title found with a form of the word "determinant".

    Evidence (1)
    • zbMATH record: "De Determinantibus functionalibus", Jacobi, 1841 zbMATH Open doc 2751099
  8. 1857

    Baltzer publishes a book on determinants

    consolidation

    Baltzer, R., Theorie und Anwendung der Determinanten, 1857

    Book-length German treatment of determinants.

    Dating or attribution note

    Earliest book-length determinant treatment located in catalogue search, not proven first; Spottiswoode-type articles not checked.

    Evidence (1)
    • Open Library lists the book with first publication year 1857 Open Library works OL13141409W
