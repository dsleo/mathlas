Leibniz: determinants

Systems of linear equations were first handled by an indexed notation for determinants (Leibniz, in a private letter and unpublished papers); the first printed determinant results are Maclaurin's and Cramer's.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1683

    Seki's determinants in Japan

    precursor

    Seki Takakazu, Method of solving the dissimulated problems, 1683

    Independent earlier appearance in Japan, with determinants up to 5x5 used on equations (not yet on linear systems).

    Evidence (1)
    • In 1683 Seki wrote a work with matrix methods; determinants of orders up to 5. MacTutor history topic: Matrices and determinants
  2. 1693–1700

    Leibniz' index notation: the 1693 letter to de l'Hopital and the 1700 Acta Eruditorum paper

    precursor

    Leibniz, letter to de l'Hopital, Hanover 28 April 1693 (first printed in Gerhardt, Mathematische Schriften II, 1850, pp. 239-241); and 'Responsio ad Dn. Nic. Fatii Duillierii imputationes. Accessit nova Artis Analyticae promotio ...', Acta Eruditorum, 1700, signed G.G.L. (Gerhardt, Math. Schriften V, pp. 340-349)

    Leibniz wrote coefficients with two-digit numeral indices (the first for the equation, the second for the unknown), eliminated two unknowns from three linear equations and stated a general combinatorial rule for the resulting condition, which is the vanishing of a determinant; the 1700 paper printed the same index notation but for series reversion, not for linear systems.

    Evidence (5)
    • Primary text read: Leibniz to de l'Hopital, Hanover 28 April 1693, Mathematische Schriften II (Briefwechsel mit Huygens und de l'Hopital), Berlin: Asher, 1850: three linear equations with two-character indices, elimination, and the general rule. Leibnizens mathematische Schriften, ed. C. I. Gerhardt, Band II (Internet Archive scan of vol. 1-2, 1849-50)
    • Primary text read: the 1700 Acta Eruditorum reply to Fatio de Duillier with the 'nova Artis Analyticae promotio' using numbers for letters (theorem on the reversion of a series with coefficients 01, 02, 10, 11, 12, 20, ...), reprinted in Mathematische Schriften V, pp. 340-349. Leibnizens mathematische Schriften, Band V (Internet Archive scan); Acta Eruditorum 1700 (Internet Archive item s1id13206680 shows the title and text)
    • Title and volume of the Acta Eruditorum for 1700 in the Internet Archive. Internet Archive item s1id13206680
    • The 1693 letter gives the vanishing-determinant condition using two-character indices; more than 50 notations tried over 50 years from 1678, mostly unpublished; only two publications (1700 and 1710) have results on coefficient systems. MacTutor history topic: Matrices and determinants
    • Leibniz introduced indexing of unknown coefficients in the 1693 letter to l'Hopital and in 1700 in Acta Eruditorum, but nobody noticed. A. A. Kosinski, 'Cramer's Rule Is Due to Cramer', Mathematics Magazine 74 (2001)
  3. 1748

    Maclaurin's Treatise of Algebra

    first statement

    Colin Maclaurin, A Treatise of Algebra, London, 1748 (posthumous)

    Maclaurin's chapter on 'general theorems for exterminating unknown quantities' gives the solution of two and three linear equations as quotients of sums of signed products of 'opposite' coefficients, and states the same pattern for four, which is Cramer's rule in determinant form.

    Evidence (4)
    • Primary text read: A Treatise of Algebra, London 1748, Part I chapter XII, Theorems I and II (sections 86-87, pp. 82-84): solutions of 2 and 3 linear equations as quotients of sums of products of opposite coefficients; the four-equation case by the same rule with signs. Internet Archive item atreatisealgebr03maclgoog (1748 first edition, title page read)
    • Library record of the 1748 edition (London: A. Millar and J. Nourse). archive.org item atreatisealgebr00lawsgoog (record dated 1748; the scan itself is the 1796 sixth edition)
    • Maclaurin's treatise, published 1748, has the first published results on determinants. MacTutor history topic: Matrices and determinants
    • Some authors credit Maclaurin for the rule from the Treatise of Algebra (pp. 82-85) edited from manuscripts and published posthumously in 1748; Maclaurin used unindexed small letters. A. A. Kosinski, 'Cramer's Rule Is Due to Cramer', Mathematics Magazine 74 (2001)
  4. 1750

    Cramer's general rule

    key development

    Gabriel Cramer, Introduction a l'analyse des lignes courbes algebriques, Geneva, 1750

    Gave the rule for n unknowns in an appendix, without proof.

    Evidence (2)
    • Primary text read: Appendix of Introduction a l'analyse des lignes courbes algebriques (Geneva, 1750): notation with indexed coefficients, solutions for two and three equations, common denominator, indeterminate and impossible cases. Internet Archive item bub_gb_HzcVAAAAQAAJ (scan, OCR text read)
    • Cramer gave the rule for n x n systems in the 1750 book. MacTutor history topic: Matrices and determinants
  5. 1801

    Gauss coins 'determinant' in the Disquisitiones

    naming

    C. F. Gauss, Disquisitiones arithmeticae, Leipzig: Fleischer, 1801

    Gauss introduces the word determinant for a quantity attached to a quadratic form.

    Evidence (2)
    • Primary text read: Disquisitiones arithmeticae (Leipzig, 1801), binary forms (arts. 153 ff.): 'determinans' of a form, the invariant of its equivalence class. Internet Archive item disquisitionesa00gaus (OCR text read)
    • In 1801 Gauss coined the term determinant in the Disquisitiones arithmeticae while discussing quadratic forms. MacTutor history topic: Matrices and determinants
  6. 1815

    Cauchy's memoir on alternating symmetric functions and determinants

    key development

    A.-L. Cauchy, Memoire sur les fonctions qui ne peuvent obtenir que deux valeurs egales et de signes contraires par suite des transpositions operees entre les variables qu'elles renferment, J. Ecole Polytechnique 17e cahier, tome 10 (1815) (Oeuvres (2) 1, pp. 91-169; read to the Institut 30 November 1812)

    Cauchy uses 'determinant' in its modern sense and proves the multiplication theorem for determinants.

    Evidence (2)
    • MacTutor: Cauchy in 1812 used 'determinant' in its modern sense; the multiplication theorem for determinants is proved for the first time in the 1812 paper. MacTutor history topic: Matrices and determinants (re-read in review)
    • Cauchy's two founding memoirs were read to the Institut on 30 November 1812 and published in 1815 (Journal de l'Ecole polytechnique, XVIIe cahier, tome X; Oeuvres (2) 1); the second one defines the determinant through the difference-product and uses the double-index notation. Revue d'histoire des mathematiques 19 (2013), 'A case of mathematical eponymy: the Vandermonde determinant' (full text read)
  7. 1841

    Jacobi writes the determinant treatises of 1841

    key development

    C. G. J. Jacobi, De formatione et proprietatibus determinantium, J. reine angew. Math. 22 (1841) 285-318, and De determinantibus functionalibus, ibid. 319-359

    Jacobi's treatises give an algorithmic definition of the determinant for numbers and functions and made it widely known.

    Evidence (4)
    • Crossref: De formatione et proprietatibus Determinantium, Crelle 22 (1841) 285-318. Crossref record
    • Crossref: De Determinantibus functionalibus, Crelle 22 (1841) 319-359. Crossref record
    • zbMATH lists De Determinantibus functionalibus, J. Reine Angew. Math. 22, 319-359 (1841). zbMATH Open doc 2751099
    • In 1841 Jacobi gave three treatises on determinants with an algorithmic definition, making the determinant widely known. MacTutor history topic: Matrices and determinants
  8. 1851

    Spottiswoode, Elementary Theorems Relating to Determinants

    consolidation

    W. Spottiswoode, Elementary Theorems Relating to Determinants, London: Longman, Brown, Green and Longman, 1851

    An English booklet on the theory of determinants that predates the German and Italian treatises of the mid 1850s.

    Dating or attribution note

    Earliest separate treatise found in an archive.org title search for 1840-60 (Brioschi, La teorica dei determinanti, Pavia 1854, and Baltzer, 1857, follow); not proven first and not read. Replaces the earlier Baltzer 1857 entry, which is not the earliest book.

    Evidence (2)
    • Archive record: Spottiswoode, Elementary Theorems Relating to Determinants, Longman et al., 1851. Internet Archive item elementarytheor01spotgoog
    • Later books: Baltzer, Theorie und Anwendung der Determinanten (1857); Brioschi, La teorica dei determinanti (1854). Internet Archive item theorieundanwen01baltgoog (Baltzer 1857) and bub_gb_BoEH5h1BtD8C (Brioschi 1854)
