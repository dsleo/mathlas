1956 · Optimization & control

Pontryagin maximum principle

Necessary conditions for optimal control extended variational calculus.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1956

    Maximum principle announced by Boltyanskii, Gamkrelidze and Pontryagin

    first statement

    V. G. Boltyanskii, R. V. Gamkrelidze, L. S. Pontryagin, On the theory of optimal processes, Dokl. Akad. Nauk SSSR 110, 7-10, 1956

    Short note announcing necessary conditions for optimal control in terms of a maximum of a Hamiltonian.

    Evidence (2)
    • zbMATH record: Dokl. Akad. Nauk SSSR 110, 7-10 (1956). zbMATH 3118979
    • The maximum principle was formulated in 1956 by Pontryagin and his students. Wikipedia, Pontryagin's maximum principle
  2. 1961

    Monograph The Mathematical Theory of Optimal Processes (Russian 1961, English 1962)

    consolidation

    Pontryagin, L. S., Boltyanskii, V. G., Gamkrelidze, R. V., Mishchenko, E. F., The Mathematical Theory of Optimal Processes, Interscience/Wiley (1962; Russian 1961)

    The first systematic treatment of the maximum principle, a monograph with the students' proofs.

    Evidence (3)
    • Open Library record for The Mathematical Theory of Optimal Processes (Wiley, 1962) Open Library
    • MacTutor Pontryagin biography: the book was published in 1961, with the English translation in 1962 MacTutor, Lev Pontryagin
    • zbMATH record of the 1961 Russian edition. zbMATH 3167339
  3. 1966

    Mangasarian's sufficient conditions

    key development

    O. L. Mangasarian, Sufficient conditions for the optimal control of nonlinear systems, SIAM J. Control 4, 1966

    Shows the necessary conditions become sufficient under convexity.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref record, SIAM J. Control 4 (1966), pp. 139-152. Crossref 10.1137/0304013
    • Necessary conditions become sufficient under convexity. Wikipedia, Pontryagin's maximum principle
  4. Date unknown

    Classical variational ideas and the rocket application

    precursor

    Before the formal statement, the variational ideas come from classical calculus of variations; the initial application was maximising a rocket's terminal speed.

    Evidence (1)
    • Wikipedia article: the result was derived from classical calculus of variations and its initial application was to rocket terminal speed Wikipedia, Pontryagin's maximum principle
  5. Date unknown

    Maximising the terminal speed of a rocket

    first application

    The principle's first use was a rocket speed-maximisation problem.

    Evidence (1)
    • Initial application was maximisation of a rocket's terminal speed. Wikipedia, Pontryagin's maximum principle
