300 · Number theory

Chinese remainder theorem

A single-remainder puzzle grew into a general method for systems of congruences, including non-coprime moduli, motivated by calendar calculation.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 300–500

    Sunzi suanjing poses the 3-5-7 remainder problem

    first statement

    Sunzi suanjing (Sun Zi mathematical manual), China, 3rd-5th century

    A single puzzle asks for a number with given remainders mod 3, 5 and 7, and gives a procedure for it; this is the earliest known occurrence of the problem type.

    Evidence (3)
    • MacTutor Chinese overview: earliest known occurrence of a Chinese remainder type problem, in Sun Zi (about 400-about 460) MacTutor, Chinese overview
    • MacTutor Sun Zi: dating of the Sunzi suanjing ranges from 3rd to 5th century AD across modern authors MacTutor, Sun Zi
    • Wikipedia CRT: earliest known statement of the problem in the 5th-century Sunzi Suanjing; it is one case, not a theorem Wikipedia, Chinese remainder theorem
  2. 499

    Aryabhata describes the kuttaka algorithm for indeterminate remainders

    key development

    Aryabhata, Aryabhatiya, 499

    Aryabhata gives a pulverising algorithm (Euclidean-style) for solving remainder problems that arise from planetary periods, a general method beyond a single puzzle.

    Dating or attribution note

    Kuttaka is for linear indeterminate equations; its identification with the full CRT for several moduli is as stated by Wikipedia and not checked in the Aryabhatiya.

    Evidence (2)
    • MacTutor Aryabhata: Aryabhatiya finished 499; kuttaka method for ax-by=c from planetary period problems, related to Euclidean algorithm MacTutor, Aryabhata I
    • Wikipedia CRT: an algorithm for the problem was described by Aryabhata (6th century) Wikipedia, Chinese remainder theorem
  3. 1202

    Fibonacci includes remainder problems in Liber Abaci

    key development

    Fibonacci, Liber Abaci, 1202

    Special cases of the remainder problem appear in the first major European arithmetic of the Hindu-Arabic era.

    Evidence (2)
    • Wikipedia CRT: special cases appear in Fibonacci Liber Abaci (1202) Wikipedia, Chinese remainder theorem
    • Open Library lists Sigler translation of Fibonacci Liber Abaci (2002) Open Library
  4. 1247

    Qin Jiushao: general Dayan rule for systems of congruences, including non-coprime moduli

    key development

    Qin Jiushao, Shushu jiuzhang (Mathematical treatise in nine sections), 1247

    Qin gives a complete, systematic algorithm for simultaneous congruences, opening his treatise with it and treating even rational and non-coprime moduli.

    Evidence (3)
    • MacTutor Qin Jiushao: Shushu Jiuzhang appeared in 1247; chapter 1 on indeterminate analysis with the Chinese remainder theorem; problems with rational moduli MacTutor, Qin Jiushao
    • Libbrecht studied the treatise (Chinese Mathematics in the Thirteenth Century, 1973) Open Library
    • Shen 1988, Historical development of the Chinese remainder theorem K. Shen, Arch. Hist. Exact Sci. 38 (1988)
  5. 1801

    Gauss introduces congruence notation and treats the theorem systematically

    consolidation

    C. F. Gauss, Disquisitiones Arithmeticae, 1801

    Gauss gives congruences their notation and theory and solves a calendar-cycle problem with the remainder method, founding the modern treatment.

    Evidence (2)
    • Wikipedia CRT: congruences first introduced by Gauss in Disquisitiones Arithmeticae of 1801, illustrated with a calendar problem Wikipedia, Chinese remainder theorem
    • Open Library lists Disquisitiones arithmeticae, 1801 Open Library
