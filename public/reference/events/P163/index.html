1685 · Analysis

Newton: infinite series and binomial theorem

Expansion of non-integer powers and functions as series supplied tools for quadrature.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1656

    Wallis' interpolation of series

    precursor

    John Wallis, Arithmetica infinitorum, 1656

    Newton started from Wallis' intercalation of series for the area of the circle and hyperbola.

    Evidence (1)
    • Newton named Wallis' intercalation of series as his starting point. MacTutor: John Wallis
  2. 1664–1665

    Newton finds the generalised binomial theorem

    precursor

    Isaac Newton, private, 1664-65

    Newton's first major discovery was the binomial expansion for any exponent, made as a student.

    Evidence (2)
    • The generalised binomial theorem was his first major mathematical discovery, 1664-65. Wikipedia: Isaac Newton
    • Binomial series gives no proof; first results on non-integer exponents due to Newton. Wikipedia: Binomial series
  3. 1669

    De analysi sent to Collins

    precursor

    Isaac Newton, De analysi per aequationes numero terminorum infinitas, manuscript, 1669

    Barrow forwarded Newton's manuscript on series, quadrature and solving equations to Collins in July 1669, so the results circulated in a small circle.

    Dating or attribution note

    Manuscript, circulated privately; it is where series are used for quadrature, but I did not read its content, so this is not recorded as first_application.

    Evidence (1)
    • In July 1669 Barrow sent Newton's De Analysi to Collins. MacTutor: Isaac Newton
  4. 1685

    Wallis prints Newton's binomial series rule

    first statement

    John Wallis, A Treatise of Algebra, both Historical and Practical, London, 1685 (chapters 91-95)

    Wallis's printed Algebra gives, with credit to Newton, the general rule for expanding a binomial to any exponent and applies it to the circle's quadrature, the first appearance of the series in print.

    Evidence (3)
    • The 1685 book credits Newton with infinite series from division and root extraction, and its contents list a chapter on his doctrine of infinite series. Full-text OCR of the 1685 edition, Internet Archive item bim_early-english-books-1641-1700_a-treatise-of-algebra-_wallis-john_1685
    • Wallis says he knows Newton's work from two letters of June and October 1676 to Oldenburg, then states the general expansion rule for a power of a binomial with a fractional or integer exponent. Same 1685 text, chapters on Newton's infinite series
    • Item date metadata gives 1685 for the book. Internet Archive metadata
  5. 1711

    De analysi printed

    key development

    Isaac Newton, Analysis per quantitatum series, fluxiones, ac differentias, ed. W. Jones, London, 1711

    Jones printed the 1669 tract together with other Newton papers.

    Evidence (1)
    • Archive.org records list the 1711 Jones edition. archive.org item analysisperquan00jonegoog
  6. 1730

    Stirling's Methodus differentialis

    consolidation

    James Stirling, Methodus differentialis, London, 1730

    Systematic treatise on the summation and interpolation of infinite series, which develops Newton's and Wallis's series methods.

    Dating or attribution note

    The Wikipedia summary says the work is more than an expansion of Newton's method; the specific claim about binomial series was not checked in the text.

    Evidence (2)
    • Open Library record of the 1730 Methodus differentialis. Open Library work record
    • Methodus differentialis (1730) is a treatise on summation and interpolation of infinite series. Wikipedia, James Stirling
  7. 1826

    Abel on convergence of the binomial series

    key development

    N. H. Abel, Untersuchungen uber die Reihe ..., J. reine angew. Math. 1, 311-339, 1826

    Abel studied the convergence of the binomial series, which Newton had stated without proof.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref DOI 10.1515/crll.1826.1.311, Crelle's Journal vol 1, pp. 311-339, 1826. Crossref
    • Newton gave no proof; Abel treated convergence in 1826. Wikipedia: Binomial series
