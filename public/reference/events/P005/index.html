1744 · Number theory

Analytic number theory I: Euler's product and Dirichlet's characters

Infinite series and characters of finite abelian groups (a Fourier analysis on Z/nZ) are used to count primes in residue classes.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1744

    Euler's product over primes

    first statement

    Euler, Variae observationes circa series infinitas, Commentarii Petropolitanae 9 (1737), printed 1744

    Euler writes the sum of powers of integers as a product over primes, linking primes to infinite series.

    Evidence (3)
    • In 1737 Euler related prime numbers to the zeta function by an infinite product Wikipedia, Dirichlet's theorem on arithmetic progressions
    • The original Euler product was proven by Euler Wikipedia, Euler product
    • Euler Archive: Variae observationes circa series infinitas, Commentarii vol. 9, dated 1744 Euler Archive record E72 (bepress metadata)
  2. 1798

    Legendre's conjecture on primes in arithmetic progressions

    precursor

    Legendre, Essai sur la theorie des nombres, Paris, 1798 (2nd ed. 1808)

    The statement that any progression with coprime start and difference holds infinitely many primes was conjectured before Dirichlet proved it.

    Dating or attribution note

    Wikipedia cites the Essai (1798, 2nd ed. 1808) for Legendre's conjecture on primes in progressions, made while trying to prove quadratic reciprocity; Gauss (Disquisitiones, 1801, art. 297) remarks that Legendre found the proof hard. Legendre's earlier memoir (c. 1785) was not checked. Wikipedia also says Euler stated the case a = 1 in 1775 (printed 1785). The content of the Essai was not read (archive.org catalogue entry only). Review G3: Dirichlet himself, in the 1837 memoir and the 1838 note, says Legendre attempted the proof with an inductively supported theorem (read in Werke I), which confirms the nature of the precursor; the Essai itself was not read.

    Evidence (3)
    • The general form was first conjectured by Legendre, as Gauss noted in the Disquisitiones Wikipedia, Dirichlet's theorem on arithmetic progressions
    • The theorem had been conjectured by Legendre MacTutor, Lejeune Dirichlet
    • Dirichlet: Legendre used the proposition as a lemma (reciprocity law) and tried to prove it, but his proof rests on a theorem established only by induction; Dirichlet could not complete it and used other means. Dirichlet cites Legendre, Theorie des Nombres, 4th part, section IX. Dirichlet, Werke I, pp. 313-316 (Abh. Berlin 1837) and p. 357 (Crelle 18, 1838)
  3. 1838

    Dirichlet's Crelle note announces the series method for primes in progressions

    first application

    Dirichlet, Sur l'usage des series infinies dans la theorie des nombres, J. reine angew. Math. 18 (1838) 259-274 (preliminary note; memoir read to the Berlin Academy 27 July 1837, printed in the Abhandlungen volume "von 1837")

    A preliminary note announces that the theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions has been proved with a class of infinite series and applies the quadratic character (Legendre symbol) series to the class number of binary quadratic forms.

    Evidence (4)
    • Dirichlet proved in 1837 that any such progression has infinitely many primes MacTutor, Lejeune Dirichlet
    • The proof is modelled on Euler's work and the theorem represents the beginning of rigorous analytic number theory Wikipedia, Dirichlet's theorem
    • Crossref: Sur l'usage des séries infinies dans la théorie des nombres, Crelle 18, 1838 Crossref record 10.1515/crll.1838.18.259
    • Preliminary note announcing the progression theorem, the series method and its application to quadratic forms; memoir "sous presse". Dirichlet, Werke I, no. XXIV (Crelle 18, 259-274), archive.org
  4. 1839

    Dirichlet's memoir proving the theorem with characters and series

    key development

    Dirichlet, Beweis des Satzes, dass jede unbegrenzte arithmetische Progression, deren erstes Glied und Differenz ganze Zahlen ohne gemeinschaftlichen Factor sind, unendlich viele Primzahlen enthaelt, Abh. Koenigl. Preuss. Akad. Wiss. Berlin, Jahrgang 1837 (printed 1839), 45-81 (read 27 July 1837)

    Dirichlet proves the theorem by series over the (p-1) characters of the residue classes, built from indices modulo a primitive root, and a limit argument at s = 1.

    Evidence (2)
    • Memoir read 27 July 1837; first part: indices modulo a primitive root, series L over integers prime to p, Euler product over primes. Dirichlet, Werke I, no. XXI (Abh. Berlin 1837, 45-81), archive.org
    • Abh. Berlin vol. 48, pp. 45-71 printed 1839 per Wikipedia (Werke gives pp. 45-81). Wikipedia, Dirichlet's theorem on arithmetic progressions
  5. 1839

    Dirichlet's class number applications

    key development

    Dirichlet, Recherches sur diverses applications de l'analyse infinitésimale à la théorie des nombres, Crelle 19 (1839) and 21 (1840)

    Dirichlet extends the analytic method to quadratic forms and class numbers.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref: Recherches sur diverses applications, Crelle 19, 1839 (continued in vol. 21, 1840) Crossref record 10.1515/crll.1839.19.324
  6. 1863

    Dirichlet's Vorlesungen über Zahlentheorie

    consolidation

    Dirichlet (ed. Dedekind), Vorlesungen über Zahlentheorie, 1863

    Dedekind's edition of Dirichlet's lectures includes the proof of the progression theorem within a systematic course.

    Evidence (3)
    • Catalogue entry, Vorlesungen über Zahlentheorie, 1863 Internet Archive catalogue record vorlesungenberz03dedegoog
    • Crossref: chapter 'Beweis des Satzes, dass jede unbegrenzte arithmetische Progression ... unendlich viele Primzahlen enthält' in the Vorlesungen reprint Crossref record 10.1017/cbo9781139237321.012
    • Open Library lists a 1863 edition of Dirichlet, Vorlesungen ueber Zahlentheorie. Open Library search
