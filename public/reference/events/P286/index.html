1955 · Category & homological algebra

Abelian categories and derived functors (Tohoku)

A common axiomatic framework unified derived functors for modules and sheaves.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1955

    Buchsbaum's exact categories

    first statement

    Buchsbaum, Exact categories and duality, Transactions of the AMS, 1955

    Buchsbaum introduces exact categories, an axiomatic setting for homological duality that Wikipedia says is the earliest form of the abelian category concept.

    Evidence (2)
    • Article record, Trans. AMS 1955 Crossref
    • Wikipedia Abelian category: Buchsbaum introduced the concept as exact category; Mac Lane's attribution to Grothendieck is disputed Wikipedia, Abelian category
  2. 1956

    Cartan and Eilenberg's Homological Algebra

    consolidation

    Cartan and Eilenberg, Homological Algebra, Princeton University Press, 1956

    The first systematic monograph on homological algebra and derived functors, the textbook base for the Tohoku framework.

    Evidence (1)
    • OpenLibrary record: Princeton University Press 1956 OpenLibrary search
  3. 1957

    Grothendieck's abelian categories

    naming

    Grothendieck, Sur quelques points d'algèbre homologique I, Tohoku Mathematical Journal, 1957

    Grothendieck gives the axioms of abelian categories and the name, the naming step for the concept.

    Dating or attribution note

    Naming credit as stated in Wikipedia; the exact naming wording in Part I was not checked.

    Evidence (2)
    • Article record, Tohoku Math. J. 1957 (Part I) Crossref
    • Wikipedia: Mac Lane says Grothendieck defined abelian categories in 1957 Wikipedia, Abelian category
  4. 1957

    Derived functors and sheaves in abelian categories

    key development

    Grothendieck, Sur quelques points d'algèbre homologique II, Tohoku Mathematical Journal, 1957

    Part II develops the framework for derived functors and sheaves of modules, making the abelian setting usable for sheaf cohomology.

    Dating or attribution note

    Content of Part II summarised from MacTutor; not verified in text.

    Evidence (2)
    • Article record, Tohoku Math. J. 1957 (Part II) Crossref
    • MacTutor: the Tohoku paper concerns abelian categories, sheaves of modules, resolutions, derived functors MacTutor, Alexander Grothendieck biography
