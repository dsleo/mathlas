1854 · Group & representation theory

The abstract group concept

Cayley, Kronecker, Weber and von Dyck gradually axiomatised groups independently of permutations, ending with the full modern definition.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1846

    Galois' groups printed (written 1832) and Cauchy's conjugate systems

    precursor

    Galois, memoir on solvability by radicals, printed by Liouville 1846; Cauchy, memoirs on substitutions, 1845

    Both used groups of permutations defined by closure alone; Galois wrote his definition in 1832 and Liouville printed it in 1846, while Cauchy gave a similar notion in 1845.

    Evidence (1)
    • MacTutor's article on the abstract group concept narrates Galois' 1832 definition printed in 1846 and Cauchy's 1845 definition MacTutor, The abstract group concept
  2. 1854

    Cayley's abstract definition

    first statement

    Cayley, On the theory of groups, as depending on the symbolic equation θⁿ = 1, Philosophical Magazine 7, 40-47, 1854

    The first attempt at a definition of a group by its law of composition rather than by permutations, though MacTutor judges it muddled.

    Evidence (3)
    • Crossref record of the 1854 paper, Phil. Mag. 7, 40-47 Crossref 10.1080/14786445408647421
    • MacTutor: Cayley was the first to try an abstract definition, in 1854 MacTutor, The abstract group concept
    • Wikipedia agrees that the abstract notion of a finite group first appears in Cayley 1854 English Wikipedia, History of group theory
  3. 1870

    Kronecker's group in number theory

    key development

    Kronecker, Auseinandersetzung einiger Eigenschaften der Klassenanzahl idealer complexer Zahlen, Monatsberichte der Berliner Akademie, 1870

    Kronecker defined a finite commutative system closed under a product with cancellation in the setting of ideal class groups, unconnected to permutation groups.

    Evidence (2)
    • zbMATH lists the 1870 Berlin Monatsberichte paper zbMATH 2721142
    • MacTutor describes the 1870 definition in the class-group context as a separate development MacTutor, The abstract group concept
  4. 1882

    Weber and von Dyck, Math. Annalen 1882

    key development

    H. Weber, Beweis des Satzes, dass jede eigentlich primitive quadratische Form unendlich viele Primzahlen darzustellen faehig ist, Math. Ann. 20 (1882) 301-329; W. von Dyck, Gruppentheoretische Studien, Math. Ann. 20 (1882) 1-44

    MacTutor describes Weber's 1882 definition as a finite semigroup with cancellation, and Wikipedia credits von Dyck's 1882 paper with the full modern definition; the Crossref record for Weber's paper concerns primes in quadratic forms, so neither claim is checked against the text.

    Dating or attribution note

    Fix pass 2: the group-definition claims are from MacTutor and Wikipedia; the papers were not read.

    Evidence (4)
    • Crossref record of von Dyck 1882, Math. Ann. 20, 1-44 Crossref 10.1007/bf01443322
    • MacTutor describes Weber's 1882 definition as a semigroup with cancellation MacTutor, The abstract group concept
    • Wikipedia says von Dyck in 1882 first defined a group in the full modern sense English Wikipedia, History of group theory
    • Crossref: Weber, Math. Ann. 20 (1882), pp. 301-329, on primes represented by primitive quadratic forms. Crossref 10.1007/bf01443599
  5. 1895

    Weber's Lehrbuch der Algebra

    consolidation

    Weber, Lehrbuch der Algebra, vol. 1, Vieweg, Braunschweig, 1895

    Weber's textbook noted that the finite definition fails for infinite groups and added inverses explicitly, making a group a set with an associative product and inverses.

    Evidence (2)
    • zbMATH lists Weber, Lehrbuch der Algebra, vol. 1, 1895 zbMATH 2678113
    • MacTutor says Weber noticed in this book that infinite groups need inverses to be postulated MacTutor, The abstract group concept
  6. 1897

    Burnside's Theory of Groups of Finite Order

    consolidation

    Burnside, Theory of Groups of Finite Order, Cambridge University Press, 1897

    The first English-language textbook to define groups abstractly, following Cayley's closure, associativity and inverses, and treating infinite groups too.

    Evidence (2)
    • Internet Archive holds the 1897 first edition archive.org theoryofgroupsof0000wbur
    • MacTutor describes Burnside's definition and its lack of an identity axiom MacTutor, The abstract group concept
  7. 1902

    Huntington's simplified definition

    key development

    Huntington, Simplified definition of a group, Bulletin of the American Mathematical Society 8, 296-300, 1902

    A short paper reducing the group axioms to a minimal independent set, near the end of the story.

    Dating or attribution note

    Role judged from title; content not read. Dickson's 1902 independent-postulates paper was not searched.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref record of Huntington 1902, Bull. AMS 8, 296-300 Crossref 10.1090/s0002-9904-1902-00898-7
