1670 · Number theory

Fermat's number theory (little theorem, sums of squares, Fermat correspondence)

Fermat's correspondence introduced the little theorem, four-square statement and other results.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1621

    Bachet's Diophantus edition

    precursor

    Bachet, Diophanti Alexandrini Arithmeticorum libri sex, 1621

    Fermat annotated a copy of this edition, the source of his observations.

    Evidence (2)
    • Record of the 1621 Bachet edition. Internet Archive record: Diophanti Arithmeticorum libri sex, 1621
    • Fermat's marginal notes relate to Bachet's edition of Diophantus. MacTutor biography of Fermat
  2. 1640

    Fermat's letters to Frenicle: little theorem

    precursor

    Fermat to Frenicle, 1640 (private correspondence)

    Fermat stated the theorem in private letters to Frenicle.

    Evidence (2)
    • Article reconstructing the Frenicle-Fermat correspondence of 1640. Crossref record: Fletcher, Historia Mathematica 1991
    • Lists the Frenicle-Fermat 1640 correspondence in its references. MacTutor biography of Fermat
  3. 1670

    Samuel Fermat publishes Diophantus with Fermat's notes

    first statement

    Bachet-Fermat, Diophanti Alexandrini Arithmeticorum libri sex ... cum observationibus P. de Fermat, Toulouse, 1670

    Fermat's marginal observations, including the Last Theorem statement, became public through his son's 1670 edition.

    Dating or attribution note

    Whether the little theorem itself is printed there is not checked.

    Evidence (2)
    • Record of the 1670 edition with Fermat's observations. Internet Archive record: Diophanti Arithmeticorum libri sex, 1670
    • Marginal notes became known when Samuel published them in 1670. MacTutor biography of Fermat
  4. 1679

    Varia opera mathematica

    consolidation

    Fermat, Varia opera mathematica, 1679

    First collected edition of Fermat's mathematical writings.

    Dating or attribution note

    Number-theory content of the volume not checked.

    Evidence (1)
    • Record of the 1679 Varia opera. Internet Archive record: Varia opera mathematica, 1679
  5. 1741

    Euler's first proof of Fermat's little theorem

    key development

    Euler, Theorematum quorundam ad numeros primos spectantium demonstratio, Commentarii Petropolitanae 8 (1741; written 1736)

    Euler gave the first proof of the little theorem, among his work that established Fermat's statements as theorems.

    Evidence (2)
    • Euler Archive E54: written 1736, published 1741, first proof of the little theorem. Euler Archive (Pacific Scholarly Commons)
    • Crossref lists a 2020 Spectrum record of E54. Crossref record: Euler E-54
