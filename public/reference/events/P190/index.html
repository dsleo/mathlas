Euler: complex logarithms and exponentials

Trigonometric and exponential functions were linked through complex arguments and logarithms of negatives.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1704

    Johann Bernoulli on inverse sine and logarithm (Paris Academy, Memoires for 1702)

    precursor

    Johann Bernoulli, Solution d'un probleme concernant le calcul integral, Mem. Acad. Roy. Sci. Paris, annee 1702 (Paris, 1704), pp. 289-297

    Bernoulli's reduction of an integral of 1/(1+x^2) to logarithms of imaginary quantities, linked to the inverse tangent, preceded Euler's treatment.

    Evidence (3)
    • MacTutor: Johann Bernoulli found the relation between the inverse sine and the logarithm in 1702. MacTutor, Trigonometric functions
    • Wikipedia cites Bernoulli (1702), 'Solution d'un probleme concernant le calcul integral', Mem. Acad. Roy. Sci. Paris 1702, 289-297: 1/(1+x^2) = (1/2)(1/(1-ix)+1/(1+ix)), logarithm not evaluated. English Wikipedia, Euler's formula
    • Dealer's record of the 1704 Paris (Boudot) offprint of the paper, pp. 289-297, described as the first printing. Lynge & Son rare-book listing
  2. 1712–1713

    Leibniz and Bernoulli correspond on the logarithms of negative numbers

    precursor

    Leibniz-Bernoulli letters, 16 March 1712 to 29 July 1713 (twelve letters); printed 1745 in the Commercium philosophicum et mathematicum

    Bernoulli argued log(-x) = log(x) (integrating dx/x = d(-x)/(-x)); Leibniz denied that negative numbers have logarithms; the quarrel set the problem Euler later settled.

    Evidence (2)
    • Twelve letters from 16 March 1712 to 29 July 1713; Bernoulli: log(x) = log(-x) by integration; Leibniz: not valid for negative arguments; correspondence published 1745. D. Bal, Leibniz, Bernoulli and the logarithms of negative numbers (summary of Cajori 1913 and Euler E168)
    • Both studied the logarithm of a negative number by 1712 and held opposing views; Bernoulli: ln(-x) = ln x by integrating dx/x; Leibniz: valid only for positive x. ProofWiki, Complex natural logarithm, historical note (after Stewart and Tall)
  3. 1714

    Cotes's Logometria

    precursor

    Cotes, Logometria, Phil. Trans. 29 (338) (1714) 5-45

    Cotes already had the logarithm of cos x + i sin x in geometric form.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref: Logometria, Phil. Trans. 29, 1714. Crossref DOI 10.1098/rstl.1714.0002
    • Text of Logometria (Philosophical Transactions 29, 1714) read in a scan: the quadrant arc as measure of the ratio between EX + XC sqrt(-1) and CE, times sqrt(-1). Internet Archive scan of Phil. Trans. 29, Logometria
  4. 1728

    Euler, aged about twenty, corresponds with Bernoulli on log(-1)

    precursor

    Euler-Bernoulli letters on the logarithms of negative numbers (c. 1727-1729)

    In letters to his teacher Euler set out arguments for and against Bernoulli's view that log(n) = log(-n); Bernoulli did not change his mind.

    Evidence (2)
    • When Euler was twenty he corresponded with his teacher Bernoulli on the subject; Bernoulli still held log(n) = log(-n). D. Bal, Leibniz, Bernoulli and the logarithms of negative numbers
    • Wikipedia: Euler's correspondence with Bernoulli shows Bernoulli did not fully understand complex logarithms. English Wikipedia, Euler's formula
  5. 1747

    Euler writes his memoir on the logarithms of negative and imaginary numbers (unpublished until 1862)

    precursor

    Euler, Sur les logarithmes des nombres negatifs et imaginaires, written 1747, Opera postuma 1 (1862) 269-281, E807

    An unpublished memoir of 1747 that proves, from i*v = log(cos v + i sin v), that every number has infinitely many logarithms.

    Evidence (2)
    • Euler Archive E807: Sur les logarithmes des nombres negatifs et imaginaires, Opera postuma 1, pp. 269-281; published 1862, written 1747. Euler Archive E807
    • The 1747 paper, unpublished until 1862, proves the same theorem from the formula i*v = log(cos v + i sin v); Cajori judged it more convincing than the 1751 paper. D. Bal, Leibniz, Bernoulli and the logarithms of negative numbers
  6. 1748

    Euler publishes the exponential-trigonometric relation

    first statement

    Euler, Introductio in analysin infinitorum, vol. 1, 1748, E101

    Euler's first book on analysis prints exp(ix) = cos x + i sin x and the exponential as the base of the elementary functions.

    Evidence (2)
    • Euler Archive E101: published 1748. Euler Archive
    • Wikipedia, Euler's formula: first published in 1748 in the Introductio. English Wikipedia, Euler's formula
  7. 1751

    Euler on the logarithms of negative and imaginary numbers

    key development

    Euler, De la controverse entre Mrs. Leibnitz et Bernoulli..., Mem. Acad. Berlin 5, pp. 139-179, 1751 (written 1747), E168

    The paper treats the dispute over the logarithm of negative numbers; Euler held that logarithms are multi-valued.

    Evidence (3)
    • Euler Archive E168: published 1751, written 1747. Euler Archive
    • Wikipedia, Euler's formula: Euler suggested complex logarithms have infinitely many values. English Wikipedia, Euler's formula
    • The paper proves that each number has an infinity of logarithms and that logarithms of negative numbers are imaginary (summary of the English translation). D. Bal, Leibniz, Bernoulli and the logarithms of negative numbers
