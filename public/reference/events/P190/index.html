1748 · Complex analysis

Euler: complex logarithms and exponentials

Trigonometric and exponential functions were linked through complex arguments and logarithms of negatives.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1702

    Johann Bernoulli on inverse sine and logarithm

    precursor

    Bernoulli's 1702 relation between inverse sine and logarithm preceded Euler's treatment.

    Evidence (1)
    • MacTutor: Bernoulli found the relation between inverse sine and logarithm in 1702. MacTutor, Trigonometric functions
  2. 1714

    Cotes's Logometria

    precursor

    Cotes, Logometria, Phil. Trans. 29, pp. 5-45, 1714

    Cotes already had the logarithm of cos x + i sin x in geometric form.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref: Logometria, Phil. Trans. 29, 1714. Crossref DOI 10.1098/rstl.1714.0002
  3. 1748

    Euler publishes the exponential-trigonometric relation

    first statement

    Euler, Introductio in analysin infinitorum, vol. 1, 1748, E101

    Euler's first book on analysis prints exp(ix) = cos x + i sin x and the exponential as the base of the elementary functions.

    Evidence (2)
    • Euler Archive E101: published 1748. Euler Archive
    • Wikipedia, Euler's formula: first published in 1748 in the Introductio. English Wikipedia, Euler's formula
  4. 1751

    Euler on the logarithms of negative and imaginary numbers

    key development

    Euler, De la controverse entre Mrs. Leibnitz et Bernoulli..., Mem. Acad. Berlin 5, pp. 139-179, 1751 (written 1747), E168

    The paper treats the dispute over the logarithm of negative numbers; Euler held that logarithms are multi-valued.

    Evidence (2)
    • Euler Archive E168: published 1751, written 1747. Euler Archive
    • Wikipedia, Euler's formula: Euler suggested complex logarithms have infinitely many values. English Wikipedia, Euler's formula
