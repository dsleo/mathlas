1715 · Analysis

Taylor: finite differences and Taylor's theorem

A treatise created the calculus of finite differences and gave the series later called Taylor's.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1671

    Gregory's earlier series result

    precursor

    Gregory had a version of the expansion in 1671, as Wikipedia puts it, an earlier version mentioned in his work.

    Evidence (1)
    • Wikipedia, Taylor's theorem: named after Taylor who stated a version in 1715, though an earlier version was mentioned by James Gregory in 1671. English Wikipedia, Taylor's theorem
  2. 1712

    Taylor's letter to Machin

    precursor

    In a letter of 26 July 1712 Taylor first mentioned a version of the theorem, prompted by a remark of Machin about Newton's series and Halley's root method.

    Evidence (1)
    • MacTutor Taylor biography gives the letter date and the coffee-house origin of the idea. MacTutor, Brook Taylor
  3. 1715

    Methodus incrementorum directa et inversa

    first statement

    Taylor, Methodus incrementorum directa et inversa, London 1715 (2nd ed. 1717)

    The book founded the calculus of finite differences and contains two forms of what is now called Taylor's series.

    Evidence (2)
    • Internet Archive holds the 1715 edition. Internet Archive
    • MacTutor: the 1715 book contains the Taylor series, and gave a new branch now called the calculus of finite differences. MacTutor, Brook Taylor
  4. 1730

    Stirling's Methodus differentialis

    key development

    Stirling, Methodus differentialis, sive tractatus de summatione et interpolatione serierum infinitarum, London 1730

    A treatise on summation and interpolation of series by differences, the next major book in the same tradition.

    Dating or attribution note

    Relevance to Taylor's calculus rests on the title only; content not read. A 1717 Stirling commentary on Newton's differential method also exists on Internet Archive.

    Evidence (1)
    • Internet Archive holds the 1730 London edition. Internet Archive
  5. 1785

    Name 'Taylor series' in use

    naming

    MacTutor says the series only came to be called Taylor's in 1785.

    Evidence (1)
    • MacTutor: the series is the Taylor series, though it only became known as this in 1785. MacTutor, Brook Taylor
