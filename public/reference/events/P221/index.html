Computational complexity

Resource-bounded computation was classified into hierarchies.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1962

    Real-time computation and time-bounded classes

    precursor

    Yamada, Real-Time Computation and Recursive Functions Not Real-Time Computable, IEEE Transactions on Electronic Computers EC-11 (1962) 753-760

    An earlier study of Turing-machine computation under a time bound, listed by Wikipedia among the papers before Hartmanis and Stearns.

    Dating or attribution note

    Role from the Wikipedia survey; the paper was not read.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref: IEEE Trans. Electronic Computers EC-11 (1962) 753-760 Crossref DOI 10.1109/tec.1962.5219459
    • Earlier papers on Turing machines with bounded resources include Myhill 1960, Smullyan 1961 and Yamada 1962 Wikipedia, Computational complexity theory
  2. 1964

    Hartmanis and Stearns, computational complexity of recursive sequences

    first statement

    Hartmanis, Stearns, Computational complexity of recursive sequences, Proc. 5th Annual Symposium on Switching Circuit Theory and Logical Design (Nov. 1964) 82-90

    The conference paper that first classifies sequences by the time needed to compute them on Turing machines; the 1965 journal paper is its full version.

    Dating or attribution note

    Earlier than the 1965 Transactions paper that is usually cited. That the conference paper contains the time-bounded classes is inferred from its title and from Stearns's retrospective; it was not read.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref: 1964 Proceedings of the Fifth Annual Symposium on Switching Circuit Theory and Logical Design, pp. 82-90 (Nov 1964) Crossref DOI 10.1109/swct.1964.6
    • Stearns, Juris Hartmanis: the beginnings of computational complexity Crossref DOI 10.1007/978-1-4612-4478-3_2
  3. 1964

    'Computational complexity' in a title

    naming

    Hartmanis, Stearns (1964) (as above)

    The field's name appears in the title of the 1964 Hartmanis-Stearns conference paper.

    Evidence (1)
    • Title 'Computational complexity of recursive sequences' (spelled 'sequnces' in the Crossref record), 1964 Crossref DOI 10.1109/swct.1964.6
  4. 1965

    Hartmanis-Stearns, On the computational complexity of algorithms

    key development

    Hartmanis, Stearns, On the computational complexity of algorithms, Trans. AMS 117 (1965) 285-306

    The journal paper that defines time and space complexity and proves the hierarchy theorems; Wikipedia and the 1993 Turing Award citation treat it as the foundation of the field.

    Evidence (2)
    • Hartmanis-Stearns 1965, Trans. AMS 117, DOI 10.1090/s0002-9947-1965-0170805-7 Crossref
    • The beginning of systematic studies in computational complexity is attributed to this 1965 paper; Hartmanis and Stearns received the 1993 Turing Award for it Wikipedia, Computational complexity theory; Juris Hartmanis
  5. 1965

    Cobham's polynomial-time thesis

    key development

    Cobham, The intrinsic computational difficulty of functions, Proc. 1964 Logic, Methodology and Philosophy of Science congress (1965)

    Cobham argues that feasible computation corresponds to polynomial-time algorithms.

    Evidence (3)
    • Cobham's thesis that feasibility corresponds to polynomial time Wikipedia: Computational complexity theory
    • Review of Cobham's paper, JSL 1970, DOI 10.2307/2270886 Crossref
    • Cobham's thesis: a problem is feasibly solvable if and only if it has a polynomial-time algorithm (the Cobham-Edmonds thesis) Wikipedia: Computational complexity theory
  6. 1967

    Axiomatic complexity measures

    key development

    Blum, A machine-independent theory of the complexity of recursive functions, J. ACM 14 (1967)

    Blum gives a machine-independent axiomatic framework for computational complexity measures.

    Evidence (1)
    • Blum 1967, J. ACM, DOI 10.1145/321386.321395 Crossref
  7. 1971

    NP-completeness of theorem proving

    key development

    Cook, The complexity of theorem-proving procedures, STOC 1971

    Cook shows that satisfiability is NP-complete, the central result that made complexity classes matter.

    Evidence (1)
    • Cook 1971, STOC, DOI 10.1145/800157.805047 Crossref
  8. 1972

    Reducibility among combinatorial problems

    key development

    Karp, Reducibility among combinatorial problems, in Complexity of Computer Computations (1972)

    Karp shows that many combinatorial problems are NP-complete via polynomial-time reductions from Cook's result.

    Evidence (1)
    • Karp 1972, DOI 10.1007/978-1-4684-2001-2_9 Crossref
