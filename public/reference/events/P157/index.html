1732 · Differential geometry

Euler: geodesics from mechanics

Euler derived the geodesic equation for motion on a surface.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1732

    Euler on the shortest line on a surface

    first statement

    Euler, De linea brevissima in superficie quacunque duo quaelibet puncta jungente, Comm. Acad. Sci. Petropol. 3, pp. 110-124 (1732; written 1730), E9

    Prompted by an exercise from Johann Bernoulli, Euler found the shortest line between two points on a surface analytically.

    Evidence (1)
    • Euler Archive E9: published 1732, written 1730; summary says it arose from a task set by Bernoulli and gave early analytic foundations of the calculus of variations. Euler Archive
  2. 1733

    Clairaut on geodesics of surfaces of revolution

    key development

    Clairaut published on the geodesics of quadrics of revolution, a topic Johann Bernoulli had also worked on.

    Evidence (1)
    • MacTutor Clairaut: in 1733 he published on geodesics of quadrics of rotation. MacTutor, Clairaut
  3. 1736

    Mechanica vol. 2: geodesic equations from motion on a surface

    first application

    Euler, Mechanica sive motus scientia analytice exposita, vol. 2, St Petersburg 1736, E16

    Euler treated a point mass constrained to a surface and derived differential equations of geodesics from the mechanics, showing the free path is locally shortest.

    Evidence (1)
    • Euler Archive E16: published 1736; summary says he derives differential equations of geodesics for free motion on a surface. Euler Archive
  4. 1744

    Methodus inveniendi treats geodesics in general

    key development

    Euler, Methodus inveniendi lineas curvas..., 1744, E65

    One of the worked problems of the general variational method is finding the geodesic on a given surface.

    Evidence (1)
    • Euler Archive E65 summary lists the geodesic joining two points on a given surface among the problems. Euler Archive
  5. 1744

    Euler's Methodus inveniendi lineas curvas

    consolidation

    Leonhard Euler, Methodus inveniendi lineas curvas maximi minimive proprietate gaudentes, 1744

    Euler's systematic treatment of curves with a maximum or minimum property, which covers the geodesic problem on surfaces.

    Evidence (2)
    • Open Library record of Euler's 1744 Methodus. Open Library work record
    • Euler first elaborated the calculus of variations, starting in 1733. Wikipedia, Calculus of variations
