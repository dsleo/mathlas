Lie theory (continuous transformation groups)

Lie combined contact transformations with integration theory of PDE to build continuous transformation groups and infinitesimal generators.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1873–1874

    Winter 1873-74: Lie starts the theory of continuous groups

    precursor

    Lie turned from PDEs to systematic work on continuous transformation groups and infinitesimal generators.

    Dating or attribution note

    Dating rests on Lie himself and on MacTutor/Hawkins. Read in Lie's preface to Lie-Engel vol. 1 (1888): he worked on first-order PDEs in 1872-73, was led to infinitesimal contact transformations, and says the discoveries "in den Jahren 1873 und 1874" were the foundation of the theory of finite continuous transformation groups; the 1880 Math. Ann. paper likewise says he has worked on it eagerly since 1873. Private development, so a precursor only.

    Evidence (4)
    • Lie began developing the theory of continuous transformation groups in winter 1873-74. MacTutor, Lie biography
    • Lie considered that winter the birth of the theory; Hawkins suggests a longer period. Wikipedia, Lie group
    • Lie's preface (Vorrede) to Theorie der Transformationsgruppen I: PDE work 1872-73 led to infinitesimal transformations; the discoveries of 1873 and 1874 founded the theory of finite continuous transformation groups. Lie and Engel, Theorie der Transformationsgruppen I (Teubner 1888), Vorrede pp. v-vi
    • Lie: the investigations that he calls "Theorie der Transformationsgruppen" occupy him eagerly since 1873. Lie, Theorie der Transformationsgruppen I, Math. Ann. 16 (1880), opening paragraph
  2. 1874

    Lie, Ueber Gruppen von Transformationen (Goettingen note)

    first statement

    S. Lie, Ueber Gruppen von Transformationen, Nachrichten von der Koenigl. Gesellschaft der Wissenschaften zu Goettingen 1874, 529-542

    Lie publishes a first short note on groups of transformations, the earliest printed statement of his continuous-group theory.

    Evidence (5)
    • Lie, Ueber Gruppen von Transformationen, Goett. Nachr. 1874, 529-542 (1874); EuDML 179698. zbMATH Open, Zbl/JFM 06.0093.01
    • Lie dated the birth of the theory to winter 1873-74; his papers from the 1870s, except the very first note, appeared in Norwegian journals. Wikipedia, Lie group
    • EuDML record 179698 confirms: Lie, Ueber Gruppen von Transformationen, Nachrichten Koenigl. Ges. Wiss. Goettingen 1874, 529-542. EuDML
    • Lie-Engel vol. 1 introduction cites "Lie, Goettinger Nachrichten 1874, 3. Decbr." for the notion of a group of transformations. Lie and Engel, Theorie der Transformationsgruppen I (1888), Einleitung p. 3 footnote
    • Lie's 1880 paper cites "Goettinger Nachrichten Nr. 22. 1874" and the Christiania archive for his earlier work. Lie, Math. Ann. 16 (1880), footnote to the opening paragraph
  3. 1876

    Lie's Theorie der Transformationsgruppen in the Christiania archive

    key development

    S. Lie, Theorie der Transformations-Gruppen I, II, Archiv for Mathematik og Naturvidenskab 1 (1876) 19-58, 152-202

    Lie gives a first full account of transformation groups and their infinitesimal generators in a Norwegian journal.

    Evidence (3)
    • Cited with pages 19-57 and 152-193, 1876. Wikipedia, Lie group (reference list)
    • zbMATH lists Theorie der Transformations-Gruppen I, II, Arch. f. Math. og Nat. 1, 19-58, 152-202 (1876). zbMATH Open, JFM 08.0212.01
    • Lie-Engel vol. 1 cites "Lie, Archiv for Mathematik og Naturv. Bd. 1, Christiania 1876" for the basic theorem on the solvability of the composition equations of an r-parameter group. Lie and Engel, Theorie der Transformationsgruppen I (1888), Kapitel 1
  4. 1880

    Theorie der Transformationsgruppen I in Math. Annalen

    key development

    S. Lie, Theorie der Transformationsgruppen I, Math. Ann. 16, 1880

    A major German-language account that brought the theory to a wide readership.

    Dating or attribution note

    Read the opening of the paper (archive.org scan of Math. Ann. 16): Lie announces a new theory "die ich die Theorie der Transformationsgruppen nenne", to be developed in a series of papers; this first one has two sections, determining all transformation groups of a one-dimensional and of a two-dimensional manifold. The rest of the memoir was not read.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref: Theorie der Transformationsgruppen I, Math. Ann. 16 (1880) 441-528. Crossref
    • Lie announces a new theory he calls the Theorie der Transformationsgruppen; this first paper determines all groups of a one- and a two-dimensional manifold. Lie, Theorie der Transformationsgruppen I, Math. Ann. 16 (1880), opening
  5. 1888

    Killing's classification work on finite continuous groups

    key development

    W. Killing, Die Zusammensetzung der stetigen endlichen Transformationsgruppen, Math. Ann. 31 (1888) 252-290; parts II-IV in vols 33 (1889), 34 (1889), 36 (1890)

    Killing began the structural classification of the algebras of continuous groups, later completed by Cartan.

    Evidence (5)
    • Crossref: Killing, Die Zusammensetzung der stetigen endlichen Transformations-gruppen, Math. Ann. 31 (1888) 252-290 (part I). Crossref record
    • zbMATH lists parts I, II: Math. Ann. 31, 252-290 (1888); 33, 1-48 (1889). zbMATH Open, JFM 20.0368.03
    • Killing studied the Lie algebras of Lie groups independently; Cartan completed the classification of semisimple algebras in 1900. MacTutor, Lie biography
    • MacTutor Killing: the classification theorems are in Die Zusammensetzung der stetigen endlichen Transformationsgruppen, four parts in Math. Annalen 1888-1890; the exceptional simple Lie algebras appear in parts III-IV; an 1884 Programmschrift (Braunsberg) already held the main ideas. MacTutor, Killing biography
    • Killing, part I, introduction: uses Lie's notation, structure constants c_iks of the brackets (Xi Xk), Jacobi relations, groups of the same composition; cites Programme 1884 and 1886. Killing, Die Zusammensetzung der stetigen endlichen Transformationsgruppen I, Math. Ann. 31 (1888) 252ff.
  6. 1888–1893

    Lie and Engel, Theorie der Transformationsgruppen (three volumes)

    consolidation

    S. Lie, F. Engel, Theorie der Transformationsgruppen, Leipzig: Teubner, 1888-93

    The first systematic treatise on continuous groups, in three volumes.

    Evidence (3)
    • Archive.org record: Leipzig, Teubner, 1888-93. Internet Archive
    • Three volumes published 1888, 1890, 1893. Wikipedia, Lie group
    • zbMATH lists the three Abschnitte: Teubner 1888, 1890, 1893. zbMATH Open, JFM 20.0368.01, 23.0364.01, 25.0623.01
  7. 1893

    'Groupes de Lie' first appears

    naming

    A. Tresse, Sur les invariants differentiels des groupes continus de transformations, 1893

    The term for Lie groups arises in French in the thesis of Lie's student Tresse.

    Evidence (2)
    • The term 'groupes de Lie' first appeared in 1893 in Tresse's thesis. Wikipedia, Lie group
    • Crossref: Tresse, Sur les invariants differentiels des groupes continus de transformations, Acta Math. 18 (1894) 1-88. Crossref record
  8. 1925

    Weyl's representation theory of semisimple groups

    key development

    H. Weyl, Theorie der Darstellung kontinuierlicher halb-einfacher Gruppen durch lineare Transformationen I, Math. Z. 23 (1925) 271-309 (II-IV in Math. Z. 24, 1926)

    Weyl turned Lie groups into a central tool for representation theory and mathematical physics.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref: Math. Z. 23 (1925). Crossref
    • MacTutor Weyl: his theory of representations of semisimple groups (1924-26) is described as very deep and as his own greatest achievement; Hurwitz and Schur introduced underlying ideas and Cartan's work on the topic is of comparable importance. MacTutor, Weyl biography
