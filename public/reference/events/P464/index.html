1935 · Topology

Fibre bundles and characteristic classes

Stiefel-Whitney, Pontryagin and Chern classes are defined on bundles and computed by curvature, giving global invariants of manifolds.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1933

    Seifert's fibred spaces

    precursor

    Seifert, 'Topologie dreidimensionaler gefaserter Raume', Acta Mathematica (1933)

    Seifert introduced the terms fibre and fibre space, but with definitions limited to a special case.

    Evidence (2)
    • Paper record: Acta Math. 1933 Crossref
    • Seifert introduced fibre and fibre space in 1933; definitions limited to a special case Wikipedia: Fiber bundle (history)
  2. 1935

    Whitney's sphere spaces (first general fibre space)

    first statement

    Whitney, 'Sphere-Spaces', Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences 21 (1935)

    Whitney gave the first general definition of a fibre space, under the name sphere space.

    Evidence (2)
    • Paper record: PNAS 1935 Crossref
    • First general definition of fibre space given by Whitney in 1935 as sphere space Wikipedia: Fiber bundle (history)
  3. 1935

    Characteristic classes for vector fields

    first application

    Stiefel, 'Richtungsfelder und Fernparallelismus in n-dimensionalen Mannigfaltigkeiten', Commentarii Mathematici Helvetici (1935)

    Stiefel introduced characteristic classes to study vector fields on manifolds, generalising the Euler characteristic.

    Evidence (3)
    • Paper record: Comment. Math. Helv. 1935 (MacTutor gives the thesis as 1935, paper 1936) Crossref
    • Characteristic classes arose in 1935 in the work of Stiefel and Whitney on vector fields Wikipedia: Characteristic class
    • Stiefel introduced characteristic classes generalising the Euler characteristic, for vector fields MacTutor: Stiefel biography
  4. 1946

    Chern's characteristic classes of Hermitian manifolds

    key development

    Chern, 'Characteristic Classes of Hermitian Manifolds', Annals of Mathematics 47 (1946)

    Chern defined characteristic classes through curvature, giving the form used in differential geometry.

    Evidence (2)
    • Paper record: Annals of Mathematics 1946 Crossref
    • Chern worked on characteristic classes in 1943-45 at Princeton; now named after him MacTutor: Chern biography
  5. 1951

    Steenrod's The Topology of Fibre Bundles

    consolidation

    Steenrod, The Topology of Fibre Bundles (Princeton University Press, 1951)

    The classic monograph on fibre bundles, which became the standard reference.

    Evidence (2)
    • Book record: Princeton 1951 Crossref
    • Steenrod's 1951 book on fibre bundles became a classic MacTutor: Steenrod biography
  6. 1974

    Milnor and Stasheff's Characteristic Classes

    consolidation

    Milnor and Stasheff, Characteristic Classes (Annals of Mathematics Studies 76, 1974)

    A systematic monograph on characteristic classes, complementing Steenrod.

    Evidence (1)
    • Book record: Annals of Math. Studies 76 (1974) Crossref
