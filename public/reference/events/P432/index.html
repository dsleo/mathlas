1799 · Algebra

Complex numbers as points of the plane and as two-dimensional real algebra

Argand located complex numbers in the plane, and Hamilton presented them as ordered pairs with rules of algebra, bridging geometric and algebraic readings of the imaginary.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1685

    Wallis anticipates a plane picture

    precursor

    John Wallis, A Treatise of Algebra, 1685

    Wallis's algebra treatise is said to have foreshadowed the idea of a complex number as a point in a plane, though not developed.

    Dating or attribution note

    Review G3: the preface of the 1685 Treatise of Algebra (archive.org scan) advertises "a Discourse of Negative Squares, and the Roots of them; on which depend (what they call Imaginary Roots of Impossible Equations)", showing "what is the true Import thereof in nature, with divers Geometrical Constructions". So Wallis does give a geometrical reading of roots of negative squares in 1685; the chapters themselves were not read, so whether he places them in a plane as Wessel and Argand do remains unchecked (Wikipedia only says he anticipated it). Precursor, not a first statement.

    Evidence (2)
    • Wikipedia notes the plane idea was anticipated in Wallis's 1685 algebra treatise and that Wessel first described it in 1799. English Wikipedia, Complex number (History)
    • Wallis's preface lists a Discourse of Negative Squares and their roots (imaginary roots), with geometrical constructions. Wallis, A Treatise of Algebra (1685), preface "to the Reader", archive.org scan
  2. 1799

    Wessel's memoir on the analytic representation of direction

    first statement

    Caspar Wessel, Om Directionens analytiske Betegning, et Forsog, anvendt fornemmelig til plane og sphæriske Polygoners Oplosning, Nye Samling af det Kongelige Danske Videnskabernes Selskabs Skrifter 5, 469-518, 1799

    A Norwegian surveyor published a geometric way to add and multiply directed line segments, equivalent to the complex plane, which went unnoticed for about a century.

    Evidence (5)
    • MacTutor says the geometric interpretation was first published by Wessel: paper submitted to the Danish Academy 10 March 1797, printed 1799, rediscovered 1895. MacTutor, Argand biography
    • Wikipedia gives Wessel 1799 for the first description of a complex number as a plane point. English Wikipedia, Complex number
    • Wessel biography confirms he invented the geometric representation before Argand. MacTutor, Caspar Wessel
    • MacTutor Wessel: presented to the Royal Danish Academy on 10 March 1797, published 1799 in its Memoires, unnoticed until 1895 (Juel); Lie republished it that year and Zeuthen's French translation appeared 1897. MacTutor, Caspar Wessel
    • Contents and opening of the memoir, Nye Samling vol. 5, pp. 469-518; volume record dated 1799. Nye Samling af det Kongelige Danske Videnskabernes Selskabs Skrifter, vol. 5 (Copenhagen), archive.org
  3. 1806

    Argand's Essai, small private printing

    precursor

    Jean-Robert Argand, Essai sur une manière de représenter les quantités imaginaires dans les constructions géométriques, Paris, 1806 (small private printing; 2nd ed. Hoüel 1874)

    Argand independently gave the rotation-by-90-degrees reading of i and the modulus, and his name became attached to the diagram.

    Evidence (3)
    • Argand produced the geometric interpretation in a memoir of 1806, possibly privately printed; copies circulated 1806-1813. MacTutor, Argand biography
    • A 1874 edition of the Essai exists, evidence for the work and title but not for the 1806 date. Internet Archive item
    • MacTutor Argand: 'no proof that it was published'; Argand privately distributed a very small number of copies 1806-1813; the memoir carried no name. MacTutor, Argand biography
  4. 1813–1814

    Argand's Essai printed in Gergonne's Annales

    key development

    J.-R. Argand, Philosophie mathematique. Essai sur une maniere de representer les quantites imaginaires, dans les constructions geometriques, Annales de mathematiques pures et appliquees 4 (1813-14) 133-147

    After Jacques Francais published an article based on Argand's ideas (Annales 4, 61-71, 1813), Argand identified himself and had a slightly modified Essai printed in the same volume, the first public printing.

    Evidence (3)
    • Numdam record: Argand, Philosophie mathematique. Essai sur une maniere de representer les quantites imaginaires, dans les constructions geometriques, Annales de mathematiques pures et appliquees 4 (1813-1814) 133-147. Numdam
    • Numdam record: J. F. Francais, Nouveaux principes de geometrie de position, et interpretation geometrique des symboles imaginaires, Annales 4 (1813-1814) 61-71. Numdam
    • MacTutor Argand: Francais published in Sept 1813 a paper based on Argand's ideas; Argand then submitted a slightly modified Essai to the Annales. MacTutor, Argand biography
  5. 1831

    Gauss shows complex integers as a lattice of points in the plane

    key development

    C. F. Gauss, Anzeige of the Theoria residuorum biquadraticorum, commentatio secunda, Goettingische gelehrte Anzeigen, 23 April 1831 (Werke II, pp. 169-178); the Commentatio itself, presented 15 April 1831, was printed in Commentationes Soc. Reg. Sci. Gottingensis recentiores 7 (1832)

    Gauss presents complex integers as a lattice of points in a plane and calls this the true metaphysics of imaginary quantities, in the announcement of his second memoir on biquadratic residues.

    Evidence (2)
    • Wikipedia says Gauss published his treatment of complex numbers as plane points in 1831, largely fixing modern notation and terminology. English Wikipedia, Complex number (History)
    • Anzeige of 23 April 1831 describes complex integers as a plane lattice of points (Werke II pp. 174-175); commentatio presented 15 April 1831, printed in vol. 7 (1832). Gauss, Werke II (Goettingen 1863), Anzeige and Theoria residuorum biquadraticorum commentatio secunda, archive.org
  6. 1833

    Hamilton presents complex numbers as ordered pairs

    precursor

    W. R. Hamilton, paper read to the Royal Irish Academy, 4 Nov 1833

    Hamilton read a paper treating complex numbers as couples of real numbers with algebraic rules, later printed.

    Evidence (1)
    • MacTutor reports the 4 November 1833 reading expressing complex numbers as algebraic couples, and the 1835 BAAS paper on algebra as the science of pure time. MacTutor, Hamilton biography
  7. 1837

    Hamilton's Theory of Conjugate Functions, or Algebraic Couples

    key development

    W. R. Hamilton, Theory of Conjugate Functions, or Algebraic Couples; with a Preliminary and Elementary Essay on Algebra as the Science of Pure Time, Trans. Royal Irish Academy 17, 1837

    The printed memoir defined complex numbers as ordered pairs of reals with sum and product rules, giving a purely algebraic foundation.

    Evidence (2)
    • The article is in Transactions of the Royal Irish Academy volume 17, whose volume record is dated 1837. Internet Archive items (volume and JSTOR article records)
    • The JSTOR article record carries the full title and volume 17. Internet Archive jstor-30078796
