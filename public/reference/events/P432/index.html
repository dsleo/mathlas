1799 · Algebra

Complex numbers as points of the plane and as two-dimensional real algebra

Argand located complex numbers in the plane, and Hamilton presented them as ordered pairs with rules of algebra, bridging geometric and algebraic readings of the imaginary.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1685

    Wallis anticipates a plane picture

    precursor

    John Wallis, A Treatise of Algebra, 1685

    Wallis's algebra treatise is said to have foreshadowed the idea of a complex number as a point in a plane, though not developed.

    Dating or attribution note

    Wikipedia statement only; Wallis text not checked.

    Evidence (1)
    • Wikipedia notes the plane idea was anticipated in Wallis's 1685 algebra treatise and that Wessel first described it in 1799. English Wikipedia, Complex number (History)
  2. 1799

    Wessel's memoir on the analytic representation of direction

    first statement

    Caspar Wessel, Om directionens analytiske betegning, Nye Samling af det Kongelige Danske Videnskabernes Selskabs Skrifter 5, 1799

    A Norwegian surveyor published a geometric way to add and multiply directed line segments, equivalent to the complex plane, which went unnoticed for about a century.

    Evidence (3)
    • MacTutor says the geometric interpretation was first published by Wessel: paper submitted to the Danish Academy 10 March 1797, printed 1799, rediscovered 1895. MacTutor, Argand biography
    • Wikipedia gives Wessel 1799 for the first description of a complex number as a plane point. English Wikipedia, Complex number
    • Wessel biography confirms he invented the geometric representation before Argand. MacTutor, Caspar Wessel
  3. 1806

    Argand's Essai on imaginary quantities in geometric constructions

    key development

    Jean-Robert Argand, Essai sur une manière de représenter les quantités imaginaires dans les constructions géométriques, Paris, 1806 (small private printing; 2nd ed. Hoüel 1874)

    Argand independently gave the rotation-by-90-degrees reading of i and the modulus, and his name became attached to the diagram.

    Evidence (2)
    • Argand produced the geometric interpretation in a memoir of 1806, possibly privately printed; copies circulated 1806-1813. MacTutor, Argand biography
    • A 1874 edition of the Essai exists, evidence for the work and title but not for the 1806 date. Internet Archive item
  4. 1831

    Gauss publishes his theory of complex numbers as plane points

    key development

    Carl Friedrich Gauss, Theoria residuorum biquadraticorum, commentatio secunda, Gottingische gelehrte Anzeigen 1831

    Gauss overcame earlier doubts, presented complex numbers as points of the plane and fixed much of the modern notation and vocabulary.

    Evidence (1)
    • Wikipedia says Gauss published his treatment of complex numbers as plane points in 1831, largely fixing modern notation and terminology. English Wikipedia, Complex number (History)
  5. 1833

    Hamilton presents complex numbers as ordered pairs

    precursor

    W. R. Hamilton, paper read to the Royal Irish Academy, 4 Nov 1833

    Hamilton read a paper treating complex numbers as couples of real numbers with algebraic rules, later printed.

    Evidence (1)
    • MacTutor reports the 4 November 1833 reading expressing complex numbers as algebraic couples, and the 1835 BAAS paper on algebra as the science of pure time. MacTutor, Hamilton biography
  6. 1837

    Hamilton's Theory of Conjugate Functions, or Algebraic Couples

    key development

    W. R. Hamilton, Theory of Conjugate Functions, or Algebraic Couples; with a Preliminary and Elementary Essay on Algebra as the Science of Pure Time, Trans. Royal Irish Academy 17, 1837

    The printed memoir defined complex numbers as ordered pairs of reals with sum and product rules, giving a purely algebraic foundation.

    Evidence (2)
    • The article is in Transactions of the Royal Irish Academy volume 17, whose volume record is dated 1837. Internet Archive items (volume and JSTOR article records)
    • The JSTOR article record carries the full title and volume 17. Internet Archive jstor-30078796
