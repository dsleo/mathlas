Islamic work on the parallel postulate

Ibn al-Haytham, Khayyam and Tusi studied quadrilaterals as tests of the parallel postulate.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 300 BCE

    Euclid's fifth postulate

    precursor

    Euclid, Elements, Book I, postulate 5

    Euclid's parallel postulate, which later commentators tried to derive from the other postulates.

    Evidence (2)
    • The fourth and fifth postulates are of a different nature from the first three. MacTutor, Euclid biography
    • History of attempted proofs from Proclus on. English Wikipedia, Parallel postulate
  2. 870–901

    Thabit ibn Qurra on the parallels postulate

    first statement

    Thabit ibn Qurra, treatise on the parallels postulate; study by Sabra, J. Warburg & Courtauld Inst. 31 (1968)

    One of the earliest Arabic attempts to prove the parallel postulate, using motion in geometry.

    Dating or attribution note

    I did not find a source that says this is the first Arabic attempt, and earlier ones are possible (Jaouiche 1986, Rosenfeld 1988 not read). Range from his lifespan 836-901.

    Evidence (1)
    • Sabra's article studies Thabit ibn Qurra's treatment of Euclid's parallels postulate. Crossref 10.2307/750634, Sabra 1968
  3. 1000–1039

    Ibn al-Haytham's attempted proof

    key development

    Ibn al-Haytham, commentary on Euclid's premises (c. 1000)

    He tried a proof by contradiction, characterising parallels as equidistant lines and using the quadrilateral later named for him and Lambert.

    Evidence (1)
    • Alhazen tried to prove the postulate by contradiction, introducing motion, and formulated the Lambert quadrilateral. English Wikipedia, Parallel postulate
  4. 1070–1100

    Khayyam: Discussion of difficulties in Euclid's postulates

    key development

    Omar Khayyam, Explanations of the Difficulties in the Postulates of Euclid (late 11th century)

    Khayyam based the parallel postulate on a different postulate and studied the quadrilateral with two right base angles, reaching results of hyperbolic and elliptic geometry without seeing it.

    Evidence (2)
    • In Commentaries on the difficult postulates of Euclid he accidentally proved properties of figures in non-Euclidean geometries. MacTutor, Khayyam biography
    • Saccheri quadrilateral first considered by Khayyam in the late 11th century. English Wikipedia, Parallel postulate
  5. 1250

    Nasir al-Din al-Tusi: Discussion which removes doubt about parallel lines

    key development

    Nasir al-Din al-Tusi, al-Risala al-shafiya 'an al-shakk fi'l-khutut al-mutawaziya (1250)

    Critiqued Khayyam's attempt and tried his own proof by contradiction on the acute and obtuse cases.

    Evidence (1)
    • Al-Tusi's text, dated 1250, critiques Khayyam's attempted proof. English Wikipedia, Parallel postulate
  6. 1594

    Arabic Euclid with al-Tusi's version printed in Rome

    key development

    Arabic edition of the Elements, Rome, 1594

    The printed edition reached European geometers and led Wallis and Saccheri to the problem.

    Dating or attribution note

    Wikipedia attributes the revision to Sadr al-Din (al-Tusi's son); not checked elsewhere.

    Evidence (1)
    • Work published in Rome in 1594 and studied by European geometers; start of Saccheri's work. English Wikipedia, Parallel postulate
  7. 1733

    Saccheri, Euclides ab omni naevo vindicatus

    key development

    Girolamo Saccheri, Euclides ab omni naevo vindicatus, Milan 1733

    Saccheri assumed the postulate false and derived consequences of non-Euclidean geometry while trying to refute it, in the same quadrilateral tradition.

    Evidence (2)
    • Euclides ab omni naevo vindicatus was published in 1733. MacTutor, Saccheri biography
    • Saccheri assumed the fifth postulate false hoping to reach a contradiction. MacTutor, Non-Euclidean geometry
