1829 · Number theory

Jacobi: four squares via elliptic (theta) functions

Jacobi derived the four-square theorem from theta functions.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1770

    Lagrange proves every integer is a sum of four squares

    precursor

    J.-L. Lagrange, 1770 (existence proof; title and venue not checked)

    Lagrange gave the first proof that every natural number is a sum of four squares, the result Jacobi's formula later refines.

    Evidence (1)
    • Article says the theorem was proved by Lagrange in 1770. English Wikipedia: Lagrange's four-square theorem
  2. 1828

    Jacobi's note 'on decomposing a number into four squares'

    precursor

    C. G. J. Jacobi, Note sur la decomposition d'un nombre donne en quatre carres, J. reine angew. Math. 3 (1828) 191

    The earliest title found that names the four-squares decomposition theme; it appears in Crelle's Journal a year before the 1829 Fundamenta nova.

    Evidence (2)
    • zbMATH record 'Note sur la decomposition d'un nombre donne en quatre carres', Jacobi, J. reine angew. Math. 3 (1828) 191 zbMATH Open API
    • Crossref/DOI record for the same 1828 article (DOI from the zbMATH links) DOI registry
  3. 1829

    Jacobi's Fundamenta nova gives the number of representations

    first statement

    C. G. J. Jacobi, Fundamenta nova theoriae functionum ellipticarum, Koenigsberg, 1829

    From his theta-function series Jacobi obtains a formula for the number of ways an odd number is a sum of four squares in terms of the sum of its divisors, and notes that Fermat's four-square theorem follows as a corollary.

    Evidence (2)
    • Scanned text of the 1829 book ends its main text with a corollary about Fermat's four-square theorem after a divisor-sum formula for odd p. Internet Archive scan, OCR page 188
    • Biography dates Fundamenta nova to 1829 and says it rests on four theta functions. MacTutor: Jacobi biography
  4. 1829

    Theta functions applied to sums of squares

    first application

    C. G. J. Jacobi, Fundamenta nova theoriae functionum ellipticarum, 1829

    The arithmetic count is derived from series expansions of the new elliptic (theta) functions, the transfer of technique from analysis to number theory.

    Dating or attribution note

    Same work as the first_statement milestone; the derivation steps were not read line by line.

    Evidence (2)
    • Same scan: the divisor-sum expression is derived in the elliptic-function text before the corollary. Internet Archive scan
    • MacTutor topic page on elliptic functions and integrals (background; does not mention the four-square result). MacTutor: Elliptic functions
  5. 1834

    Jacobi's paper on composition of numbers from four squares

    key development

    C. G. J. Jacobi, De compositione numerorum e quatuor quadratis, J. reine angew. Math. 12 (1834) 167-172

    A dedicated short paper on the same four-square count; Wikipedia dates the theorem to this year.

    Evidence (2)
    • Title, journal, volume 12 and pages 167-172 confirmed, dated 1834. Crossref record 10.1515/crll.1834.12.167
    • Article says the theorem was proved in 1834 by Jacobi. English Wikipedia: Jacobi's four-square theorem
