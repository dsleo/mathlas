1710 · Statistics & decision theory

Arbuthnot sex-ratio argument as an early significance test

Arbuthnot used probability to argue birth ratios were not chance.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1710

    Arbuthnot states a chance argument

    first statement

    J. Arbuthnot, An argument for Divine Providence, Philosophical Transactions (1710)

    The public paper frames an unusually persistent birth-sex ratio as unlikely by chance.

    Evidence (1)
    • Arbuthnot, An argument for divine providence, taken from the constant regularity observed in the births of both sexes, Phil. Trans. 27 (1710) Crossref record
  2. 1710

    Coin-toss calculation on christening data

    first application

    J. Arbuthnot, An argument for Divine Providence, Philosophical Transactions (1710)

    The chance calculation is applied to annual London christening counts.

    Evidence (1)
    • Same paper applies a chance (coin-toss) calculation to 82 years of christening data Crossref record
  3. 1925

    Fisher's significance-testing textbook

    consolidation

    R. A. Fisher, Statistical Methods for Research Workers (1925)

    The much later textbook consolidates significance testing as a method.

    Evidence (1)
    • Fisher, Statistical methods for research workers (1925), textbook of significance testing zbMATH JFM 51.0414.08
