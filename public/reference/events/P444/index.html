1799 · Algebra

Fundamental theorem of algebra: analytic and topological proofs

d'Alembert and Argand proved existence of roots using minima of the modulus, while Gauss's first and third proofs are described as topological, importing non-algebraic methods into algebra.

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1629

    Girard's assertion that degree n gives n roots

    precursor

    Girard, L'invention nouvelle en l'Algèbre, 1629

    Girard asserts that a polynomial of degree n has n roots, not requiring them to be real.

    Evidence (1)
    • Girard asserted in 1629 that an equation of degree n has n solutions, not necessarily real Wikipedia, Fundamental theorem of algebra
  2. 1746

    D'Alembert's attempted proof

    precursor

    d'Alembert, 1746

    D'Alembert tries to prove the theorem by an analytic argument; the proof is incomplete.

    Evidence (1)
    • A first attempt at proving the theorem was made by d'Alembert in 1746, but was incomplete Wikipedia, Fundamental theorem of algebra
  3. 1799

    Gauss presents a geometric proof approach

    first statement

    Gauss, Demonstratio nova theorematis ... , Helmstedt, 1799

    Gauss presents a geometric argument for the fundamental theorem of algebra in his dissertation; the original argument has a topological gap.

    Evidence (3)
    • Gauss published a proof in 1799, mainly geometric with a topological gap filled by Ostrowski in 1920 Wikipedia, Fundamental theorem of algebra
    • Crossref record of the title in the Werke reprint, confirming the work's identity Crossref record 10.1017/cbo9781139058247.001
    • Gauss dissertation published in 1799 Humboldt University digital dissertation archive
  4. 1806–1813

    Argand's first rigorous proof

    first application

    Argand, 1806 memoir, revisited in Annales de mathématiques, 1813

    Argand gives a proof that stands without assuming roots and states the theorem for complex coefficients.

    Evidence (2)
    • The first rigorous proof was published by Argand in 1806 and revisited in 1813, the first for complex coefficients Wikipedia, Fundamental theorem of algebra
    • Argand's 1806 memoir may have been privately distributed with no proof of publication; republished in Gergonne's journal in 1813 MacTutor, Jean-Robert Argand
  5. 1821

    Cauchy's Cours d'analyse includes the proof

    consolidation

    Cauchy, Cours d'analyse de l'École Royale Polytechnique, 1821

    The first textbook containing a proof of the theorem, essentially Argand's, uncredited.

    Evidence (2)
    • The first textbook containing a proof was Cauchy's Cours d'analyse (1821), containing Argand's proof Wikipedia, Fundamental theorem of algebra
    • Catalogue entry, Cauchy, Cours d'analyse, 1821 Internet Archive catalogue record TO0E037871_TO0324_PNI-1898_000000
