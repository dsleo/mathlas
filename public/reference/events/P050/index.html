1936 · Combinatorics

Erdős discrepancy problem

A combinatorial discrepancy question is reduced to correlations of multiplicative functions, then settled with entropy-decrement and short-interval results from analytic number theory.

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1932

    Erdős conjectures the discrepancy bound

    precursor

    Erdős is credited with the conjecture in 1932, before any printed statement.

    Evidence (1)
    • Tao proved the conjecture made by Erdős in 1932 MacTutor: Terence Tao
  2. 1936

    Erdős and Turán on sequences of integers

    first statement

    P. Erdős, P. Turán, On some sequences of integers, J. London Math. Soc. (1) 11 (1936) 261-264

    Printed paper in which the discrepancy problem for sign sequences is posed, to the best of the sources cited.

    Evidence (1)
    • DOI record: J. London Math. Soc. (1) 11 (1936) Crossref
  3. 1964

    Roth's lower bound for discrepancy

    key development

    K. F. Roth, Remark concerning integer sequences, Acta Arith. 9 (1964)

    Roth proves the first nontrivial lower bound for discrepancy along arithmetic progressions in the general (not only multiplicative) setting.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref record: Roth, Remark concerning integer sequences, Acta Arithmetica, 1964. Crossref, DOI 10.4064/aa-9-3-257-260
  4. 1996

    Matousek and Spencer's matching bound

    key development

    J. Matoušek, J. Spencer, Discrepancy in arithmetic progressions, J. Amer. Math. Soc. 9 (1996)

    Shows that the general discrepancy on arithmetic progressions grows like a power of the length, so the conjecture is only true for the sign-sequence setting of Erdős.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref record: Matoušek, Spencer, Discrepancy in arithmetic progressions, JAMS, 1996. Crossref, DOI 10.1090/s0894-0347-96-00175-0
  5. 2010

    Polymath5 project on the conjecture

    key development

    Polymath5 collaborative project, 2010

    An online collaboration reduced the problem to multiplicative sequences, setting up Tao's later proof.

    Evidence (1)
    • Wikipedia says Tao built on work done in 2010 during Polymath5. English Wikipedia, Sign sequence
  6. 2014

    Konev and Lisitsa: SAT proof for C = 2

    key development

    B. Konev, A. Lisitsa, A SAT attack on the Erdős discrepancy conjecture, SAT 2014, LNCS 8561

    A computer search proves every sequence of length 1161 has discrepancy above 2.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref record: Konev, Lisitsa, A SAT attack on the Erdős discrepancy conjecture, LNCS 2014. Crossref, DOI 10.1007/978-3-319-09284-3_17
    • Wikipedia reports the length-1161 result of February 2014. English Wikipedia, Sign sequence
  7. 2014

    Le Bras, Gomes and Selman, 'On the Erdos discrepancy problem'

    naming

    R. Le Bras, C. P. Gomes, B. Selman, On the Erdos discrepancy problem, in: Principles and Practice of Constraint Programming (CP 2014), LNCS, 2014

    The earliest zbMATH title that names the problem 'Erdos discrepancy problem'.

    Evidence (1)
    • zbMATH record 'On the Erdos discrepancy problem', Le Bras, Gomes and Selman, CP 2014, Zbl 1343.68221 zbMATH Open API
  8. 2015

    Tao proves the Erdős discrepancy conjecture

    key development

    T. Tao, The Erdős discrepancy problem, arXiv:1509.05363 (2015); Discrete Analysis 2016:1

    Proved that every ±1 sequence has unbounded discrepancy along arithmetic progressions, settling the conjecture.

    Evidence (2)
    • arXiv record, submitted 17 Sep 2015; journal reference Discrete Analysis 2016:1 arXiv
    • Terence Tao solved the Erdős discrepancy problem in 2015 MacTutor: Terence Tao
