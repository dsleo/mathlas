1929 · Number theory

Unipotent flows and the Oppenheim conjecture

Values of indefinite quadratic forms at integer points are controlled by showing orbits of unipotent subgroups on homogeneous spaces are well-behaved.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1929

    Oppenheim's conjecture

    first statement

    Oppenheim, The minima of indefinite quaternary quadratic forms, Proc. Natl. Acad. Sci. USA (1929)

    Conjectures that values of an indefinite irrational quadratic form at integer points are dense in the reals when the form has more than three variables.

    Evidence (2)
    • PNAS 1929 paper (DOI 10.1073/pnas.15.9.724) Crossref / publisher record
    • Oppenheim conjecture made in 1929 MacTutor Margulis biography
  2. 1946

    Circle method for five variables

    first application

    Davenport, Heilbronn, On indefinite quadratic forms in five variables, J. London Math. Soc. (1946)

    Applies the circle method to prove the conjecture for five variables, the first purposeful analytic attack.

    Dating or attribution note

    The n=5 result is from the paper; claim that this is the first purposeful attack rests on Wikipedia's summary.

    Evidence (2)
    • J. London Math. Soc. 1946 paper (DOI 10.1112/jlms/s1-21.3.185) Crossref / publisher record
    • Davenport and Heilbronn partial results, circle method Wikipedia: Oppenheim conjecture (raw)
  3. 1987

    Margulis's proof of the Oppenheim conjecture

    key development

    Margulis, G. A., Formes quadratiques indefinies et flots unipotents sur les espaces homogenes, C. R. Acad. Sci. Paris Ser. I 304 (1987) 249-253

    Proves the conjecture in full for three or more variables using ergodic theory of unipotent flows on homogeneous spaces.

    Evidence (3)
    • Margulis proved the full conjecture in 1986 MacTutor Margulis biography
    • Margulis proved the conjecture in 1987 by ergodic theory Wikipedia: Oppenheim conjecture (raw)
    • zbMATH: Margulis, 'Formes quadratiques indefinies et flots unipotents sur les espaces homogenes', C. R. Acad. Sci. Paris Ser. I 304 (1987) 249-253 zbMATH Open 4012713
  4. 1991

    Measure rigidity for unipotent flows

    key development

    Ratner, On Raghunathan's measure conjecture, Ann. of Math. (1991)

    Proves measure rigidity for unipotent flows, the general framework containing the Oppenheim case via Raghunathan's conjecture.

    Evidence (2)
    • Ann. of Math. 1991 paper (DOI 10.2307/2944357) Crossref / publisher record
    • Raghunathan's measure conjecture gives the Oppenheim special case MacTutor Ratner biography
  5. 1991

    Margulis's monograph on discrete subgroups

    consolidation

    Margulis, Discrete Subgroups of Semisimple Lie Groups, Springer (1991)

    Systematic treatment of discrete subgroups and the ergodic methods behind the Oppenheim proof.

    Dating or attribution note

    Catalogue record only; DOI not checked.
