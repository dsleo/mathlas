2010 · Combinatorics

Algorithmic discrepancy theory (Bansal) and the Komlos conjecture

Designing efficient vector-balancing algorithms, rather than existence arguments, turned out to reproduce and then improve the best known discrepancy bounds, including a new bound towards Komlos.

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 2010

    Constructive discrepancy breakthrough posted

    first statement

    N. Bansal, Constructive Algorithms for Discrepancy Minimization (2010)

    Bansal gives polynomial-time algorithms matching key existential discrepancy bounds.

    Evidence (1)
    • The source documents constructive discrepancy breakthrough posted in 2010 arXiv
  2. 2010

    Algorithm attains Spencer-type discrepancy bounds

    first application

    N. Bansal, Constructive Algorithms for Discrepancy Minimization (2010)

    A semidefinite-programming-based constructive method targets discrepancy minimization.

    Evidence (1)
    • The source documents algorithm attains spencer-type discrepancy bounds in 2010 arXiv
  3. 2014

    Bansal's survey on algorithmic aspects of discrepancy

    consolidation

    Bansal, N., Algorithmic aspects of combinatorial discrepancy, Lecture Notes in Mathematics (2014)

    An expository chapter on algorithms for combinatorial discrepancy.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref chapter record, 2014 Crossref
  4. 2016

    Algorithm reaches Banaszczyk Komlós bound

    key development

    N. Bansal, D. Dadush and S. Garg, An Algorithm for Komlós Conjecture Matching Banaszczyk's bound (2016)

    The later algorithm matches the best then-known nonconstructive bound, without proving the constant-bound conjecture.

    Evidence (1)
    • The source documents algorithm reaches banaszczyk komlós bound in 2016 arXiv
  5. 2017

    Bansal-Garg beyond partial coloring

    key development

    Bansal, N., Garg, S., Algorithmic discrepancy beyond partial coloring, STOC 2017

    Extends the algorithmic discrepancy method beyond partial coloring, reaching bounds the earlier methods could not.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref record, STOC 2017 Crossref
  6. 2022

    Smoothed Komlós case proved

    key development

    N. Bansal et al., Smoothed Analysis of the Komlós Conjecture (2022)

    The conjecture is proved for a perturbed-input regime, a conditional model rather than the full conjecture.

    Evidence (1)
    • The source documents smoothed komlós case proved in 2022 arXiv
  7. 2023

    Bansal's ICM survey on discrepancy theory

    consolidation

    Bansal, N., Discrepancy theory and related algorithms, ICM 2022 proceedings (2023)

    An ICM survey of discrepancy theory and the algorithms that realize it.
