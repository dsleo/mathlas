Saint-Vincent and Sarasa: hyperbolic area and the logarithm

Area under the hyperbola was shown to behave as a logarithm; Leibniz tied log to the integral of dy/y.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1614

    Napier's logarithms

    precursor

    Napier, Mirifici logarithmorum canonis descriptio, 1614

    Logarithms as a numerical tool existed before the geometric link.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref lists the 1614 Descriptio. Crossref record: Smithsonian Libraries record, issued 1614
  2. 1647

    Saint-Vincent, Opus geometricum quadraturae circuli et sectionum coni

    first statement

    Saint-Vincent, Opus geometricum quadraturae circuli et sectionum coni, Antwerp, 1647

    The book shows equal hyperbola areas for equal ratios of abscissae, the essential geometric logarithm property.

    Evidence (2)
    • Record of the 1647 Opus geometricum. Internet Archive record: Opus geometricum quadraturae circuli, 1647
    • The area under xy=k over [a,b] equals that over [c,d] if a/b=c/d. MacTutor biography of Saint-Vincent
  3. 1649

    Sarasa links hyperbola areas to logarithms

    key development

    Sarasa, Solutio problematis a R. P. Marino Mersenno proposito, Antwerp, 1649

    Saint-Vincent's pupil identified the hyperbola area with logarithms.

    Evidence (2)
    • Open Library lists Sarasa's Solutio problematis (1649). Open Library search: Sarasa entry
    • Connection made by his pupil Sarasa. MacTutor biography of Saint-Vincent
  4. 1668

    Mercator's Logarithmotechnia

    key development

    Mercator, Logarithmotechnia, London, 1668

    The hyperbola-area logarithm received a power-series expansion.

    • Record of Logarithmotechnia 1668. Internet Archive record: Logarithmotechnia, 1668
