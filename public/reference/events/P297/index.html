Cap set

Croot-Lev-Pach and Ellenberg-Gijswijt used a low-degree polynomial argument to bound cap sets.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 2016

    Croot–Lev–Pach polynomial method

    precursor

    E. Croot, V. Lev and P. Pach, Progression-free sets in Z_4^n are exponentially small, arXiv:1605.01506 (2016)

    The precursor applies a polynomial argument to progression-free sets in a different group.

    Evidence (1)
    • First public version posted 5 May 2016 arXiv
  2. 2016

    Exponential cap-set bound posted

    first statement

    J. S. Ellenberg and D. Gijswijt, On large subsets of F_q^n with no three-term arithmetic progression, arXiv:1605.09223 (2016)

    The note states an exponential bound, including the F3 cap-set problem.

    Evidence (1)
    • First public version posted 30 May 2016 arXiv
  3. 2016

    Polynomial method applied to cap sets

    first application

    J. S. Ellenberg and D. Gijswijt, On large subsets of F_q^n with no three-term arithmetic progression, arXiv:1605.09223 (2016)

    The authors explicitly use the Croot–Lev–Pach method to bound cap sets.

    Evidence (1)
    • The abstract names the method and target problem arXiv
  4. 2023

    Bourbaki survey of three-term progression-free bounds

    consolidation

    Bourbaki Seminar exposé 1196, Recent progress on bounds for sets with no three terms in arithmetic progression (after Bloom and Sisask, Croot, Lev, and Pach, and Ellenberg and Gijswijt)

    A Bourbaki survey that reviews the post-2016 cap-set bounds and their follow-ups.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref record, DOI 10.24033/ast.1197, dated 2023-04-04, Bourbaki exposé 1196 Crossref
