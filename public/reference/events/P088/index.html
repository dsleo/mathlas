1971 · Analysis

Concentration of measure

Isoperimetric inequalities in high dimension give sharp deviation bounds for Lipschitz functions, used for Banach space geometry and probability in product spaces.

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1943

    Khinchin's rigorous ensemble-equivalence results

    precursor

    Khinchin (1943) on equivalence of ensembles in statistical mechanics

    Rigorous statements that equivalence of statistical ensembles follows from a concentration of energy-shell measure, an early precursor of the concentration phenomenon.

    Dating or attribution note

    Khinchin's 1943 work is not checked in a bibliographic record. It is a physics precursor, not the mathematical concept.

    Evidence (1)
    • Wikipedia: rigorous results on energy concentration under regularity conditions, attributed to Khinchin (1943) Wikipedia, Concentration of measure
  2. 1971

    Milman's new proof of Dvoretzky's theorem

    first statement

    Milman, New proof of the theorem of A. Dvoretzky on intersections of convex bodies, Funct. Anal. Appl. 5 (1971)

    Milman's proof of Dvoretzky's theorem uses concentration of the measure of Lipschitz functions on high-dimensional spheres, giving the first public statement of the concentration mechanism in analysis.

    Dating or attribution note

    The earlier concentration-on-sphere ideas of Levy are not verified here, since the Levy 1922 record was not found.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref: journal article, Functional Analysis and Its Applications, 1971, Milman, New proof of the theorem of A. Dvoretzky Crossref
    • Wikipedia: Milman is credited with the concentration phenomenon in asymptotic geometry Wikipedia, Vitali Milman
  3. 1971

    Dvoretzky's theorem via concentration

    first application

    Milman, New proof of the theorem of A. Dvoretzky on intersections of convex bodies, Funct. Anal. Appl. 5 (1971)

    The first purposeful use of concentration on a target problem: sections of convex bodies in high dimension.

    Evidence (1)
    • Same bibliographic record as the first_statement milestone Crossref
  4. 1986

    Milman-Schechtman monograph

    consolidation

    Milman, Schechtman, Asymptotic Theory of Finite Dimensional Normed Spaces, Lecture Notes in Math. 1200, Springer (1986)

    First systematic treatment of concentration and related asymptotic methods for finite-dimensional normed spaces.

    Evidence (1)
    • Open Library record: Vitali D. Milman, Asymptotic theory of finite dimensional normed spaces, Springer-Verlag, first published 1986 Open Library
  5. 1995

    Talagrand's isoperimetric inequalities in product spaces

    key development

    Talagrand, Concentration of measure and isoperimetric inequalities in product spaces, Publ. Math. IHES 81 (1995)

    Sharp concentration and isoperimetric inequalities in product spaces, making the phenomenon a general tool in probability.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref: journal article, Publications Mathematiques de l'IHES, 1995 Crossref
  6. 2001

    'The Concentration of Measure Phenomenon' in a title

    naming

    Ledoux, The Concentration of Measure Phenomenon, Mathematical Surveys and Monographs 89, AMS (2001)

    The title fixes the name 'concentration of measure phenomenon' for the field.

    Evidence (1)
    • Open Library record: Michel Ledoux, The Concentration of Measure Phenomenon, AMS, first published 2001 Open Library
