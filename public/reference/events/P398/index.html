1844 · Number theory

Liouville and early transcendence theory

Liouville gave explicit ways to prove numbers transcendental, opening a theory later extended to e by Hermite and pi by Lindemann.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1761

    Lambert proves pi is irrational

    precursor

    J. H. Lambert, memoir read 1761

    Irrationality of pi, not enough to settle squaring the circle, since some irrationals are constructible.

    Evidence (1)
    • Topic page says Lambert proved pi irrational in 1761 and explains why this was not enough. MacTutor: Squaring the circle
  2. 1844

    Liouville's classes of numbers that cannot be algebraic

    first statement

    J. Liouville, note presented 13 May 1844, Comptes rendus 18

    Liouville shows via continued fractions that certain explicit numbers are not roots of integer polynomials, the first proofs of transcendence of specific numbers.

    Evidence (3)
    • Scanned Comptes rendus vol. 18 (Jan-June 1844): the meeting of 13 May 1844 opens with Liouville's oral communication on classes of numbers not reducible to algebraic irrationals and starts with continued fractions. Internet Archive scan of Comptes rendus 18
    • Biography says he proved existence of a transcendental number in 1844 using continued fractions. MacTutor: Liouville biography
    • Article says Liouville proved his bound in 1844 and exhibited the first transcendental numbers. English Wikipedia: Liouville number
  3. 1873

    Hermite proves e is transcendental

    first application

    C. Hermite, Sur la fonction exponentielle, Comptes rendus 77 (1873)

    Hermite gives the first proof of transcendence of a number not built for that purpose, using analytic approximation of the exponential function.

    Evidence (3)
    • Scanned Comptes rendus vol. 77 (1873) contains several parts titled Sur la fonction exponentielle by Hermite. Internet Archive scan of Comptes rendus 77
    • Biography says Hermite published the first proof that e is transcendental in 1873. MacTutor: Hermite biography
    • Article says e was the first number proved transcendental without being constructed for that aim. English Wikipedia: Transcendental number
  4. 1882

    Lindemann proves pi is transcendental

    key development

    F. Lindemann, Ueber die Zahl pi, Math. Ann. 20 (1882) 213-225

    Using Hermite's method Lindemann shows pi is transcendental, so squaring the circle with ruler and compass is impossible.

    Dating or attribution note

    The Squaring the circle MacTutor page says 1880; the journal paper is 1882. Weierstrass' 1885 simplification (Pi: A Source Book reprints, 10.1007/978-1-4757-2736-4_23) was not verified as a first record.

    Evidence (3)
    • Title, journal, volume 20, pages 213-225, June 1882 confirmed. Crossref record 10.1007/bf01446522
    • Biography gives 1882 and says the method was similar to Hermite's. MacTutor: Lindemann biography
    • Article says the 1882 paper was the first complete proof for pi. English Wikipedia: Transcendental number
  5. 1893

    Hilbert's short proof of transcendence of e and pi

    key development

    D. Hilbert, Ueber die Transcendenz der Zahlen e und pi, Math. Ann. 43 (1893) 216-219

    A much shorter proof of both results.

    Dating or attribution note

    Role as simplification inferred from the title and length; its place in history not read in a source here.

    Evidence (1)
    • Title, journal, volume 43, pages 216-219, June 1893 confirmed. Crossref record 10.1007/bf01443645
  6. 1897

    Weber's survey 'Transcendental numbers'

    consolidation

    H. Weber, Transcendental numbers, Bull. Amer. Math. Soc. (2) 3 (1897) 174-195

    An expository survey, under the title 'Transcendental numbers', published in the American Mathematical Society Bulletin. It is the earliest title with that phrase found in zbMATH.

    Evidence (2)
