1656 · Analysis

Wallis: interpolation and infinite products

Interpolating integer-power integrals gave an infinite product for pi.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1635

    Cavalieri's indivisibles

    precursor

    Bonaventura Cavalieri, Geometria indivisibilibus, 1635

    Wallis built his integral methods on Cavalieri's method of indivisibles and on Kepler's continuity.

    Evidence (1)
    • Wallis built on Cavalieri's indivisibles and Kepler's continuity to devise interpolation. MacTutor: John Wallis
  2. 1656

    Arithmetica infinitorum

    first statement

    John Wallis, Arithmetica infinitorum, in Operum mathematicorum pars altera, Oxford, 1656

    Wallis evaluated integrals of integer powers and by interpolation reached the infinite product for pi.

    Evidence (3)
    • Archive.org record lists Wallis, Operum mathematicorum, dated 1656. archive.org item bim_early-english-books-1641-1700_operum-mathematicorum-_wallis-john_1656
    • MacTutor dates Arithmetica infinitorum to 1656 and states the product for half of pi. MacTutor: John Wallis
    • The Wallis product was published in 1656 and derived by interpolation. Wikipedia: Wallis product
  3. 1656

    Interpolation applied to the area of the circle

    first application

    Wallis, Arithmetica infinitorum, 1656

    Interpolating the series of integrals of (1-x^2)^n to fractional n to reach the quadrature of the circle yielded the product for pi.

    Evidence (2)
    • Wallis used interpolation to reach the formula; Newton later read the series by intercalation for area of circle and hyperbola. MacTutor: John Wallis
    • Wallis derived the product by interpolation, not rigorous. Wikipedia: Wallis product
  4. 1656

    Interpolation introduced as a term

    naming

    Wallis, Arithmetica infinitorum, 1656

    MacTutor states that Wallis introduced the word interpolation in this work.

    Evidence (1)
    • MacTutor says interpolation is a word he introduced in this work. MacTutor: John Wallis
  5. 1664–1665

    Newton's binomial series builds on Wallis' interpolation

    key development

    Isaac Newton, generalised binomial theorem, 1664-65

    Newton cites Wallis' intercalation of series as the starting point of his own mathematics and moved from there to the general binomial series.

    Evidence (2)
    • Newton wrote that Wallis' intercalation of series was at the start of his studies. MacTutor: John Wallis
    • Newton's first major discovery was the generalised binomial theorem in 1664-65. Wikipedia: Isaac Newton
  6. 1748

    Euler's Introductio and the product for sine

    key development

    Leonhard Euler, Introductio in analysin infinitorum, Lausanne 1748

    Euler's sine product gives a second route to Wallis' product and sets infinite products in the main line of analysis.

    Dating or attribution note

    The derivation claim comes from Wikipedia; the page of the Introductio was not read.

    Evidence (2)
    • Internet Archive scan of the 1748 Introductio. Internet Archive bub_gb_jQ1bAAAAQAAJ
    • Wikipedia notes the derivation of the product from Euler's sine product. English Wikipedia, Wallis product
