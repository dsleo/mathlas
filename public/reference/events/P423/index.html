1937 · Group & representation theory

Lie-ring methods enter the Burnside problem (Magnus to Zelmanov)

Magnus tied Baker-Hausdorff-type Lie machinery to exponent questions on groups, defining the restricted Burnside problem; this Lie-algebraic route was eventually completed by Zelmanov.

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1902

    Burnside's problem posed

    precursor

    Burnside, On an unsettled question in the theory of discontinuous groups, 1902

    Burnside asked whether a finitely generated periodic group is necessarily finite.

    Evidence (2)
    • Burnside, On an unsettled question in the theory of discontinuous groups (1902), zbMATH record zbMATH record (year 1902)
    • Burnside introduced the general and bounded-exponent problems in 1902 MacTutor, A history of the Burnside problem
  2. 1937

    Magnus's Lie-ring method for the lower central series

    first statement

    Magnus, Über Beziehungen zwischen höheren Kommutatoren, J. reine angew. Math. 177 (1937) 105-115

    Magnus used Lie-ring methods on commutators of free groups, the first public statement of the Lie-ring route.

    Evidence (2)
    • Magnus, Über Beziehungen zwischen höheren Kommutatoren, J. reine angew. Math. 177 (1937) 105-115 zbMATH record (1937, Crelle, pp. 105-115)
    • Magnus introduced Lie ring methods to study the lower central series of free groups in the 1930s MacTutor, Wilhelm Magnus biography
  3. 1950

    'Restricted Burnside problem' coined

    naming

    Magnus introduced the term 'restricted Burnside problem' in 1950.

    Evidence (1)
    • The term 'Restricted Burnside Problem' was coined by Magnus in 1950 (cited as [7] in MacTutor) MacTutor, A history of the Burnside problem
  4. 1958

    Kostrikin's theorem for prime exponent

    key development

    Kostrikin, On Burnside's problem, Doklady Akademii Nauk SSSR (1958) 1081-1084

    Kostrikin showed that the restricted Burnside groups exist for prime exponent, a main step on the Lie-type route.

    Evidence (2)
    • Kostrikin, On Burnside's problem, Dokl. Akad. Nauk SSSR (1958) 1081-1084 zbMATH record (1958, pp. 1081-1084)
    • Kostrikin showed that B0(m,p) exists for every prime p MacTutor, A history of the Burnside problem
  5. 1990

    Kostrikin's monograph Around Burnside

    consolidation

    Kostrikin, Around Burnside, Springer (1990)

    A monograph on the Burnside problems, collecting the restricted-exponent and Lie-type results.

    Evidence (1)
    • Kostrikin, Around Burnside, Springer (1990) Crossref DOI 10.1007/978-3-642-74324-5
  6. 1991

    Zelmanov solves the restricted Burnside problem for odd exponent

    key development

    Zel'manov, Solution of the restricted Burnside problem for groups of odd exponent, Math. USSR-Izv. 36 (1991) 41-60

    Zelmanov's solution for groups of odd exponent completes the restricted problem along the Lie-type route.

    Dating or attribution note

    The separate 2-group paper and the 1989 announcement were not checked against records.

    Evidence (2)
    • Zel'manov, Solution of the restricted Burnside problem for groups of odd exponent, Math. USSR-Izv. 36 (1991) 41-60 Crossref DOI 10.1070/im1991v036n01abeh001946
    • Zelmanov solved the restricted Burnside problem, after a 1989 announcement MacTutor, Efim Zelmanov biography
  7. 1994

    Zelmanov's Fields Medal

    recognition

    Zelmanov received the Fields Medal in 1994, partly for this result.

    Evidence (1)
    • Zelmanov was awarded a Fields Medal in 1994 for the restricted Burnside solution MacTutor, Efim Zelmanov biography
