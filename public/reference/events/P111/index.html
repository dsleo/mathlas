499 · Number theory

Indian place-value notation and zero

Brahmi digits developed into a positional decimal system whose schools around 500-650 CE worked out place value and zero.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 250 BCE

    Brahmi numerals appear

    precursor

    Ashokan inscriptions and Brahmi numerals, c. 3rd century BC

    Additive Brahmi numeral signs for 1-9 and for tens and hundreds exist; they are not yet a place-value system.

    Evidence (2)
    • MacTutor Indian numerals: Brahmi numerals came into being around the middle of the third century BC; not place-value MacTutor, Indian numerals
    • Wikipedia Hindu-Arabic numeral system: Brahmi numerals predate the Common Era, on Ashoka edicts, and were not positional Wikipedia, Hindu-Arabic numeral system
  2. 458

    Lokavibhaga: Jain cosmology text using decimal place value and zero

    precursor

    Lokavibhaga, internally dated 458 CE; surviving in a later Sanskrit translation

    A Jain cosmological text, dated within itself to 458, may preserve one of the earliest positional uses of zero.

    Evidence (2)
    • Wikipedia, 0: Lokavibhaga internally dated to AD 458 uses decimal place value, including a zero; survives in later Sanskrit translation Wikipedia, 0
    • Wikipedia Hindu-Arabic numeral system: Sanskrit translation of the lost 5th century Prakrit text may preserve an early instance Wikipedia, Hindu-Arabic numeral system
  3. 499

    Aryabhatiya states the place-value principle

    first statement

    Aryabhata, Aryabhatiya, 499

    Aryabhata states that each place is ten times the previous one and uses a word meaning emptiness for the empty position.

    Evidence (3)
    • Wikipedia 0: Aryabhatiya (499) states each place ten times the preceding Wikipedia, 0
    • MacTutor Zero: around 500 Aryabhata devised a positional number system with no zero symbol; word kha for place MacTutor, Zero
    • MacTutor Aryabhata: Aryabhatiya finished 499 MacTutor, Aryabhata I
  4. 594

    Earliest dated place-value document: Sankheda copper-plate charter

    first application

    Donation charter of Dadda III of Sankheda, dated 346 in the Chhedi era (594 CE)

    A dated charter writes a date in decimal place-value form, the oldest dated Indian document in that form according to MacTutor.

    Evidence (1)
    • MacTutor Indian numerals: oldest dated Indian document with place-value numbers is a charter dated Chhedi 346 = 594 AD MacTutor, Indian numerals
  5. 628

    Brahmagupta states arithmetic rules for zero and negatives

    key development

    Brahmagupta, Brahmasphutasiddhanta, 628

    Brahmagupta defines zero as a number and sets sum, product and division rules, including a wrong rule for 0/0.

    Evidence (3)
    • MacTutor Brahmagupta: defines zero as a number minus itself and gives rules; attempts division by zero MacTutor, Brahmagupta
    • Wikipedia, 0: rules for zero in Brahmasphuta Siddhanta (7th century) Wikipedia, 0
    • Wikipedia Brahmagupta: Brahmasphutasiddhanta dated 628 Wikipedia, Brahmagupta
  6. 799–1102

    Bakhshali manuscript (zero as a dot); date disputed

    precursor

    Bakhshali manuscript, birch bark, Gandhara region

    The manuscript uses place value with a dot for zero, but its folios were radiocarbon dated across centuries.

    Evidence (2)
    • Wikipedia Bakhshali manuscript: 2017 dates 224-383, 680-779, 885-993 CE; October 2024 revision 799-1102 CE; was regarded as earliest zero symbol until Sept 2024 Wikipedia, Bakhshali manuscript
    • Hayashi 1995, The Bakhshali Manuscript (Brill) records the study of the text Hayashi (1995)
  7. 876

    Gwalior inscription: first undisputed dated zero symbol

    key development

    Chaturbhuj Temple inscription, Gwalior, dated 876 CE

    A stone inscription writes 270 and 50 with a small circle for zero, the first dated writing of zero accepted as genuine.

    Evidence (3)
    • MacTutor Zero: first dated, agreed-genuine record of zero is the Gwalior inscription of 876 MacTutor, Zero
    • Wikipedia 0: first indubitable dated zero glyph in the Gwalior inscription AD 876; Khmer inscription of 683 mentioned Wikipedia, 0
    • Wikipedia 0: first indubitable zero symbol on the Gwalior inscription, AD 876; Sambor 605 Saka (683 CE) noted as earlier decimal use Wikipedia, 0 (number)
  8. 1935

    Datta's History of Hindu Mathematics

    consolidation

    Bibhutibhusan Datta, History of Hindu Mathematics, Lahore, 1935

    Book-length survey of Hindu mathematics that covers the place-value system but is not limited to it.

    Evidence (2)
    • Open Library holds a 1935 edition titled History of Hindu Mathematics; its author field is garbled, and a 1962 reprint is credited to Datta. Open Library work records
    • 1962 reprint credited to Bibhutibhusan Datta. Open Library work record
