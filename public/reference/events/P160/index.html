860 · Algebra

Al-Mahani: geometric problems reduced to algebra

Classical construction problems were recast as algebraic (cubic) equations.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 350 BCE

    Menaechmus: conics solve two mean proportionals

    precursor

    Menaechmus, solution of cube duplication by conic sections (as reported by Eutocius)

    A cubic problem (doubling the cube) was solved by intersecting conics, the geometric tool later applied to cubic equations.

    Evidence (1)
    • Menaechmus found conic sections while trying to duplicate the cube, solving the two mean proportionals problem with them; described by Eutocius. MacTutor, Menaechmus biography
  2. 225 BCE

    Archimedes poses the sphere-section problem

    precursor

    Archimedes, On the Sphere and Cylinder II.4

    Archimedes set the task of cutting a sphere by a plane so the pieces have a given volume ratio, and promised a solution later supplied by Eutocius' text.

    Evidence (2)
    • al-Mahani tried the Archimedes problem of cutting a sphere by a plane into segments of given volume ratio. MacTutor, Al-Mahani biography
    • Eutocius records the missing solution promised by Archimedes in On the Sphere and Cylinder II.4. MacTutor, Eutocius biography
  3. 860

    Al-Mahani reduces the sphere problem to a cubic

    first statement

    Al-Mahani, commentary on On the Sphere and Cylinder (lost); equation reported by Khayyam

    He recast the Archimedean division of a sphere as an algebraic equation with cubes, squares and numbers, but could not solve it.

    Evidence (2)
    • al-Mahani reached an equation of cubes, squares and numbers he could not solve; Khayyam gives this account. MacTutor, Al-Mahani biography
    • The resulting 'al-Mahani equation' is x^3 + c^2 b = c x^2, as reported by Khayyam. English Wikipedia, Al-Mahani
  4. 950–971

    Al-Khazin solves al-Mahani's equation with conics

    first application

    Abu Ja'far al-Khazin, solution of the sphere-division equation (10th century)

    The equation was solved by intersecting conic sections, making the geometry-to-cubic link complete.

    Evidence (2)
    • The problem was declared impossible until Ja'far al-Khazin solved the equation with conic sections. MacTutor, Al-Mahani biography
    • Khayyam credits al-Mahani and al-Khazin with translating geometric problems into algebraic ones. MacTutor, Khayyam biography
  5. 1070–1074

    Khayyam classifies cubics solved by conics

    key development

    Omar Khayyam, Treatise on Demonstration of Problems of Algebra (Samarkand, from 1070)

    Khayyam gave a systematic classification of cubic equations with conic solutions, citing al-Mahani and al-Khazin as the starting point.

    Evidence (1)
    • Khayyam's Treatise gave a complete classification of cubics with solutions by intersecting conics and describes al-Mahani and al-Khazin. MacTutor, Khayyam biography
