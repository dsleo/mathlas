1736 · Graph theory

Graph theory from the Königsberg bridges

A puzzle about walks across bridges is stripped to vertices and edges, and a parity condition settles it, an early instance of 'geometry of position'.

Also known as: Seven Bridges of Königsberg

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1735

    Euler presents the solution to the St Petersburg Academy

    precursor

    Euler, talk of 26 August 1735

    The solution was presented to the Academy six years before it was printed.

    Evidence (1)
    • Euler's work was presented to the St Petersburg Academy on 26 August 1735 Wikipedia, Seven Bridges of Königsberg
  2. 1736

    Euler's Königsberg bridge paper

    first statement

    Euler, Solutio problematis ad geometriam situs pertinentis, 1736 (presented)

    Euler solved the seven-bridges problem and generalised it to a parity condition on vertex degrees, a paper that history pages regard as the first in graph theory.

    Evidence (2)
    • Wikipedia calls Euler's 1736 paper the first paper in the history of graph theory English Wikipedia, Graph theory
    • MacTutor dates the first beginnings of topology to this 1736 paper and states its degree-parity criterion MacTutor, A history of Topology
  3. 1741

    Solutio problematis ad geometriam situs pertinentis

    first statement

    Euler, Solutio problematis ad geometriam situs pertinentis, Commentarii Academiae Scientiarum Petropolitanae 8 (1736), printed 1741

    Euler recasts the bridge puzzle as land masses and links, shows the walk is impossible and states the parity rule for general networks.

    Evidence (2)
    • Published in the St Petersburg Commentarii in 1741; mathematical formalisation and proof of impossibility laid the foundations of graph theory Wikipedia, Seven Bridges of Königsberg
    • Euler Archive: Commentarii vol. 8, dated 1741 Euler Archive record E53 (bepress metadata)
  4. 1847

    Kirchhoff's network equations

    first application

    Kirchhoff, Ueber die Auflösung der Gleichungen ... galvanischer Ströme, Ann. Phys. 1847

    An electrical engineering problem is analysed through the loop and branch structure of the network, which graph-theory histories count as the first use of modern algebra on graph-like structures.

    Dating or attribution note

    Use of graph structure for a target problem outside pure combinatorics; the paper content itself was not read. Treated as the earliest application of the graph viewpoint, not as the start of the subject.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref record of the 1847 paper Crossref 10.1002/andp.18471481202
    • Wikipedia says the first example of algebraic techniques applied to graphs is Kirchhoff's work on circuit laws English Wikipedia, Graph theory
  5. 1852

    De Morgan's letter on the colouring of maps

    precursor

    De Morgan to Hamilton, letter of 23 October 1852

    A student's question about colouring map regions reached De Morgan, who wrote it to Hamilton the same day; this private letter is the first record of the four colour problem, later reformulated for graphs.

    Evidence (2)
    • MacTutor describes the letter and its date, naming Francis Guthrie as the source of the question MacTutor, The four colour theorem
    • Wikipedia gives 1852 and De Morgan's letter to Hamilton as the first written record English Wikipedia, Graph theory
  6. 1857

    Cayley's theory of trees

    key development

    Cayley, On the theory of the analytical forms called trees, Philosophical Magazine 13, 172-176, 1857

    Cayley introduced rooted trees as a way to count analytic forms from the differential calculus, starting enumerative graph theory that he later tied to chemistry.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref record: Cayley 1857, Phil. Mag. 13, 172-176 Crossref 10.1080/14786445708642275
    • Wikipedia describes Cayley's study of trees and its link with chemical composition as the start of enumerative graph theory English Wikipedia, Graph theory
  7. 1873

    Hierholzer proves sufficiency

    key development

    Hierholzer, Ueber die Möglichkeit, einen Linienzug ohne Wiederholung und ohne Unterbrechung zu umfahren, Mathematische Annalen, 1873

    Euler stated that even degrees suffice for a closed tour of a connected network; Hierholzer later gave the proof.

    Evidence (2)
    • The condition is also sufficient, a result stated by Euler and later proved by Carl Hierholzer Wikipedia, Seven Bridges of Königsberg
    • Crossref: Hierholzer and Wiener, Math. Ann., 1873 Crossref record 10.1007/bf01442866
  8. 1878

    Sylvester introduces the word 'graph'

    naming

    Sylvester, Chemistry and Algebra, Nature 17, 1878

    Sylvester used the word graph for diagrams of atoms and bonds, giving the field its name.

    Evidence (3)
    • The term graph was introduced by Sylvester in a paper in Nature in 1878 Wikipedia, Graph theory
    • Crossref: 'Chemistry and Algebra' by Sylvester, Nature, 7 Feb 1878 Crossref record 10.1038/017284a0
    • Crossref record of Sylvester, On an application of the new atomic theory ..., Am. J. Math. 1, 64 (1878) Crossref 10.2307/2369436
  9. 1936

    König's first monograph on graphs

    consolidation

    Kőnig, Theorie der endlichen und unendlichen Graphen, Leipzig, 1936

    A book-length systematic treatment of graph theory as a subject.

    Evidence (4)
    • Crossref: contemporary review of Kőnig's Theorie der endlichen und unendlichen Graphen, Leipzig 1936 Crossref record 10.1002/zamm.19360160640
    • Crossref: Vietoris, review, 1937 Crossref record 10.1007/bf01792729
    • zbMATH lists the 1936 edition of Kőnig's Theorie der endlichen und unendlichen Graphen zbMATH 3020563
    • Wikipedia calls Kőnig's 1936 book the first textbook on graph theory English Wikipedia, Graph theory
