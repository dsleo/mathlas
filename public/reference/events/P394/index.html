1893 · Algebraic geometry

Hilbert's Nullstellensatz and algebraic geometry as ideal theory

Hilbert linked ideals of polynomials to geometric zero sets, starting the algebraisation of algebraic geometry.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1890

    Finite basis theorem for polynomial ideals

    precursor

    Hilbert, D., Ueber die Theorie der algebraischen Formen, Mathematische Annalen, 1890

    Hilbert proved that every ideal of a polynomial ring is finitely generated, the algebraic tool later used for the zero-set link; this is a precursor, not the geometric statement itself.

    Evidence (2)
    • Paper on algebraic forms appears in Mathematische Annalen in 1890 Crossref record, DOI 10.1007/bf01208503
    • Basis theorem of 1890 precedes the 1893 Nullstellensatz paper Wikipedia, Hilbert's Nullstellensatz (article text)
  2. 1893

    Hilbert's Nullstellensatz

    first statement

    Hilbert, D., Ueber die vollen Invariantensysteme, Mathematische Annalen, 1893

    Hilbert's second invariant-theory paper proves the theorem relating polynomial ideals to their common zero sets.

    Evidence (2)
    • Paper appears in Mathematische Annalen in 1893 Crossref record, DOI 10.1007/bf01444162
    • Nullstellensatz proved by Hilbert in his 1893 invariant-theory paper Wikipedia, Hilbert's Nullstellensatz
  3. 1905

    Lasker's primary decomposition of ideals

    key development

    Lasker, E., Zur Theorie der Moduln und Ideale, Mathematische Annalen, 1905

    Lasker proved that ideals in polynomial rings decompose into primary components, a tool later central to ideal-based algebraic geometry.

    Dating or attribution note

    Content (primary decomposition) comes from general knowledge of the field; the paper text was not read.

    Evidence (1)
    • Paper appears in Mathematische Annalen in 1905 Crossref record, DOI 10.1007/bf01447495
  4. 1916

    Macaulay's monograph on modular systems

    consolidation

    Macaulay, F. S., The Algebraic Theory of Modular Systems, Cambridge, 1916

    Macaulay's monograph gives a systematic treatment of polynomial ideals and modular systems used in algebraic geometry.

    Evidence (2)
    • Book The Algebraic Theory of Modular Systems published 1916 by F. S. Macaulay Open Library search record
    • MacTutor describes Macaulay's 1916 book as a key work in the development of modern algebra MacTutor biography of Macaulay
  5. 1921

    Noether's ideal theory in rings

    key development

    Noether, E., Idealtheorie in Ringbereichen, Mathematische Annalen, 1921

    Noether generalised ideal theory to abstract rings, giving the framework in which Hilbert's ideal approach to algebraic geometry was later developed.

    Dating or attribution note

    Role statement about its influence on algebraic geometry is from general knowledge; the paper text was not read.

    Evidence (1)
    • Paper appears in Mathematische Annalen in 1921 Crossref record, DOI 10.1007/bf01464225
  6. 1930

    van der Waerden's Moderne Algebra

    consolidation

    van der Waerden, B. L., Moderne Algebra, Springer, 1930

    Van der Waerden's textbook presents ideal theory and polynomial ideals as standard algebra, consolidating the ideal approach.

    Evidence (1)
    • Moderne Algebra book chapters are dated 1930 in the Springer record Crossref record, DOI 10.1007/978-3-662-41906-9_7
