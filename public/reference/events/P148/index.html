544 · Numerical analysis

Interpolation and finite differences in Chinese astronomy and mathematics

Second- and third-order difference methods in astronomy were later used by Zhu Shijie to sum sequences.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 544–610

    Liu Zhuo introduces second-order interpolation for calendar computation

    first statement

    Liu Zhuo, Huangji calendar (Sui dynasty); lifespan 544-610

    Liu Zhuo uses second-order equal-interval differences to interpolate the sun irregular motion in the calendar.

    Evidence (3)
    • MacTutor Chinese overview: Liu Zhuo (544-610) introduced quadratic interpolation with second order difference MacTutor, Chinese overview
    • Ji Zhigang 1995 reconstructs Liu Zhuo second-order equal-interval interpolation (zbMATH) Ji Zhigang, J. Northwest Univ. (1995), zbMATH 883363
    • Ji Zhigang, From Liu Zhuo to Hua Hengfang (2002) Historical Perspectives on East Asian Sciences, Tech. and Medicine
  2. 665

    Brahmagupta second-order interpolation of sines (parallel Indian tradition)

    key development

    Brahmagupta, Khandakhadyaka, 665

    An independent Indian formula equivalent to second-order interpolation used for sine tables; not a source for the Chinese work.

    Evidence (1)
    • Wikipedia Brahmagupta: in 665 devised special case of second-order Newton-Stirling interpolation to find sine values Wikipedia, Brahmagupta
  3. 1280

    Guo Shoujing: cubic interpolation with tabulated differences in the Shoushi calendar

    key development

    Guo Shoujing, Shoushi (Works and Days) calendar, completed 1280

    Guo tabulates first, second and third differences of the accumulated difference, a third-order scheme resembling Newton forward differences.

    Evidence (2)
    • MacTutor Guo Shoujing: calendar completed by 1280; cubic interpolation formula with tabulated first, second and third differences MacTutor, Guo Shoujing
    • Ji Zhigang 1995 (zbMATH 883363): no breakthrough until Guo third-order method in 13th century Ji Zhigang (1995), zbMATH
  4. 1303

    Zhu Shijie uses finite differences to sum series

    first application

    Zhu Shijie, Siyuan yujian (Jade Mirror of the Four Unknowns), 1303

    Zhu uses second, third and fourth differences to sum polynomial-type series such as the recruiting problem, moving the technique from astronomy to pure mathematics.

    Evidence (3)
    • MacTutor Zhu Shijie: Siyuan yujian 1303; solves recruiting-cubes problem by working out second, third, fourth differences MacTutor, Zhu Shijie
    • Wikipedia Zhu Shijie: Jade Mirror 1303 contains series and progressions classified via Pascal triangle coefficients Wikipedia, Zhu Shijie
    • Chemla, Siyuan yujian entry in Encyclopaedia of the History of Science (2008) Chemla (2008)
  5. 1800

    Lacroix publishes a treatise on differences and series

    consolidation

    Lacroix, S.-F., Traite des differences et des series, 1800

    Book-length treatment of finite differences, following Lacroix's differential calculus.

    Dating or attribution note

    Catalogue record only; "first systematic treatment" not checked against earlier Chinese or Indian texts in book form.

    Evidence (1)
    • Open Library lists Lacroix, Traite des differences et des series, first published 1800 Open Library works OL10709269W
  6. 1820

    Herschel and Lacroix title their examples book with the finite-differences name

    naming

    Herschel, J. F. W. (with Lacroix), A collection of examples of the applications of the calculus of finite differences, 1820

    The earliest title found that contains the phrase "calculus of finite differences".

    Evidence (1)
    • Open Library lists the title with first publication year 1820 Open Library works OL1554698W
