1748 · Analysis

Euler's Introductio in analysin infinitorum

Functions replaced curves as the object of calculus, with e^{ix} relation, exponentials and logs.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1694

    Bernoulli's mathematical use of 'function'

    precursor

    J. Bernoulli, letter to Leibniz, 1694

    The term function enters mathematics before Euler, but without a general object of study.

    Evidence (1)
    • Bernoulli described a function in 1694; Leibniz was pleased the term was used in his sense MacTutor, The function concept
  2. 1748

    Euler makes functions the basis of analysis

    first statement

    Euler, Introductio in analysin infinitorum, vols. 1-2, Lausanne, 1748

    Chapter 1 puts variables and functions first and introduces exponentials, logarithms and trigonometric functions without calculus; includes e^{ix} = cos x + i sin x.

    Evidence (4)
    • The Introductio, 1748, lays the foundations of analysis; chapter 8 presents Euler's formula Wikipedia, Introductio in analysin infinitorum
    • Euler defined a function and stated that analysis is the study of functions MacTutor, Leonhard Euler
    • Euler Archive, vol. 1, dated 1748 Euler Archive record E101 (bepress metadata)
    • Euler Archive, vol. 2, dated 1748 Euler Archive record E102 (bepress metadata)
  3. 1748

    The Introductio as a textbook

    consolidation

    Euler, Introductio, 1748

    A two-volume survey of the concepts of analysis preliminary to calculus, called the first precalculus book.

    Evidence (1)
    • Considered the first precalculus book; survey of concepts preliminary to calculus Wikipedia, Introductio in analysin infinitorum
  4. 1755

    Institutiones: a fully general definition

    key development

    Euler, Institutiones calculi differentialis, 1755

    Euler restates the function concept in its widest form, building differential calculus on it.

    Evidence (2)
    • In 1755 Euler gave an entirely general definition of a function MacTutor, The function concept
    • Catalogue entry, Institutiones calculi differentialis, 1755 Internet Archive catalogue record 10053431bsb
  5. 1950

    Boyer compares the Introductio with Euclid's Elements

    recognition

    Boyer, lecture at the 1950 International Congress of Mathematicians

    A historian ranks it as the foremost textbook of modern times.

    Evidence (1)
    • Boyer's 1950 ICM lectures compared its influence to Euclid's Elements Wikipedia, Introductio in analysin infinitorum
