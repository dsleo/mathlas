Matrix algebra as an abstract calculus (Cayley)

Eisenstein and Cayley treated arrays of coefficients as algebraic objects with their own addition, product and inverse; Cayley's memoir gave the first abstract definition and showed quaternions embed as matrices.

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1844

    Eisenstein multiplies linear substitutions

    precursor

    F. G. Eisenstein, Theoremes sur les formes cubiques..., J. reine angew. Math. 27 (1844) 75-79

    Eisenstein denotes substitutions by single letters and shows how to add and multiply them, apart from non-commutativity.

    Evidence (2)
    • Topic page says Eisenstein in 1844 treated linear substitutions as forming an algebra and quotes the 1844 paper. MacTutor: Matrices and determinants
    • Crossref lists this Eisenstein paper in Crelle vol. 27, 1844. Crossref record 10.1515/crll.1844.27.75
  2. 1850

    Sylvester coins the word matrix

    precursor

    J. J. Sylvester, Additions to the articles ... On a new class of theorems, Phil. Mag. (3) 37 (1850) 363-370

    Sylvester uses matrix for an oblong arrangement of terms from which determinants are formed.

    Dating or attribution note

    The title matches the follow-up to Sylvester's September 1850 paper; exact page of the word not checked. Reclassified from naming to precursor: it predates this event's first statement (1855) and belongs to the wider field.

    Evidence (3)
    • Topic page says Sylvester first used the term in 1850. MacTutor: Matrices and determinants
    • Biography quotes Feldmann that the term was introduced by Sylvester in 1850. MacTutor: Cayley biography
    • Crossref record for the November 1850 paper, vol. 37, pp. 363-370. Crossref record 10.1080/14786445008646629
  3. 1855

    Cayley's first matrix paper (in French)

    first statement

    A. Cayley, Remarques sur la notation des fonctions algebriques, J. reine angew. Math. 50 (1855) 282-285

    Cayley's first expository paper on matrices introduces the inverse and compounding (multiplication) of arrays.

    Evidence (2)
    • Title, journal, volume 50, pages 282-285, 1855 confirmed. Crossref record 10.1515/crll.1855.50.282
    • Biography quotes Feldmann: the introductory paper, in French in a German periodical, appeared in 1855 and sketches inverse and multiplication. MacTutor: Cayley biography
  4. 1858

    Cayley, A memoir on the theory of matrices

    consolidation

    A. Cayley, A memoir on the theory of matrices, Phil. Trans. R. Soc. 148 (1858) 17-37

    The memoir gives the first abstract definition of a matrix, an algebra of sum, product, scalar multiple and inverse, and the Cayley-Hamilton statement for small cases; it also shows quaternions as matrices.

    Evidence (3)
    • Title, journal and pages 17-37, 1858 confirmed. Crossref record 10.1098/rstl.1858.0002
    • Topic page calls the memoir the first abstract definition of a matrix and describes the algebra and the 2x2 characteristic-equation result. MacTutor: Matrices and determinants
    • Topic page says Cayley noticed quaternions could be represented by matrices in 1858. MacTutor: Abstract linear spaces
