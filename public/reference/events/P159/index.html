1714 · Complex analysis

Cotes: logarithm of complex arguments

Cotes showed log(cos+i sin)=i theta.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1714

    Cotes, Logometria

    first statement

    R. Cotes, Logometria, Phil. Trans. R. Soc. 29 (1714)

    The logarithm of cos + i sin is stated, in geometric language, as proportional to the angle.

    Evidence (2)
    • Phil. Trans. 29(338), March 1714; formula stated geometrically (p. 32 per secondary source) Crossref / Royal Society
    • Cotes published only this one paper in his lifetime MacTutor Cotes
  2. 1714

    Logometria: logarithmic measure of circular arcs

    first application

    R. Cotes, Logometria (1714)

    The relation is used to express circular arcs by logarithms in a work on logarithmic quadratures.

    Evidence (1)
    • Logometria is a work on logarithmic quadratures MacTutor Cotes
  3. 1722

    Cotes, Harmonia mensurarum (posthumous)

    consolidation

    R. Cotes, Harmonia mensurarum, ed. R. Smith, Cambridge (1722)

    Posthumous collection reprinting Logometria and applying the method to integrating rational fractions.

    Evidence (2)
    • Posthumous edition by R. Smith Open Library
    • MacTutor: method of integrating rational fractions MacTutor Cotes
  4. 1748

    Euler, Introductio in analysin infinitorum

    key development

    L. Euler, Introductio in analysin infinitorum (1748)

    Euler gives the exponential form e^{ix} = cos x + i sin x, the analytic form of Cotes' relation.

    Evidence (2)
    • Introductio, 1748 Open Library
    • Euler formula in the Introductio MacTutor Euler
