620 · Numerical analysis

Numerical solution of polynomial equations in China (Ruffini-Horner)

Root-extraction algorithms were extended step by step to cubics and higher-degree polynomial equations.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 620–626

    Wang Xiaotong: numerical solution of cubics

    first statement

    Wang Xiaotong, Jigu suanjing (written before 626)

    The first known Chinese treatment of cubic equations, solved numerically by extending the cube-root algorithm.

    Evidence (2)
    • The problems reduce to cubic equations which Wang solves numerically; no earlier Chinese work on cubics is known. MacTutor, Wang Xiaotong biography
    • Jigu suanjing was written before the year 626. English Wikipedia, Wang Xiaotong
  2. 1050

    Jia Xian: additive-multiplicative root method for any degree

    key development

    Jia Xian, Shi suo suan shu (lost), reported by Yang Hui, 1261

    Generalised root extraction to nth roots and polynomial equations using binomial coefficients from his triangle; essentially the Ruffini-Horner scheme.

    Evidence (1)
    • The Zeng cheng kaifang method for roots and polynomial equations is essentially the Ruffini-Horner method. MacTutor, Jia Xian biography
  3. 1247

    Qin Jiushao: equations up to degree 10

    key development

    Qin Jiushao, Shushu jiuzhang (Mathematical Treatise in Nine Sections), 1247

    Applied the scheme to cubic, quartic and a tenth-degree equation in worked problems.

    Evidence (3)
    • Shushu jiuzhang appeared in 1247 and solves cubics and quartics by the Ruffini-Horner method. MacTutor, Qin Jiushao biography
    • Treatise covers numerical solution up to the tenth degree. English Wikipedia, Qin Jiushao
    • Libbrecht's 1973 study of Qin's Shu-shu chiu-chang. zbMATH 3455178, Libbrecht, MIT Press 1973
  4. 1303

    Zhu Shijie: transformation method to degree 14

    key development

    Zhu Shijie, Siyuan yujian (Jade Mirror of the Four Unknowns), 1303

    Gave a transformation method for numerical solution of equations up to degree 14, based on the same scheme.

    Evidence (1)
    • Contains a transformation method for equations up to degree 14, based on the method rediscovered by Horner and Ruffini. MacTutor, Zhu Shijie biography
  5. 1804

    Ruffini's method

    key development

    Paolo Ruffini, memoir awarded 1804, with refinements 1807 and 1813

    In Europe, Ruffini gave a rule equivalent to the method; it won a prize and was published in 1804.

    Evidence (2)
    • Ruffini's method was awarded first place and published in 1804, with refinements in 1807 and 1813. English Wikipedia, Ruffini's rule
    • Cajori argues that Horner's method was anticipated by Ruffini. Crossref 10.1090/s0002-9904-1911-02072-9, Cajori, Bull. AMS 17 (1911)
  6. 1819

    Horner, A new method of solving numerical equations

    key development

    W. G. Horner, Phil. Trans. R. Soc. 109 (1819)

    The English paper that gave the method its name in the West.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref record of the paper. Crossref 10.1098/rstl.1819.0023
    • MacTutor reports that Fuller found the 1819 article does not contain the method, and that one published by Horner in 1830 does. MacTutor, Horner biography
  7. 1973

    Libbrecht's monograph on thirteenth-century Chinese mathematics

    consolidation

    Ulrich Libbrecht, Chinese Mathematics in the Thirteenth Century, MIT Press, 1973

    Monograph on the thirteenth-century Chinese mathematics in which the numerical equation methods of Qin Jiushao and Zhu Shijie are set out.

    Evidence (1)
    • Open Library record of Libbrecht's 1973 monograph on thirteenth-century Chinese mathematics. Open Library work record
  8. Date unknown

    Square and cube roots in the Nine Chapters

    precursor

    Jiuzhang suanshu (Nine Chapters on the Mathematical Art), root-extraction problems

    The classical Chinese algorithms for square and cube roots, which later authors generalised.

    Evidence (2)
    • Chapter 4 problems extract square and cube roots; the Han-era text was commented on by Liu Hui in 263. MacTutor, Nine chapters
    • Wikipedia lists the Nine Chapters among the sources where Horner's method was already known. English Wikipedia, Horner's method
