499 · Astronomy & trigonometry

Indian sine (jya) and the birth of trigonometric functions

Siddhantic astronomy replaced chords by half-chords, giving the sine; Aryabhata tabulated it.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 140 BCE

    Hipparchus's table of chords

    precursor

    Hipparchus, chord table, lost; reported by later authors

    Greek astronomers tabulate full chords of arcs; the Indian step is to switch to half-chords, i.e. the sine.

    Evidence (2)
    • MacTutor Trigonometric functions: first known table of chords by Hipparchus about 140 BC MacTutor, Trigonometric functions
    • Wikipedia, Aryabhata sine table: earlier tables by Hipparchus, Menelaus and Ptolemy were chord tables, not half-chord Wikipedia, Aryabhata sine table (Aryabhata sine table redirect)
  2. 400–500

    Siddhanta texts (incl. Surya Siddhanta) define sine as half-chord

    precursor

    Siddhantas, 4th-5th century; extant Surya Siddhanta probably revised c. 800

    Astronomical treatises are credited with first defining sine as half of the chord of double the arc, but dating of surviving texts is uncertain.

    Evidence (2)
    • Wikipedia History of trigonometry: Siddhantas of the 4th-5th century first defined the sine as the half-chord relation Wikipedia, History of trigonometry
    • Wikipedia Surya Siddhanta: text dated end of 4th-5th century by most scholars; extant version composed or revised c. 800 Wikipedia, Surya Siddhanta
  3. 499

    Aryabhata gives the first sine table (sine differences)

    first statement

    Aryabhata, Aryabhatiya, Dasagitika verse 12, 499

    Aryabhata tabulates the sine differences of 24 arcs of 3 degrees 45 minutes with radius 3438, the earliest surviving table of half-chords.

    Evidence (3)
    • MacTutor Aryabhata: Aryabhatiya finished 499; table of sines at intervals of 90/24 degrees MacTutor, Aryabhata I
    • Wikipedia Aryabhata sine table: first table of sines, first differences, radius 3438, verse 12 of Dasagitika Wikipedia, Aryabhata sine table
    • MacTutor Trigonometric functions: first actual appearance of the sine in the Hindu work, Aryabhata about 500, jya MacTutor, Trigonometric functions
  4. 665

    Brahmagupta interpolates sine values with a second-order formula

    key development

    Brahmagupta, Khandakhadyaka, 665 (also reproduces the table in 628)

    Brahmagupta uses a second-order difference formula to compute sines between tabulated values; the same table appears in his 628 work.

    Evidence (2)
    • Wikipedia Brahmagupta: in 665 devised second-order interpolation to find new sine values from tabulated ones Wikipedia, Brahmagupta
    • MacTutor Trigonometric functions: the table reproduced in the work of Brahmagupta in 628 MacTutor, Trigonometric functions
  5. 1150

    Bhaskara II gives detailed methods to construct sine tables for any angle

    key development

    Bhaskara II, Siddhantasiromani, 1150

    Bhaskara gives detailed methods for constructing a sine table for any angle, extending earlier Indian tables.

    Evidence (1)
    • MacTutor Trigonometric functions: detailed method for constructing a table of sines for any angle given by Bhaskara in 1150 MacTutor, Trigonometric functions
  6. 1202

    Fibonacci uses sinus rectus arcus; Latin sinus replaces Arabic jiba from jya

    naming

    Fibonacci, Liber Abaci / Practica geometriae; translations of Arabic mathematics

    Indian jya became Arabic jiba then jaib and was rendered Latin sinus; Fibonacci use of sinus rectus arcus helped fix the term.

    Evidence (2)
    • MacTutor Trigonometric functions: jya adopted by Arabs as jiba, then jaib, translated sinus; Fibonacci sinus rectus arcus encouraged universal use MacTutor, Trigonometric functions
    • Wikipedia History of trigonometry (etymology of sine via jya-ardha, jiva, jiba, sinus) Wikipedia, History of trigonometry
  7. 1350–1425

    Madhava of Sangamagrama gives power series for sine and refined sine tables

    key development

    Madhava, c. 1350-1425

    Continued attempts to improve the Aryabhata table culminated in series expansions and a seven- or eight-digit sine table.

    Evidence (1)
    • Wikipedia Aryabhata sine table: improvements culminated in series for sine and cosine by Madhava (c. 1350-c. 1425) Wikipedia, Aryabhata sine table
