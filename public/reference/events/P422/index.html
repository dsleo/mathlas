1911 · Group & representation theory

Undecidability of the word problem for groups

After Dehn posed decision problems for presented groups and Church proposed a logical route, Post, Markov, Boone and Novikov proved word problems insoluble and Higman's embedding theorem tied recursive presentation to finite presentation.

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1911

    Dehn formulates the word problem for presented groups

    first statement

    Dehn, 'Über unendliche diskontinuierliche Gruppen', Mathematische Annalen 71 (1911)

    Dehn states the identity (word) problem for groups given by generators and relations as a question needing a finite decision method.

    Evidence (2)
    • Paper with the word problem stated; year 1911 Crossref record
    • Dehn states the identity, conjugacy and isomorphism problems MacTutor: Word problems
  2. 1912

    Dehn's algorithm solves the word problem for surface-group relators

    first application

    Dehn, 'Transformationen der Kurven auf zweiseitigen Flächen' (1912)

    Dehn gives a direct method (Dehn's algorithm) that solves the word problem for one-relator groups from surfaces in special cases.

    Evidence (1)
    • Dehn's 1912 solution in special cases; year 1912 as given in the history page MacTutor: Word problems
  3. 1947

    Post proves the semigroup word problem insoluble

    key development

    Post, 'Recursive unsolvability of a problem of Thue', Journal of Symbolic Logic 12 (1947)

    Post shows that the word problem for finitely presented semigroups is recursively unsolvable, using the computability notions of the 1930s; Markov obtained the same result independently in 1947.

    Evidence (2)
    • Post 1947 paper, year 1947 Crossref record
    • Post's semigroup result; Markov's independent 1947 proof MacTutor: Word problems
  4. 1955

    Novikov proves a finitely presented group with undecidable word problem

    key development

    Novikov, 'On the algorithmic unsolvability of the word problem in group theory', Trudy Mat. Inst. Steklov 44 (1955)

    Novikov shows that some finitely presented group has an undecidable word problem, the first proof for groups.

    Evidence (1)
    • Novikov 1955 citation, Steklov 44 Wikipedia: Word problem for groups
  5. 1958

    Boone gives an independent proof of undecidability

    key development

    Boone, 'The word problem', Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences 44 (1958)

    Boone proves independently that the word problem for finitely presented groups is unsolvable.

    Evidence (1)
    • Boone 1958 PNAS record Crossref record
  6. 1961

    Higman's embedding theorem

    key development

    Higman, 'Subgroups of finitely presented groups', Proceedings of the Royal Society A (1961)

    Higman shows that every recursively presented group embeds in a finitely presented group, linking recursive and finite presentations.

    Evidence (2)
    • Higman 1961 record Crossref record
    • Higman's HNN-based embedding discussed MacTutor: Word problems
  7. 1966

    Combinatorial group theory monograph

    consolidation

    Magnus, Karrass, Solitar, Combinatorial Group Theory (Interscience, 1966)

    The 1966 monograph gives the first systematic treatment of presentations, decision problems and their solutions.

    Evidence (1)
    • 1966 book record, author listed as Magnus Open Library search
