250 BCE · Geometry

Archimedes' mechanical method for areas and volumes

Balancing figures on a lever (centres of gravity) is used to discover area and volume results later proved by exhaustion.

Also known as: Method of Mechanical Theorems

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 400 BCE–370 BCE

    Democritus asserts the cone and pyramid volumes

    precursor

    Democritus first stated that cone and pyramid are one third of cylinder and prism, a result Archimedes says he discovered by his method.

    Evidence (1)
    • Democritus was first to assert these volumes; Eudoxus first proved them. Heath, The Method of Archimedes (1912), introduction
  2. 250 BCE–212 BCE

    Quadrature of the Parabola outlines the mechanical method

    first statement

    Archimedes, Quadrature of the Parabola (letter to Dositheus)

    Archimedes gives the area of a parabolic segment first by a lever argument and then by exhaustion.

    Evidence (2)
    • He first outlines the mechanical proof in Quadrature of the Parabola next to the geometric one, and gives a fuller account in The Method. Wikipedia, Archimedes
    • Heath's order places the Parabola before the Method. MacTutor, Archimedes
  3. 250 BCE–212 BCE

    Mechanical discovery of the parabola's area

    first application

    Archimedes, Quadrature of the Parabola

    The lever argument is used to find the area of a parabolic segment, later confirmed by exhaustion.

    Evidence (1)
    • Same source as above: the mechanical argument appears alongside the geometric proof. Wikipedia, Archimedes
  4. 250 BCE–212 BCE

    The Method of Mechanical Theorems

    key development

    Archimedes, Method concerning mechanical theorems, letter to Eratosthenes

    Archimedes explains in a letter how balancing slices of figures led him to results such as sphere and cylinder volumes, which he then proved by exhaustion.

    Evidence (3)
    • It is a letter to Eratosthenes and holds the first attested explicit use of indivisibles. Wikipedia, The Method of Mechanical Theorems
    • Archimedes states that some results became clear by a mechanical method before geometric proof. MacTutor, Archimedes
    • Heath's 1912 English edition (scanned). Heath, The Method of Archimedes
  5. 1906

    Heiberg finds the Method in a palimpsest

    key development

    J. L. Heiberg, discovery of the Archimedes Palimpsest, summer 1906

    Heiberg recognised a tenth-century prayer book as carrying Archimedes' lost Method, so the text reached modern readers.

    Evidence (2)
    • In the summer of 1906 Heiberg discovered the manuscript that includes The Method. MacTutor, Archimedes
    • The work was thought lost until its rediscovery in the palimpsest in 1906. Wikipedia, The Method of Mechanical Theorems
  6. 1912

    Heath's English edition

    key development

    T. L. Heath, The Method of Archimedes (Cambridge, 1912)

    Heath's edition made the Method available in English with commentary.

    Evidence (1)
    • Internet Archive record of the 1912 edition. Heath, The Method of Archimedes, recently discovered by Heiberg
  7. Date unknown

    Law of the lever and centres of gravity

    precursor

    Archimedes, On the Equilibrium of Planes

    The mechanical method rests on the lever law and centres of gravity proved in this earlier treatise.

    Evidence (1)
    • The mechanical method relies on the centroid and lever law demonstrated in On the Equilibrium of Planes. Wikipedia, The Method of Mechanical Theorems
