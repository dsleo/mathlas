1965 · Differential equations

Fermi-Pasta-Ulam problem and solitons

Numerical experiments lead to the soliton concept.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1834

    Scott Russell's wave of translation

    precursor

    Russell describes a stable solitary water wave, the physical observation behind the later soliton concept; a precursor only.

    Evidence (1)
    • Wikipedia Soliton history: in 1834 Russell described his wave of translation Wikipedia, Soliton
  2. 1955

    Fermi-Pasta-Ulam numerical lattice experiment

    precursor

    Fermi, Pasta, Ulam and Tsingou, Studies of nonlinear problems, Los Alamos report LA-1940, 1955

    A Los Alamos computer experiment on a nonlinear chain showing unexpected recurrence; the puzzle later explained by solitons. A precursor, not the soliton concept.

    Evidence (2)
    • Wikipedia Soliton article: Zabusky and Kruskal explained the puzzling earlier work of Fermi, Pasta, Ulam and Tsingou Wikipedia, Soliton
    • Wikipedia FPUT article on the 1955 numerical experiment Wikipedia, Fermi-Pasta-Ulam-Tsingou problem
  3. 1965

    Zabusky and Kruskal's 'soliton' paper

    first statement

    Zabusky and Kruskal, Interaction of 'Solitons' in a collisionless plasma and the recurrence of initial states, Physical Review Letters, 1965

    Zabusky and Kruskal show numerically that KdV solitary waves pass through each other unchanged, explaining FPUT recurrence, and name the objects solitons.

    Evidence (2)
    • Article record, PRL 15 (1965) 240 Crossref
    • Wikipedia: in 1965 Zabusky and Kruskal first demonstrated soliton behaviour for the KdV equation Wikipedia, Soliton
  4. 1965

    Coinage of 'soliton' in the title

    naming

    Zabusky and Kruskal, 1965 (same paper as above)

    The name 'Solitons' appears in the 1965 title; this is the same paper, listed again only to record the naming role.

    Evidence (1)
    • Article title (quoted in the record) contains the term 'Solitons' Crossref
  5. 1967

    Inverse scattering solves the KdV initial-value problem

    first application

    Gardner, Greene, Kruskal and Miura, Method for solving the Korteweg-deVries equation, Physical Review Letters, 1967

    The inverse scattering transform is used to solve the KdV initial-value problem, the first exact method for soliton dynamics.

    Evidence (1)
    • Article record, PRL 19 (1967) 1095 Crossref
  6. 1968

    Lax pairs and integrals of nonlinear evolution equations

    key development

    Lax, Integrals of nonlinear equations of evolution and solitary waves, Communications on Pure and Applied Mathematics, 1968

    Lax introduces the operator pair structure that generalises inverse scattering to many soliton-generating systems.

    Evidence (2)
    • Article record, CPAM 1968 Crossref
    • Wikipedia: Lax's work on Lax pairs extended the theory to many soliton-generating systems Wikipedia, Soliton
  7. 1981

    Solitons and the Inverse Scattering Transform

    consolidation

    Ablowitz and Segur, Solitons and the Inverse Scattering Transform, SIAM, 1981

    The first systematic monograph on solitons and inverse scattering.

    Evidence (1)
    • OpenLibrary record: SIAM 1981, author listed as Ablowitz; co-author Segur from the book itself, not checked OpenLibrary search
