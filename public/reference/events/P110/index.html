1350 · Group & representation theory

Oresme: graphing motion (configurations of qualities and motions)

Uniform acceleration was studied and shown by area under a velocity diagram.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1330

    Merton College calculators quantify qualities

    precursor

    Merton School (Heytesbury, Swineshead and others), 1330s-1350s

    Oxford scholars treated intensity and speed of change quantitatively without diagrams.

    Evidence (1)
    • Study of the Merton School's quantification of qualities exists. Sylla 1971, Arch. Hist. Exact Sci. 8, doi:10.1007/BF00327218
  2. 1350

    Oresme's De configurationibus qualitatum et motuum

    first statement

    Nicole Oresme, Tractatus de configurationibus qualitatum et motuum (about 1350)

    Represents a varying quantity by a figure whose area shows total change; proves the mean-speed result with it.

    Evidence (3)
    • Oresme found the equivalence of tabulating and graphing in De configurationibus; first to prove Merton's theorem. MacTutor, Oresme biography
    • Oresme in 1350 described laws of nature as dependence of one quantity on another. MacTutor, The function concept
    • Clagett's edition and study (1968) is indexed. zbMATH 0192.32001
  3. 1482

    Tractatus de latitudinibus formarum printed at Padua

    key development

    Tractatus de latitudinibus formarum, printed 1482

    Printing carried the diagram idea to a wider readership (catalogued under Oresme).

    Evidence (2)
    • Printed 1482 edition catalogued under Oresme with Blasius of Parma questions. Internet Archive ita-bnc-in2-00001198-003
    • Oresme's text was reprinted several times in the following century. MacTutor, Oresme biography
  4. 1638

    Galileo's Discorsi uses the mean-speed argument with a diagram

    first application

    Galileo, Discorsi e dimostrazioni matematiche intorno a due nuove scienze, Leiden, 1638

    Galileo proves the distance rule for uniformly accelerated fall from the mean-speed result, drawing the speed-time figure that Oresme's diagrams anticipated.

    Evidence (2)
    • Discorsi printed 1638. Open Library, Discorsi e dimostrazioni matematiche
    • Page describes the mean speed theorem, Oresme's geometric verification and Galileo's demonstration in the Discorsi. English Wikipedia, Mean speed theorem
  5. 1959

    Clagett's survey of medieval mechanics

    consolidation

    Marshall Clagett, The Science of Mechanics in the Middle Ages, Univ. of Wisconsin Press, 1959

    A modern systematic account of medieval kinematics, including the Merton rules and Oresme's graphs.

    Evidence (2)
    • Book first published 1959. Open Library, The science of mechanics in the Middle Ages
    • Wikipedia's Oresme article cites Clagett's edition and study for Oresme's method of figuring latitudes. English Wikipedia, Nicole Oresme
