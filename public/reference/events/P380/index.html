Model theory beginnings (Löwenheim-Skolem)

Loewenheim and Skolem showed that first-order theories with infinite models have countable ones, opening semantic study of formal theories.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1915

    Löwenheim's downward result in the relational calculus

    first statement

    Löwenheim, 'Über Möglichkeiten im Relativkalkül', Mathematische Annalen 76 (1915)

    Löwenheim shows that a formula with a model has a model on a countable domain, in the relational calculus.

    Evidence (2)
    • 1915 Math. Ann. record Crossref record
    • Löwenheim's 1915 work extended by Skolem to the theorem MacTutor: Skolem biography
  2. 1920

    Skolem's extension to first-order theories

    key development

    Skolem, Löwenheim-Skolem papers (Videnskapsselskapets Skrifter, 1920)

    Skolem extends Löwenheim's result to first-order predicate calculus: a theory with a model has a countable model.

    Evidence (1)
    • Skolem published the Löwenheim-Skolem theorem in 1920 MacTutor: Skolem biography
  3. 1930

    Goedel's completeness theorem makes compactness explicit

    key development

    Kurt Goedel, Die Vollstaendigkeit der Axiome des logischen Funktionenkalkuels, Monatsh. Math. Phys. 37, 1930

    The compactness theorem appears as a lemma, giving the semantic toolkit for Skolem-style arguments.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref record, Monatsh. Math. Phys. 37 (1930), pp. 349-360. Crossref 10.1007/bf01696781
    • Compactness was implicit in Skolem but first published in Goedel's 1930 proof. Wikipedia, Model theory
  4. 1954

    Tarski's 'theory of models'

    naming

    Alfred Tarski, Contributions to the theory of models I, Indag. Math. 57, 1954

    First published use of the phrase theory of models, naming the discipline.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref record, Indag. Math. 57 (1954), pp. 572-581. Crossref 10.1016/s1385-7258(54)50074-0
    • Tarski first used 'Theory of Models' in publication in 1954. Wikipedia, Model theory
  5. 1957

    Tarski-Vaught on elementary extensions

    key development

    Alfred Tarski and Robert L. Vaught, Arithmetical extensions of relational systems, Compositio Math. 13, 1957

    Studies elementary extensions, giving the upward direction and the Tarski-Vaught test for the theorem.

    Evidence (1)
    • zbMATH record, Compositio Math. 13, 82-102 (1957). zbMATH 3148408
  6. 1963

    Robinson's Introduction to Model Theory

    consolidation

    Abraham Robinson, Introduction to Model Theory and to the Metamathematics of Algebra, Amsterdam, 1963

    First textbook with the term model theory in its title.

    Dating or attribution note

    'First textbook' is not checked beyond the catalogue; Tarski's papers and Mal'cev's earlier work are earlier.

    Evidence (1)
    • First published 1963, North-Holland. Open Library record OL1308944W
  7. 1973

    Chang and Keisler's Model Theory

    consolidation

    Chang & Keisler, Model Theory (North-Holland, 1973)

    The 1973 monograph is the standard systematic treatment of model theory and fixes the field's name.

    Evidence (1)
    • 1973 'Model theory' record, author listed as Chang Open Library search
