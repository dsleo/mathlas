Domain theory

Continuous lattices gave models for the lambda calculus.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1960

    Scott's late-1960s work on lambda-calculus semantics

    precursor

    Scott's unpublished late-1960s work on denotational semantics for the lambda calculus is the motivating start of domain theory.

    Dating or attribution note

    Decade only; the 1969 Oxford manuscripts were not checked in a record.

    Evidence (1)
    • Wikipedia (Domain theory): domain theory initiated by Dana Scott in the late 1960s Wikipedia
  2. 1972

    Continuous lattices

    first statement

    Dana Scott, Continuous lattices, in Toposes, Algebraic Geometry and Logic, Lecture Notes in Mathematics 274, 1972

    Scott introduces continuous lattices as the models of computation that became domains.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref: chapter 'Continuous lattices' by Scott, Lecture Notes in Mathematics, 1972 Crossref
  3. 1976

    Data types as lattices

    key development

    Dana Scott, Data types as lattices, SIAM Journal on Computing, 1976

    Scott frames data types as lattices of information, a key step to the domain-theory account of recursive types.

    Evidence (1)
    • SIAM Journal on Computing paper 'Data Types as Lattices', 1976 Crossref
  4. 1976

    Powerdomain construction

    key development

    Gordon Plotkin, A powerdomain construction, SIAM Journal on Computing, 1976

    Plotkin constructs powerdomains for nondeterminism, extending the domain-theoretic toolkit.

    Evidence (1)
    • SIAM Journal on Computing paper 'A Powerdomain Construction', 1976 Crossref
  5. 1995

    Handbook survey of domain theory

    consolidation

    Samson Abramsky, Achim Jung, Domain Theory, in Handbook of Logic in Computer Science, Oxford University Press, vol. 3

    A survey chapter that treats domain theory systematically for computer science.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref: chapter 'Domain Theory' in Handbook of Logic in Computer Science (record year 1995) Crossref
  6. 2003

    Continuous Lattices and Domains monograph

    consolidation

    G. Gierz, K. H. Hofmann, K. Keimel, J. D. Lawson, M. Mislove, D. S. Scott, Continuous Lattices and Domains, Cambridge University Press, 2003

    The reference monograph on continuous lattices and domains.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref: 'Continuous Lattices and Domains', Cambridge University Press, 2003 Crossref
