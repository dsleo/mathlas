2019 · Combinatorics

Lorentzian polynomials and log-concavity

A class of polynomials unifies log-concavity statements across matroids, Hodge theory and discrete convex analysis, giving the strongest Mason conjecture.

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 2015

    Matroid Hodge theory proves log-concavity

    precursor

    K. Adiprasito, J. Huh and E. Katz, Hodge Theory for Combinatorial Geometries, arXiv:1511.02888 (2015)

    The earlier matroid Hodge theorem motivates later Lorentzian-polynomial methods.

    Evidence (1)
    • First public version posted November 2015 arXiv
  2. 2019

    Lorentzian polynomial class developed

    first statement

    P. Brändén and J. Huh, Lorentzian polynomials, arXiv:1902.03719 (2019)

    The preprint defines the class and connects it to matroids and M-convex sets.

    Evidence (1)
    • First public version posted February 2019 arXiv
  3. 2019

    Lorentzian method proves strongest Mason conjecture

    first application

    P. Brändén and J. Huh, Lorentzian polynomials, arXiv:1902.03719 (2019)

    The paper applies Lorentzian-polynomial theory to the homogenized matroid Tutte polynomial and strongest Mason conjecture.

    Evidence (1)
    • The abstract explicitly states these applications arXiv
  4. 2022

    Log-concavity of Schur polynomials

    key development

    Huh, Matherne, Mészáros, St. Dizier, Logarithmic concavity of Schur and related polynomials, 2022, pp. 4411-4427

    A later application of log-concavity methods to Schur-type polynomials, extending the Lorentzian-based approach.

    Dating or attribution note

    Link to the Lorentzian method is taken from the authors' area; not checked against the abstract.
