2005 · Algebra

Boolean function analysis / invariance principle (Mossel-O'Donnell-Oleszkiewicz)

An invariance principle transferred Gaussian results to low-influence Boolean functions, used to settle majority-is-stablest.

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 2005

    Low-influence invariance principle stated

    first statement

    E. Mossel, R. O’Donnell and K. Oleszkiewicz, Noise stability of functions with low influences: invariance and optimality, arXiv:math/0503503 (2005)

    The public preprint proves an invariance principle for low-influence multilinear polynomials.

    Evidence (1)
    • ArXiv dates the public submission to 2005 and states the principle. Original bibliographic record
  2. 2005

    Majority Is Stablest proved

    first application

    E. Mossel, R. O’Donnell and K. Oleszkiewicz, Noise stability of functions with low influences, arXiv:math/0503503 (2005)

    The same preprint applies the invariance principle to the Majority Is Stablest conjecture.

    Evidence (1)
    • The abstract explicitly names the application. Original bibliographic record
  3. 2016

    Majority is stablest: discrete and SoS

    key development

    De, A.; Mossel, E., Majority is stablest: discrete and SoS, Theory of Computing 12 (2016) art. 4, DOI 10.4086/toc.2016.v012a004

    Gives a discrete and sum-of-squares version of the majority-is-stablest invariance argument.

    Evidence (1)
    • zBMATH record: Theory of Computing 12 (2016) art. 4 zBMATH Open record (search API)
  4. 2018

    Invariance principle on the slice

    key development

    Filmus, Y.; Kindler, G., Invariance principle on the slice, ACM Trans. Comput. Theory 10 (2018) art. 11, DOI 10.1145/3186590

    Extends the invariance-principle method from the Boolean cube to the slice.

    Evidence (1)
    • zBMATH record: ACM TOCT 10 (2018) art. 11 zBMATH Open record (search API)
