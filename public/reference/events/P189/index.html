Menelaus: Sphaerica and spherical trigonometry

Menelaus' Sphaerica gave theorems on spherical triangles.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 330 BCE

    Autolycus' On the Moving Sphere

    precursor

    Autolycus of Pitane, On the Moving Sphere

    Autolycus supplied early theorems on the rotating sphere used in astronomy.

    Evidence (1)
    • An important text on spherical geometry was written by Autolycus around 330 BC. MacTutor, Greek astronomy
  2. 100 BCE

    Theodosius' Sphaerics

    precursor

    Theodosius, Sphaerics, c. 100 BC

    Theodosius gives the geometry of the sphere as a supplement to Euclid, with no trigonometry.

    Evidence (2)
    • Sphaerics contains no trigonometry and was written to supplement Euclid with sphere geometry; Theodosius lived close to 100 BC. MacTutor, Theodosius
    • Menelaus' Book 2 largely follows Theodosius' propositions with better proofs. MacTutor, Menelaus
  3. 98–130

    Menelaus defines the spherical triangle and proves his theorem

    first statement

    Menelaus of Alexandria, Sphaerica, Book I and III.1

    Menelaus defines the spherical triangle and proves the collinearity theorem on the sphere, now named after him.

    Evidence (2)
    • He gave the first definition of a spherical triangle, and Book III opens with Menelaus' theorem. MacTutor, Menelaus
    • Wikipedia notes he named the figures trilaterals and proved the theorem. Wikipedia, Menelaus of Alexandria
  4. 98–130

    Euclid-style treatment of spherical triangles

    consolidation

    Menelaus of Alexandria, Sphaerica, Book I

    Book I builds spherical triangles in the way Euclid builds plane triangles, in some respects more completely.

    Evidence (1)
    • He set up the basis for treating spherical triangles as Euclid treated plane ones. MacTutor, Menelaus
  5. 98–130

    Spherical geometry applied to astronomy

    first application

    Menelaus of Alexandria, Sphaerica, Book II

    Book II applies the spherical theorems to astronomical problems.

    Evidence (1)
    • Book 2 applies spherical geometry to astronomy. MacTutor, Menelaus
  6. 850–1100

    Arabic translations and revisions of Sphaerica

    key development

    Arabic versions (including Ibn Iraq, al-Mahani, al-Harawi)

    The Greek text is lost; Sphaerica survives through several Arabic versions, which differ and were studied by al-Tusi and others.

    Evidence (3)
    • Many Arabic translations and commentaries exist and make reconstruction hard. MacTutor, Menelaus
    • Edition of the early translation and the al-Mahani/al-Harawi version. Rashed and Papadopoulos, Menelaus' Spherics (De Gruyter, 2017)
    • Study of Ibn Iraq's treatment of Spherics III.5. Rashed and Papadopoulos, Arabic Sciences and Philosophy (2014)
  7. 1758

    Halley's printed edition

    key development

    E. Halley, Menelai Sphaericorum libri III (Oxford, 1758)

    Halley's edition, built from Hebrew and Arabic manuscripts, first printed the work in the West.

    Evidence (2)
    • Open Library records an edition dated 1758. Menelai Sphaericorum libri III
    • Internet Archive record naming Halley and Costard. Menelai Sphaericorum libri III
  8. 2017

    Modern critical edition

    key development

    Rashed and Papadopoulos, Menelaus' Spherics (2017)

    A scholarly edition with translation of the Arabic versions replaced earlier partial editions.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref and zbMATH record for the 2017 volume. Menelaus' Spherics: Early Translation and al-Mahani/al-Harawi's Version
