1673 · Differential geometry

Huygens: evolutes and the isochronous pendulum

The cycloid's tautochrone property and evolute theory tied curve geometry to pendulum clocks.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 200 BCE

    Apollonius treats evolutes in Conics V

    precursor

    Apollonius, Conics, Book V (c. 200 BC)

    Evolutes appear already in the ancient theory of normals to conics.

    Evidence (1)
    • Apollonius discussed evolutes in Book V of the Conics; Huygens is sometimes credited with first studying them (1673). English Wikipedia, Evolute
  2. 1656–1657

    Pendulum clock begun

    precursor

    Huygens, pendulum regulation, from December 1656; patent 1657

    Huygens turned to pendulum clocks in late 1656 and patented a working model in 1657, the practical aim behind the later mathematics.

    Evidence (1)
    • Interest in the freely suspended pendulum began in December 1656; working model and patent the next year. Wikipedia: Horologium Oscillatorium
  3. 1658

    Huygens' short tract Horologium

    precursor

    Christiaan Huygens, Horologium, The Hague, 1658

    A short public description of the pendulum clock, before the cycloid and evolute theory.

    Evidence (1)
    • The design was published in a short tract titled Horologium (1658). English Wikipedia, Christiaan Huygens
  4. 1659

    Evolute theory and isochronism of the cycloid found

    precursor

    Huygens, October 1659 onward (private)

    Most of the book was composed in three months from October 1659; the cycloid's isochronism was first found with infinitesimal methods, and the evolute theory dates to about the same time.

    Evidence (2)
    • Book composed from October 1659; evolute theory formulated around 1659. Wikipedia: Horologium Oscillatorium
    • Huygens formulated his theory of evolutes around 1659. Wikipedia: Evolute
  5. 1673

    Horologium oscillatorium printed

    first statement

    Christiaan Huygens, Horologium oscillatorium sive de motu pendulorum, Paris, 1673

    The book defines evolutes and involutes, finds those of the cycloid and parabola, and proves the cycloid's isochronism.

    Evidence (3)
    • Archive.org records list the 1673 Paris edition. archive.org item horologium-oscillatorium
    • MacTutor describes the 1673 work: evolutes and involutes defined, evolute of cycloid and parabola found. MacTutor: Christiaan Huygens
    • Part III introduces the evolute as the curve unrolled. Wikipedia: Horologium Oscillatorium
  6. 1673

    Cycloidal cheeks justified by the evolute of the cycloid

    first application

    Huygens, Horologium oscillatorium, 1673

    Huygens used the evolute to show the thin plates in the clock's design give a cycloidal path and hence an isochronous swing.

    Evidence (1)
    • He uses evolutes to justify the cycloidal shape of the plates in Part I. Wikipedia: Horologium Oscillatorium
  7. 1692

    Jakob Bernoulli's general method for evolutes

    key development

    Jakob Bernoulli, c. 1692

    Bernoulli found evolutes of general curves as envelopes of circles of curvature, extending Huygens' special cases.

    Evidence (1)
    • MacTutor: a general method for evolutes as envelope of circles of curvature, and caustics around 1692. MacTutor: Jacob Bernoulli
  8. 1988

    Yoder's study of Huygens' mathematics

    consolidation

    Joella G. Yoder, Unrolling Time: Christiaan Huygens and the Mathematization of Nature, Cambridge UP, 1988

    A modern scholarly study of Huygens' route to the cycloidal pendulum and evolute theory.

    Dating or attribution note

    Subject matter inferred from the title; modern study, not a first treatment.

    Evidence (1)
    • Unrolling Time, 1988. Open Library
