1935 · Algebraic geometry

Hodge theory of harmonic integrals

Harmonic forms represent cohomology on Kaehler manifolds.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1929

    Lefschetz topological ideas for integrals on curves

    precursor

    Lefschetz's 1929 topological work on integrals on curves gave Hodge the ideas he applied.

    Evidence (1)
    • Hodge applied topological ideas from a 1929 paper by Lefschetz on integrals on a curve MacTutor: William Hodge
  2. 1933

    Kähler metric on complex manifolds

    precursor

    E. Kähler, Über eine bemerkenswerte Hermitesche Metrik, Abh. Math. Sem. Univ. Hamburg 9 (1933)

    Introduced the Kähler metric, the geometric setting in which harmonic forms represent cohomology.

    Dating or attribution note

    Link to Hodge theory is from the event summary and general knowledge; the paper was not read. Precursor role only.

    Evidence (1)
    • DOI record: Abh. Math. Sem. Hamburg 9 (1933) Crossref
  3. 1935

    Hodge: harmonic integrals associated with algebraic varieties

    first statement

    W. V. D. Hodge, Harmonic integrals associated with algebraic varieties, Proc. London Math. Soc. (2) 39 (1935)

    First public statement of the harmonic-integral decomposition for algebraic varieties.

    Evidence (2)
    • DOI record: Proc. London Math. Soc. (2) 39 (1935) Crossref
    • Hodge published his theory in 1941 after the earlier papers MacTutor: William Hodge
  4. 1941

    Hodge: The Theory and Applications of Harmonic Integrals

    consolidation

    W. V. D. Hodge, The Theory and Applications of Harmonic Integrals, Cambridge University Press (1941)

    Polished account and first book-length treatment of the theory.

    Evidence (2)
    • Open Library record (1941) Open Library
    • Hodge published a polished account in 1941 in a book MacTutor: William Hodge
