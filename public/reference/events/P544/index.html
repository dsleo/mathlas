2020 · Group & representation theory

MIP*=RE: entangled provers, computability and the Connes embedding problem

Verification with entangled provers was shown to capture exactly the halting-problem class, which refutes Tsirelson's problem and hence the Connes embedding conjecture.

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 2012

    Entangled-prover NEXP lower bound

    precursor

    T. Ito and T. Vidick, A multi-prover interactive proof for NEXP sound against entangled provers, arXiv:1207.0550 (2012)

    A lower bound for MIP* predates the equality with recursively enumerable languages.

    Evidence (1)
    • Public preprint posted July 2012 arXiv
  2. 2019

    NEEXP lower bound for MIP*

    precursor

    A. Natarajan and J. Wright, NEEXP in MIP*, arXiv:1904.05870 (2019)

    The stronger lower bound is part of the proof trajectory toward MIP*=RE.

    Evidence (1)
    • Public preprint posted April 2019 arXiv
  3. 2020

    MIP*=RE theorem posted

    first statement

    Z. Ji, A. Natarajan, T. Vidick, J. Wright and H. Yuen, MIP*=RE, arXiv:2001.04383 (2020)

    The preprint states the equality of entangled multiprover proofs and recursively enumerable languages.

    Evidence (1)
    • First public preprint posted January 2020 arXiv
  4. 2020

    Halting problem reduced to nonlocal-game value

    first application

    Z. Ji et al., MIP*=RE, arXiv:2001.04383 (2020)

    The theorem yields undecidability of entangled game value and a negative answer to Connes embedding.

    Evidence (1)
    • The abstract explicitly states both consequences arXiv
