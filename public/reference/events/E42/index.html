Jones polynomial from operator algebras

A representation of the braid groups arising from von Neumann algebras yields a new knot invariant, later interpreted through quantum field theory.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1985

    Jones polynomial published

    first statement

    V. Jones, A polynomial invariant for knots via von Neumann algebras, Bull. AMS 12 (1985)

    Jones announces a polynomial knot invariant arising from von Neumann algebra methods.

    Evidence (1)
    • Published 1985 knot-invariant article AMS / Crossref
  2. 1985

    Operator algebras applied to knots

    first application

    V. Jones, A polynomial invariant for knots via von Neumann algebras, Bull. AMS 12 (1985)

    The published construction deliberately transfers algebraic operator methods to knot theory.

    Evidence (1)
    • Published 1985 knot-invariant article AMS / Crossref
  3. 1987

    Braid-group and link-polynomial extension

    key development

    V. Jones, Hecke algebra representations of braid groups and link polynomials, Ann. Math. 126 (1987)

    Jones develops Hecke-algebra representations and a two-variable link invariant.

    Evidence (1)
    • Annals article, volume 126 (1987), pages 335–388 Annals of Mathematics
  4. 1987

    Kauffman's monograph On Knots

    consolidation

    Kauffman, L. H., On Knots, Annals of Mathematics Studies 115, 1987

    A book-length treatment of knot polynomials built on the bracket and state-model approach that led to the Jones polynomial.
