≈ 1751 · Algebra

Spectral ideas: eigenvalues from vibrating systems and quadratic forms

The eigenvalue idea first appeared in d'Alembert's treatment of a loaded string and Sturm's extension to differential systems, then Cauchy proved symmetric matrices diagonalise, joining quadratic forms with spectral theory.

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1751–1765

    Euler on principal axes of a rotating body

    precursor

    Euler, Du mouvement de rotation des corps solides autour d'un axe variable (written 1751, published 1765, Mem. Acad. Berlin 14)

    Euler proves every rigid body has a principal axis of rotation, an early eigenvector problem.

    Evidence (2)
    • In 1751 Euler proved any body has a principal axis of rotation; Lagrange realised the axes are eigenvectors of the inertia matrix Wikipedia, Eigenvalues and eigenvectors
    • Euler Archive E292: written 1751, published 1765 in Mem. Acad. Berlin 14, pp. 154-193 Euler Archive E292
  2. 1839

    Cauchy's équation caractéristique

    naming

    Cauchy, Mémoire sur l'intégration des équations linéaires, Comptes rendus 8, 1839

    Cauchy coined 'characteristic root', source of the terms characteristic equation and later eigenvalue.

    Evidence (1)
    • Cauchy coined racine caractéristique; his term survives in characteristic equation; 1839 memoir quoted Wikipedia, Eigenvalues and eigenvectors
  3. 1855

    Hermite extends to Hermitian matrices

    key development

    Hermite, 1855

    Hermite extends Cauchy's real-symmetric result to the complex Hermitian case.

    Evidence (1)
    • This was extended by Charles Hermite in 1855 to Hermitian matrices Wikipedia, Eigenvalues and eigenvectors
  4. 1904

    Hilbert introduces 'eigen'

    naming

    Hilbert, first communication on integral equations, Nachrichten Göttingen, 1904, pp. 49-91

    Hilbert, studying integral operators as infinite matrices, was the first to use the German word eigen.

    Evidence (1)
    • Hilbert was the first to use the German word eigen; the cited text is a 1904 Göttingen Nachrichten paper Wikipedia, Eigenvalues and eigenvectors
  5. Date unknown

    Cauchy: real symmetric matrices diagonalise

    first statement

    Cauchy, early 19th-century work on quadrics and symmetric systems (Hawkins 1975 analyses it)

    Cauchy classified quadric surfaces and showed the result generalises to arbitrary dimension, giving real roots for symmetric systems.

    Evidence (2)
    • In the early 19th century Cauchy saw how the work could classify quadric surfaces and generalised it to arbitrary dimensions Wikipedia, Eigenvalues and eigenvectors
    • Cauchy proved the spectral theorem for symmetric matrices (cites Hawkins, Cauchy and the spectral theory of matrices, 1975) Wikipedia, Spectral theorem
  6. Date unknown

    Sturm and Liouville on second-order differential eigenproblems

    key development

    Sturm, memoir on linear differential equations of the second order, Journal de Mathématiques Pures et Appliquées, 1836 (Collected Works edition)

    Sturm and Liouville develop eigenvalue problems for differential operators, bringing Fourier's ideas to Cauchy's attention.

    Evidence (2)
    • Sturm elaborated on Fourier's ideas and brought them to Cauchy; Liouville studied similar problems, giving Sturm-Liouville theory Wikipedia, Eigenvalues and eigenvectors
    • Sturm is best remembered for the Sturm-Liouville problem; elected to the Academy 1836, a period of his differential equation results MacTutor, Charles-François Sturm
