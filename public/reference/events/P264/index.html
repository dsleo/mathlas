1900 · Combinatorics

Algebraic combinatorics: Young tableaux and symmetric functions

Symmetric functions and tableaux are linked to representations (Schensted, Stanley).

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1900

    Young: 'On quantitative substitutional analysis'

    first statement

    Alfred Young, On quantitative substitutional analysis, Proc. London Math. Soc. (1) 33 (1900) 97-145

    Introduces the tableaux (now Young tableaux) to study the symmetric group.

    Evidence (3)
    • Young 1900 Proc. LMS record Crossref
    • Tableaux introduced by Young in 1900 Wikipedia, Young tableau
    • Young's work on substitutional analysis MacTutor, Young biography
  2. 1961

    Schensted: longest increasing subsequences via tableau insertion

    first application

    Craige Schensted, Longest increasing and decreasing subsequences, Canad. J. Math. 13 (1961) 179-191

    Applies a tableau-insertion operation to find longest increasing subsequences of permutations, the first combinatorial algorithm on tableaux in this line.

    Evidence (2)
    • Schensted 1961 Canad. J. Math. record Crossref
    • Operation from Schensted's 1961 study of longest increasing subsequences Wikipedia, Plactic monoid
  3. 1970

    Knuth: generalized Young tableaux and the tableau algebra

    key development

    Donald E. Knuth, Permutations, matrices, and generalized Young tableaux, Pacific J. Math. 34 (1970) 709-727

    Generalises Schensted's insertion to two-line arrays (the RSK correspondence) and discovers the plactic monoid as a tableau algebra.

    Evidence (2)
    • Knuth 1970 Pacific J. Math. record Crossref
    • Plactic monoid discovered by Knuth in 1970 Wikipedia, Plactic monoid
  4. 1981

    Lascoux and Schutzenberger name the plactic monoid

    naming

    Alain Lascoux and Marcel-Paul Schutzenberger, Le monoide plaxique, in Noncommutative Structures in Algebra and Geometric Combinatorics, CNR Rome (1981)

    Lascoux and Schutzenberger coin the name 'monoide plaxique' and study the associative product on tableaux.

    Evidence (1)
    • Plactic monoid named 'monoide plaxique' by Lascoux-Schutzenberger 1981 Wikipedia, Plactic monoid
  5. 1997

    Fulton, Young Tableaux

    consolidation

    William Fulton, Young Tableaux, with Applications to Representation Theory and Geometry, Cambridge University Press (1997)

    A systematic monograph on tableaux and their applications to representations.

    Evidence (1)
    • Fulton 1997 book cited in Wikipedia (ISBN given) Wikipedia, Young tableau
