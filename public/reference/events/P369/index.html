1906 · Probability

Markov chains

Markov studied dependent trials and showed the law of large numbers extends to chains, later applying them to letter sequences.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1906

    Markov's first paper on chains

    first statement

    Markov, A. A., Rasprostranenie zakona bol'shih chisel na velichiny, zavisyashchie drug ot druga, Izvestiya SPb (1906)

    Markov shows that the law of large numbers extends to a chain of dependent trials.

    Evidence (1)
    • First paper on Markov chains published 1906; reference cited Wikipedia: Markov chain, History
  2. 1913

    Markov's statistical study of Eugene Onegin

    first application

    Markov, A. A., Versuche einer statistischen Untersuchung uber den Text des Romans 'Eugen Onegin' (1913)

    Markov applies the chain model to the letter sequence of a literary text.

    Evidence (1)
    • The original Onegin statistical study appeared in the Imperial Academy bulletin 7(3) (1913), pp. 153–162. Math-Net.Ru digitized original
  3. 1931

    Kolmogorov's analytical methods for Markov processes

    key development

    Kolmogorov, A., Uber die analytischen Methoden in der Wahrscheinlichkeitsrechnung, Mathematische Annalen (1931)

    Kolmogorov's 1931 analytic method for Markov processes underpins the continuous-time theory.

    Dating or attribution note

    Scope of Markov-process content not read in the text.

    Evidence (1)
    • Paper exists with this title, journal and year Crossref
  4. 1953

    Doob's Stochastic processes

    consolidation

    Doob, J. L., Stochastic processes (1953)

    Doob's 1953 monograph gives a systematic treatment of Markov processes within stochastic process theory.

    Evidence (1)
    • Monograph with this title and year zbMATH Open
