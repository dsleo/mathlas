1976 · Combinatorics

Computers enter proof: four colour theorem and the Kepler conjecture

Exhaustive case analysis by computer is accepted as proof in graph theory, and later whole proofs are re-verified in proof assistants.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1969

    Heesch's computer-search methods

    precursor

    Heesch, H., Untersuchungen zum Vierfarbenproblem, Bibliographisches Institut, 1969

    Heesch developed computer-searched discharging methods that later formed part of the Appel-Haken proof.

    Dating or attribution note

    The book record was verified; its content was not read.

    Evidence (2)
    • Untersuchungen zum Vierfarbenproblem, Bibliographisches Institut, 1969 Open Library record
    • Heesch was first to use discharging and developed computer methods for a proof Wikipedia, Four color theorem
  2. 1976

    Appel and Haken announce a computer-assisted proof

    first statement

    K. Appel and W. Haken, Every planar map is four colorable, Bull. Amer. Math. Soc. 82 (1976), 711-712

    Appel and Haken announce, in a two-page note, a proof of the four colour theorem that relies on a large computer analysis.

    Dating or attribution note

    Full papers are Illinois J. Math. 21 (1977), already the first_application. Note text not read; computer role taken from Wikipedia.

    Evidence (2)
    • zbMATH lists the note in Bull. AMS 82 (1976), pp. 711-712. zbMATH Open document 3517182
    • Announced 21 June 1976 as a computer-aided proof. Wikipedia, Four color theorem
  3. 1977

    Appel-Haken computer-assisted proof of the four colour theorem

    first application

    Appel, K., Haken, W., Every planar map is four colorable. Part I: Discharging, Illinois Journal of Mathematics, 1977

    The first major theorem proved with extensive computer assistance, announced in 1976 and published in 1977.

    Evidence (2)
    • Every planar map is four colorable, Part I: Discharging, Illinois J. Math., 1977 Crossref record
    • Appel and Haken announced the proof on June 21, 1976 and it was the first major theorem proved with extensive computer assistance Wikipedia, Four color theorem
  4. 1989

    Appel and Haken, 'Every planar map is four colorable' (book)

    consolidation

    K. Appel, W. Haken, Every Planar Map is Four Colorable, Contemporary Mathematics 98, Providence: AMS, 1989

    The book-length presentation of the computer-assisted proof of the four colour theorem, after the 1977 Illinois papers.

    Evidence (1)
    • zbMATH record 'Every planar map is four colorable', Appel and Haken, Contemp. Math. 98, AMS 1989, Zbl 0681.05027 zbMATH Open API
  5. 1997

    Simpler computer proof with 633 configurations

    key development

    Robertson, N., Sanders, D. P., Seymour, P. D., Thomas, R., The four-colour theorem, Journal of Combinatorial Theory, Series B, 1997

    Reduces the unavoidable set of reducible configurations to 633 and gives a quadratic-time coloring algorithm.

    Evidence (2)
    • The four-colour theorem, JCTB, 1997 Crossref record
    • The 1997 proof reduced the configurations to 633 Wikipedia, Four color theorem
  6. 2005

    Hales's computer-assisted proof of the Kepler conjecture

    key development

    Hales, T. C., A proof of the Kepler conjecture, Annals of Mathematics, 2005

    The published version of a proof by exhaustion that uses extensive computer calculation; the proof was announced in 1998.

    Evidence (2)
    • A proof of the Kepler conjecture, Ann. Math., 2005 Crossref record
    • Hales announced the proof in August 1998 with 250 pages of notes and 3 GB of programs Wikipedia, Kepler conjecture
  7. 2005

    Coq formalisation of the four colour theorem

    key development

    The four colour theorem is formalised in the Coq proof assistant, removing the need to trust the external programs.

    Evidence (1)
    • Werner and Gonthier formalised a proof of the four colour theorem inside Coq in 2005 Wikipedia, Four color theorem
