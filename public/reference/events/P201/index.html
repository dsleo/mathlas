1948 · Information & cryptography

Information theory

Entropy and channel capacity were defined, with coding theorems for noisy communication.

Also known as: Shannon information theory

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1924

    Nyquist on telegraph speed and intelligence

    precursor

    Nyquist H., Certain factors affecting telegraph speed, Bell System Tech. J. 3, 324-346, 1924

    Nyquist's telegraph-speed analysis quantified 'intelligence' and the line speed at which it can be transmitted.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref record: Bell System Tech. J. 3, 324-346, 1924 Crossref
    • Wikipedia notes Nyquist's 1924 quantification of intelligence and line speed Wikipedia, Information theory (History)
  2. 1928

    Hartley's measure of information

    precursor

    Hartley R. V. L., Transmission of information, Bell System Tech. J. 7, 535-563, 1928

    Hartley used the word 'information' as a measurable quantity based on the number of distinguishable message sequences.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref record: Bell System Tech. J. 7, 535-563, 1928 Crossref
    • Wikipedia describes Hartley's 1928 quantitative use of 'information' Wikipedia, Information theory (History)
  3. 1948

    Shannon's mathematical theory of communication

    first statement

    Shannon C. E., A mathematical theory of communication, Bell System Tech. J. 27, 379-423 and 623-656, 1948

    Shannon defined entropy and channel capacity and proved coding theorems for noisy communication.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref record: Bell System Tech. J. 27, 379-423, 1948 Crossref
    • MacTutor describes the 1948 paper as founding the subject MacTutor, Claude Shannon biography
  4. 1949

    Shannon: communication in the presence of noise

    key development

    Shannon C. E., Communication in the presence of noise, Proc. IRE 37(1), 10-21, 1949

    Follow-up on noisy channels that refined the capacity analysis for continuous signals.

    Dating or attribution note

    Content is inferred from the title and Shannon's known programme; the text was not checked, so treat the claim as provisional.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref record: Proc. IRE 37(1), 10-21, 1949 Crossref
  5. 1949

    Shannon: communication theory of secrecy systems

    first application

    Shannon C. E., Communication theory of secrecy systems, Bell System Tech. J., 1949

    Entropy and equivocation were applied to cryptographic secrecy systems, a first purposeful use of the theory beyond telecommunication.

    Dating or attribution note

    Application claim rests on the paper's title; content not checked in this run.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref record: Bell System Tech. J., 1949 Crossref
  6. 1968

    Gallager, Information Theory and Reliable Communication

    consolidation

    Gallager R. G., Information Theory and Reliable Communication, 1968

    A book-length treatment of coding theorems and reliable communication, consolidating the subject.

    Evidence (1)
    • Open Library record: 1968, Springer-Verlag Open Library
