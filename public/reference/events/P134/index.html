300 BCE · Geometry

Euclid's Elements: axiomatic geometry

Results from earlier geometers were arranged as proofs from stated definitions, postulates and common notions in one treatise.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 300 BCE

    Euclid composes the Elements

    first statement

    Euclid, Elements (Stoicheia), Alexandria, c. 300 BC

    Euclid arranges definitions, postulates and common notions and proves geometry deductively from them.

    Evidence (2)
    • Euclid lived c. 325 - c. 265 BC; Proclus places him under the first Ptolemy MacTutor Euclid
    • Historical estimate c. 300 BCE (not a bibliographic date) MacTutor extra
  2. 300 BCE

    The Elements as the systematic textbook of geometry

    consolidation

    Euclid, Elements

    The treatise is itself the systematic treatment and centred mathematics teaching for about two thousand years.

    Evidence (1)
    • Elements the centre of mathematical teaching for 2000 years MacTutor Euclid
  3. 1482

    First printed Elements (Campanus, Ratdolt, Venice)

    key development

    Euclid, Elements, Latin ed. Campanus, Venice: Ratdolt (1482)

    The first printed edition made the text widely available.

    Evidence (2)
    • First printed edition 1482 Open Library
    • MacTutor: first printed 1482 MacTutor Euclid
  4. 1883–1888

    Heiberg's critical edition (1883-1888)

    key development

    J. L. Heiberg, Euclidis Opera omnia, vol. 1 (Teubner, 1883)

    Standard critical text of the Greek Elements.

    Evidence (1)
    • Heiberg edition archive.org
  5. 1908

    Heath's translation

    key development

    T. L. Heath, The Thirteen Books of Euclid's Elements (1908)

    Standard English translation with commentary.

    Evidence (1)
    • Heath translation archive.org
  6. Date unknown

    Hippocrates of Chios compiles an earlier Elements

    precursor

    Hippocrates of Chios (c. 470 - c. 410 BC), Elements (lost)

    Proclus records Hippocrates as the first to compile an Elements; the work is lost.

    Evidence (1)
    • Hippocrates recorded as first to compile an Elements (quoting Proclus) MacTutor Hippocrates
