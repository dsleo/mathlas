1894 · Statistics & decision theory

Biometric mathematical statistics

Pearson developed correlation and chi-square for biological data and founded Biometrika.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1889

    Galton: Co-relations and their measurement

    precursor

    Galton, Proc. R. Soc. 45, read 1888, pub. 1889

    Galton introduced the measure of co-relation from anthropometric data, the starting point for Pearson's correlation work.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref: Proceedings of the Royal Society 45, issued 1889. Crossref
    • Wikipedia: the correlation coefficient was first developed by Bravais and Galton, then defined by Pearson as a product-moment. Wikipedia: Karl Pearson
  2. 1892

    Weldon applies correlation to biological populations

    precursor

    Weldon, Certain correlated variations in Crangon vulgaris, Proc. R. Soc. 51, 1892

    Weldon measured correlated variation in a marine animal, a first biological application of Galton's method.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref: Proceedings of the Royal Society 51, 1892. Crossref
    • MacTutor: Weldon was a pioneer applying statistics to biology and a founder of Biometrika. MacTutor Biographies/Weldon
  3. 1894

    Pearson: Contributions to the mathematical theory of evolution

    first statement

    Pearson, Phil. Trans. R. Soc. A 185, 1894

    The start of Pearson's series of mathematical memoirs on heredity and evolution, fitting asymmetrical frequency curves to biological data.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref: Philosophical Transactions A, 1894. Crossref
    • MacTutor: eighteen papers titled Mathematical Contributions to the Theory of Evolution, 1893-1912, hold his best work. MacTutor Biographies/Pearson
  4. 1896

    Pearson: regression, heredity and panmixia (product-moment correlation)

    key development

    Pearson, Mathematical contributions to the theory of evolution III, Phil. Trans. A 187, 1896

    Pearson defined the product-moment correlation coefficient and linked it to regression.

    Dating or attribution note

    That the product-moment coefficient is first defined in this specific paper rests on standard accounts.

    Evidence (3)
    • Crossref: Philosophical Transactions A 187, 1896. Crossref
    • Wikipedia: Pearson defined the correlation coefficient as a product-moment and studied its relation to linear regression. Wikipedia: Karl Pearson
    • MacTutor: the series contains contributions to regression analysis and the correlation coefficient. MacTutor Biographies/Pearson
  5. 1900

    Pearson: the chi-square criterion

    key development

    Pearson, On the criterion that a given system of deviations ... random sampling, Phil. Mag. 50, 1900

    Pearson gave the chi-square goodness-of-fit test to judge whether deviations are consistent with random sampling.

    Evidence (3)
    • Crossref: Philosophical Magazine 50, pp. 157-175, 1900. Crossref
    • zbMATH record, 1900. zbMATH Open
    • MacTutor: the series includes the chi-square test of 1900. MacTutor Biographies/Pearson
  6. 1901

    Biometrika founded

    institution

    Weldon, Pearson and Galton, Biometrika vol. 1, 1901

    A journal named for the field, founded to develop statistical theory for biology.

    Evidence (3)
    • Crossref: 'The Scope of Biometrika', Biometrika 1, 1901. Crossref
    • Wikipedia: Pearson, Weldon and Galton founded Biometrika in 1901. Wikipedia: Karl Pearson
    • MacTutor: Biometrika came about after the Royal Society resisted his 1900 paper. MacTutor Biographies/Pearson
  7. 1906

    Elderton: Frequency Curves and Correlation

    consolidation

    Elderton, Frequency-curves and correlation, 1906

    A textbook account of fitting Pearson's frequency curves and measuring correlation.

    Evidence (1)
    • Internet Archive record dated 1906. Internet Archive
  8. 1911

    Department of Applied Statistics at University College London

    institution

    UCL, Galton Chair of Eugenics and Department of Applied Statistics, 1911

    Pearson's Biometric Laboratory and Galton's Eugenics laboratory merged into a department he headed as first Galton Professor.

    Evidence (1)
    • MacTutor: in 1911 the two laboratories were combined into the Department of Applied Statistics, with Pearson as Galton Professor. MacTutor Biographies/Pearson
