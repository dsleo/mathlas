1874 · Logic & foundations

Cantor's set theory

Cantor's study of exceptional sets for trigonometric series led to countability, uncountability of the reals, and then transfinite ordinals and cardinals.

Also known as: Transfinite set theory

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1872

    Cantor's derived sets in the uniqueness of trigonometric series

    precursor

    G. Cantor, Ueber die Ausdehnung eines Satzes aus der Theorie der trigonometrischen Reihen, Math. Ann. 5, 1872

    Cantor extended uniqueness of trigonometric series to exceptional point sets defined by repeated derived sets.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref: Math. Ann. 5 (1872). Crossref
    • Set theory arose from the study of singularities of functions in analysis. Wikipedia, Arithmetization of analysis
  2. 1873

    Uncountability of the reals found in correspondence

    precursor

    Cantor had proved the reals are not countable by December 1873, in discussion with Dedekind.

    Evidence (1)
    • Proved by December 1873 and published in 1874; letters with Dedekind. MacTutor, Cantor biography
  3. 1874

    Cantor's proof that the reals are uncountable

    first statement

    G. Cantor, Ueber eine Eigenschaft des Inbegriffs aller reellen algebraischen Zahlen, Crelle 77, 1874

    Showed the algebraic numbers are countable but the real numbers are not, with an implicit one-to-one correspondence idea.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref: Crelle 77 (1874). Crossref
    • The 1874 paper uses a one-one correspondence implicitly. MacTutor, Cantor biography
  4. 1878

    Ein Beitrag zur Mannigfaltigkeitslehre

    naming

    G. Cantor, Ein Beitrag zur Mannigfaltigkeitslehre, Crelle 84, 1878

    Cantor named his subject 'Mannigfaltigkeitslehre' in a paper comparing the size of sets by one-to-one correspondence.

    Dating or attribution note

    First use of the name in a title is inferred from the record; earlier titles were not exhaustively checked.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref: Crelle 84 (1878). Crossref
  5. 1883

    Transfinite ordinals in Punktmannichfaltigkeiten V and Grundlagen

    key development

    G. Cantor, Ueber unendliche, lineare Punktmannichfaltigkeiten V, Math. Ann. 21, 1883; separately as Grundlagen einer allgemeinen Mannigfaltigkeitslehre

    The fifth paper of the series introduced the transfinite numbers as a systematic extension of the naturals.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref: Math. Ann. 21 (1883), pp. 545-591. Crossref
    • The fifth paper of the six, 1883, also issued as a monograph. Wikipedia, Georg Cantor
  6. 1891

    Cantor's diagonal argument

    key development

    G. Cantor, Ueber eine elementare Frage der Mannigfaltigkeitslehre, Jahresbericht der DMV 1, 1891

    A short note with the diagonal argument showing there is no largest cardinality.

    Evidence (1)
    • Cited as Jahresbericht DMV 1 (1891), pp. 75-78. Wikipedia, Georg Cantor (reference list)
  7. 1895–1897

    Beitrage zur Begrundung der transfiniten Mengenlehre

    consolidation

    G. Cantor, Beitrage zur Begrundung der transfiniten Mengenlehre, Math. Ann. 46, 1895 and 49, 1897

    Cantor's systematic two-part presentation of cardinals and ordinals; also the first appearance of 'Mengenlehre' in a title in this search.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref: Math. Ann. 46 (1895), second part 49 (1897). Crossref
    • Second part. Crossref
  8. 1908

    Zermelo's axiomatisation of set theory

    key development

    Zermelo, Untersuchungen ueber die Grundlagen der Mengenlehre I, Math. Ann. 65 (1908)

    The first axiom system for set theory, made in response to the paradoxes of naive set theory.

    Dating or attribution note

    The 'first axiom system' wording is the standard account; the paper's text was not read.

    Evidence (2)
    • Zermelo 1908 paper recorded in Crossref. Crossref
    • Wikipedia: axiom systems proposed in the early 20th century after the paradoxes, Zermelo-Fraenkel being best known. English Wikipedia, Set theory
