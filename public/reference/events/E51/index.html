≈ 2008 · Combinatorics

The polynomial method in combinatorics

Low-degree polynomials vanishing on a point set give short proofs of the finite-field Kakeya problem and the Erdos distinct-distances problem in the plane.

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 2008

    Dvir applies polynomials to finite-field Kakeya

    first application

    Z. Dvir, On the size of Kakeya sets in finite fields, arXiv:0803.2336 (2008)

    A low-degree polynomial argument proves the finite-field Kakeya bound.

    Evidence (1)
    • Public preprint submitted in March 2008 arXiv
  2. 2010

    Guth–Katz polynomial partitioning

    key development

    L. Guth and N. H. Katz, On the Erdős distinct distances problem in the plane, arXiv:1011.4105 (2010)

    A different polynomial technique attacks the Erdős distinct-distance problem.

    Evidence (1)
    • Public preprint submitted in November 2010 arXiv
  3. 2016

    Guth's Polynomial Methods in Combinatorics

    consolidation

    L. Guth, Polynomial Methods in Combinatorics, University Lecture Series 64 (2016)

    The AMS lecture-series volume gives a named systematic treatment of several polynomial methods.

    Evidence (1)
    • AMS named lecture-series book, 2016 AMS book record
