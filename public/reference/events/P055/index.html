1958 · Topology

K-theory and Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch: algebraic geometry enters topology

A construction made for a Riemann-Roch theorem in algebraic geometry (a Grothendieck group of bundles) is imported into topology as a generalised cohomology theory.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1954

    Hirzebruch's arithmetic genera and Riemann-Roch

    precursor

    Hirzebruch, F., Arithmetic genera and the theorem of Riemann-Roch for algebraic varieties (1954)

    Hirzebruch's topological route to Riemann-Roch (for algebraic varieties) is the precursor of the later K-theory approach.

    Evidence (1)
    • MacTutor Hirzebruch biography names the 1954 paper as the source of the Hirzebruch-Riemann-Roch theorem MacTutor, Friedrich Hirzebruch
  2. 1957

    Grothendieck's homological work behind the Riemann-Roch theorem

    precursor

    Grothendieck, A., Sur quelques points d'algèbre homologique, Tohoku Mathematical Journal 9 (1957)

    Grothendieck's homological algebra behind the new Riemann-Roch statement; the public version is the Borel-Serre exposition of 1958.

    Evidence (2)
    • Tohoku Mathematical Journal record for Sur quelques points d'algèbre homologique I, year 1957 Crossref
    • Wikipedia GRR article: Grothendieck's version conveyed in a letter around 1956-57 and made public at Bonn in 1957 Wikipedia, Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch theorem
  3. 1958

    Borel-Serre exposition of Grothendieck's Riemann-Roch

    first statement

    Borel, A. and Serre, J.-P., Le théorème de Riemann-Roch, Bull. Soc. Math. France 86 (1958)

    The first published statement of Grothendieck's generalised Riemann-Roch theorem, in the Borel-Serre exposition.

    Evidence (2)
    • Bulletin de la Société Mathématique de France record for Borel-Serre, year 1958 Crossref
    • Wikipedia GRR article: the final published paper was in effect the Borel-Serre exposition Wikipedia, Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch theorem
  4. 1959

    Atiyah-Hirzebruch Riemann-Roch for differentiable manifolds

    first application

    Atiyah, M. and Hirzebruch, F., Riemann-Roch theorems for differentiable manifolds, Bull. AMS 65 (1959)

    K-theory is used to prove a Riemann-Roch type theorem for differentiable manifolds: the first topological application.

    Evidence (2)
    • Bulletin of the AMS record for Atiyah-Hirzebruch, year 1959 Crossref
    • MacTutor Atiyah biography: the K-theory technique was first developed with Hirzebruch MacTutor, Michael Atiyah
  5. 1961

    Atiyah-Hirzebruch: topological K-theory

    key development

    M. F. Atiyah and F. Hirzebruch, 'Vector bundles and homogeneous spaces', Proc. Sympos. Pure Math. 3 (Differential Geometry), AMS, 1961, 7-38

    Atiyah and Hirzebruch define K-theory for topological spaces as a generalised cohomology theory, carrying Grothendieck's construction into topology.

    Evidence (1)
    • Atiyah, Hirzebruch, Vector bundles and homogeneous spaces, Proc. Sympos. Pure Math. 3 (1961) 7-38, DOI 10.1090/pspum/003/0139181 Crossref
  6. 1964

    Atiyah's K-theory lecture notes at Harvard

    consolidation

    M. F. Atiyah, K-Theory, Harvard University lecture notes (1964)

    Atiyah's Harvard lecture notes give the first systematic treatment of K-theory; a 1967 Benjamin book followed.

    Evidence (2)
    • Open Library work record: K-theory by Atiyah, Harvard University, 1964 Open Library
    • Open Library record: K-theory, W. A. Benjamin, 1967 Open Library
