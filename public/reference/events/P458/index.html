Complex numbers from the cubic: Cardano and Bombelli

The Cardano-Tartaglia cubic formula gave square roots of negative numbers in the irreducible case, which Bombelli analysed.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1539

    Cardano's letter to Tartaglia

    precursor

    Cardano to Tartaglia, 4 August 1539 (private letter)

    Private exchange in which the formula's failure condition appears, with negative numbers under roots.

    Evidence (1)
    • Cardan hit square roots of negative numbers while solving cubics; letter of 4 August 1539. MacTutor, Cardan biography
  2. 1545

    Ars Magna

    first statement

    Gerolamo Cardano, Artis magnae sive de regulis algebraicis liber unus, Nuremberg 1545

    Prints the cubic and quartic solutions and the first calculation with square roots of negatives, product 5+sqrt(-15) times 5-sqrt(-15).

    Evidence (3)
    • Gives the first calculation with complex numbers in Ars Magna. MacTutor, Cardan biography
    • 1545 edition scan. Internet Archive hieronymicardan00card
    • Study of the irreducible case in Ars Magna. zbMATH 1329.01017 (Confalonieri, 2015)
  3. 1572

    Bombelli's L'Algebra

    first application

    Rafael Bombelli, L'Algebra, Bologna 1572

    First rules for adding and multiplying such numbers; uses them to get correct real roots from the cubic formula.

    Evidence (4)
    • First to write the rules for addition, subtraction and multiplication of complex numbers; real solutions from the formula in the irreducible case. MacTutor, Bombelli biography
    • L'Algebra 1572 first three books published. MacTutor, Bombelli biography
    • Record of the 1572 book. Internet Archive sucho-id-l-algebra-parte-maggiore-dellarimetica
    • Study. zbMATH 1023.01005 (La Nave and Mazur, Reading Bombelli, 2002)
  4. 1637

    Descartes introduces the word "imaginary"

    naming

    René Descartes, La Géométrie (appendix to Discours de la méthode), Leiden 1637

    Descartes labels roots of negative quantities as imaginary, the origin of the standard name.

    Dating or attribution note

    The scan is the 1637 volume; the specific passage was not read here, the coinage rests on Wikipedia.

    Evidence (2)
    • Wikipedia credits Descartes in 1637 with coining the term imaginary for such quantities. English Wikipedia, Complex number
    • Internet Archive scan of the 1637 Discours de la méthode volume that carries La Géométrie. Internet Archive discoursdelamet00desca
  5. 1799

    Wessel's geometric representation of complex numbers

    key development

    Caspar Wessel, Om directionens analytiske betegning, Copenhagen Academy, 1799

    Wessel represents complex numbers as points or directed segments of a plane, an early geometric interpretation that went largely unnoticed.

    Evidence (2)
    • Wikipedia dates the first description of the complex plane to Wessel in 1799 and says it was largely unnoticed. English Wikipedia, Complex number
    • zbMATH lists the 1896 Danish reprint of Wessel's Om directionens analytiske Betegning; the original appearance in 1799 rests on Wikipedia. zbMATH record 27.0465.12
  6. 1806

    Argand's Essai on representing imaginary quantities

    key development

    Jean-Robert Argand, Essai sur une manière de représenter les quantités imaginaires dans les constructions géométriques, 1806

    Argand independently presents the plane representation, which made geometric complex numbers widely known.

    Evidence (2)
    • Wikipedia dates the Argand diagram to papers of 1806 and 1814 that largely rediscovered Wessel. English Wikipedia, Complex number
    • Internet Archive copy of the Essai (1874 reprint edition). Internet Archive essaisurunemani00argauoft
  7. 1831

    Gauss popularises the term "complex number"

    naming

    Carl Friedrich Gauss, Theoria residuorum biquadraticorum, commentatio secunda, 1831

    Gauss gives the geometric picture authority and uses complex number as the standard term.

    Evidence (1)
    • MacTutor biography of Gauss, the source for his 1831 memoir on biquadratic residues and complex integers. MacTutor, biography of Gauss
