All topics / Graph theory

2020 · Graph theory

Set-theoretic measurable coloring meets distributed local algorithms

Efficient local algorithms for colouring networks were shown to translate into measurable colourings of infinite graphs, so thresholds in descriptive set theory and in distributed computing classify the same problems.

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 2016

    Measurable Lovász local lemma

    precursor

    A. Bernshteyn, Measurable versions of the Lovász Local Lemma and measurable graph colorings, arXiv:1604.07349 (2016)

    Measurable coloring tools precede the explicit distributed-algorithm transfer.

    Evidence (1)
    • Public precursor preprint posted in 2016 arXiv
  2. 2020

    Distributed-to-descriptive transfer stated

    first statement

    A. Bernshteyn, Distributed Algorithms, the Lovász Local Lemma, and Descriptive Combinatorics, arXiv:2004.04905 (2020)

    The public paper states that efficient finite-graph local algorithms can yield well-behaved Borel-graph colorings.

    Evidence (1)
    • Public preprint posted in April 2020 arXiv
  3. 2020

    Randomized local algorithms yield measurable colorings

    first application

    A. Bernshteyn, Distributed Algorithms, the Lovász Local Lemma, and Descriptive Combinatorics, arXiv:2004.04905 (2020)

    The paper applies randomized LOCAL algorithms to measurable and Baire-measurable coloring problems.

    Evidence (1)
    • The abstract explicitly states the measurable-coloring application arXiv
  4. 2021

    Homomorphism graphs link descriptive and distributed methods

    key development

    S. Brandt et al., On Homomorphism Graphs, arXiv:2111.03683 (2021)

    A later paper adapts descriptive-combinatorial methods to the distributed LOCAL model.

    Evidence (1)
    • Public preprint posted in November 2021 arXiv
  5. 2023

    Bernshteyn: distributed algorithms and descriptive combinatorics

    key development

    Bernshteyn, Distributed algorithms, the Lovász local lemma, and descriptive combinatorics, 2023, pp. 495-542

    Develops the link between distributed algorithms and descriptive combinatorics that the 2020 transfer statement relies on.

    Evidence (1)
    • zbMATH record for this title, year 2023, pages 495-542 zbMATH Open
