1931 · Logic & foundations

Gödel's incompleteness theorems

Coding of syntax by numbers was used to show that consistent arithmetical systems have unprovable true statements.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1931

    Gödel's 1931 correspondence with Zermelo on incompletability

    precursor

    Gödel corresponded privately with Zermelo in 1931 about the incompletability theorem before the paper was accepted.

    Evidence (1)
    • Gödel met Zermelo in 1931; the correspondence is discussed by Grattan-Guinness MacTutor: Kurt Gödel
  2. 1931

    Gödel publishes the incompleteness theorems

    first statement

    K. Gödel, Über formal unentscheidbare Sätze der Principia Mathematica und verwandter Systeme I, Monatsh. Math. Phys. 38 (1931)

    Published proof that consistent formal arithmetic contains true but unprovable statements.

    Evidence (2)
    • DOI record: Monatshefte für Mathematik und Physik (1931) Crossref
    • Gödel published these results in 1931 MacTutor: Kurt Gödel
  3. 1936

    Rosser replaces ω-consistency with plain consistency

    key development

    J. B. Rosser, Extensions of some theorems of Gödel and Church, J. Symbolic Logic 1 (1936) 87-91

    Showed the ω-consistency hypothesis in Gödel's proof can be replaced by simple consistency, which made the result much more widely applicable.

    Evidence (2)
    • DOI record: J. Symbolic Logic 1 (1936) Crossref
    • Rosser proved in 1936 that simple consistency suffices Wikipedia: Gödel's incompleteness theorems
  4. 1952

    Kleene: Introduction to Metamathematics

    consolidation

    S. C. Kleene, Introduction to Metamathematics (1952)

    Textbook-level systematic treatment of metamathematics including the incompleteness theorems.

    Dating or attribution note

    Textbook choice is the consolidation role; other early treatments (Hilbert–Bernays, 1934-1939) were not checked.

    Evidence (1)
    • Open Library record (1952) Open Library
