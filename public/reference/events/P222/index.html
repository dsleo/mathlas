NP-completeness

Satisfiability was shown universal for NP, with many combinatorial problems reduced to it.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1971

    Cook: satisfiability is universal for NP

    first statement

    Cook, The complexity of theorem-proving procedures, Proc. 3rd ACM STOC (1971)

    Cook shows that satisfiability is NP-complete under polynomial-time reductions, the first public statement of the notion.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref: proceedings article, third annual ACM symposium, 1971, The complexity of theorem-proving procedures Crossref
    • Wikipedia: the concept of NP-completeness was introduced in 1971, the term later Wikipedia, NP-completeness
  2. 1972

    Karp's 21 NP-complete problems

    first application

    Karp, Reducibility among combinatorial problems, in Complexity of Computer Computations (1972)

    Karp uses polynomial reductions to show that many combinatorial problems are NP-complete, the first systematic use of the notion on target problems.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref: book chapter, Complexity of Computer Computations, 1972, Reducibility among combinatorial problems Crossref
  3. 1973

    Levin's universal sequential search problems

    key development

    Levin, Universal sequential search problems, Problemy Peredachi Informatsii (1973)

    Levin independently proves the existence of universal search problems in the Soviet literature, paralleling Cook.

    Evidence (1)
    • Wikipedia: Levin is credited with the discovery of NP-completeness Wikipedia, Leonid Levin
  4. 1979

    Garey and Johnson, Computers and Intractability

    consolidation

    Garey, Johnson, Computers and Intractability: A Guide to the Theory of NP-Completeness, W.H. Freeman (1979)

    The first systematic book-length treatment, with a catalogue of NP-complete problems.

    Evidence (1)
    • Open Library record: Michael R. Garey, Computers and intractability, W.H. Freeman, first published 1979 Open Library
