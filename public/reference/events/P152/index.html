1733 · Probability

De Moivre: normal approximation to the binomial

Stirling-type asymptotics were applied to binomial sums, yielding the normal curve.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1729

    Stirling's letter on the central binomial coefficient

    precursor

    Stirling to de Moivre, 19 June 1729 (private letter)

    A private letter gave de Moivre the constant in his large-n estimate of the central coefficient, later called Stirling's formula.

    Evidence (2)
    • On 19 June 1729 Stirling sent de Moivre a letter showing his calculation of the middle binomial coefficient Wikipedia, Abraham de Moivre
    • Stirling wrote to de Moivre about Miscellanea Analytica and told him of Methodus Differentialis MacTutor, James Stirling
  2. 1730

    Large-n approximation of binomial terms in the Miscellanea Analytica

    precursor

    De Moivre, Miscellanea Analytica de Seriebus et Quadraturis, London, 1730

    De Moivre uses asymptotic series and a table of log n! to approximate binomial coefficients, the tool later used for the normal curve.

    Evidence (3)
    • Miscellanea Analytica (1730) contains the formula later attributed to Stirling, which de Moivre used in 1733 to get the normal curve MacTutor, Abraham de Moivre
    • In 1730 the book included tables of log n! and approximated the terms of a binomial expansion Wikipedia, Abraham de Moivre
    • Catalogue entry, Miscellanea analytica de seriebus et quadraturis, 1730 Internet Archive catalogue record bub_gb_TFX1165yEc4C
  3. 1733

    Approximatio ad summam terminorum binomii

    first statement

    De Moivre, Approximatio ad summam terminorum binomii (a+b)^n in seriem expansi, pamphlet dated 12 or 13 November 1733

    A Latin pamphlet gives the approximation of binomial sums by the normal probability integral, the first appearance of that integral.

    Evidence (2)
    • The result was first published in a Latin pamphlet of 13 November 1733, containing the first occurrence of the normal probability integral MacTutor, Abraham de Moivre
    • On 12 November 1733 he privately published and distributed the pamphlet Wikipedia, Abraham de Moivre
  4. 1738

    Normal approximation in the second edition of The Doctrine of Chances

    consolidation

    De Moivre, The Doctrine of Chances, 2nd ed., London, 1738

    The 1738 edition prints the approximation within a systematic treatise, with credit to Stirling for the constant.

    Evidence (3)
    • The theorem appeared in the second edition of The Doctrine of Chances, 1738 Wikipedia, De Moivre-Laplace theorem
    • In the second edition de Moivre gives credit to Stirling for an improvement MacTutor, Abraham de Moivre
    • Catalogue entry, second edition, 1738 Internet Archive catalogue record bim_eighteenth-century_the-doctrine-of-chances_moivre-abraham-de_1738
  5. 1812

    Laplace's Théorie analytique des probabilités

    key development

    Laplace, Théorie analytique des probabilités, Paris, 1812

    Laplace's treatise is the source of the general form usually credited with the de Moivre-Laplace name.

    Dating or attribution note

    I did not read what the 1812 book proves about the binomial; the link comes from the theorem's name only. Treat as a lead.

    Evidence (2)
    • The first edition of Laplace's Théorie analytique des probabilités was published in 1812 MacTutor, Pierre-Simon Laplace
    • The result is named the de Moivre-Laplace theorem, a special case of the central limit theorem Wikipedia, De Moivre-Laplace theorem
