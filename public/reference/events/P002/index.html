1684 · Analysis

The calculus (differential and integral calculus)

Tangent, quadrature and series techniques are unified into an algorithm with a notation, with the inverse relation between differentiation and integration as its core.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1670

    Barrow's Lectiones geometricae

    precursor

    Barrow, Lectiones geometricae, 1670

    Barrow's lectures, edited by Collins, give a tangent method close to calculus and recognise integration and differentiation as inverse.

    Dating or attribution note

    MacTutor claims Barrow as first to recognise the inverse relation; recorded as a precursor, not verified independently.

    Evidence (1)
    • Lectiones Geometricae published 1670; Barrow first to recognise differentiation and integration as inverse operations. MacTutor biography of Barrow
  2. 1671

    Newton's De methodis serierum et fluxionum written

    precursor

    Newton, De methodis serierum et fluxionum (written 1671; printed 1736)

    Newton unified area, tangent, length and extremum problems under the method of fluxions, with integration as the inverse of differentiation, but kept it unpublished.

    Evidence (1)
    • Method of fluxions; De methodis written 1671, not printed until Colson's 1736 translation. MacTutor biography of Newton
  3. 1684

    Leibniz, Nova methodus pro maximis et minimis

    first statement

    Leibniz, Nova methodus pro maximis et minimis, itemque tangentibus, Acta Eruditorum, 1684

    The first published account of the differential calculus, with the d notation and rules for powers, products and quotients (no proofs).

    Evidence (2)
    • Leibniz published the differential calculus in Acta Eruditorum in 1684. MacTutor biography of Leibniz
    • Article on dating and numbering the editions of the Nova methodus (October 1684). Crossref record: Printing Leibniz's Calculus, The Library 2021
  4. 1686

    Leibniz's integral calculus with the integral sign in print

    key development

    Leibniz, Acta Eruditorum, 1686

    A 1686 paper gave the integral calculus and the first printed use of the integral sign.

    Evidence (1)
    • 1686 Acta paper with first printed integral sign. MacTutor biography of Leibniz
  5. 1696

    L'Hôpital, Analyse des infiniment petits

    consolidation

    L'Hôpital, Analyse des infiniment petits, Paris, 1696

    The first textbook of the differential calculus, based on Johann Bernoulli's lessons.

    Evidence (1)
    • Record shows the 1716 edition of the Analyse (first edition year not shown). Internet Archive record: Analyse des infiniment petits, 1716 edition
  6. 1711

    Newton's fluxion tract first printed

    key development

    Newton, Analysis per quantitatum series, fluxiones, 1711

    Newton's early analysis appeared in print only in 1711, after the priority dispute had begun.

    Evidence (1)
    • MacTutor shows the title page of Analysis per quantitatum series fluxiones (1711). MacTutor biography of Newton
