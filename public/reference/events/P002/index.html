1684 · Analysis

The calculus (differential and integral calculus)

Tangent, quadrature and series techniques are unified into an algorithm with a notation, with the inverse relation between differentiation and integration as its core.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1670

    Barrow's Lectiones geometricae

    precursor

    Barrow, Lectiones geometricae, 1670

    Barrow's geometrical lectures give a tangent method close to the differential calculus and a geometric proof that areas and tangents are inverse problems.

    Evidence (2)
    • Lectiones Geometricae published 1670; Barrow first to recognise differentiation and integration as inverse operations. MacTutor biography of Barrow
    • Lectiones geometricae listed as 1670; Barrow credited with a proof of the fundamental theorem and a differential-triangle tangent method. English Wikipedia, Isaac Barrow
  2. 1671

    Newton's De methodis serierum et fluxionum written

    precursor

    Newton, De methodis serierum et fluxionum (written 1671; printed 1736)

    Newton unified area, tangent, length and extremum problems under the method of fluxions, with integration as the inverse of differentiation, but kept it unpublished.

    Evidence (3)
    • Method of fluxions; De methodis written 1671, not printed until Colson's 1736 translation. MacTutor biography of Newton
    • Newton began the method of fluxions about 1665-67 and finished the treatise in 1671; it was published only in 1736 (Colson). English Wikipedia, Method of Fluxions
    • Newton's October 1666 tract on fluxions was not published until 1737; De analysi (1669) and the Method of Fluxions (1671) were written but not printed. English Wikipedia, Leibniz-Newton calculus controversy
  3. 1675

    Leibniz's private notebooks introduce differentials and the integral sign

    precursor

    Leibniz, manuscript notebooks, 1675 (unpublished)

    In 1675 Leibniz first wrote differentials and the long-s integral sign in private notebooks, nine years before any print.

    Evidence (2)
    • Leibniz's notebooks of 1675 first show differentials. English Wikipedia, Leibniz-Newton calculus controversy
    • The notation for the indefinite integral was introduced by Leibniz in 1675. English Wikipedia, Integral
  4. 1684

    Leibniz, Nova methodus pro maximis et minimis

    first statement

    Leibniz, Nova methodus pro maximis et minimis, itemque tangentibus, Acta Eruditorum, 1684

    The first published account of the differential calculus, with the d notation and rules for powers, products and quotients (no proofs).

    Evidence (3)
    • Leibniz published the differential calculus in Acta Eruditorum in 1684. MacTutor biography of Leibniz
    • Article on dating and numbering the editions of the Nova methodus (October 1684). Crossref record: Printing Leibniz's Calculus, The Library 2021
    • Leibniz publishes Nova Methodus pro Maximis et Minimis in 1684. English Wikipedia, Leibniz-Newton calculus controversy
  5. 1686

    Leibniz's integral calculus with the integral sign in print

    key development

    Leibniz, Acta Eruditorum, 1686

    A June 1686 Acta paper (De geometria recondita) gave the integral calculus and the first printed use of the integral sign.

    Evidence (2)
    • 1686 Acta paper with first printed integral sign. MacTutor biography of Leibniz
    • The June 1686 Acta Eruditorum paper contains the first public occurrence of the integral sign (Cajori, Isis 1929). Cajori, 'Leibniz, the master-builder of mathematical notations'
  6. 1687

    Newton's Principia presents a geometrical form of the calculus

    key development

    I. Newton, Philosophiae naturalis principia mathematica, London, 1687

    Newton's first printed use of his calculus is geometrical, in limit form, and does not use fluxion notation.

    Evidence (2)
    • 1687: Newton's Principia presents a geometrical form of calculus. English Wikipedia, Leibniz-Newton calculus controversy
    • Open Library work for the Principia, first published 1687. Open Library
  7. 1696

    L'Hôpital, Analyse des infiniment petits

    consolidation

    L'Hôpital, Analyse des infiniment petits, Paris, 1696

    The first textbook of the differential calculus, based on Johann Bernoulli's lessons, published anonymously.

    Evidence (3)
    • Open Library lists the Analyse des infiniment petits, first published 1696. Open Library
    • First edition 1696, Paris; called the first textbook on the infinitesimal calculus of Leibniz. English Wikipedia, Analyse des infiniment petits
    • Record shows the 1716 edition of the Analyse (first edition year not shown). Internet Archive record: Analyse des infiniment petits, 1716 edition
  8. 1704

    Newton's De quadratura curvarum printed with the Opticks

    key development

    I. Newton, Tractatus de quadratura curvarum, appended to Opticks, London, 1704

    The fullest printed account of Newton's fluxions appeared as an appendix to the Opticks, more than twenty years after Leibniz.

    Dating or attribution note

    Wikipedia says Newton published his fluxional notation in partial form in 1693 (Wallis's Opera) and more fully in 1704; the 1693 print was not checked separately.

    Evidence (1)
    • 1693 and 1704: Newton publishes his fluxional notation in partial then fuller form; De Quadratura Curvarum appears in 1704. English Wikipedia, Leibniz-Newton calculus controversy
  9. 1711

    Newton's fluxion tract first printed

    key development

    Newton, Analysis per quantitatum series, fluxiones, 1711

    Newton's early analysis (the 1669 De analysi) was first printed in 1711 by William Jones, after the priority dispute had begun.

    Evidence (2)
    • MacTutor shows the title page of Analysis per quantitatum series fluxiones (1711). MacTutor biography of Newton
    • 1711: Newton enters the dispute; De Analysi is listed under 1711. English Wikipedia, Leibniz-Newton calculus controversy
