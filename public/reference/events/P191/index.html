1827 · Algebraic geometry

Homogeneous coordinates (Moebius, Pluecker)

Algebraic coordinates made duality and line geometry computational.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1827

    Feuerbach's coefficients of a point for the tetrahedron

    precursor

    K. W. Feuerbach, Grundriss zu analytischen Untersuchungen der dreyeckigen Pyramide, Nuernberg 1827 (48 pp.; announced in Isis 1826, pp. 565-569)

    Feuerbach assigns to a point of space four 'coordinated coefficients', the ratios of its distances from the faces of a tetrahedron to the distances of the opposite vertices, a barycentric-type system used to express tetrahedron invariants.

    Evidence (3)
    • Primary account read: Feuerbach's Grundriss (Nuernberg 1827, 48 pp.), the Isis announcement of 1826 and the manuscript of 1826; section 82 defines coordinated coefficients of a point for a tetrahedron (sum 1). M. Cantor, 'Karl Wilhelm von Feuerbach', Sitzungsberichte Heidelberger Akademie 1910, no. 25 (Heidelberger Texte zur Mathematikgeschichte)
    • E. Straume, survey: Feuerbach's 1827 investigation of tetrahedra yields a quadruple of volumes that is a kind of homogeneous coordinates closely related to Moebius' barycentric coordinates, but Feuerbach was after theorems, not a coordinate system. E. Straume, A Survey of the Development of Geometry up to 1870 (arXiv 1409.1140)
    • Internet Archive record of the 1827 booklet. Internet Archive item grundrisszuanal00feuegoog
  2. 1827

    Moebius introduces barycentric (homogeneous) coordinates

    first statement

    A. F. Moebius, Der barycentrische Calcul, Leipzig, 1827

    Moebius fixes a point of the plane by the ratios of weights p:q:r placed at three fundamental points (a point at infinity when the weights sum to zero), which are homogeneous coordinates in the modern sense.

    Evidence (6)
    • Primary text read: sections 28-32 (pp. 50-53) define a point of a line, plane or space by the ratios of the coefficients of fundamental points with P = pA + qB + rC; points with coefficient sum zero are at infinity. A. F. Moebius, Der barycentrische Calcul (1827), Gesammelte Werke vol. 1 (1885), Internet Archive gesammeltewerkeh01mbuoft
    • Baltzer's preface (1885, same volume): the barycentric coordinates of a point are the first homogeneous coordinates introduced into geometry; Pluecker's Entwickelungen soon surpassed them with homogeneous coordinates and equations. R. Baltzer, Vorrede ueber Moebius, Gesammelte Werke vol. 1 (1885)
    • Internet Archive (Bayerische Staatsbibliothek) scan: Der barycentrische Calcul: ein neues Huelfsmittel zur analytischen Behandlung der Geometrie, Moebius, 1827; the scan's OCR is too poor to read the passage. Internet Archive scan
    • Article attributes the introduction of homogeneous coordinates to Moebius in his 1827 work. English Wikipedia: Homogeneous coordinates
    • Biography says the 1827 book introduced homogeneous coordinates and projective transformations. MacTutor: Mobius biography
    • Open Library: Der barycentrische calcul, first publication 1827. Open Library works OL13462549W
  3. 1827

    Mobius titles his book with the barycentric-calculus name

    naming

    Mobius, A. F., Der barycentrische Calcul, 1827

    The first title found using "barycentric calculus", an alias for the event.

    Evidence (2)
    • Open Library lists the title with first publication year 1827 Open Library works OL13462549W
    • Contents of the reprint: 'Der barycentrische Calcul 1827', Vorrede und Inhalt p. 1, sections I-III; title as given. Moebius, Gesammelte Werke vol. 1 (1885), Internet Archive gesammeltewerkeh01mbuoft
  4. 1827–1828

    Bobillier's triangle of linear functions and abridged notation

    precursor

    E. Bobillier, Essai sur un nouveau mode de recherche des proprietes de l'etendue, Annales de mathematiques pures et appliquees 18 (1827-28) 320-339

    Bobillier takes three independent linear functions A, B, C of x and y as the sides of a triangle and writes lines and the conics circumscribed to it as combinations of A, B, C and of the products BC, CA, AB with undetermined constants.

    Evidence (2)
    • Primary text (garbled OCR) read: opening of Bobillier's Essai, triangle of three linear functions A, B, C of x and y and conics circumscribed to it. Numdam, Annales de mathematiques pures et appliquees 18 (1827-1828), item AMPA_1827-1828__18__320_1
    • Straume: the abridged notation of Bobillier (1827-28) with the triangle Ci = 0 gives homogeneous line parameters, although he did not have a coordinate concept in mind; the idea of homogeneous coordinates arose almost simultaneously around 1827 with Bobillier, Moebius, Feuerbach and Plucker. E. Straume, A Survey of the Development of Geometry up to 1870 (arXiv 1409.1140)
  5. 1830

    Pluecker's new coordinate system of distances from a triangle, with homogeneous equations

    first application

    J. Pluecker, Ueber ein neues Coordinatensystem, J. reine angew. Math. 5 (1830) 1-36

    Pluecker takes the distances p, q, r of a point from the three sides of a triangle as coordinates, studies equations homogeneous in p, q, r, represents the line at infinity by a linear equation and applies the system to conics, poles and polars and Poncelet's theorems.

    Evidence (3)
    • Primary text read: Pluecker, Ueber ein neues Coordinatensystem, Crelle 5 (1830) 1-36: distances p, q, r from a triangle as coordinates, equations homogeneous in p, q, r, the line at infinity as a linear equation. Zenodo copy of J. reine angew. Math. 5 (1830) 1-36, record 1778033 (read)
    • Crossref: 'Ueber ein neues Coordinatensystem', Crelle's Journal 5 (1830), pages 1-36. Crossref DOI 10.1515/crll.1830.5.1
    • Plucker's own preface to the second volume of the Entwickelungen: the paper in Crelle vol. 5 was only an imperfect preparation. Analytisch-geometrische Entwickelungen, vol. 2 (Essen 1831), Vorrede (Internet Archive analytischgeome02plgoog; read)
  6. 1831

    Pluecker uses homogeneous line coordinates and the principle of reciprocity

    key development

    J. Pluecker, Analytisch-geometrische Entwickelungen, vol. 2, Essen: G. D. Baedeker, 1831

    In the second volume Pluecker takes the constants of a line as its coordinates (u, v, w), writes loci of the second class by homogeneous equations in u, v, w, and derives the principle of reciprocity, making duality a matter of symbolic calculation.

    Evidence (3)
    • Primary text read: title page of the second volume (1831) and nos. 401-416, 454: line coordinates u, v, w, homogeneous equations, loci of the second class; preface on the origin of the idea (August 1829) and on the earlier paper in Crelle 5. J. Pluecker, Analytisch-geometrische Entwickelungen, vol. 2 (1831), Internet Archive analytischgeome02plgoog
    • Library record: Essen, G. D. Baedeker, 1828-1831, two volumes. Internet Archive item analytischgeome01plgoog (catalogue record)
    • MacTutor: vol. 1 (1828) uses non-homogeneous affine point coordinates; vol. 2 uses homogeneous line coordinates in the plane and treats conics as envelopes of lines. MacTutor: Plucker biography
  7. 1835

    Pluecker's system of analytic geometry with point and line coordinates

    key development

    J. Pluecker, System der analytischen Geometrie, Berlin, 1835

    Pluecker uses both point and line coordinates for a detailed theory of plane cubic curves.

    Evidence (4)
    • Primary text read: introduction (nos. 7-8): coordinates p, q, r, homogeneous equations; no. 44: systems of line coordinates. J. Pluecker, System der analytischen Geometrie (Berlin 1835), Internet Archive bub_gb_0b_Y9GirdDQC (OCR text read)
    • Archive catalogue lists the 1835 System der analytischen Geometrie. Internet Archive scan
    • Biography says the book treats point and line coordinates and mainly plane cubics. MacTutor: Plucker biography
    • Open Library: Pluecker, System der analytischen Geometrie, auf neue Betrachtungsweisen gegruendet, first published 1835. Open Library works OL7169341W
  8. 1839

    Pluecker's theory of algebraic curves (Pluecker equations)

    key development

    J. Pluecker, Theorie der algebraischen Curven, Bonn, 1839

    The book studies singular points and points at infinity and states the relations between the order and class of a curve, a result of the dual coordinate viewpoint.

    Evidence (3)
    • Primary text read: title page and contents (sections 62-70), six relations between order, class and singularities. J. Pluecker, Theorie der algebraischen Curven (Bonn 1839), Internet Archive theoriederalgeb00plgoog (OCR text read)
    • Archive catalogue lists the 1839 Theorie der algebraischen Curven. Internet Archive scan
    • Biography says this work contains the equations relating order and class. MacTutor: Plucker biography
