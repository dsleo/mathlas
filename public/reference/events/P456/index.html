1785 · Statistics & decision theory

Social choice theory: Condorcet's probability to Arrow's theorem

Condorcet applied probability to majority decisions and found cycles, and Arrow proved no aggregation satisfies reasonable fairness conditions at once.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1299

    Llull's Ars Electionis

    precursor

    A manuscript of 1299 discussed pairwise comparison as a fair election method, then lay unknown for centuries.

    Evidence (2)
    • MacTutor says Llull's idea of a fair election was proposed again 500 years later by Condorcet MacTutor, Voting
    • Wikipedia says Llull's 1299 manuscript prefigured Condorcet but was lost until the early 21st century English Wikipedia, Social choice theory
  2. 1785

    Condorcet's Essai on majority decisions

    first statement

    Condorcet, Essai sur l'application de l'analyse à la probabilité des décisions rendues à la pluralité des voix, Paris, 1785

    Condorcet applied probability to majority voting and gave the example of a cyclic majority, now called the Condorcet paradox.

    Evidence (2)
    • MacTutor says the Essay of 1785 contains the Condorcet paradox MacTutor, Voting
    • MacTutor, Condorcet, places the Essai of 1785 as his most important probability work MacTutor, Condorcet
  3. 1948

    Black's 'On the Rationale of Group Decision-making'

    key development

    D. Black, J. Political Economy 56 (1948), 23-34

    Black analysed committee decision rules and majority cycles in an economics journal, shortly before Arrow.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref record: D. Black, On the Rationale of Group Decision-making, Journal of Political Economy 56 (1948), 23-34 Crossref 10.1086/256633
  4. 1950

    Arrow's 'A Difficulty in the Concept of Social Welfare'

    key development

    K. J. Arrow, J. Political Economy 58 (1950), 328-346

    The paper presents the impossibility of aggregating individual rankings under a few fairness conditions.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref record: K. J. Arrow, A Difficulty in the Concept of Social Welfare, Journal of Political Economy 58 (1950), 328-346 Crossref 10.1086/256963
    • MacTutor, Voting, says Arrow showed in 1951 that no voting system is fair in the stated sense MacTutor, Voting
  5. 1951

    Arrow's Social Choice and Individual Values

    consolidation

    K. J. Arrow, Social Choice and Individual Values, Cowles Commission Monograph 12, 1951

    The monograph, whose title names the field, is generally credited with inaugurating the modern period of social choice.

    Evidence (1)
    • Wikipedia says Arrow's book is often recognized as inaugurating the modern era of social choice theory English Wikipedia, Social choice theory
  6. 1973

    Gibbard's manipulation result

    key development

    A. Gibbard, Econometrica 41 (1973)

    A general result on manipulation of voting schemes extends Arrow's impossibility to strategic voting.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref record: A. Gibbard, Manipulation of Voting Schemes: A General Result, Econometrica 41 (1973), 587 Crossref 10.2307/1914083
  7. 1984

    Social Choice and Welfare launched

    institution

    Social Choice and Welfare, vol. 1, 1984

    A journal devoted to the field starts, with volume 1 in 1984.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref record: Social Choice and Welfare vol 1 (1984), e.g. Saposnik, A power rule for social choice, pp. 97-103 Crossref 10.1007/bf00452881
