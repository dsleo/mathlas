Differential geometry of surfaces (Clairaut, Euler, Monge, Gauss)

Clairaut, Euler and Monge studied curvature of curves and surfaces.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1731

    Clairaut's work on curves of double curvature

    precursor

    Clairaut, Recherches sur les courbes a double courbure, Paris, 1731 (research completed 1729)

    A teenager's study of curves in space, the first step toward curvature theory beyond plane curves.

    Evidence (1)
    • Clairaut did research on curves of double curvature completed in 1729, leading to his Academy election in 1731 MacTutor, Alexis Clairaut
  2. 1767

    Euler on curvature of surfaces

    first statement

    Euler, Recherches sur la courbure des surfaces, Mémoires de l'Académie de Berlin, vol. 16 (read 1760, printed 1767)

    Euler's memoir gives the curvature of plane sections through a point of a surface, the start of the theory of principal curvatures.

    Evidence (3)
    • Curvature of general surfaces was first studied by Euler; in 1760 he proved a formula for the curvature of a plane section Wikipedia, Differential geometry of surfaces
    • Euler made substantial contributions to the theory of surfaces and curvature; many unpublished results were rediscovered by Gauss MacTutor, Leonhard Euler
    • Euler Archive: Recherches sur la courbure des surfaces, published 1767 Euler Archive record E333 (bepress metadata)
  3. 1772

    Euler on developable surfaces

    key development

    Euler, De solidis quorum superficiem in planum explicare licet, Novi Commentarii Petropolitani, 1772

    Euler studies surfaces that can be unrolled onto a plane, using a parametric description of surfaces.

    Evidence (2)
    • In 1771 he considered surfaces given in parametric form Wikipedia, Differential geometry of surfaces
    • Euler Archive dates 'De solidis quorum superficiem in planum explicare licet' to 1772 Euler Archive record E419 (bepress metadata)
  4. 1784

    Monge's memoir on the theory of déblais and remblais and the lines of curvature

    key development

    Monge, Sur la théorie des déblais et des remblais, Histoire de l'Académie royale des sciences (1781), pp. 666-704

    Monge studies the transport of earth between surfaces and in doing so introduces the lines of curvature of a surface and their differential equation, going beyond Euler's treatment of normal sections.

    Evidence (1)
    • Gives the memoir at Mém. de l'Académie 1781, pp. 666-704, and notes it establishes the curves of curvature of a surface BnF, Des tas de sable aux pixels (bibliography)
  5. 1795

    Monge's treatise on analysis applied to geometry

    consolidation

    Monge, Application de l'analyse à la géométrie (lectures from 1795, École Polytechnique)

    Monge's course gathers curves and surfaces under analytic methods and introduces lines of curvature.

    Evidence (2)
    • Monge laid the foundations of the theory in his classical memoir that appeared in 1795 Wikipedia, Differential geometry of surfaces
    • Monge is considered the father of differential geometry, introducing lines of curvature; the lectures of 1795 formed the basis of the book MacTutor, Gaspard Monge
  6. 1828

    Gauss: intrinsic geometry and the Theorema Egregium

    key development

    Gauss, Disquisitiones generales circa superficies curvas, Göttingen, 1828 (presented 1827)

    Gauss shows that curvature is intrinsic, unchanged by bending, turning the subject from curves-in-space to geometry of the surface itself.

    Evidence (4)
    • The defining contribution was made by Gauss in two papers; the Theorema Egregium established that Gaussian curvature is intrinsic Wikipedia, Differential geometry of surfaces
    • Theorema Egregium proved by Gauss in 1827 Wikipedia, Theorema Egregium
    • Catalogue entry for the 1828 printing of Disquisitiones generales circa superficies curvas Internet Archive catalogue record disquisitionesg00gausgoog
    • Crossref: 1828 Philosophical Magazine account of the paper communicated to the Göttingen society on 18 October 1827 Crossref record 10.1080/14786442808674658
