1435 · Geometry

Projective geometry from Renaissance perspective

Painters' perspective became a geometric theory in Alberti and Piero, del Monte proved vanishing points, and Desargues treated conics projectively with points at infinity.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1413–1425

    Brunelleschi's perspective demonstrations

    precursor

    Filippo Brunelleschi, panels of Florence, about 1413 (lost)

    Credited with the first correct linear perspective, with a single vanishing point; he wrote no explanation.

    Evidence (2)
    • Credited with first correct formulation, about 1413; wrote no explanation. MacTutor, Mathematics and art - perspective
    • Dates Brunelleschi's perspective experiment at the Baptistery to c. 1420 and notes that no writing by him survives Smarthistory, Linear perspective: Brunelleschi's experiment
  2. 1435

    Alberti's De pictura

    first statement

    Leon Battista Alberti, De pictura (Latin 1435; Italian Della pittura 1436)

    First written mathematical account of perspective as a section of the visual pyramid.

    Evidence (4)
    • Alberti wrote the first treatise on the laws of perspective in 1435. MacTutor, Alberti biography
    • Book 1 gives the mathematical description; Italian version the next year. MacTutor, Mathematics and art - perspective
    • Edition of De pictura catalogued. Open Library OL1417313W
    • Andersen's history of perspective (2007). Crossref doi:10.1007/978-0-387-48946-9
  3. 1474–1482

    Piero della Francesca's treatise on perspective

    key development

    Piero della Francesca, De prospectiva pingendi (1460s or mid-1470s)

    Euclid-style geometric theorems with original results behind perspective drawing.

    Evidence (3)
    • Most mathematical of the Renaissance perspective works; written 1460s or mid 1470s. MacTutor, Mathematics and art - perspective
    • Edition catalogued. Open Library OL10425649W
    • Wikipedia: De prospectiva pingendi probably created between 1474 and 1482, not in the 1460s Wikipedia, De prospectiva pingendi
  4. 1600

    Guidobaldo del Monte's Perspectivae libri sex

    key development

    Guidobaldo del Monte, Perspectivae libri sex, Pesaro 1600

    Six books on perspective by a mathematician; the harvest summary credits it with a vanishing-point theorem.

    Dating or attribution note

    Content claim (vanishing points) comes from the earlier harvest, not checked here.

    Evidence (1)
    • 1600 edition exists. Internet Archive gvidivbaldiemarc00mont
  5. 1639

    Desargues' Brouillon project on conic sections

    key development

    Girard Desargues, Brouillon project d'une atteinte aux evenemens des rencontres du Cone avec un Plan, Paris 1639

    Treats conics by projection with points at infinity and involution; the founding text of projective geometry.

    Evidence (2)
    • Printed in Paris 1639; Pascal cites it; Desargues invented a new geometry there. MacTutor, Desargues biography
    • Works reprinted in Poudra's edition 1864. Internet Archive oeuvresruniese01desauoft
  6. 1822

    Poncelet's Traite des proprietes projectives

    consolidation

    J. V. Poncelet, Traite des proprietes projectives des figures, Bachelier, 1822

    Poncelet's treatise is the first book-length systematic account of projective properties of figures, which is the field's first monograph-level treatment.

    Evidence (1)
    • Open Library record lists the Traite des proprietes projectives des figures with first publication year 1822, publisher Bachelier. Open Library search API
