1704 · Analysis

Trigonometry and the exponential: Cotes, De Moivre, Euler

Bernoulli related inverse sine to logarithm, Cotes and De Moivre linked angle to complex logarithm, and Euler gave exp(ix) = cos x + i sin x.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1704

    Johann Bernoulli links inverse sine and logarithm

    first statement

    Johann Bernoulli, Solution d'un probleme concernant le calcul integral, Mem. Acad. Roy. Sci. Paris, annee 1702 (Paris, 1704), pp. 289-297

    Bernoulli's treatment of integrals of the form dx/(a^2+x^2) led to the logarithm of an imaginary quantity and so to a relation between the inverse trigonometric functions and logarithms.

    Evidence (3)
    • MacTutor: Johann Bernoulli found the relation between the inverse sine and the logarithm in 1702. MacTutor, Trigonometric functions
    • 1702: Bernoulli found that 1/(1+x^2) = 1/2(1/(1-ix) + 1/(1+ix)) but did not fully understand complex logarithms. English Wikipedia, Euler's formula
    • Bookseller record of the 1704 Paris (Boudot) offprint from the Memoires de l'Academie des Sciences, Annee 1702, pp. 289-297, described as the first printing; the paper shows dz/(1+z^2) as a sum of differentials of imaginary logarithms. Lynge & Son rare-book listing (Bernoulli, Solution d'un probleme)
  2. 1707

    De Moivre's series for cos nx in terms of cos x

    precursor

    A. de Moivre, Aequationum quarundam potestatis tertiae, quintae, septimae, nonae... resolutio analytica, Phil. Trans. 25(309) (1707) 2368-2371

    A 1707 paper gives a series relation between cos x and cos nx, the real-variable ancestor of the multiple-angle theorem.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref record: Phil. Trans. 25(309), pp. 2368-2371, March 1707. Crossref DOI 10.1098/rstl.1706.0037
    • 1707: de Moivre's Phil. Trans. paper gives a series relation for cos x and cos nx. English Wikipedia, De Moivre's formula
  3. 1714

    Cotes's Logometria

    key development

    Cotes, Logometria, Philosophical Transactions 29 (338), pp. 5-45, March 1714

    Cotes gave a geometric statement equivalent to ix = log(cos x + i sin x), up to a misplaced factor of i.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref: Logometria, Phil. Trans. 29, pp. 5-45, dated 1714. Crossref DOI 10.1098/rstl.1714.0002
    • 1714: Cotes presented a geometrical argument that, after correcting a misplaced factor of sqrt(-1), reads ix = ln(cos x + i sin x). English Wikipedia, Euler's formula
  4. 1722

    De Moivre's multiple-angle theorem (De sectione anguli)

    key development

    A. de Moivre, De sectione anguli, Phil. Trans. 32(374) (1722) 228-230

    The 1722 paper on the section of an angle is the paper MacTutor credits with De Moivre's theorem on powers of cos x + i sin x.

    Evidence (3)
    • MacTutor: De Moivre published his theorem in 1722. MacTutor, Trigonometric functions
    • Crossref record: De sectione anguli, Phil. Trans. 32(374), pp. 228-230, 1722. Crossref DOI 10.1098/rstl.1722.0039
    • The formula is named after de Moivre but he never stated it in his works; 1730 Miscellanea analytica treats cos theta and cos n theta. English Wikipedia, De Moivre's formula
  5. 1730

    De Moivre's Miscellanea analytica states the cosine multiple-angle relation

    key development

    A. de Moivre, Miscellanea analytica de seriebus et quadraturis, London, 1730, Lemma 1

    Lemma 1 of the book gives the cosine of a multiple arc through the n-th root of l + sqrt(l^2-1), the explicit form of the multiple-angle theorem.

    Evidence (2)
    • In 1730 de Moivre explicitly considered the cases of cos x and cos nx (Miscellanea analytica, p. 1, Lemma 1). English Wikipedia, De Moivre's formula
    • Open Library work record: Miscellanea analytica de seriebus et quadraturis, first published 1730. Open Library OL7258966W
  6. 1740

    Euler turns to the exponential and corresponds with Bernoulli

    precursor

    Around 1740 Euler compared the series for the exponential and the trigonometric functions; in letters with Bernoulli he also suggested logarithms have infinitely many values.

    Evidence (1)
    • Wikipedia, Euler's formula: around 1740 Euler derived the relation by comparing series; his correspondence with Bernoulli shows Bernoulli did not grasp complex logarithms and Euler suggested infinitely many values. English Wikipedia, Euler's formula
  7. 1748

    Euler publishes exp(ix) = cos x + i sin x

    key development

    Euler, Introductio in analysin infinitorum, vol. 1, Lausanne 1748 (written 1745), E101

    The exponential form of the formula appears in print in the first volume of the Introductio.

    Evidence (2)
    • Euler Archive E101: quarto book published 1748, written 1745. Euler Archive
    • MacTutor: Euler in 1748 gave the formula exp(ix) = cos x + i sin x, equivalent to Cotes's. MacTutor, Trigonometric functions
  8. 1748

    Introductio makes the function central to analysis

    consolidation

    Euler, Introductio in analysin infinitorum, 1748, E101

    The same book also gives the first systematic treatment of elementary functions, series and exponentials as the base of analysis.

    Evidence (1)
    • Euler Archive summary of E101: lays the foundations of modern analysis, centres the function concept and the role of e^x. Euler Archive
  9. 1751

    Euler settles the logarithms of negative numbers

    key development

    Euler, De la controverse entre Mrs. Leibnitz et Bernoulli sur les logarithmes des nombres negatifs et imaginaires, Mem. Acad. Berlin 5, pp. 139-179 (1751; written 1747), E168

    Euler resolved the old Leibniz-Bernoulli dispute by showing that logarithms of complex numbers are many-valued.

    Evidence (1)
    • Euler Archive E168: published 1751, written 1747, Berlin Memoires vol. 5 pp. 139-179. Euler Archive
