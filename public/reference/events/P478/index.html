1968 · Computer science

Analysis of algorithms (Knuth)

Generating functions and asymptotics are used to compute the running time of programs, creating a mathematical theory of algorithm performance.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1968

    The Art of Computer Programming, Volume 1

    consolidation

    Knuth, The Art of Computer Programming, Vol. 1: Fundamental Algorithms, Addison-Wesley, 1968

    The first systematic treatment of program performance and mathematical analysis, which Knuth later names the subject after.

    Evidence (2)
    • Knuth published the first volume of TAOCP in 1968; its analysis led him to name the subject Wikipedia: Donald Knuth
    • Review of TAOCP Vol. 1 (SIAM Review 1969), DOI 10.1137/1011017 Crossref
  2. 1968

    Knuth's Art of Computer Programming Vol. 1

    first statement

    D. E. Knuth, The Art of Computer Programming, Vol. 1: Fundamental Algorithms, Addison-Wesley, 1968

    The first volume applies mathematical analysis to running times of algorithms; Knuth later chose the name analysis of algorithms from this book's approach.

    Dating or attribution note

    Same book already recorded as consolidation; it plays both roles here. Chapter-level content was not checked, and no earlier Knuth paper stating the idea was searched exhaustively.

    Evidence (3)
    • Open Library lists the first edition as 1968, Addison-Wesley. Open Library work OL257263W
    • zbMATH lists Vol. 1 as 1968. zbMATH Open document 3303654
    • Wikipedia says Knuth coined the term and, per Quanta, adopted the name after his work on the book. Wikipedia, Analysis of algorithms and Donald Knuth
  3. 1971

    Optimum binary search trees

    first application

    Knuth, Optimum binary search trees, Acta Informatica 1 (1971)

    Knuth uses a mathematical analysis of expected search cost to find optimal search-tree structures.

    Dating or attribution note

    Chosen as a concrete application of analysis of algorithms; the claim that it is the first such application was not checked against earlier analyses.

    Evidence (1)
    • Knuth, Optimum binary search trees, Acta Informatica, DOI 10.1007/bf00264289 Crossref
  4. 1976

    Asymptotic notation standardised

    key development

    Knuth, Big Omicron and big Omega and big Theta, SIGACT News 8(2) (1976)

    Knuth's article standardises the O, Omega and Theta notation used to state algorithmic running times.

    Evidence (1)
    • Knuth, Big Omicron and big Omega and big Theta, DOI 10.1145/1008328.1008329 Crossref
  5. 2009

    Analytic Combinatorics

    consolidation

    Flajolet, Sedgewick, Analytic Combinatorics, Cambridge University Press, 2009

    A systematic monograph on generating functions and asymptotics for the analysis of algorithms.

    Evidence (1)
    • Flajolet-Sedgewick, Analytic Combinatorics, DOI 10.1017/cbo9780511801655 Crossref
