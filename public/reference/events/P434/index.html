1878 · Topology

Cardinality versus dimension: R^n equinumerous with R

Cantor defined equivalence of sets by bijection and showed that Euclidean n-space has the same cardinality as the line, which forced dimension to be defined by something finer than counting points.

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1877

    Cantor's letter to Dedekind

    precursor

    Cantor to Dedekind, 1877 (private letter)

    Cantor privately reported a one-to-one match between the interval and higher-dimensional space and was himself astonished.

    Evidence (1)
    • Says he wrote to Dedekind in 1877 proving a one-to-one correspondence between [0,1] and p-dimensional space. MacTutor biography of Cantor
  2. 1878

    Cantor's published contribution to the theory of manifolds

    first statement

    Cantor, Ein Beitrag zur Mannigfaltigkeitslehre, J. reine angew. Math. 84, 242-258, 1878

    Cantor published the bijection between R^n and R and the notion of equivalence of sets by one-to-one correspondence.

    Evidence (2)
    • Record: Crelle 84 (1878) 242-258. Crossref
    • Notes the dimension paper submitted to Crelle in 1877 was delayed by Kronecker's suspicion. MacTutor biography of Cantor
  3. 1879

    Netto: no continuous bijection between dimensions

    key development

    Netto, Beitrag zur Mannigfaltigkeitslehre, Crelle 86, 263-268, 1879

    Netto showed a continuous one-to-one map cannot join spaces of different dimension, in the restricted setting of the time.

    Dating or attribution note

    Author attribution rests on the standard citation, not on the record's metadata.

    Evidence (2)
    • Record: Crelle 86 (1879) 263-268, same title; author missing from the Crossref entry. Crossref
    • Crossref: Netto, 'Beitrag zur Mannigfaltigkeitslehre', J. reine angew. Math. 86 (1879), pp. 263-268. Crossref 10.1515/crll.1879.86.263
  4. 1890

    Peano's space-filling curve

    key development

    Peano, Sur une courbe, qui remplit toute une aire plane, Math. Ann. 36, 157-160, 1890

    Peano built a continuous map from the line onto a square, showing continuity alone does not protect dimension.

    Evidence (1)
    • Record: Math. Ann. 36 (1890) 157-160. Crossref
  5. 1911

    Brouwer proves invariance of dimension

    key development

    Brouwer, Beweis der Invarianz der Dimensionenzahl, Math. Ann. 70, 161-165, 1911

    Brouwer proved that homeomorphic Euclidean spaces have equal dimension, settling the question raised by Cantor's bijections.

    Evidence (2)
    • Record: Math. Ann. 70 (1911) 161-165. Crossref
    • Associates the invariance of domain theorem with Brouwer, published 1912. Wikipedia: Invariance of domain
  6. 1923–1926

    Intrinsic definitions of dimension

    key development

    Menger, Ueber die Dimensionalitaet von Punktmengen, Monatsh. Math. Phys. 33, 1923; Urysohn, Memoire sur les multiplicites Cantoriennes, Fund. Math. 7, 1925

    Menger and Urysohn introduced topological notions of dimension defined from the space itself rather than from point counts.

    Dating or attribution note

    Description based on titles and standard history; paper contents were not read.

    Evidence (2)
    • Record: Monatsh. Math. Phys. 33 (1923) 148-160. Crossref
    • Record: Fund. Math. 7 (1925) 30-137. Crossref
  7. 1941

    Hurewicz-Wallman monograph

    consolidation

    Hurewicz and Wallman, Dimension Theory, Princeton, 1941

    First English systematic treatment of dimension theory.

    Evidence (1)
    • Archive.org record for Dimension Theory (the listed record is a later printing dated 1948). Internet Archive
