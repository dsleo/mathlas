1955 · Algebraic geometry

Coherent sheaves (FAC) in algebraic geometry

Sheaf cohomology was imported into algebraic varieties over Zariski topology.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1955

    Serre's Faisceaux algébriques cohérents

    first statement

    Serre, Faisceaux algébriques cohérents, Annals of Mathematics, 1955

    Serre introduces coherent algebraic sheaves and sheaf cohomology on varieties, the public statement of the FAC framework.

    Evidence (2)
    • Article record, Annals 1955 Crossref
    • MacTutor lists Faisceaux algébriques cohérents (1955) among Serre's major texts MacTutor, Jean-Pierre Serre biography
  2. 1955

    Title use of 'faisceaux algébriques cohérents'

    naming

    Serre, Faisceaux algébriques cohérents, Annals of Mathematics, 1955 (same paper as above)

    The 1955 title is the first use of the name found; the same paper as the first_statement.

    Evidence (1)
    • Article title in the Annals record Crossref
  3. 1956

    Serre's GAGA comparison of analytic and algebraic coherent sheaves

    first application

    Serre, Géométrie algébrique et géométrie analytique, Annales de l'Institut Fourier, 1956

    Serre uses coherent sheaf cohomology to compare analytic and algebraic coherent sheaves on projective varieties, the first major use of the framework.

    Evidence (1)
    • Article record, Ann. Inst. Fourier 1956 Crossref
  4. 1977

    Hartshorne's Algebraic Geometry

    consolidation

    Hartshorne, Algebraic Geometry, Graduate Texts in Mathematics 52, Springer, 1977

    A graduate textbook that systematically develops coherent sheaves and cohomology for algebraic varieties.

    Dating or attribution note

    Hartshorne's GTM text is one of several systematic treatments (EGA omitted; not checked).

    Evidence (1)
    • OpenLibrary record, first publication year 1977 (Springer) OpenLibrary search
  5. Date unknown

    Oka coherence theorem for analytic sheaves

    precursor

    Oka's coherence theorem for holomorphic functions is the analytic background for the algebraic notion; a precursor with no dated record checked.

    Evidence (1)
    • Wikipedia Coherent sheaf article states the Oka coherence theorem for holomorphic functions; no date given there Wikipedia, Coherent sheaf
