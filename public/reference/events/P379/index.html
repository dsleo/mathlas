1894 · Algebra

Italian algebraic geometry

Castelnuovo, Enriques and Severi classified algebraic surfaces up to birational equivalence with geometric methods lacking rigorous foundations.

Also known as: Italian school of algebraic geometry

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1860

    Cremona founds the Italian geometry school

    precursor

    Luigi Cremona, Italian school of geometry, from 1860

    Cremona's school of geometry in Italy is regarded as the root of the later algebraic-geometry school.

    Evidence (1)
    • Wikipedia, citing Guerraggio and Nastasi and Brigaglia and Ciliberto, calls Cremona the founder, with Segre keeping the school Cremona established in 1860. Wikipedia: Italian school of algebraic geometry
  2. 1894

    Enriques: Ricerche di geometria sulle superficie algebriche

    first statement

    Enriques, Ricerche di geometria sulle superficie algebriche, Mem. Accad. Torino, 1893-94

    A major early work on the birational geometry of algebraic surfaces, written after advice from Castelnuovo.

    Evidence (2)
    • zbMATH record dated 1894. zbMATH Open
    • MacTutor: in 1893 Enriques published this work, an important contribution to the theory of algebraic surfaces. MacTutor Biographies/Enriques
  3. 1894

    Castelnuovo: rationality of plane involutions

    key development

    Castelnuovo, Sulla razionalita delle involuzioni piane, Math. Ann. 44, 1894

    Castelnuovo proved a Luroth-type rationality theorem for involutions of the plane, a key tool for surface classification.

    Dating or attribution note

    The paper's specific role in the classification is from standard accounts and was not checked against the text.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref: Mathematische Annalen 44, 1894. Crossref
    • Wikipedia: Castelnuovo proved important parts of the classification. Wikipedia: Enriques%E2%80%93Kodaira classification
  4. 1900

    Castelnuovo and Enriques on rationality of double planes

    key development

    Castelnuovo and Enriques, Sulle condizioni di razionalita dei piani doppi, Rend. Circ. Mat. Palermo 14, 1900

    A joint paper giving conditions for rationality of double planes.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref: Rendiconti del Circolo Matematico di Palermo 14, 1900. Crossref
  5. 1905

    Enriques on surfaces of geometric genus zero

    key development

    Enriques, Sulle superficie algebriche di genere geometrico zero, Rend. Circ. Mat. Palermo 20, 1905

    Enriques' study of surfaces of genus zero, the area where his classification of surfaces into types took shape.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref: Rendiconti del Circolo Matematico di Palermo 20, 1905. Crossref
    • Wikipedia credits Enriques with describing the classification of complex projective surfaces. Wikipedia: Enriques%E2%80%93Kodaira classification
  6. 1921

    Severi: Vorlesungen ueber algebraische Geometrie

    consolidation

    Severi, Vorlesungen ueber algebraische Geometrie (German edition), 1921

    Lectures presenting the Italian school's algebraic geometry in book form.

    Evidence (1)
    • zbMATH record: German lectures, 1921. zbMATH Open
  7. 1949

    Enriques: Le superficie algebriche

    consolidation

    Enriques, Le superficie algebriche, Zanichelli, 1949

    Enriques' summary treatise on algebraic surfaces, with a preface by Castelnuovo.

    Evidence (1)
    • zbMATH record: Enriques, Le superficie algebriche, 1949. zbMATH Open
  8. 1960

    Kodaira: On compact complex analytic surfaces, I

    key development

    Kodaira, Annals of Math. 71, 1960

    Kodaira recovered the Italian classification in complex-manifold language and extended it to non-algebraic surfaces.

    Evidence (3)
    • Crossref: Annals of Mathematics 71, 1960. Crossref
    • Wikipedia: Kodaira recovered the Enriques classification in modern language in the 1950s. Wikipedia: Italian school of algebraic geometry
    • Wikipedia: Kodaira extended the classification to non-algebraic compact surfaces. Wikipedia: Enriques%E2%80%93Kodaira classification
