1893 · Algebra

Abstract (axiomatic) rings and fields

Dedekind, Weber, Steinitz and Fraenkel gave axiomatic definitions of fields and rings, culminating in van der Waerden's textbook.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1871

    Dedekind's field and ideal language

    precursor

    R. Dedekind, supplement to Dirichlet's Vorlesungen über Zahlentheorie (1871)

    A field and ideal vocabulary precedes explicit axiomatic treatments.

    Evidence (1)
    • Dirichlet, Vorlesungen uber Zahlentheorie, 2nd ed. by Dedekind, Braunschweig: Vieweg (1871) zbMATH JFM 03.0063.01
  2. 1893

    Weber's abstract field framework

    first statement

    H. Weber, Die allgemeinen Grundlagen der Galois'schen Gleichungstheorie (1893)

    A checked published paper develops the abstract field framework.

    Evidence (1)
    • Weber's paper supplies a checked abstract-field/Galois framework in 1893. Springer original article record
  3. 1910

    Steinitz's algebraic theory of fields

    key development

    E. Steinitz, Algebraische Theorie der Körper (1910)

    Systematic field concepts extend the abstract programme.

    Evidence (1)
    • Steinitz, Algebraische Theorie der Korper, J. reine angew. Math. 137 (1910) 167ff. Crossref record
  4. 1921

    Noether applies abstract ring theory to ideals

    first application

    E. Noether, Idealtheorie in Ringbereichen (1921)

    The abstract ring viewpoint is used to solve ideal-theoretic problems.

    Evidence (1)
    • Noether applies abstract commutative ring ideas to ideal theory. Springer original article record
  5. 1930

    Moderne Algebra synthesizes the subject

    consolidation

    B. L. van der Waerden, Moderne Algebra I (1930)

    The textbook brings the abstract ring and field programme into a named course of study.

    Evidence (1)
    • van der Waerden, Moderne Algebra I (1930), II (1931), Grundlehren zbMATH JFM 56.0138.01; 0002.00804
