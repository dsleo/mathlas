1949 · Differential equations

Feynman-Kac formula

Path integrals matched with expectations over Brownian paths.

Also known as: Feynman-Kac representation

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1948

    Feynman's space-time approach to quantum mechanics

    precursor

    Feynman R. P., Space-time approach to non-relativistic quantum mechanics, Rev. Mod. Phys. 20, 367-387, 1948

    Feynman's path-integral formulation of non-relativistic quantum mechanics, the physical side of the later link to diffusion.

    Dating or attribution note

    Precursor: physical path integral, not the Wiener/Brownian expectation identity. Feynman's 1942 thesis is not checked.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref record: Rev. Mod. Phys. 20, 367-387, 1948 Crossref
    • Wikipedia says the formula relates to Feynman's path integrals and Kac's work, 1947-1949 Wikipedia, Feynman-Kac formula
  2. 1949

    Kac: distributions of Wiener functionals

    first statement

    Kac M., On distributions of certain Wiener functionals, Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. 65, 1-13, 1949

    Kac expressed solutions of the heat and Schrodinger-type equations as expectations over Wiener (Brownian) paths, the probabilistic formulation of the formula.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref record: Trans. AMS 65, 1-13, 1949 Crossref
    • Wikipedia says the original formulation about Wiener functionals was published by Kac in 1949 Wikipedia, Feynman-Kac formula
  3. 1949

    Kac applies the Wiener-functional formula to distributions of functionals

    first application

    M. Kac, On distributions of certain Wiener functionals, Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. 65 (1949) 1-13

    Kac uses the Wiener-functional formula to compute distributions of functionals of Brownian paths, the first purposeful use in the sources checked.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref: Trans. AMS 65 (1949) 1-13, On distributions of certain Wiener functionals Crossref, Kac 1949
  4. 1951

    Kac: connections between probability theory and differential equations

    key development

    Kac M., On some connections between probability theory and differential and integral equations, Proc. 2nd Berkeley Symp., 1951

    A broader treatment of the link between probability and differential and integral equations, extending the Wiener-functional identity.

    Dating or attribution note

    Content inferred from the title and the Wikipedia history; the text was not checked.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref record: Proc. Second Berkeley Symp., 1951, DOI 10.1525/9780520411586-016 Crossref
  5. 1979

    Simon, Functional Integration and Quantum Physics

    consolidation

    Simon B., Functional Integration and Quantum Physics, Academic Press, 1979

    A monograph treating the functional-integral (Feynman-Kac) representation as a tool in quantum physics.

    Dating or attribution note

    Priority as the first systematic monograph was not checked; earlier surveys may exist.

    Evidence (1)
    • Open Library record: Academic Press, 1979 Open Library
  6. 1988

    Quantum Monte Carlo with the Feynman-Kac formula

    key development

    Caffarel M., Claverie P., Development of a pure diffusion quantum Monte Carlo method using a full generalized Feynman-Kac formula, J. Chem. Phys. 88, 1988

    A pure diffusion quantum Monte Carlo method built on the generalised Feynman-Kac formula, applied to the Schrodinger equation in quantum chemistry.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref record: J. Chem. Phys., 1988, DOI 10.1063/1.454227 Crossref
    • Wikipedia says the formula is used in quantum chemistry with pure diffusion Monte Carlo Wikipedia, Feynman-Kac formula
