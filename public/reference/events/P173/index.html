1248 · Algebra

Tian yuan (celestial element) algebra

A positional notation for polynomials in an unknown, extended to four unknowns, let equations be set up and manipulated.

Also known as: tian yuan shu

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1248

    Ce yuan hai jing sets out the tian yuan array notation

    first statement

    Li Zhi (Li Ye), Ce yuan hai jing (Sea mirror of circle measurements), 1248

    Earliest surviving source for writing an equation as an array of coefficients around the unknown (tian yuan), used to solve circle-in-triangle geometry problems.

    Evidence (3)
    • Li Zhi's book is the earliest source of the method; it was completed in 1248; the method was invented earlier. MacTutor, Li Zhi biography
    • Ce yuan hai jing written 1248, contains the tian yuan method. MacTutor, Overview of Chinese mathematics
    • Modern translation/study of Ceyuan haijing is indexed (confirms the work and title). zbMATH 0594.01019 (Bai Shangshu, 1985)
  2. 1299

    Zhu Shijie's Introduction to Mathematical Studies

    consolidation

    Zhu Shijie, Suanxue qimeng, 1299

    A teaching text that covers polynomial algebra by the coefficient-array method and brought it to southern China.

    Evidence (1)
    • Suanxue qimeng published 1299; Zhu treats polynomial algebra by the celestial-unknown method. MacTutor, Zhu Shijie biography
  3. 1303

    Jade mirror of the four unknowns

    key development

    Zhu Shijie, Siyuan yujian, 1303

    Extends the notation to polynomials in up to four unknowns and gives 288 problems solved with it.

    Evidence (2)
    • Siyuan yujian published 1303; extends the method to four unknowns. MacTutor, Zhu Shijie biography
    • Study of the Jade Mirror is indexed. zbMATH 0481.01006 (Hoe, Zhu Shijie and his Jade Mirror, 1981)
  4. Date unknown

    Wang Xiaotong's cubic-equation algorithm

    precursor

    Wang Xiaotong, Jigu suanjing (Continuation of ancient mathematics)

    Extended the cube-root algorithm to solve cubic equations; MacTutor sees this as a first step toward the celestial-unknown notation.

    Evidence (1)
    • Wang Xiaotong's cubic work is called a first step toward the tian yuan method. MacTutor, Overview of Chinese mathematics
