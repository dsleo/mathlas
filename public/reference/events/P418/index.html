1923 · Number theory

Hasse local-global principle

Hasse showed a quadratic form represents zero over the rationals when it does so over all completions.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1891

    Minkowski's reduction of positive quadratic forms

    precursor

    H. Minkowski, 'Ueber die positiven quadratischen Formen und ueber kettenbruchaehnliche Algorithmen', J. reine angew. Math. 107, 1891

    Minkowski's work on rational quadratic forms is the earlier local-to-global result that Hasse later generalised.

    Evidence (3)
    • Minkowski 1891 Crelle paper with this title exists Crossref DOI 10.1515/crll.1891.107.278
    • Wikipedia credits Minkowski with the rational case of the local-global principle for quadratic forms Wikipedia: Hasse principle (raw wikitext)
    • zbMATH 23.0212.01: Minkowski, 'On the positive quadratic forms and on the continued fractions like algorithms', J. Math. 107 (1891) 278-297. zbMATH Open, document 23.0212.01
  2. 1923

    Hasse's local-global theorem for quadratic forms over Q

    first statement

    H. Hasse, 'Ueber die Aequivalenz quadratischer Formen im Koerper der rationalen Zahlen', J. reine angew. Math. 152, 1923

    Hasse showed that equivalence of quadratic forms over the rationals is decided by all completions.

    Evidence (3)
    • Hasse 1923 Crelle paper with this title exists Crossref DOI 10.1515/crll.1923.152.205
    • Wikipedia states the Hasse principle is the local-global statement for quadratic forms over Q Wikipedia: Hasse principle (raw wikitext)
    • Crossref: Hasse, 'Ueber die Aequivalenz quadratischer Formen im Koerper der rationalen Zahlen', J. reine angew. Math. 152 (1923), pp. 205-224. Crossref 10.1515/crll.1923.152.205
  3. 1923

    Hasse's representation of numbers by quadratic forms

    first application

    H. Hasse, 'Ueber die Darstellbarkeit von Zahlen durch quadratische Formen im Koerper der rationalen Zahlen', J. reine angew. Math. 152, 1923

    Hasse used the local-global criterion to decide which integers are represented by a given rational quadratic form.

    Evidence (2)
    • Second 1923 Hasse Crelle paper with this title exists Crossref DOI 10.1515/crll.1923.152.129
    • Crossref: Hasse, 'Ueber die Darstellbarkeit von Zahlen durch quadratische Formen...', J. reine angew. Math. 152 (1923), pp. 129-148. Crossref 10.1515/crll.1923.152.129
  4. 1932

    Albert-Brauer-Hasse-Noether theorem

    key development

    R. Brauer, 'Beweis eines Hauptsatzes in der Theorie der Algebren', J. reine angew. Math. 167, 1932 (with Hasse and Noether)

    The local-global principle was extended from quadratic forms to central simple algebras over number fields.

    Evidence (2)
    • Brauer 1932 Crelle paper with this title exists Crossref DOI 10.1515/crll.1932.167.399
    • Wikipedia has an Albert-Brauer-Hasse-Noether section in the Hasse principle article Wikipedia: Hasse principle (raw wikitext)
  5. 1970

    Serre's textbook 'Cours d'arithmetique' (Chapter on quadratic forms over the rationals)

    consolidation

    J.-P. Serre, Cours d'arithmetique, Le Mathematicien 2, Paris: Presses Universitaires de France, 1970

    A textbook that presents quadratic forms over the rationals and the local-global (Hasse-Minkowski) principle as a systematic topic.

    Dating or attribution note

    Textbook role from the record; the chapter on quadratic forms was not read in this pass, so the claim about the local-global principle is not verified. Cassels' 'Rational quadratic forms' (1978, Zbl 0395.10029) is a later monograph on the same theory and is not used as the consolidation.

    Evidence (1)
