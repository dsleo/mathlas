1890 · Algebra

Commutative algebra from invariant theory and ideals

Non-constructive finiteness arguments for invariants and ideals of polynomials replace computation, and rings become the unit of study.

Also known as: Commutative ring theory

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1871

    Dedekind's ideals and modules

    precursor

    R. Dedekind, supplement to Dirichlet's Vorlesungen ueber Zahlentheorie, 2nd ed. (1871); expanded in the 3rd ed. (1879)

    Dedekind defined ideals and the word module for rings of algebraic integers, the number-theoretic root of the subject.

    Evidence (4)
    • MacTutor: Dedekind defined an ideal and introduced the word module in 1871. MacTutor HistTopics/Ring_theory
    • zbMATH lists the 1879 third edition edited by Dedekind. zbMATH Open
    • Dedekind's supplement is indexed only through its 1964 reprint in the Gesammelte Werke Crossref DOI 10.1007/978-3-322-98606-1_1 (reprint)
    • Open Library lists a 1871 edition of Dirichlet-Dedekind, Vorlesungen ueber Zahlentheorie. Open Library search
  2. 1890

    Hilbert: Ueber die Theorie der algebraischen Formen

    first statement

    Hilbert, Math. Ann. 36, 1890

    Hilbert proved the finite generation of invariants and ideals of polynomials by a non-constructive argument, replacing explicit computation.

    Evidence (5)
    • Crossref: Mathematische Annalen 36, pp. 473-534, 1890. Crossref
    • Wikipedia: Hilbert introduced a more abstract approach, replacing computational methods of classical invariant theory. Wikipedia: Commutative algebra
    • Crossref: Hilbert, 'Ueber die Theorie der algebraischen Formen', Math. Ann. 36 (1890), pp. 473-534. Crossref 10.1007/bf01208503
    • MacTutor: Hilbert's basis theorem arose from invariant theory MacTutor Ring theory topic; MacTutor Hilbert biography
    • Wikipedia: Hilbert stated and proved the basis theorem in his 1890 article on invariant theory, Math. Ann. 36, pp. 473-534. Wikipedia, Hilbert's basis theorem
  3. 1893

    Hilbert: Ueber die vollen Invariantensysteme

    key development

    Hilbert, Math. Ann. 42, 1893

    Hilbert's second memoir on invariants, completing the finiteness theory for polynomial ideals.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref: Mathematische Annalen 42, pp. 313-373, 1893. Crossref
    • MacTutor dates Hilbert's basis theorem to 1893 and notes Gordan's reaction to it. MacTutor HistTopics/Ring_theory
  4. 1905

    Lasker: primary decomposition in polynomial rings

    key development

    Lasker, Zur Theorie der Moduln und Ideale, Math. Ann. 60, 1905

    Lasker introduced primary ideals and proved that ideals in polynomial rings decompose into them.

    Evidence (3)
    • Crossref: Mathematische Annalen 60, 1905. Crossref
    • MacTutor: primary ideals were introduced in 1905 by Lasker in polynomial rings. MacTutor HistTopics/Ring_theory
    • Lasker 1905 Math. Ann. paper with this title exists Crossref DOI 10.1007/bf01447495
  5. 1921

    Noether: Idealtheorie in Ringbereichen

    key development

    Noether, Math. Ann. 83, 1921

    Noether proved primary decomposition for any commutative ring with the ascending chain condition, unifying polynomial rings and rings of numbers.

    Evidence (4)
    • Crossref: Mathematische Annalen 83, 1921. Crossref
    • MacTutor: this 1921 paper gave the decomposition in any commutative ring with ascending chain condition. MacTutor Biographies/Noether_Emmy
    • Noether 1921 Math. Ann. paper with this title exists Crossref DOI 10.1007/bf01464225
    • MacTutor: about 1921 Noether brought the polynomial and number rings under one abstract theory MacTutor Ring theory topic
  6. 1929

    Krull: Idealtheorie in Ringen ohne Endlichkeitsbedingung

    key development

    Krull, Math. Ann. 101, 1929

    Krull extended abstract ideal theory beyond the chain-condition setting.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref: Mathematische Annalen 101, 1929. Crossref
    • MacTutor: the axiomatic treatment of commutative rings was developed in the 1920s by Noether and Krull. MacTutor HistTopics/Ring_theory
  7. 1930

    van der Waerden: Moderne Algebra

    consolidation

    van der Waerden, Moderne Algebra, Springer, 1930-31

    The textbook that made Noether's abstract results widely available.

    Evidence (3)
    • MacTutor: Noether's results became widely known through van der Waerden's 1930 Modern Algebra. MacTutor HistTopics/Ring_theory
    • Crossref lists a chapter of Moderne Algebra dated 1930. Crossref
    • Crossref: Moderne Algebra, Springer, 1930; a Mathematical Gazette review dates Teil I (Grundlehren 33) 1930. Crossref API
  8. 1958

    Zariski-Samuel: Commutative Algebra

    naming

    Zariski and Samuel, Commutative Algebra, vol. 1, 1958

    The earliest book title found that names the field 'commutative algebra' in place of ideal theory.

    Evidence (3)
    • zbMATH record: Commutative algebra, vol. 1, 1958. zbMATH Open
    • Open Library lists the 1958 Commutative algebra (Zariski) first publication Open Library search
    • Open Library lists Zariski-Samuel, Commutative algebra, with first publication year 1958 (Van Nostrand). Open Library search
