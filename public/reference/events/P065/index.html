1896 · Number theory

Geometry of numbers (Minkowski)

Convex bodies and lattices in R^n are used to prove existence of integer points, with consequences for quadratic forms and algebraic number fields.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1887

    Minkowski's unpublished lecture on spatial intuition and minima of quadratic forms

    precursor

    Minkowski, Raeumliche Anschauung und Minima positiv definiter quadratischer Formen (lecture; published 1991)

    A lecture, unpublished until 1991, already contained an early instance of the method later called the geometry of numbers.

    Evidence (1)
    • MacTutor, quoting Dieudonne: it contains the first example of the method later developed as the geometry of numbers. MacTutor Biographies/Minkowski
  2. 1891

    Minkowski's Crelle paper giving a geometric proof of Hermite's theorem on positive quadratic forms

    precursor

    Minkowski, Ueber die positiven quadratischen Formen und ueber kettenbruchaehnliche Algorithmen, J. reine angew. Math. 107 (1891) 278-297

    A new geometric proof, via the ellipsoid, of Hermite's bound on the minimum of a positive quadratic form; Minkowski later saw that only convexity and central symmetry were needed.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref record: Minkowski, Ueber die positiven quadratischen Formen und ueber kettenbruchaehnliche Algorithmen, Crelle 107 (1891) 278-297. Crossref 10.1515/crll.1891.107.278
    • Minkowski's Anzeige in the 1910 edition: he obtained a new, more fruitful proof of Hermite's theorem in vol. 107 of Crelle's Journal and then noticed that the property rests on the ellipsoid being a centrally symmetric nowhere-concave surface. Minkowski, Geometrie der Zahlen (1910), Anzeige (archive.org full text, read)
  3. 1896

    Minkowski: Geometrie der Zahlen (first instalment)

    first statement

    Minkowski, Geometrie der Zahlen, Teubner, Leipzig, 1896 (completed 1910)

    Minkowski presented lattice points in convex bodies, with the first and second theorems, as a geometric method for number theory.

    Evidence (5)
    • Editors' preface (Hilbert, Speiser): the first instalment, pp. 1-240, appeared in 1896; the second was delayed and found as a manuscript after Minkowski's death. Minkowski, Geometrie der Zahlen (1910), Vorwort der Herausgeber (archive.org full text, read)
    • Minkowski states the convex-body theorem (a centrally symmetric nowhere-concave body of large enough volume contains a lattice point other than the origin) and cites his book Geometrie der Zahlen (1896, Teubner). Minkowski, Sur les proprietes des nombres entiers qui sont derivees de l'intuition de l'espace, Nouvelles annales de mathematiques 3e s. 15 (1896) 393-403 (Numdam; read)
    • MacTutor: the first 240 of the book's 256 pages appeared as its first section in 1896; the complete work was first published in 1910. MacTutor Biographies/Minkowski (re-read in review)
    • Wikipedia: in 1896 he presented his geometry of numbers. Wikipedia: Hermann Minkowski
    • Wikipedia attributes the founding of the line of research to Minkowski's work of that name. Wikipedia: Geometry of numbers
  4. 1896

    The title 'Geometrie der Zahlen'

    naming

    Minkowski, Geometrie der Zahlen, 1896

    The field's name is the title of Minkowski's book.

    Evidence (2)
    • Wikipedia says the term arose from Minkowski's embedding of numbers in higher-dimensional space and his book of that name. Wikipedia: Geometry of numbers
    • Minkowski: 'Geometrie der Zahlen habe ich diese Schrift betitelt, weil ich zu den Methoden ... durch raeumliche Anschauung gefuehrt bin.' Minkowski, Geometrie der Zahlen (1910), Anzeige (archive.org full text, read)
  5. 1896

    Class-number finiteness, units and discriminants from the lattice-point theorem

    first application

    Minkowski, Geometrie der Zahlen, Teubner, 1896 (sections 41-44 and 51); Nouvelles annales 15 (1896) 393-403

    Minkowski applies the convex-body theorem to Dirichlet's theorem on units, to a lower bound for the discriminant of a number field and to the finiteness of the number of classes of positive quadratic forms.

    Dating or attribution note

    Same instalment as the first statement, so the application is contemporaneous with it. The 1896 Nouvelles annales text says the theorems allowed Dirichlet's unit theorem, the finiteness of the number of ideal classes and the divisibility of every field discriminant by a prime to be proved, the last 'for the first time'; the finiteness of the ideal class number is Minkowski's own claim and its proof was not read. Wikipedia says geometry of numbers gave the first proof that the ideal class group is finite.

    Evidence (3)
    • Table of contents of the book: section 42 lower bound for a discriminant (p. 133), section 44 Dirichlet's theorem on complex units (p. 137), section 51 finiteness of class numbers of positive quadratic forms (p. 196), all within pp. 1-240. Minkowski, Geometrie der Zahlen (1910), Inhalt (archive.org full text, read)
    • Minkowski: the theorems on linear forms are applicable to the proof of Dirichlet's unit theorem and of the finiteness of the number of ideal classes, and established for the first time that the discriminant of every algebraic number field is divisible by at least one prime. Minkowski, Nouvelles annales de mathematiques 3e s. 15 (1896) 393-403, section IV (Numdam; read)
    • Wikipedia: the geometry of numbers gave the first proof that the ideal class group is finite. Wikipedia: Geometry of numbers
  6. 1907

    Minkowski: Diophantische Approximationen

    key development

    Minkowski, Diophantische Approximationen, Teubner, 1907

    An elementary account linking the geometry of numbers to Diophantine approximation and algebraic numbers.

    Evidence (2)
    • Internet Archive metadata: Minkowski, Diophantische Approximationen: eine Einfuehrung in die Zahlentheorie, 1907. Internet Archive
    • MacTutor: the 1907 book gave an elementary account of this work and its applications to Diophantine approximation and algebraic numbers. MacTutor Biographies/Minkowski
  7. 1910

    Geometrie der Zahlen, completed edition

    consolidation

    Minkowski, Geometrie der Zahlen, Teubner, 1910

    The completed book edition collecting the instalments, issued after Minkowski's death in 1909.

    Evidence (3)
    • Internet Archive record of the 1910 edition. Internet Archive
    • Wikipedia cites the 1910 Teubner edition. Wikipedia: Hermann Minkowski
    • Editors' preface to the 1910 edition: the second instalment was found as a finished manuscript in Minkowski's papers and published to give the work a certain conclusion. Minkowski, Geometrie der Zahlen (1910), Vorwort (archive.org full text, read)
  8. 1959

    Cassels: An Introduction to the Geometry of Numbers

    consolidation

    Cassels, An Introduction to the Geometry of Numbers, Springer, 1959

    A modern systematic monograph on the subject after the 1930-1960 growth of research.

    Evidence (3)
    • zbMATH record: Cassels, 1959. zbMATH Open
    • Wikipedia cites Cassels and describes 1930-1960 research by Mordell, Davenport and Siegel. Wikipedia: Geometry of numbers
    • Open Library: Cassels, An Introduction to the Geometry of Numbers, Springer, first published 1959. Open Library work OL1376413W
