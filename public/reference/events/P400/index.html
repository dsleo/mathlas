Russell paradox, type theory and Principia Mathematica

Russell's paradox led to the ramified theory of types and the formal derivation of mathematics.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1902

    Russell's private letter to Frege

    precursor

    Russell, B., letter to Frege (1902), unpublished

    Russell communicates the paradox privately to Frege in 1902, before any public statement.

    Evidence (1)
    • Russell found the paradox in 1902, independently found by Zermelo MacTutor, Beginnings of set theory (saved page)
  2. 1903

    Russell's paradox in The Principles of Mathematics

    first statement

    Russell, B., The Principles of Mathematics (1903)

    The paradox is stated publicly in the appendix of the 1903 book.

    Dating or attribution note

    The appendix placement of the paradox is taken from the Principia Mathematica Wikipedia lead; the Principles text itself was not read.

    Evidence (1)
    • The 1903 original text discusses the contradiction in Chapter X. Russell, The Principles of Mathematics, digitized text
  3. 1908

    Mathematical logic as based on the theory of types

    key development

    Russell, B., 'Mathematical logic as based on the theory of types', Amer. J. Math. 30 (1908)

    Presents the ramified theory of types as the response to the paradoxes.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref record: American Journal of Mathematics, year 1908 Crossref, DOI 10.2307/2369948
  4. 1910

    Principia Mathematica, volume 1

    consolidation

    Whitehead, A. N. and Russell, B., Principia Mathematica, vol. 1 (Cambridge UP, 1910)

    The formal derivation of mathematics from the theory of types, the first systematic treatment.

    Evidence (1)
    • zbMATH record: 'Principia mathematica', volume 1 (1910) zbMATH API, query 'Whitehead Russell Principia Mathematica'
