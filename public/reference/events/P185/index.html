De Moivre: probability and the trigonometry-complex number link

De Moivre extended probability to approximation by analysis and linked powers of complex numbers to trigonometry.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1707

    Early form of the multiple-angle relation

    first statement

    De Moivre, Aequationum quarundam potestatis tertiae, quintae, septimae ... resolutio analytica, Philosophical Transactions, 1707

    A paper on solving higher-odd-degree equations contains a relation from which the later power formula for cos x + i sin x can be deduced.

    Dating or attribution note

    The history pages say the 1707 relation implies the formula, not that it states it. I did not read the paper itself, so the exact content is taken from the history pages.

    Evidence (3)
    • A closely related formula appears in a 1707 paper; the equation of 1707 lets the later formula be deduced MacTutor, Abraham de Moivre
    • In 1707 de Moivre derived an equation from which the formula follows, proved for positive integers Wikipedia, Abraham de Moivre
    • Crossref dates the 1707 Phil. Trans. paper by de Moivre on equations of degree three, five, seven Crossref record 10.1098/rstl.1706.0037
  2. 1711

    De mensura sortis published

    first statement

    De Moivre, De mensura sortis, seu de probabilitate eventuum in ludis a casu fortuito pendentibus, Philosophical Transactions, 1711

    De Moivre's first probability treatise, later expanded into The Doctrine of Chances.

    Evidence (2)
    • A Latin version of the probability work was presented to the Royal Society and published in 1711 MacTutor, Abraham de Moivre
    • Crossref issue date 1711 for De mensura sortis Crossref record 10.1098/rstl.1710.0018
  3. 1718

    The Doctrine of Chances (1st English edition)

    consolidation

    De Moivre, The Doctrine of Chances, London, 1718

    The first book-length systematic treatment of the calculus of chances in English; later editions of 1738 and 1756 expanded it.

    Evidence (2)
    • The Doctrine of Chances was published in 1718, with new editions in 1738 and 1756 MacTutor, Abraham de Moivre
    • Catalogue entry of the 1718 edition Internet Archive catalogue record bim_eighteenth-century_the-doctrine-of-chances_moivre-abraham-de_1718
  4. 1722

    Power formula in cos + i sin form

    key development

    De Moivre, De sectione anguli, Philosophical Transactions, 1722

    A 1722 paper gives equations from which the familiar formula for (cos x + i sin x)^n is read off, tying trigonometry to complex numbers.

    Evidence (3)
    • The formula appears in this form in a paper of 1722 MacTutor, Abraham de Moivre
    • In 1722 he presented equations from which the better known form can be deduced Wikipedia, Abraham de Moivre
    • Crossref lists De sectione anguli by de Moivre, Phil. Trans. 1722 Crossref record 10.1098/rstl.1722.0039
  5. 1738

    Normal approximation to the binomial

    key development

    A. de Moivre, The Doctrine of Chances, 2nd ed., London, 1738, pp. 235-243 (English version of the Latin Approximatio of 1733)

    De Moivre approximates sums of binomial terms by what is now the normal curve, the first form of the central limit theorem.

    Evidence (2)
    • De Moivre first published the result in a Latin pamphlet of 1733 and in the 1738 Doctrine of Chances. MacTutor, De Moivre
    • Approximatio privately printed 1733, translated in the 1738 second edition Wikipedia, Abraham de Moivre
  6. 1748

    Euler links the formula to the complex exponential

    key development

    Euler, Introductio in analysin infinitorum, vol. 1, Lausanne, 1748

    Euler's treatise presents e^{ix} = cos x + i sin x, from which de Moivre's formula follows at once and its standing as a law of complex numbers is secured.

    Evidence (2)
    • De Moivre's formula is a precursor to Euler's formula; chapter 8 of the Introductio gives Euler's formula Wikipedia, De Moivre's formula and Introductio in analysin infinitorum
    • Euler Archive dates Introductio volume 1 to 1748 Euler Archive record E101 (bepress metadata)
