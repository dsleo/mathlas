1965 · Number theory

Sato-Tate conjecture (Frobenius angle distribution of elliptic curves)

Potential automorphy of symmetric powers gave equidistribution of Frobenius angles of elliptic curves.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1960

    Sato and Tate pose the conjecture independently

    precursor

    Sato and Tate independently pose the conjecture around 1960, before any publication.

    Evidence (1)
    • Sato and Tate independently posed the conjecture around 1960 Wikipedia, Sato-Tate conjecture
  2. 1965

    Tate's statement in 'Algebraic cycles and poles of zeta functions'

    first statement

    John Tate, Algebraic cycles and poles of zeta functions, in Arithmetical Algebraic Geometry (1965) 93-110

    The first published mention of the conjecture, in Tate's 1965 conference paper.

    Evidence (2)
    • Wikipedia cites Tate 1965 as the first published mention Wikipedia, Sato-Tate conjecture
    • Tate's work on zeta functions and cycles MacTutor, Tate biography
  3. 1968

    Birch: numerical test of the distribution

    first application

    Bryan Birch, How the number of points of an elliptic curve over a fixed prime field varies, J. London Math. Soc. 43 (1968) 57-60

    Birch tests the distribution of point counts numerically over a fixed prime field, the first systematic check of the conjectured law.

    Evidence (1)
    • Birch 1968 J. LMS record Crossref
  4. 1970

    Ogg titles a remark on the Sato-Tate conjecture

    naming

    Ogg, A. P., A remark on the Sato-Tate conjecture, 1970

    Earliest zbMATH title found that names the Sato-Tate conjecture.

    Evidence (1)
    • zbMATH record: "A remark on the Sato-Tate conjecture", Ogg, 1970 zbMATH Open doc 3348298
  5. 2008

    Clozel, Harris, Shepherd-Barron, Taylor: automorphy of l-adic lifts

    key development

    Laurent Clozel, Michael Harris, Nicholas Shepherd-Barron, Richard Taylor, Automorphy for some l-adic lifts of automorphic mod l Galois representations, Publ. Math. IHES 108 (2008) 1-181

    Proves the Sato-Tate conjecture for elliptic curves over totally real fields under a multiplicative-reduction hypothesis.

    Evidence (2)
    • CHSBT 2008 IHES record Crossref
    • Proof of Sato-Tate under mild assumptions in 2008 Wikipedia, Sato-Tate conjecture
  6. 2010

    Harris, Shepherd-Barron, Taylor: Calabi-Yau family and potential automorphy

    key development

    Michael Harris, Nicholas Shepherd-Barron, Richard Taylor, A family of Calabi-Yau varieties and potential automorphy, Annals of Math. 171 (2010)

    Potential automorphy for a family of Calabi-Yau varieties (Dwork-type), the input method for the Sato-Tate proof.

    Dating or attribution note

    Link to Sato-Tate taken from the input summary (Dwork families) and the 2011 follow-up; the 2010 paper's role is not read in full.

    Evidence (1)
    • HST 2010 Annals record Crossref
  7. 2011

    Barnet-Lamb, Geraghty, Harris, Taylor: completion of the Sato-Tate proof

    key development

    Thomas Barnet-Lamb, David Geraghty, Michael Harris, Richard Taylor, A family of Calabi-Yau varieties and potential automorphy II, Publ. RIMS 47 (2011)

    Completes the proof of Sato-Tate for elliptic curves over totally real fields.

    Evidence (2)
    • BGHT 2011 record (Publ. RIMS) Crossref
    • Completion in 2011 Wikipedia, Sato-Tate conjecture
