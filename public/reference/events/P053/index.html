1859 · Number theory

Riemann's zeta function and the prime number theorem

The zeta function is continued to the complex plane and its zeros are tied to the distribution of primes, so function-theoretic facts (no zeros on Re s = 1) give the asymptotic law.

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1737

    Euler's zeta function over the reals

    precursor

    Euler studied the zeta series and its product over primes for real arguments, linking primes to analysis.

    Evidence (1)
    • Euler used the zeta function with real argument as early as 1737. Wikipedia, Prime number theorem
  2. 1848–1850

    Chebyshev's partial results

    precursor

    Chebyshev, using real zeta values, showed the prime counting ratio is bounded above and below and that any limit must equal one.

    Evidence (1)
    • Two papers from 1848 and 1850 attempted the asymptotic law. Wikipedia, Prime number theorem
  3. 1859

    Riemann's memoir on the number of primes

    first statement

    B. Riemann, Ueber die Anzahl der Primzahlen unter einer gegebenen Grosse, Monatsberichte der Berliner Akademie, 1859

    Riemann continued zeta to the complex plane and tied its zeros to the primes, proposing complex methods for counting primes.

    Evidence (2)
    • The 1859 memoir connects the primes to zeros of the continued zeta function. Wikipedia, Prime number theorem
    • Crossref records the memoir reprinted in the Gesammelte Werke (original year from the citation). Crossref
  4. 1895

    von Mangoldt proves Riemann's formula

    key development

    H. von Mangoldt, Zu Riemanns Abhandlung 'Ueber die Anzahl der Primzahlen unter einer gegebenen Grosse', Crelle 114, 1895

    von Mangoldt gave rigorous proofs of the explicit formula suggested by Riemann, preparing the ground for the final proof.

    Dating or attribution note

    Description of content from the title and background knowledge; not read in the paper.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref: Crelle 114 (1895), title referencing Riemann's memoir. Crossref
  5. 1896

    Hadamard proves the prime number theorem

    first application

    J. Hadamard, Sur la distribution des zeros de la fonction zeta(s) et ses consequences arithmetiques, Bull. SMF 24, 1896

    Hadamard showed zeta has no zeros on the line Re s = 1 and deduced the asymptotic law for primes.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref: Bull. Soc. Math. France, 1896. Crossref
    • Both 1896 proofs use complex analysis and non-vanishing on Re s = 1. Wikipedia, Prime number theorem
  6. 1896

    de la Vallee Poussin's independent proof

    key development

    C.-J. de la Vallee Poussin, Recherches analytiques sur la theorie des nombres premiers, Ann. Soc. Sci. Bruxelles 20-21, 1896

    An independent proof in the same year, with a later 1899 memoir adding an error term.

    Evidence (2)
    • Proved independently of Hadamard in 1896. Wikipedia, Prime number theorem
    • Hadamard and de la Vallee Poussin proved it in 1896. MacTutor, Prime numbers
  7. 1909

    Landau's Handbuch der Lehre von der Verteilung der Primzahlen

    consolidation

    E. Landau, Handbuch der Lehre von der Verteilung der Primzahlen, Leipzig: Teubner, 1909

    A two-volume treatise collecting the analytic theory of primes, including the complex-analytic proof.

    Evidence (1)
    • Archive.org record dated 1909. Internet Archive
