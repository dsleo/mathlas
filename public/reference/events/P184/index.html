370 BCE · Astronomy & trigonometry

Eudoxus' homocentric spheres

Planetary motion was modelled by nested rotating spheres whose combined motion produces the observed paths.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 387 BCE–367 BCE

    Academy programme of uniform circular motion

    precursor

    Eudoxus worked in Plato's circle, where heavenly motion was expected to be simple and regular; this is the setting, not a dated text.

    Evidence (2)
    • Eudoxus is believed to have been influenced by Plato and Pythagoras when he designed the spheres. Wikipedia, Concentric spheres
    • Eudoxus fits the Pythagorean and Platonic ideal of simplicity; a cited article argues the inspiration was Pythagorean, not Platonic. MacTutor, Greek astronomy; MacTutor, Eudoxus
  2. 370 BCE–355 BCE

    Eudoxus proposes the homocentric spheres

    first statement

    Eudoxus, On Speeds (lost), c. 370-355 BC

    Eudoxus explained planetary paths, including retrogression, by nested spheres all centred on the Earth; his book is lost and the system is known through Aristotle and Simplicius.

    Evidence (3)
    • Eudoxus built a system of rotating spheres to account for the heavenly motions; the work On Speeds is lost. MacTutor, Eudoxus
    • The model had 27 spheres; our knowledge comes from Aristotle and Simplicius. Wikipedia, Concentric spheres
    • Aristotle describes Eudoxus' spheres for sun, moon and planets. Aristotle, Metaphysics XII.8, 1073b (Perseus)
  3. 335 BCE–322 BCE

    Aristotle turns the spheres into a physical cosmos

    key development

    Aristotle, Metaphysics XII.8 and On the Heavens

    Aristotle added unrolling spheres between the planetary sets and treated them as real bodies, giving 55 (or 47) spheres.

    Evidence (3)
    • Aristotle accepted the theory as physically real, not just a mathematical model. MacTutor, Greek astronomy
    • He added to Callippus' system for a total of fifty-five spheres and later doubted the count. Wikipedia, Concentric spheres
    • Technical study of Aristotle's corrections. Bechler, Aristotle corrects Eudoxus, Centaurus 15 (1971)
  4. 330 BCE

    Callippus tests the spheres against observed seasons

    first application

    Callippus, adjustments reported in Aristotle, Metaphysics XII.8

    Callippus found 27 spheres too few, added seven, and tied the scheme to measured season lengths and a 76-year cycle starting in 330 BC.

    Evidence (3)
    • He added two spheres each for sun and moon and one each for Mercury, Venus, Mars, and measured the seasons. Wikipedia, Callippus
    • He worked with Aristotle from 330 BC, correcting and completing Eudoxus' discoveries; his cycle began in 330 BC. MacTutor, Callippus
    • Aristotle reports Callippus' extra spheres. Aristotle, Metaphysics XII.8, 1073b (Perseus)
  5. 1875

    Schiaparelli reconstructs the spheres

    key development

    Schiaparelli, Le sfere omocentriche di Eudosso, di Callippo e di Aristotele (Milan, 1875)

    Schiaparelli's geometric reconstruction of the figure-eight hippopede made the lost system understandable and set the later debate.

    Evidence (3)
    • Scanned record of the 1875 book. Internet Archive record
    • Modern reconstructions of the planetary model follow Schiaparelli's. Wikipedia, Concentric spheres
    • Later technical studies of the hippopede and the geometry. Yavetz, On the homocentric spheres of Eudoxus, Arch. Hist. Exact Sci. 52 (1998)
  6. 1875

    Schiaparelli titles the spheres as homocentric

    naming

    Schiaparelli, G. V., Le sfere omocentriche di Eudosso, di Callippo e di Aristotele, 1875

    The earliest title found that uses the homocentric-spheres name (Italian form).

    Evidence (2)
    • zbMATH record gives the title with year 1875 (Brera series) zbMATH Open doc 2714957
    • Open Library lists the same title with first publication year 1874 Open Library works OL39025650W
