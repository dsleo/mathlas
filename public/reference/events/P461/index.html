1961 · Analysis

Nonstandard analysis (Robinson's infinitesimals via model theory)

Robinson used model theory to build a field with true infinitesimals and reprove or extend results in analysis; the 1974 congress president called it a fresh direction.

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1961

    Robinson, 'Non-standard analysis'

    first statement

    Abraham Robinson, Non-standard analysis, Indagationes Math. 23 (1961)

    Robinson builds a model-theoretic extension of the reals containing infinitesimals and states its analysis programme.

    Evidence (2)
    • Robinson 1961 Indag. Math. record Crossref
    • Non-standard analysis introduced in 1961 MacTutor, Robinson biography
  2. 1961

    The name 'Non-standard analysis' in a title

    naming

    Abraham Robinson, Non-standard analysis, Indag. Math. 23 (1961)

    The field name appears in the title of the 1961 paper.

    Evidence (1)
    • 1961 article has the title 'Non-standard analysis' Crossref
  3. 1966

    Robinson, Non-standard Analysis (monograph)

    consolidation

    Abraham Robinson, Non-standard Analysis, North-Holland (1966)

    Robinson's monograph is the systematic treatment of the field.

    Evidence (2)
    • 1966 monograph, cited in MacTutor and Wikipedia MacTutor, Robinson biography
    • Monograph published in 1966 (North-Holland) Wikipedia, Nonstandard analysis
  4. 1975

    Loeb measure: nonstandard to standard measure spaces

    key development

    Peter A. Loeb, Conversion from nonstandard to standard measure spaces and applications in probability theory, Trans. AMS 211 (1975) 113-122

    Loeb's construction turns a nonstandard measure space into a standard measure space, making NSA useful in probability.

    Evidence (1)
    • Loeb 1975 TAMS record Crossref
  5. 1976

    Keisler, Elementary Calculus: An Infinitesimal Approach

    consolidation

    H. Jerome Keisler, Elementary Calculus: An Infinitesimal Approach, Prindle, Weber and Schmidt (1976)

    A textbook teaching calculus with infinitesimals, the main pedagogical consolidation.

    Dating or attribution note

    Not verified in a bibliographic index.
