1913 · Mathematical physics

General relativity and the absolute differential calculus

Riemannian geometry and the tensor calculus are adopted as the language of gravitation, then fed back as a source of problems for geometry and analysis.

Also known as: General theory of relativity

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1854

    Riemann's unpublished lecture on the foundations of geometry

    precursor

    Riemann, Probevorlesung (habilitation lecture), 1854

    Riemann's lecture on spaces with a quadratic length element set the geometric ideas that Ricci later turned into a calculus; it was not printed until after his death.

    Evidence (1)
    • MacTutor's Ricci-Curbastro biography says the ideas were developed in Riemann's 1854 test lecture MacTutor biography of Ricci-Curbastro
  2. 1869

    Christoffel's transformation of quadratic differential forms

    precursor

    Christoffel, Ueber die Transformation der homogenen Differentialausdrücke zweiten Grades, Journal für die reine und angewandte Mathematik 70, 46-70, 1869

    Ricci and Levi-Civita credit Christoffel with the algorithm of the calculus, which they placed within the geometry of n-dimensional varieties.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref record of Christoffel 1869, Crelle 70, 46-70 Crossref 10.1515/crll.1869.70.46
    • MacTutor quotes Ricci and Levi-Civita's preface that the algorithm is found complete in a remark due to Christoffel MacTutor biography of Ricci-Curbastro
  3. 1900

    Ricci and Levi-Civita on absolute differential calculus

    precursor

    Ricci and Levi-Civita, Méthodes de calcul différentiel absolu et leurs applications, Mathematische Annalen 54, 125-201, 1900

    A long joint paper presenting the whole calculus and its applications to geometry and mechanics, the form in which Einstein later learned it; the title is also the earliest I found carrying the name.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref record of the paper, Math. Ann. 54, 125-201 Crossref 10.1007/bf01454201
    • MacTutor describes the paper as the fundamental joint paper after Ricci's work of 1888-92 MacTutor biography of Ricci-Curbastro
  4. 1913

    Einstein and Grossmann: gravitation as a metric tensor

    first statement

    Einstein and Grossmann, Entwurf einer verallgemeinerten Relativitätstheorie und einer Theorie der Gravitation, Zeitschrift für Mathematik und Physik 62, 1913

    The first paper in which gravitation is described by a metric tensor with Ricci and Levi-Civita's calculus; Grossmann, who supplied the mathematics, had advised Einstein to use it.

    Evidence (3)
    • zbMATH lists the 1913 Entwurf (Einstein: physical part; Grossmann: mathematical part) zbMATH 2622785
    • MacTutor's Grossmann biography calls it the first paper on general relativity and says Grossmann pointed Einstein to the tensor calculus MacTutor biography of Grossmann
    • MacTutor's history of general relativity says gravitation was described for the first time by the metric tensor in 1913 MacTutor, General relativity
  5. 1915

    Einstein's field equations

    key development

    Einstein, Die Feldgleichungen der Gravitation, Sitzungsberichte der Preussischen Akademie der Wissenschaften, 1915

    Einstein presented the generally covariant field equations in November 1915, days before Hilbert reached the same equations, ending the search.

    Evidence (2)
    • zbMATH lists Einstein, Die Feldgleichungen der Gravitation, Berl. Ber. 1915 zbMATH 2618647
    • MacTutor says the final steps were taken by Einstein and Hilbert almost simultaneously in November 1915 MacTutor, General relativity
  6. 1915

    Explanation of Mercury's perihelion

    first application

    Einstein, Erklärung der Perihelbewegung des Merkur aus der allgemeinen Relativitätstheorie, Sitzungsberichte der Preussischen Akademie der Wissenschaften, 1915

    Einstein used the new field equations to account for the long-known anomalous advance of Mercury's perihelion, the first successful application of the theory to an astronomical problem.

    Evidence (2)
    • zbMATH lists the Mercury paper, Berl. Ber. 1915 zbMATH 2618646
    • MacTutor describes the 18 November 1915 discovery and Le Verrier's earlier anomaly MacTutor, General relativity
  7. 1916

    Einstein's systematic exposition

    consolidation

    Einstein, Die Grundlage der allgemeinen Relativitätstheorie, Annalen der Physik 49, 769-822, 1916

    The first full account of the theory in a single review article, with the tensor calculus built up in the exposition.

    Dating or attribution note

    Role inferred from title and standard accounts; content not read.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref record of Einstein 1916, Ann. Phys. 354, 769-822 Crossref 10.1002/andp.19163540702
  8. 1920

    Eclipse measurement of light deflection

    recognition

    Dyson, Eddington and Davidson, A determination of the deflection of light by the Sun's gravitational field, Philosophical Transactions A 220, 291-333, 1920

    The expedition results from the 1919 eclipse supported Einstein's predicted bending of starlight.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref record of the Philosophical Transactions paper (1920, vol. 220) Crossref 10.1098/rsta.1920.0009
