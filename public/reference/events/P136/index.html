1070 · Algebraic geometry

Khayyam cubics via conics

Cubics were classified and solved geometrically through intersections of conics, with a claim that ruler and compass are insufficient.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 350 BCE

    Menaechmus: conics for the cube

    precursor

    Menaechmus, solution of two mean proportionals by conics

    Conic sections used to solve a cubic problem of geometry.

    Evidence (1)
    • Menaechmus discovered conic sections while duplicating the cube and solved it with them (reported by Eutocius). MacTutor, Menaechmus biography
  2. 510

    Eutocius' commentary collects conic solutions

    precursor

    Eutocius, commentary on Archimedes' On the Sphere and Cylinder

    Preserved earlier solutions of the cube-doubling problem, including those by conics, and the solution promised by Archimedes.

    Evidence (1)
    • Eutocius's commentary records several solutions of cube duplication (Menaechmus, Diocles and others) and the solution promised by Archimedes. MacTutor, Eutocius biography
  3. 860

    Al-Mahani's equation

    precursor

    Al-Mahani, reduction of Archimedes' sphere problem to a cubic

    Turned a geometric problem into a cubic equation, which he could not solve.

    Evidence (2)
    • Khayyam notes that al-Mahani and al-Khazin translated geometric problems into algebraic ones. MacTutor, Khayyam biography
    • al-Mahani reached an equation with cubes, squares and numbers. MacTutor, Al-Mahani biography
  4. 950–971

    Al-Khazin solves it with conics

    precursor

    Abu Ja'far al-Khazin, solution by conic sections (10th century)

    First recorded conic solution of a cubic equation in Arabic.

    Evidence (1)
    • al-Khazin solved the equation with the help of conic sections. MacTutor, Al-Mahani biography
  5. 1070–1074

    Khayyam, Treatise on Demonstration of Problems of Algebra

    first statement

    Omar Khayyam, Treatise on Demonstration of Problems of Algebra (Samarkand)

    A classification of cubic types with geometric solutions by intersecting conics, with the claim that ruler and compass cannot suffice.

    Evidence (2)
    • Contains a complete classification of cubic equations with geometric solutions found by intersecting conic sections; states ruler and compass cannot solve them. MacTutor, Khayyam biography
    • Khayyam gave a general solution of third-degree equations by intersecting two conics. English Wikipedia, Omar Khayyam
  6. 1170–1213

    Sharaf al-Din al-Tusi analyses roots of cubics

    key development

    Sharaf al-Din al-Tusi, Treatise on equations

    Pursued the same cubic types algebraically, using maxima of polynomials to decide existence of roots.

    Evidence (1)
    • He divides equations of degree at most three into 25 types and studies roots through maxima. MacTutor, Al-Tusi Sharaf biography
  7. 1637

    Descartes, La Geometrie

    key development

    Rene Descartes, La Geometrie (Leiden, 1637)

    The geometric treatment of cubics by intersection of curves is compared with Khayyam's, often credited to Descartes.

    Evidence (2)
    • La Geometrie was published at Leiden in 1637. MacTutor, Descartes biography
    • Khayyam's two-conic method is often later attributed to Descartes. English Wikipedia, Omar Khayyam
  8. 1851

    Woepcke's French edition of Khayyam's algebra

    consolidation

    Franz Woepcke, L'algebre d'Omar Alkhayyami, Paris, 1851

    French edition and translation of Khayyam's algebra, which contains his cubic-by-conics method, made available to European readers.

    Evidence (2)
    • Open Library record of the 1851 edition and translation by Woepcke. Open Library work record
    • Khayyam's algebra is described as the first systematic study of cubic equations, and Woepcke's 1851 translation is credited. Wikipedia, Omar Khayyam
