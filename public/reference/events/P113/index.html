1822 · Geometry

Poncelet and projective geometry

Poncelet's treatise and the duality principle swapped points and lines.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1813–1814

    Poncelet's Saratov notebook

    precursor

    J.-V. Poncelet, notes written as a prisoner of war at Saratov

    Held captive after the Russian campaign, Poncelet recalled and extended geometry, developing projective properties of conics.

    Evidence (1)
    • MacTutor describes the imprisonment at Saratov, release in 1814, and the notebook, only partly published in 1862. MacTutor, Poncelet biography
  2. 1820

    Cauchy's report on Poncelet's work

    precursor

    report by Cauchy dated 5 June 1820

    By 1820 Poncelet's manuscript was already judged, with Cauchy attacking the principle of continuity.

    Evidence (1)
    • MacTutor reports Cauchy's report of 5 June 1820 doubting the principle of continuity. MacTutor, Poncelet biography
  3. 1822

    Traite des proprietes projectives des figures

    first statement

    J.-V. Poncelet, Traite des proprietes projectives des figures, Paris, 1822

    The treatise studied properties preserved by projection, using the principle of continuity and cross-ratio, and made projective geometry an independent subject.

    Evidence (3)
    • Library record for the Traite, Paris 1822. Internet Archive item bub_gb_wJxlHB59JgQC
    • MacTutor: published in 1822; the term projective geometry comes from its title. MacTutor, Poncelet biography
    • Wikipedia: Poncelet and others established projective geometry as an independent field. English Wikipedia, Projective geometry
  4. 1822

    The term projective geometry arises from the treatise title

    naming

    J.-V. Poncelet, Traite des proprietes projectives des figures, 1822

    MacTutor traces the modern name of the field to the title word projectives.

    Dating or attribution note

    Same work as the first_statement; it truly plays both roles. Earlier uses of the phrase not checked.

    Evidence (1)
    • MacTutor states that the term projective geometry comes from the title of this book. MacTutor, Poncelet biography
  5. 1826–1827

    Duality in Gergonne's Annales and the priority dispute

    key development

    J. D. Gergonne, articles in Annales de mathematiques pures et appliquees, 1826-1827; Poncelet's protest of December 1826

    Duality of points and lines was exploited in Gergonne's journal, and Poncelet disputed priority until 1829.

    Evidence (1)
    • MacTutor: Gergonne's articles used duality; Poncelet protested in December 1826, published March 1827, dispute lasting to May 1829 with Plucker. MacTutor, Poncelet biography
  6. 1829

    Poncelet on reciprocal polars

    key development

    J.-V. Poncelet, Memoire sur la theorie generale des polaires reciproques, J. reine angew. Math. 4 (1829) 1-71

    Poncelet set out his general theory of reciprocal polars, the duality of figures, in Crelle's Journal.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref record, Crelle's Journal 1829, pages 1-71. Crossref DOI 10.1515/crll.1829.4.1
  7. 1847

    Staudt's Geometrie der Lage: projective geometry from axioms of position

    key development

    Staudt, Geometrie der Lage, Nuremberg, 1847

    Staudt builds projective geometry from axioms of position, a landmark in its rigorous foundations.

    Evidence (1)
    • Wikipedia: The book Geometrie der Lage (1847) was a landmark in projective geometry Wikipedia, Karl von Staudt
