1936 · Computer science

Computer science separates from mathematics

Computability, switching theory and programming grow into a discipline with its own departments, journals and questions (complexity, algorithms) while keeping heavy mathematical exchange.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1936

    Church's unsolvable problem and the thesis

    first statement

    Church, 'An Unsolvable Problem of Elementary Number Theory', American Journal of Mathematics 58 (1936)

    Church proved that no decision procedure exists for a class of arithmetical statements, using lambda-definability; the Church thesis of effective calculability first appeared in print in 1936.

    Evidence (2)
    • Paper record: Am. J. Math. 1936 Crossref
    • Church's Theorem and Church's Thesis both first appeared in print in 1936 MacTutor: Church biography
  2. 1937

    Turing's computable numbers

    first statement

    A. M. Turing, On Computable Numbers, with an Application to the Entscheidungsproblem, Proc. London Math. Soc. (2) 42 (1936-37) 230-265

    Turing introduced the abstract machine and the notion of computable number, the basis of computability theory.

    Evidence (3)
    • Crossref: Proc. LMS (2) 42, pp. 230-265; published-print date 1937 Crossref 10.1112/plms/s2-42.1.230
    • Reprint records of the paper (The Essential Turing, 2004; Annual Review, 1960) Crossref
    • Turing's 1936 paper introduced the abstract machine; delayed in Proc. LMS MacTutor: Turing biography
  3. 1953

    Cambridge Diploma in Computer Science

    institution

    Cambridge Diploma in Computer Science, Cambridge University Computer Laboratory

    Cambridge began what Wikipedia calls the world's first computer science degree programme in its Computer Laboratory.

    Evidence (1)
    • Cambridge Diploma in Computer Science began at the Computer Laboratory in 1953 Wikipedia: Computer science (history)
  4. 1959

    Term 'computer science' in print

    naming

    Fein, 'The role of the university in computers, data processing, and related fields', Communications of the ACM (1959)

    Fein's CACM article argued for a graduate school in 'computer sciences', the earliest print use Wikipedia records for the term.

    Dating or attribution note

    The term appears in the article's text, not its title. The 1956 proposal is not verified.

    Evidence (2)
    • Paper record: CACM 1959 Crossref
    • Term 'computer science' appears in Fein's 1959 CACM article; first proposed 1956 Wikipedia: Computer science (etymology)
  5. 1962

    First US computer science department at Purdue

    institution

    Purdue University computer science department (formed 1962)

    Purdue formed the first computer science department in the United States.

    Dating or attribution note

    Institutional evidence from Wikipedia only; not checked against Purdue records.

    Evidence (1)
    • First US computer science department formed at Purdue in 1962 Wikipedia: Computer science (history)
  6. 1967

    Newell, Perlis and Simon's 'Computer Science'

    key development

    Newell, Perlis and Simon, 'Computer Science', Science 157 (1967) 1373-1374

    A 1967 statement of the field's scope and its relation to mathematics, written when the discipline was being set up.

    Evidence (1)
    • Paper record: Science 1967, title 'Computer Science' Crossref
