1936 · Computer science

Computer science separates from mathematics

Computability, switching theory and programming grow into a discipline with its own departments, journals and questions (complexity, algorithms) while keeping heavy mathematical exchange.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1935

    Church's 1935 abstract in the Bulletin of the AMS

    precursor

    Church, An unsolvable problem of elementary number theory. Preliminary report (abstract), Bulletin of the American Mathematical Society 41 (1935) 332-333, within 'Abstracts of papers' pp. 327-350

    A short abstract of a ten-minute talk given to the AMS on 19 April 1935 (abstract received 22 March 1935), a year before the full paper in the American Journal of Mathematics.

    Evidence (3)
    • Crossref: Bull. AMS 41(5), May 1935, pp. 327-350, title 'Abstracts of papers' (Church's abstract is on pp. 332-333) Crossref 10.1090/S0002-9904-1935-06097-5
    • Church thesis announced to the AMS on 19 April 1935 in a ten-minute contributed talk; abstract received 22 March 1935 Annales UMCS (Lublin) paper on the Church-Turing thesis
    • Wikipedia, Church-Turing thesis, cites Church, May 1935, Bull. AMS 41(5) 332-333, Preliminary report English Wikipedia, Church-Turing thesis (references)
  2. 1936

    Church's unsolvable problem and the thesis

    first statement

    Church, 'An Unsolvable Problem of Elementary Number Theory', American Journal of Mathematics 58 (1936)

    Church proved that no decision procedure exists for a class of arithmetical statements, using lambda-definability; the Church thesis of effective calculability first appeared in print in 1936.

    Evidence (2)
    • Paper record: Am. J. Math. 1936 Crossref
    • Church's Theorem and Church's Thesis both first appeared in print in 1936 MacTutor: Church biography
  3. 1937

    Turing's computable numbers

    first statement

    A. M. Turing, On Computable Numbers, with an Application to the Entscheidungsproblem, Proc. London Math. Soc. (2) 42 (1936-37) 230-265

    Turing introduced the abstract machine and the notion of computable number, the basis of computability theory.

    Evidence (3)
    • Crossref: Proc. LMS (2) 42, pp. 230-265; published-print date 1937 Crossref 10.1112/plms/s2-42.1.230
    • Reprint records of the paper (The Essential Turing, 2004; Annual Review, 1960) Crossref
    • Turing's 1936 paper introduced the abstract machine; delayed in Proc. LMS MacTutor: Turing biography
  4. 1953

    Cambridge Diploma in Numerical Analysis and Automatic Computing (later Diploma in Computer Science)

    institution

    Cambridge University Computer Laboratory, Diploma in Numerical Analysis and Automatic Computing, begun 1953 (renamed Diploma in Computer Science)

    Cambridge began a one-year postgraduate course in the Computer Laboratory that Wikipedia calls the world's first computer science degree programme; it was then titled Numerical Analysis and Automatic Computing.

    Evidence (2)
    • Computer Laboratory history: 1953 Diploma in Numerical Analysis and Automatic Computing began, the first formal course leading to a university qualification in computing; 1954 first examinations University of Cambridge Computer Laboratory, EDSAC 99 history page
    • Wikipedia: Cambridge Diploma in Computer Science began at the Computer Laboratory in 1953 Wikipedia: Computer science (history)
  5. 1959

    Term 'computer science' in print

    naming

    Fein, 'The role of the university in computers, data processing, and related fields', Communications of the ACM (1959)

    Fein's CACM article argued for a graduate school in 'computer sciences', the earliest print use Wikipedia records for the term.

    Evidence (2)
    • Paper record: CACM 1959 Crossref
    • Term 'computer science' appears in Fein's 1959 CACM article; first proposed 1956 Wikipedia: Computer science (etymology)
  6. 1962

    First US computer science department at Purdue

    institution

    Purdue University computer science department (formed 1962)

    Purdue formed the first computer science department in the United States.

    Evidence (3)
    • Purdue's Department of Computer Sciences established October 1962, described as the first in the United States Purdue University Computer Science, department history
    • First US computer science department formed at Purdue in 1962 Wikipedia: Computer science (history)
    • Purdue's Department of Computer Science was established on 24 October 1962 and is described as the first in an American university Wikipedia, Department of Computer Science, Purdue University
  7. 1967

    Newell, Perlis and Simon's 'Computer Science'

    key development

    Newell, Perlis and Simon, 'Computer Science', Science 157 (1967) 1373-1374

    A 1967 statement of the field's scope and its relation to mathematics, written when the discipline was being set up.

    Evidence (1)
    • Paper record: Science 1967, title 'Computer Science' Crossref
  8. 1974

    Knuth: Computer science and its relation to mathematics

    key development

    D. E. Knuth, 'Computer Science and its Relation to Mathematics', American Mathematical Monthly 81 (1974) 323-343

    Knuth sets out how computer science differs from mathematics in its algorithmic outlook while drawing on it, a statement of the separation by a leading practitioner.

    Evidence (1)
    • Knuth, Amer. Math. Monthly 81 (1974) 323-343, DOI 10.1080/00029890.1974.11993556 Crossref
