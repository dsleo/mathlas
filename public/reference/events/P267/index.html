Cellular automata and self-reproducing machines

Von Neumann and Ulam model self-reproduction by lattice automata.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1949

    Von Neumann's Illinois lectures on complicated automata

    precursor

    J. von Neumann, 'Theory and Organization of Complicated Automata', five lectures, University of Illinois, December 1949 (published as Part I of Theory of Self-Reproducing Automata, 1966)

    Von Neumann lectures on the logic of self-reproduction; the transcripts stayed unpublished until Burks edited them.

    Evidence (1)
    • 'Five lectures delivered at the University of Illinois in December, 1949. This is Part I of the present volume.' (Burks, editor's introduction) von Neumann, Theory of Self-Reproducing Automata, ed. Burks, 1966 (scan)
  2. 1951

    Von Neumann's general and logical theory of automata

    first statement

    Von Neumann J., The general and logical theory of automata, in Jeffress L. A. (ed.), Cerebral Mechanisms in Behavior: The Hixon Symposium, Wiley, 1951

    Von Neumann's Hixon Symposium talk on automata and self-reproduction, the first public statement of the self-reproducing machine idea (not yet cellular).

    Dating or attribution note

    The talk's presence in the 1951 volume is inferred from the Hixon Symposium volume record; contents not checked; medium confidence.

    Evidence (2)
    • Open Library record of the 1951 Hixon Symposium volume, which holds von Neumann's talk Open Library
    • Wikipedia notes von Neumann's work on self-replicating systems Wikipedia, Self-replicating machine
  3. 1962

    Ulam: patterns of growth of figures

    key development

    Ulam S., On some mathematical problems connected with patterns of growth of figures, Proc. Symp. Appl. Math. 14, 1962

    Ulam published a formal account of lattice growth models, which later became the standard example of a cellular automaton.

    Dating or attribution note

    Connection to cellular automata is inferred from the 1940s lattice-growth history; the text was not checked.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref record: Proc. Symp. Appl. Math. 14, 1962, DOI 10.1090/psapm/014/9947 Crossref
  4. 1966

    Von Neumann's self-reproducing automaton in a cellular space

    first application

    Von Neumann J., Theory of Self-Reproducing Automata, ed. A. W. Burks, University of Illinois Press, 1966

    The first full construction of a self-reproducing machine as a cellular automaton, published posthumously from manuscripts of the early 1950s.

    Evidence (1)
    • The edited Theory of Self-Reproducing Automata was published by University of Illinois Press in 1966. National Diet Library catalogue
  5. 1968

    Codd's monograph Cellular Automata

    consolidation

    E. F. Codd, Cellular Automata, Academic Press, New York 1968

    Codd's 1968 monograph is a systematic book on cellular automata, following von Neumann and Burks's 1966 book on self-reproduction.

    Evidence (1)
    • zbMATH 3338162: Codd, Cellular automata, New York-London: Academic Press, 1968 zbMATH 0213.18301
  6. 1969

    Hedlund: endomorphisms of the shift dynamical system

    key development

    Hedlund G. A., Endomorphisms and automorphisms of the shift dynamical system, Math. Systems Theory 3, 320-375, 1969

    Mathematical characterisation of cellular automata as endomorphisms of the full shift, which became the basis of the formal theory.

    Dating or attribution note

    The claim that this is the foundational paper for the formal theory is the author's characterisation; the text was not checked.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref record: Math. Systems Theory 3, 320-375, 1969 Crossref
  7. 2002

    Wolfram, A New Kind of Science

    key development

    Wolfram S., A New Kind of Science, Wolfram Media, 2002

    A 2002 book by Wolfram on cellular automata, published after the earlier monographs, so it is not the consolidation.

    Evidence (1)
    • Open Library record: Wolfram Media, 2002 Open Library
  8. Date unknown

    Ulam's lattice growth and von Neumann's self-reproduction ideas (unpublished, 1940s)

    precursor

    Ulam studied crystal-like growth on a lattice and von Neumann worked on self-reproducing systems at Los Alamos in the 1940s; these were not published at the time.

    Evidence (1)
    • Wikipedia says Ulam and von Neumann conceived the concept in the 1940s at Los Alamos Wikipedia, Cellular automaton (History)
