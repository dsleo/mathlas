Trigonometry from astronomy: Hipparchus and chord tables

Hipparchus compiled chord tables for astronomical computation, and later Menelaus, Indian astronomers and Pitiscus developed spherical trigonometry, sine tables and the name.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 330 BCE

    Greek spherical geometry for astronomy

    precursor

    Autolycus, On the Moving Sphere, c. 330 BC

    Autolycus wrote the earliest surviving text on spherical geometry for astronomy, without trigonometry.

    Evidence (2)
    • A key text on spherical geometry was written by Autolycus in Athens around 330 BC. MacTutor, Greek astronomy
    • Theodosius' Sphaerics, c. 100 BC, has no trigonometry. MacTutor, Theodosius
  2. 140 BCE

    Hipparchus' table of chords

    first statement

    Hipparchus, twelve books on chords (lost), c. 140 BC

    Hipparchus tabulated chord lengths against arcs so that triangles could be solved, usually counted as the start of trigonometry.

    Evidence (3)
    • The first known table of chords is by Hipparchus about 140 BC; the books are lost. MacTutor, Trigonometric functions
    • He is the first mathematician known to have a trigonometric table; Theon names the work. Wikipedia, Hipparchus
    • Standard study. Toomer, The chord table of Hipparchus and the early history of Greek trigonometry, Centaurus 18 (1974)
  3. 140 BCE

    Chords used to compute eccentricities

    first application

    Hipparchus (lost works, via Ptolemy)

    Hipparchus needed the table to compute the eccentricity of the Moon's and Sun's orbits.

    Evidence (2)
    • He needed the table when computing eccentricities of lunar and solar orbits. Wikipedia, Hipparchus
    • The purpose of the table was a method for solving triangles without starting from first principles. MacTutor, Hipparchus
  4. 100

    Menelaus' Sphaerica

    key development

    Menelaus of Alexandria, Sphaerica, c. 100 AD

    Menelaus proves the theorem on the sphere now named after him, the earliest surviving work on spherical trigonometry.

    Evidence (2)
    • Menelaus' work on spherics is the earliest known work on spherical trigonometry. MacTutor, Trigonometric functions
    • Book III contains Menelaus' theorem. MacTutor, Menelaus
  5. 150

    Ptolemy's chord table and addition formulas

    consolidation

    Ptolemy, Almagest I.10-11, c. 150 AD

    Ptolemy builds a chord table at half-degree steps and proves sum, difference and half-angle rules, the first surviving systematic trigonometry.

    Evidence (3)
    • He derived addition formulas from his theorem and computed chords by inscribed polygons and half-arc rules. MacTutor, Trigonometric functions
    • The table is in Book I, chapter 11 of the Almagest and is the earliest extensive trigonometric table. Wikipedia, Ptolemy's table of chords
    • Standard English translation. Toomer, Ptolemy's Almagest (Springer, 1984)
  6. 499

    Aryabhata's table of half-chords

    key development

    Aryabhata, Aryabhatiya, 499 AD

    Aryabhata gives a table of half-chords, effectively the first sine table, using the word jya.

    Evidence (3)
    • Around 500 Aryabhata gave tables of half-chords which are sine tables. MacTutor, Trigonometric functions
    • He finished the Aryabhatiya in 499 at age 23. MacTutor, Aryabhata the Elder
    • The idea of Greek descent for the table has been refuted; scholars argue for an independent origin. Wikipedia, History of trigonometry
  7. 1250–1274

    Al-Tusi's Treatise on the Quadrilateral

    consolidation

    Nasir al-Din al-Tusi, Treatise on the Quadrilateral

    Al-Tusi treats plane and spherical trigonometry as a subject of its own, apart from astronomy.

    Evidence (2)
    • It is described as the first work treating trigonometry as an independent branch of pure mathematics. MacTutor, Al-Tusi
    • Wikipedia gives the same assessment and notes European credit often went to Regiomontanus. Wikipedia, History of trigonometry
  8. 1595

    Pitiscus' Trigonometria

    naming

    Bartholomaeus Pitiscus, Trigonometria, in Scultetus, Sphaericorum libri tres (Heidelberg, 1595)

    The word trigonometry first appears in the title of Pitiscus' tract, printed with Scultetus' book on the sphere.

    Evidence (3)
    • The word is due to Pitiscus and first occurs in this 1595 title. MacTutor, Pitiscus
    • Scanned 1595 volume with the appended treatise on solving triangles. Internet Archive record
    • Same date in the general history. MacTutor, Trigonometric functions
