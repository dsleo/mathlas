Unique games conjecture

A conjecture about label cover instances yields tight hardness for many problems via Fourier-analytic dictatorship tests and SDP integrality gaps.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 2002

    Unique Games Conjecture posed

    first statement

    S. Khot, On the power of unique 2-prover 1-round games, STOC (2002)

    Khot’s public conference paper formulates the hardness conjecture for unique two-prover games.

    Evidence (1)
    • ACM bibliographic record dates the conference paper to 2002. Original publication or repository record
  2. 2004

    Optimal inapproximability results for MAX-CUT

    key development

    Khot, Kindler, Mossel, Optimal inapproximability results for MAX-CUT and other 2-variable CSPs (FOCS 2004; SIAM J. Comput. 2007)

    Uses the unique games conjecture to prove tight inapproximability for MAX-CUT and other two-variable constraint problems.

    Evidence (1)
    • zbMATH record: 2004 conference version and 2007 SIAM J. Comput. publication of the Khot-Kindler-Mossel MAX-CUT paper zbMATH Open record
  3. 2008

    Optimal algorithms and inapproximability for every CSP

    key development

    Raghavendra, Optimal algorithms and inapproximability results for every CSP?, STOC 2008

    Shows that under the unique games conjecture a single semidefinite-programming threshold is tight for every constraint satisfaction problem.

    Evidence (1)
    • zbMATH record: 2008 STOC paper by Raghavendra on optimal algorithms and inapproximability for every CSP zbMATH Open record
