1959 · Algebra

Hyperfunctions and algebraic analysis

Sato defines hyperfunctions as boundary values of holomorphic functions and develops microlocal methods.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1958

    Sato's first announcement of hyperfunctions (Japanese)

    precursor

    Sato introduces hyperfunctions in a Japanese-language announcement before the English papers.

    Evidence (1)
    • Wikipedia: hyperfunctions were introduced by Sato in 1958 in Japanese, with English papers in 1959 and 1960 Wikipedia Hyperfunction
  2. 1959

    Theory of hyperfunctions I

    first statement

    Sato, Theory of hyperfunctions I, J. Fac. Sci. Univ. Tokyo Sect. I 8 (1959) 139-193

    Sato defines hyperfunctions as boundary values of holomorphic functions and gives the basic theory.

    Evidence (1)
    • zbMATH record 'Theory of hyperfunctions. I' in J. Fac. Sci. Univ. Tokyo 8, 1959 zbMATH
  3. 1960

    Theory of hyperfunctions II

    key development

    Sato, Theory of hyperfunctions II, J. Fac. Sci. Univ. Tokyo Sect. I 8 (1960) 387-437

    The second part develops the theory further and makes it usable for analysis.

    Evidence (1)
    • zbMATH record 'Theory of hyperfunctions. II', 1960 zbMATH
  4. 1970

    Hyperfunctions and partial differential equations

    key development

    Sato, Hyperfunctions and partial differential equations (1970)

    Sato's 1970 paper applies hyperfunctions to linear partial differential equations, the route to algebraic and microlocal analysis.

    Evidence (1)
    • zbMATH record 'Hyperfunctions and partial differential equations', Sato, 1970 zbMATH
  5. 1973

    Introduction to the theory of hyperfunctions (Komatsu)

    consolidation

    Komatsu, An introduction to the theory of hyperfunctions, Springer LNM (1973)

    A lecture-notes introduction giving the first systematic exposition in English.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref record: 'An introduction to the theory of hyperfunctions', Lecture Notes in Mathematics, 1973 Crossref
