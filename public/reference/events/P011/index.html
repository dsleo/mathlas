1895 · Algebraic topology

Algebraic topology: Betti numbers to homology groups

Numerical invariants (Betti numbers) of manifolds from the study of integrals and differential equations are replaced by groups, so algebra becomes the language of topology.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1870

    Betti numbers of n-dimensional spaces

    precursor

    Betti, E., Sopra gli spazi di un numero qualunque di dimensioni, Annali di Matematica Pura ed Applicata, 1870

    Betti introduced numerical invariants of n-dimensional spaces that later became Betti numbers; these are the numbers the event replaces with groups.

    Evidence (2)
    • Paper appears in Annali di Matematica Pura ed Applicata with issue date 1870 Crossref record, DOI 10.1007/bf02420029
    • MacTutor topic page says Betti extended Listing's connectivity ideas to n dimensions MacTutor HistTopics, Topology in mathematics (saved text)
  2. 1895

    Poincare's Analysis situs

    first statement

    Poincare, H., Analysis situs, Journal de l'Ecole Polytechnique, 1895

    Poincare's Analysis situs gives the first systematic combinatorial treatment in which homology is defined through groups of chains and boundaries.

    Dating or attribution note

    The claim that homology groups are defined there is from general knowledge; the paper text was not read. Earlier discussions by Betti are not counted as the group-theoretic start.

    Evidence (4)
    • Analysis situs appears in the Journal de l'Ecole Polytechnique in 1895 zbMATH record, Zbl 26.0541.07
    • MacTutor describes Analysis situs (1895) as an early systematic treatment of topology MacTutor biography of Poincare
    • MacTutor Topology in mathematics: Poincare gave a precise definition of Betti numbers via homology MacTutor Topology in mathematics topic (saved text)
    • Wikipedia calls Poincare's Analysis situs the first recognisable theory of homology (1895) Wikipedia: Homology (mathematics) (raw wikitext)
  3. 1925–1928

    Noether's algebraic homology groups (with Vietoris and Mayer)

    key development

    Noether, with Vietoris and Mayer, developed the algebraic homology groups in the years 1925-28 that made the theory a group-valued invariant.

    Evidence (1)
    • Wikipedia: Noether, independently of Vietoris and Mayer, developed algebraic homology groups in 1925-28 Wikipedia: Homology (mathematics) (raw wikitext)
  4. 1935

    Alexandroff and Hopf's Topologie I

    consolidation

    P. Alexandroff and H. Hopf, Topologie I, Springer, 1935

    Alexandroff and Hopf wrote the first large systematic treatment of combinatorial and homological topology.

    Dating or attribution note

    Systematic-treatment role assigned from the book's title and series; not checked against a preface.

    Evidence (2)
    • Springer Topologie I (1935) chapter record exists Crossref DOI 10.1007/978-3-662-02021-0
    • Hopf's MacTutor biography covers his topology work MacTutor Hopf biography
  5. 1942

    Lefschetz's Algebraic Topology (AMS Colloquium)

    naming

    S. Lefschetz, Algebraic Topology, American Mathematical Society Colloquium Publications 27, 1942

    The 1942 colloquium volume uses the name 'algebraic topology' in its title for the subject.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref lists Lefschetz 1942 Algebraic Topology, Colloquium Publications 27 Crossref DOI 10.1090/coll/027
  6. 1945

    Axiomatic approach to homology theory

    key development

    Eilenberg, S. and Steenrod, N., Axiomatic Approach to Homology Theory, Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 1945

    Eilenberg and Steenrod state the axioms that characterise homology theories, making homology an abstract functor-based theory.

    Evidence (2)
    • Axiomatic Approach to Homology Theory appears in PNAS in 1945 Crossref record, DOI 10.1073/pnas.31.4.117
    • MacTutor says the axioms were set out in 1945 with proofs deferred to the 1952 book MacTutor biography of Eilenberg
  7. 1952

    Foundations of Algebraic Topology

    consolidation

    Eilenberg, S. and Steenrod, N., Foundations of Algebraic Topology, Princeton University Press, 1952

    The Eilenberg-Steenrod book gives the first systematic treatment of axiomatic homology theory.

    Evidence (2)
    • Foundations of Algebraic Topology published 1952 Open Library search record
    • MacTutor names the 1952 book as the text where the proofs appeared MacTutor biography of Eilenberg
  8. 1952

    Eilenberg-Steenrod axioms and Foundations of Algebraic Topology

    key development

    S. Eilenberg and N. Steenrod, Foundations of Algebraic Topology, Princeton, 1952

    The axiomatic treatment showed that homology theories are determined by a few axioms.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref lists the 1952 Princeton volume Crossref DOI 10.1515/9781400877492
