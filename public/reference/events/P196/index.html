825 · Number theory

Transmission of Hindu-Arabic numerals to the Islamic world and Europe

A treatise on Indian decimal calculation, later translated into Latin, spread the positional system and gave 'algorithm' its name.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 700–799

    Indian place-value reckoning taken up in Islamic mathematics

    precursor

    8th century, recorded later by al-Qifti

    Indian developments reach Islamic scholars in the 8th century according to a 13th-century chronicle.

    Evidence (1)
    • Wikipedia Hindu-Arabic numeral system: taken up in Islamic mathematics in the 8th century as recorded by al-Qifti Wikipedia, Hindu-Arabic numeral system
  2. 825

    Al-Khwarizmi writes on calculation with Hindu numerals

    first statement

    Al-Khwarizmi, On the Calculation with Hindu Numerals, c. 825; Arabic text lost, Latin Algoritmi de numero Indorum

    The book explains the Indian decimal place-value method in Arabic; it is credited as chiefly responsible for spreading it in the Middle East and Europe.

    Evidence (2)
    • Wikipedia Al-Khwarizmi: On the Calculation with Hindu Numerals written about 825, spread the system; Latin Algoritmi de numero Indorum Wikipedia, Al-Khwarizmi
    • Wikipedia Hindu-Arabic numeral system: Khwarizmi, about 825 CE Wikipedia, Hindu-Arabic numeral system
  3. 830

    Al-Kindi writes on the use of the Hindu numerals

    key development

    Al-Kindi, On the Use of the Hindu Numerals, c. 830

    A second early Arabic treatise on the Indian numerals appears about the same time.

    Evidence (2)
    • Wikipedia Hindu-Arabic numeral system: Al-Kindi book around 830 CE Wikipedia, Hindu-Arabic numeral system
    • MacTutor lists Al-Kindi (born about 801, died 873) MacTutor, Al-Kindi
  4. 952

    Al-Uqlidisi: earliest extant Arabic arithmetic, with decimal fractions

    key development

    Al-Uqlidisi, Kitab al-fusul fi al-hisab al-hindi, Damascus 952-53; ed. Saidan

    The earliest surviving Arabic arithmetic in the Indian system extends it to decimal fractions.

    Evidence (3)
    • MacTutor Al-Uqlidisi: composed text in Damascus in 952-53; place value, operations, decimal and sexagesimal MacTutor, Al-Uqlidisi
    • Saidan 1966, The earliest extant Arabic arithmetic: Kitab al-Fusul Saidan, Isis 57 (1966)
    • Wikipedia Hindu-Arabic numeral system: first to describe positional decimal fractions Wikipedia, Hindu-Arabic numeral system
  5. 1100–1199

    Latin Algoritmi: algorithm takes its name from al-Khwarizmi

    naming

    12th-century Latin translation of the Hindu reckoning treatise

    The Latin version Algoritmi de numero Indorum gives the Western word algorithm and the term algorism for the new arithmetic.

    Dating or attribution note

    12th century as a range; translator and manuscript not checked.

    Evidence (1)
    • Wikipedia Al-Khwarizmi: 12th-century Latin translation introduced the term algorithm to the West Wikipedia, Al-Khwarizmi
  6. 1202

    Fibonacci Liber Abaci: the new numerals for Latin merchants

    consolidation

    Fibonacci, Liber Abaci, 1202

    Fibonacci presents the Indian method with applications to commerce, the major source for European adoption.

    Evidence (3)
    • Wikipedia Liber Abaci: 1202 Latin work introducing base-10 positional notation in Europe Wikipedia, Liber Abaci
    • Sigler translation of Liber Abaci (2002), Open Library Open Library
    • MacTutor Fibonacci biography MacTutor, Fibonacci
