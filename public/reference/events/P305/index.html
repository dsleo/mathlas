≈ 1998 · Analysis

Higher-order Fourier analysis

Gowers uniformity norms, matched with Host-Kra ergodic seminorms, produced a nonabelian Fourier theory for polynomial patterns.

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1998

    Fourier method applied to four-term progressions

    key development

    W. T. Gowers, A new proof of Szemerédi’s theorem for arithmetic progressions of length four, GAFA 8 (1998)

    Gowers developed quantitative uniformity methods for four-term arithmetic progressions.

    Evidence (1)
    • The 1998 journal record identifies the four-term proof. Original publication or repository record
  2. 2001

    Higher-order uniformity method extended

    key development

    W. T. Gowers, A new proof of Szemerédi’s theorem, GAFA 11 (2001)

    The longer proof develops uniformity methods across progression lengths.

    Evidence (1)
    • Publisher record identifies the 2001 proof. Original publication or repository record
  3. 2010

    Green-Tao: primes via higher-order Fourier analysis

    key development

    Green, B., Tao, T., Linear equations in primes, Annals of Mathematics 171 (2010)

    Applies Gowers-norm (higher-order Fourier) methods to count solutions of linear systems in the primes.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref record, Annals 171 (2010) Crossref
  4. 2010

    Tao-Ziegler inverse theorem for Gowers norms

    key development

    Tao, T., Ziegler, T., The inverse conjecture for the Gowers norm over finite fields via the correspondence principle, Analysis & PDE 3 (2010)

    Proves the inverse conjecture for the Gowers norm over finite fields, the structural input higher-order Fourier analysis needs.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref record, Analysis & PDE 3 (2010) Crossref
  5. 2012

    Tao's book Higher Order Fourier Analysis

    consolidation

    Tao, T., Higher Order Fourier Analysis, AMS Graduate Studies in Mathematics (2012)

    A monograph developing higher-order Fourier analysis and its inverse theorems as a systematic theory.
