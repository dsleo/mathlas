Feigenbaum universality (period-doubling universality)

Renormalisation explains universal constants in the period-doubling route.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1975

    Feigenbaum's unpublished numerical finding

    precursor

    Feigenbaum found the numerical ratio of period-doubling distances in 1975 and then recognised that it holds for a class of maps; this was not published at the time.

    Evidence (1)
    • Feigenbaum found the number in August 1975 and recognised its universality in October 1975 MacTutor biography of Mitchell Feigenbaum
  2. 1978

    Quantitative universality paper

    first statement

    Feigenbaum, M. J., Quantitative universality for a class of nonlinear transformations, Journal of Statistical Physics, 1978

    The first published statement of the universal constants for a class of one-dimensional maps with a quadratic maximum.

    Evidence (2)
    • Quantitative universality for a class of nonlinear transformations, J. Stat. Phys., 1978 Crossref record
    • The paper was submitted in 1976 and published in 1978 after long refereeing MacTutor biography of Mitchell Feigenbaum
  3. 1978

    Feigenbaum, 'Quantitative universality for a class of nonlinear transformations'

    naming

    M. J. Feigenbaum, 'Quantitative universality for a class of nonlinear transformations', Journal of Statistical Physics, DOI 10.1007/BF01020332, 1978

    Feigenbaum's 1978 paper names the universality property in its title, the first title found with the term for this field.

    Evidence (1)
    • Crossref record gives title 'Quantitative universality for a class of nonlinear transformations', author Feigenbaum, container Journal of Statistical Physics, year 1978 Crossref, DOI 10.1007/BF01020332
  4. 1979

    Universal metric properties paper

    key development

    Feigenbaum, M. J., The universal metric properties of nonlinear transformations, Journal of Statistical Physics, 1979

    The second paper extends the theory to the metric (scaling) properties that define the universal constants.

    Evidence (1)
    • The universal metric properties of nonlinear transformations, J. Stat. Phys., 1979 Crossref record
  5. 1982

    Mercury experiment measuring the period-doubling cascade

    first application

    Libchaber, A., Laroche, C., Fauve, S., Period doubling cascade in mercury, a quantitative measurement, Journal de Physique Lettres, 1982

    A physical experiment that quantitatively measures the period-doubling cascade, testing the universal predictions on a target system.

    Dating or attribution note

    Only the title and record were checked; that the measurement tests the Feigenbaum constant is inferred from the title and the period-doubling context, not from the full text.

    Evidence (1)
    • Period doubling cascade in mercury, a quantitative measurement, J. Physique Lettres, 1982 Crossref record
  6. 1982

    First computer-assisted proof of the Feigenbaum conjectures

    key development

    Lanford, O. E. III, A computer-assisted proof of the Feigenbaum conjectures, Bulletin of the American Mathematical Society, 1982

    The first proof of the conjectured universality, carried out with computer assistance.

    Evidence (2)
    • A computer-assisted proof of the Feigenbaum conjectures, Bull. AMS, 1982 Crossref record
    • The first proof of universality was computer-assisted by Lanford in 1982 Wikipedia, Feigenbaum constants
  7. 1987

    Complete proof with corrected computer step

    key development

    Eckmann, J.-P., Wittwer, P., A complete proof of the Feigenbaum conjectures, Journal of Statistical Physics, 1987

    A correction and completion of Lanford's proof, cited in Wikipedia as the corrected version.

    Evidence (1)
    • A complete proof of the Feigenbaum conjectures, J. Stat. Phys., 1987 Crossref record
