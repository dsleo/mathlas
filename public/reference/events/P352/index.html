≈ 2002 · Combinatorics

Tropical geometry

Algebraic varieties were replaced by piecewise-linear shadows, allowing enumerative problems to be solved by counting combinatorial objects.

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 2002

    Lattice paths count curves in toric surfaces

    first application

    G. Mikhalkin, Counting curves via lattice paths in polygons, arXiv:math/0209253 (2002)

    The public note applies the emerging tropical viewpoint to enumerative curve counts.

    Evidence (1)
    • ArXiv dates the note to September 2002 and states its curve-counting application. Original publication or repository record
  2. 2003

    Enumerative tropical geometry elaborated

    key development

    G. Mikhalkin, Enumerative tropical algebraic geometry in R2, arXiv:math/0312530 (2003)

    The later preprint proves curve-enumeration formulas using tropical geometry.

    Evidence (1)
    • ArXiv dates the paper to December 2003 and references its 2002 announcement. Original publication or repository record
  3. 2007

    Itenberg-Mikhalkin-Shustin tropical algebraic geometry

    consolidation

    Itenberg, Mikhalkin, Shustin, Tropical Algebraic Geometry, Oberwolfach Seminars 35, Birkhäuser, 2007

    An early textbook on tropical algebraic geometry built around enumerative counts.

    Evidence (1)
    • zbMATH record Tropical algebraic geometry, year 2007, ISBN 978-3-7643-8309-1 zbMATH Open
  4. 2015

    Maclagan-Sturmfels textbook on tropical geometry

    consolidation

    Maclagan and Sturmfels, Introduction to Tropical Geometry, Graduate Studies in Mathematics 161, AMS, 2015

    The standard graduate textbook on tropical geometry.

    Evidence (1)
    • zbMATH record Introduction to tropical geometry, year 2015, ISBN 978-0-8218-5198-2 zbMATH Open
