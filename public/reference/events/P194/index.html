1615 · Analysis

Kepler's Nova stereometria doliorum vinariorum (infinitesimal volumes of solids of revolution)

Volumes of revolved solids were found by summing infinitely many thin pieces.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 250 BCE

    Archimedes' measurement of solids

    precursor

    Archimedes, On the Method of Mechanical Theorems (rediscovered by Heiberg, 1906)

    Classical measurement of curved solids by balancing thin slices, the ancestor of Kepler's and Cavalieri's pieces.

    Evidence (2)
    • Heath's 1912 supplement presents the Method as recently discovered by Heiberg. Internet Archive record methodofarchimed00arch
    • Kepler built on Archimedes' work. MacTutor, Kepler biography
  2. 1615

    Nova stereometria doliorum vinariorum published

    first statement

    Kepler, Nova stereometria doliorum vinariorum, Linz, 1615

    Kepler computed volumes of many revolved solids by treating them as sums of infinitely many thin pieces, following Archimedes and motivated by gauging wine barrels.

    Evidence (3)
    • Record of the 1615 Linz edition. Internet Archive record: Nova stereometria doliorum vinariorum, 1615
    • Says the Linz 1615 study of solids of revolution used a resolution into indivisibles and is part of the ancestry of infinitesimal calculus. MacTutor biography of Kepler
    • Says Kepler, basing himself on Archimedes, used a resolution into indivisibles in the 1615 Linz book. MacTutor, Kepler biography
  3. 1627

    Cavalieri reports finishing the Geometria

    precursor

    Cavalieri tells Galileo and Cardinal Borromeo that the book on indivisibles is complete, eight years before it was printed.

    Evidence (1)
    • On 16 December 1627 he wrote that he had completed his Geometria. MacTutor, Cavalieri biography
  4. 1635

    Cavalieri's Geometria indivisibilibus builds on Kepler

    key development

    Cavalieri, Geometria indivisibilibus continuorum nova quadam ratione promota, Bologna, 1635

    Cavalieri's theory of indivisibles developed Archimedes' exhaustion with Kepler's infinitely small quantities, systematising what Kepler did case by case.

    Evidence (4)
    • The 1635 theory incorporates Kepler's infinitesimally small geometric quantities. MacTutor biography of Cavalieri
    • Says Kepler's barrel method was later developed by Cavalieri. MacTutor biography of Kepler
    • Geometria indivisibilibus continuorum noua quadam ratione promota, 1635, digitised record. Internet Archive record ita-bnc-mag-00001345-001
    • Describes the theory of indivisibles as a development of Archimedes' exhaustion incorporating Kepler's infinitesimals. MacTutor, Cavalieri biography
  5. 1656

    Wallis' Arithmetica infinitorum

    key development

    John Wallis, Arithmetica infinitorum, Oxford, 1656

    Turns the method of indivisibles into arithmetic, summing series of infinitesimal slices to get areas and volumes.

    Evidence (2)
    • Names Arithmetica infinitorum as Wallis' most famous work, published 1656, after he studied Kepler and Cavalieri. MacTutor, Wallis biography
    • Digitised record of the work. Internet Archive record ArithmeticaInfinitorum
