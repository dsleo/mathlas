1758 · Topology

Euler's polyhedron formula (V - E + F = 2)

A relation between numbers of vertices, edges and faces was found for convex polyhedra.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1537

    Maurolico's unpublished statement for Platonic solids

    precursor

    An unpublished manuscript is said to state the vertex-edge-face relation for the regular solids, so it is a private forerunner and not a public statement.

    Dating or attribution note

    Not checked against a primary or bibliographic source; Descartes' related unpublished work on solid angles was not examined.

    Evidence (1)
    • Wikipedia says the relation was stated for Platonic solids in 1537 in an unpublished manuscript by Maurolico English Wikipedia, Euler characteristic
  2. 1750

    Euler's letter to Goldbach

    precursor

    Euler communicated the relation among vertices, edges and faces of a polyhedron to Goldbach in a private letter.

    Evidence (1)
    • MacTutor topology history says Euler gave the formula v - e + f = 2 in a 1750 letter to Goldbach MacTutor, Topology in mathematics
  3. 1758

    Euler's 'Elementa doctrinae solidorum' printed

    first statement

    Euler, Elementa doctrinae solidorum, Novi Commentarii Acad. Sci. Petropolitanae 4 (1752/53), pp. 109-140, published 1758

    Euler's first public statement of the relation between faces, solid angles and edges of bodies bounded by planes, printed in the St Petersburg Novi Commentarii.

    Evidence (1)
    • Euler Archive record: Elementa doctrinae solidorum, Novi Comm. Acad. Sci. Petropol. 4, pp. 109-140, published 1758, written 1750 Euler Archive E230
  4. 1758

    Euler's proof attempt 'Demonstratio nonnullarum insignium proprietatum'

    key development

    Euler, Demonstratio nonnullarum insignium proprietatum, quibus solida hedris planis inclusa sunt praedita, Novi Comm. 4, 1758

    A companion paper in the same volume argues the formula by removing vertices one at a time, but later sources call the argument incomplete.

    Evidence (2)
    • Euler Archive record E231, Novi Comm. 4, pp. 140-160, published 1758, a continuation of E230 that proves the main theorem by vertex removal Euler Archive E231
    • Wikipedia says Euler introduced the invariant for convex polyhedra but failed to prove it rigorously English Wikipedia, Euler characteristic
  5. 1812

    Lhuilier's direct proof and exceptions

    key development

    Lhuilier, Mémoire sur la polyédrométrie, Annales de Gergonne 3 (1812-13), 169-189

    Lhuilier published a direct demonstration of Euler's theorem together with an examination of the exceptions, notably solids with holes, which pointed toward genus and later topology.

    Evidence (2)
    • Numdam record: Lhuilier, Mémoire sur la polyédrométrie, Annales de mathématiques pures et appliquées 3 (1812-1813), pp. 169-189, title announces a direct proof and the exceptions Numdam AMPA 1812-1813 vol 3
    • MacTutor says Lhuilier's work on the formula and its exceptions mattered for the development of topology MacTutor, Lhuilier
