1713 · Probability

Bernoulli's Ars Conjectandi and the law of large numbers

Bernoulli proved the first law of large numbers.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1654

    Pascal-Fermat correspondence

    precursor

    Letters on the problem of points are the date historians cite for the start of modern probability.

    Evidence (1)
    • Wikipedia, Ars Conjectandi: historians cite the Pascal-Fermat correspondence of 1654 as the beginning of modern probability. English Wikipedia, Ars Conjectandi
  2. 1657

    Huygens's treatise on reckoning in games of chance

    precursor

    Huygens, De ratiociniis in ludo aleae, in van Schooten, Exercitationum mathematicarum libri quinque, Leiden 1657

    The first printed treatise on probability, built on expectation for games of chance.

    Evidence (2)
    • Internet Archive holds the 1657 printing of De ratiociniis in ludo aleae. Internet Archive
    • Wikipedia, Ars Conjectandi, describes Huygens as having published the first treatise on probability. English Wikipedia, Ars Conjectandi
  3. 1684–1689

    Jakob Bernoulli's Meditationes notebook

    precursor

    Jakob Bernoulli, Meditationes (private notes), 1684-1689

    Bernoulli's private notebook records three working periods from games of chance to a posteriori probabilities and the measuring of probabilities.

    Evidence (1)
    • Wikipedia, Ars Conjectandi: the Meditationes show progress in three periods, 1684-85, 1685-86 and 1687-89; text written 1684-1689. English Wikipedia, Ars Conjectandi
  4. 1713

    Ars Conjectandi published

    first statement

    Jakob Bernoulli, Ars conjectandi, opus posthumum, Basel: Thurneysen, 1713

    The posthumous book contains the first version of the law of large numbers and a systematic treatment of combinatorics and expectation.

    Evidence (2)
    • Internet Archive holds the 1713 Basel edition (several scans). Internet Archive
    • MacTutor: Ars Conjectandi published in Basel in 1713, eight years after Bernoulli's death. MacTutor, Jakob Bernoulli
  5. 1718

    De Moivre's Doctrine of Chances

    key development

    De Moivre, The Doctrine of Chances, London 1718

    De Moivre's English treatise extended the calculus of chances after Bernoulli's book; his 1711 De mensura sortis preceded it.

    Evidence (2)
    • Internet Archive holds the 1718 first edition. Internet Archive
    • Wikipedia, Ars Conjectandi, notes the book influenced de Moivre. English Wikipedia, Ars Conjectandi
