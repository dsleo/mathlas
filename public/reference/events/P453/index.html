1879 · Graph theory

Map colouring and the four colour problem (Kempe and Heawood)

A map-colouring question became a vertex-colouring problem on planar graphs, with Kempe's chains and Heawood's bound via Euler characteristic bringing in topology.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1852

    Guthrie's question and De Morgan's letter

    precursor

    De Morgan to Hamilton, 23 October 1852

    A student's observation that four colours seem enough for any map reached De Morgan, who asked Hamilton the same day; the letter is the first record of the conjecture.

    Evidence (2)
    • MacTutor tells the story of the Guthrie brothers, De Morgan's letter of 23 October 1852 and Hamilton's reply three days later MacTutor, The four colour theorem
    • Wikipedia gives the same first record English Wikipedia, Graph theory
  2. 1879

    Cayley poses the problem in print

    first statement

    Cayley, On the colouring of maps, Proceedings of the Royal Geographical Society 1, 259-261, 1879

    After asking the London Mathematical Society in June 1878 whether the problem had been solved, Cayley wrote up why it is hard in a note published in 1879.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref record of Cayley, On the Colouring of Maps (1879) Crossref 10.2307/1799998
    • MacTutor describes the 1878 question to the LMS and the 1879 paper MacTutor, The four colour theorem
  3. 1879

    Kempe's chain argument

    first application

    Kempe, On the geographical problem of the four colours, American Journal of Mathematics 2, 193-200, 1879

    Kempe announced a proof in Nature in July 1879 and gave it in the American Journal of Mathematics, using chains of alternately coloured regions; the proof was later shown to be flawed.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref record of Kempe's paper, Am. J. Math. 2, 193 Crossref 10.2307/2369235
    • MacTutor explains Kempe's announcement of 17 July 1879 and the method of Kempe chains MacTutor, The four colour theorem
  4. 1890

    Heawood refutes Kempe and bounds colours on surfaces

    key development

    Heawood, Map-colour theorem, Quarterly Journal of Pure and Applied Mathematics 24, 332-338, 1890

    Heawood found the gap in Kempe's argument, proved that five colours suffice for the plane and gave a bound for surfaces of higher genus through the Euler characteristic.

    Evidence (3)
    • zbMATH lists Heawood, Map-colour theorem, Quart. J. 1890 zbMATH 2689944
    • MacTutor's Heawood biography summarises the five-colour theorem and genus bound of this paper MacTutor biography of Heawood
    • Wikipedia dates the Heawood conjecture to 1890 English Wikipedia, Heawood conjecture
  5. 1898

    Heawood's 'On the four colour map theorem'

    naming

    P. J. Heawood, On the four colour map theorem, 1898

    The earliest zbMATH-indexed title naming the problem as the four colour map theorem.

    Evidence (1)
    • zbMATH record: Heawood, 'On the four colour map theorem', year 1898. zbMATH Open, API ti:"four colour" search
  6. 1913

    Birkhoff introduces reducibility

    key development

    Birkhoff, The reducibility of maps, American Journal of Mathematics 35, 115-128, 1913

    Birkhoff's concept of reducible configurations became the basis of most later attacks on the problem.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref record of Birkhoff 1913, Am. J. Math. 35, 115 Crossref 10.2307/2370276
    • MacTutor says Birkhoff introduced reducibility, on which most later work rested MacTutor, The four colour theorem
  7. 1967

    Ore's monograph The Four-Color Problem

    consolidation

    O. Ore, The Four-Color Problem, Academic Press, 1967

    A monograph devoted to the four colour problem, one of the first book-length treatments available as a bibliographic record.

    Evidence (1)
    • Open Library records: The four-color problem by Ore, first publication year 1967, Academic Press. Open Library search API
  8. 1968

    Ringel and Youngs prove Heawood's formula

    key development

    Ringel and Youngs, Solution of the Heawood map-coloring problem, PNAS 60, 438-445, 1968

    The surface version of the conjecture was proved for all genera above zero (with the Klein bottle as the exception) by constructing extreme examples case by case.

    Evidence (2)
    • Crossref record of the PNAS paper Crossref 10.1073/pnas.60.2.438
    • Wikipedia dates the proof to 1968 English Wikipedia, Heawood conjecture
