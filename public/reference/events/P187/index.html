300 BCE · Combinatorics

Jaina permutations and combinations (early Indian combinatorics)

Jaina texts treated counting of permutations and combinations alongside multi-level infinities.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 300 BCE–100 BCE

    Bhagavati Sutra gives counting rules for selections from n

    first statement

    Bhagabati (Bhagavati) Sutra, Jaina canon, undated; MacTutor puts it around 300 BC

    The canon states how many permutations and combinations arise when one, two or three items are chosen from n, and works out cases n = 2, 3, 4.

    Evidence (2)
    • MacTutor Jaina mathematics: Bhagabati Sutra dated around 300 BC, rules for permutations and combinations of 1, 2, 3 from n MacTutor, Jaina mathematics
    • Wikipedia Indian mathematics: Jain texts incl. Sthananga Sutra, c. 300 BCE-200 CE, as the main Jain mathematical sources Wikipedia, Indian mathematics (Jain section)
  2. 300 BCE–101 BCE

    Pingala enumerates metres as binary patterns of short and long syllables

    first application

    Pingala, Chandahsastra (Chandas Sutra), Sanskrit prosody

    Pingala systematically lists and counts all light/heavy syllable patterns of a given length, with a triangular array of binomial counts, using combinatorics on a purposeful target (metre).

    Evidence (1)
    • Wikipedia Pingala: Chandahshastra enumerates metres by recursive generation of light/guru patterns, counts via binomial coefficients, meru-prastara triangle; Pingala c. 3rd-2nd century BCE Wikipedia, Pingala
  3. 200 BCE–100

    Anuyogadvara Sutra: earliest Indian description of factorials

    key development

    Anuyogadvara Sutra, Jaina canon, c. 200 BCE-100 CE

    The Anuyogadvara Sutra is reported to contain the earliest known Indian description of factorial products, extending counting of arrangements.

    Evidence (1)
    • Wikipedia Indian mathematics: Anuyogadwara Sutra (c. 200 BCE-100 CE) includes the earliest known description of factorials in Indian mathematics Wikipedia, Indian mathematics (Jain section)
  4. 150 BCE

    Sthananga Sutra lists permutations and combinations among the topics of mathematics

    key development

    Sthananga (Sthanaanga) Sutra, Jaina canon, about the 2nd century BC

    The Sthananga Sutra names permutations and combinations as a standing branch of mathematics and uses them, setting the curriculum for later Indian work.

    Evidence (2)
    • MacTutor: Sthananga Sutra from about the 2nd century BC lists topics incl. permutations and combinations; permutations and combinations used in it MacTutor, Jaina mathematics
    • MacTutor Indian mathematics: main topics of Jaina mathematics c. 150 BC include permutations and combinations MacTutor, Indian mathematics overview
  5. 850

    Mahavira gives special rules for permutations and combinations in a textbook

    consolidation

    Mahavira, Ganita Sara Samgraha, c. 850 (Mysore); English edition by M. Rangacarya 1912

    Mahavira treats permutations and combinations as a regular topic of a mathematics compendium that updates Brahmagupta, the first systematic textbook treatment found here.

    Dating or attribution note

    MacTutor Zero gives 830 for the same book; 850 is used here as given on his own biography page. I did not read the text itself, so the exact formulas (whether the general n-choose-r rule is stated) are not verified.

    Evidence (2)
    • MacTutor Mahavira: Ganita Sara Samgraha dated 850 AD; he gave special rules for permutations and combinations, a topic of special interest in Jaina mathematics MacTutor, Mahavira
    • Open Library records an edition of the Ganita-sara-sangraha with English translation (1912) Open Library
