1614 · Analysis

Napier's logarithms

Napier linked arithmetic and geometric progressions to speed trigonometric calculation; Briggs made decimal tables.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1614

    Mirifici logarithmorum canonis descriptio

    first statement

    Napier, Mirifici logarithmorum canonis descriptio, Edinburgh, 1614

    Napier published the idea of logarithms with a table of logarithms of sines, reducing multiplication and division to addition and subtraction.

    Dating or attribution note

    Joost Burgi arrived independently at a related idea; his Progress Tabulen appeared in 1620 (not verified here).

    Evidence (4)
    • Crossref lists the 1614 Descriptio (digitised copy). Crossref record: Smithsonian Libraries record, issued 1614
    • Open Library lists the 1614 Descriptio. Open Library search: Descriptio entries
    • States that Napier first published the concept with tables in the 1614 Descriptio, though Burgi approached it. English Wikipedia: Mirifici Logarithmorum Canonis Descriptio
    • Napier's discussion appears in the 1614 Descriptio. MacTutor biography of Napier
  2. 1615

    Napier and Briggs agree on base-ten logarithms

    key development

    Briggs letter 10 March 1615; Napier-Briggs meetings

    Briggs, struck by the Descriptio, proposed a scale with log 1 = 0 and log 10 = 1 and discussed the change with Napier.

    Evidence (2)
    • Quotes Briggs' 1615 letter and says the change to log 1 = 0 came from Napier-Briggs discussions. MacTutor biography of Napier
    • Briggs proposed base-10 logarithms at Gresham College after reading the Descriptio. English Wikipedia: Henry Briggs (mathematician)
  3. 1619

    Mirifici logarithmorum canonis constructio published

    key development

    Napier, Mirifici logarithmorum canonis constructio, 1619 (posthumous)

    The companion volume explains how the tables were built from geometric progressions, and uses a decimal point.

    Evidence (2)
    • Constructio published by Napier's son in 1619. English Wikipedia: Mirifici Logarithmorum Canonis Descriptio
    • Open Library lists a Constructio dated 1620 (probably an edition date). Open Library search: Constructio entries
  4. 1624

    Briggs' Arithmetica logarithmica

    consolidation

    Briggs, Arithmetica logarithmica, London, 1624

    The first large published table of common logarithms (to 14 places for 1-20,000 and 90,000-100,000), which made logarithms standard working tools.

    Evidence (2)
    • Record of Arithmetica logarithmica 1624. Internet Archive record: Arithmetica logarithmica, 1624
    • Describes the 1624 work and its table ranges. MacTutor biography of Briggs
