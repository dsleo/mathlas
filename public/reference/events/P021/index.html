1946 · Algebraic geometry

Sheaf theory and sheaf cohomology

Local-to-global problems from topology and several complex variables are packaged as sheaves with cohomology, then moved into algebraic geometry.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1946

    Leray publishes the sheaf idea

    first statement

    J. Leray, L'anneau d'homologie d'une représentation, C. R. Acad. Sci. Paris 222 (1946)

    The published note supplies an early public sheaf formulation.

    Evidence (1)
    • Leray's 1946 published note defines a faisceau and applies it to homology. Leray, Comptes rendus 222 (1946), digitized note
  2. 1946

    Leray uses sheaves in homology

    first application

    J. Leray, L'anneau d'homologie d'une représentation, C. R. Acad. Sci. Paris 222 (1946)

    Sheaves are used on a representation's homological problem.

    Evidence (1)
    • Leray uses sheaves for the homology of a representation in the same published note that introduces them. MathSciNet bibliographic record
  3. 1950–1951

    Cartan's seminar covers sheaves

    institution

    H. Cartan, Séminaire Henri Cartan, 1950–51: cohomologie, suites spectrales, faisceaux

    The named lecture series brings sheaves into sustained teaching.

    Evidence (1)
    • Seminaire Henri Cartan 3e annee 1950/51: Cohomologie des groupes, suite spectrale, faisceaux zbMATH 0067.15405 (printed edition 1955)
  4. 1955

    Serre applies coherent sheaves to algebraic geometry

    key development

    J.-P. Serre, Faisceaux algébriques cohérents, Ann. Math. 61 (1955)

    Coherent sheaves become a central algebraic-geometric tool.

    Evidence (1)
    • Serre, Faisceaux algebriques coherents, Ann. of Math. 61 (1955) 197-278 (move into algebraic geometry) zbMATH 0067.16201
