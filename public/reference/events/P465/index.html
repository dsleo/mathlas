1957 · Topology

Bott periodicity: Morse theory on loop spaces computes homotopy of Lie groups

Morse theory applied to spaces of geodesics on loop spaces gives the periodicity of the stable homotopy groups of classical groups.

Locate this event on the map ↗

Historical record

These milestones show distinct stages of the idea. Dates and priority claims can be uncertain; consult the evidence and notes for each stage.

  1. 1956

    Bott's application of Morse theory to Lie groups

    precursor

    Bott, R., An application of the Morse theory to the topology of Lie-groups, Bull. Soc. Math. France 84 (1956)

    Morse theory on the loop space gives the homotopy of compact Lie groups, the first purposeful use in this line.

    Evidence (2)
    • Bulletin de la SMF record for Bott's paper, year 1956 Crossref
    • MacTutor Bott biography: the Morse-Bott functions introduced in the work on Lie groups MacTutor, Raoul Bott
  2. 1957

    Bott's periodicity announcement

    first statement

    Bott, R., The stable homotopy of the classical groups, PNAS 43 (1957)

    Announces the periodicity of the stable homotopy groups of the classical groups.

    Evidence (1)
    • PNAS record for Bott's stable homotopy paper, year 1957 Crossref
  3. 1959

    Bott's full computation of stable homotopy of classical groups

    key development

    Bott, R., The stable homotopy of the classical groups, Annals of Mathematics 70 (1959)

    The complete proof and computation, which became central to K-theory and stable homotopy.

    Evidence (1)
    • Annals of Mathematics record for Bott's 1959 paper Crossref
  4. 1967

    Atiyah's K-theory monograph (periodicity)

    consolidation

    Atiyah, M., K-Theory, W. A. Benjamin (1967; lecture notes 1964)

    Systematic treatment of K-theory including the periodicity theorem.

    Evidence (2)
    • Open Library record for K-theory by Atiyah (W. A. Benjamin), first publication 1964 for the lecture notes Open Library
    • MacTutor Atiyah biography describes his K-theory book as published in 1967 MacTutor, Michael Atiyah
