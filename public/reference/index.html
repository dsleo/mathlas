Algebra 43 events

  1. ≈ 2,000 BCE
    Old Babylonian quadratic and cubic problem-solving

    Questions about rectangle areas and excavation volumes were turned into procedures for solving quadratic and some cubic equations, without symbolic notation.

  2. 250
    Diophantus and the Arithmetica (symbolic algebra and Diophantine problems)

    Arithmetica introduced symbols for unknowns and sought rational solutions to polynomial equations.

  3. 820
    Algebra as a discipline (al-Khwarizmi, al-Jabr)

    Problem-solving with unknowns is organised around a small set of normal-form equations with systematic geometric justification, giving algebra its own objects and rules.

  4. 860
    Al-Mahani: geometric problems reduced to algebra

    Classical construction problems were recast as algebraic (cubic) equations.

  5. 1000
    Al-Karaji: binomial coefficients and early induction

    A table of binomial coefficients and stepwise arguments for sums of integers and cubes anticipate induction.

  6. 1010
    Al-Karaji polynomial algebra

    Algebra was freed from geometric operations, with powers and product rules defined for x and 1/x and polynomial arithmetic.

  7. 1165
    Sharaf al-Din al-Tusi

    Existence of roots of cubics was analysed through maxima of polynomials.

  8. 1248
    Tian yuan (celestial element) algebra

    A positional notation for polynomials in an unknown, extended to four unknowns, let equations be set up and manipulated.

  9. 1545
    Complex numbers from the cubic: Cardano and Bombelli

    The Cardano-Tartaglia cubic formula gave square roots of negative numbers in the irreducible case, which Bombelli analysed.

  10. 1591
    Viete: symbolic algebra (the analytic art)

    Letters were used for both unknown and known quantities in a systematic method for equations.

  11. 1593
    Viete: trigonometry applied to algebra (van Roomen's problem)

    A degree-45 equation posed by van Roomen was solved by recognising a trigonometric identity.

  12. 1685
    Seki: resultants, determinants, elimination

    Seki Takakazu found resultant and determinant in elimination.

  13. 1700
    Leibniz: determinants

    Systems of linear equations were handled by an indexed notation for determinants, left unpublished.

  14. 1743
    Spectral ideas: eigenvalues from vibrating systems and quadratic forms

    The eigenvalue idea first appeared in d'Alembert's treatment of a loaded string and Sturm's extension to differential systems, then Cauchy proved symmetric matrices diagonalise, joining quadratic forms with spectral theory.

  15. 1799
    Complex numbers as points of the plane and as two-dimensional real algebra

    Argand located complex numbers in the plane, and Hamilton presented them as ordered pairs with rules of algebra, bridging geometric and algebraic readings of the imaginary.

  16. 1799
    Fundamental theorem of algebra: analytic and topological proofs

    d'Alembert and Argand proved existence of roots using minima of the modulus, while Gauss's first and third proofs are described as topological, importing non-algebraic methods into algebra.

  17. 1799
    Impossibility of solving the general quintic by radicals

    Ruffini used permutation groups and Abel gave a complete proof of unsolvability of quintic.

  18. 1844
    Axioms for linear (vector) spaces: Moebius, Grassmann, Peano

    Directed quantities and weighted points led Grassmann to an abstract calculus of extension, and Peano in 1888 gave the first axioms for a real linear space, soon extended to infinite-dimensional spaces.

  19. 1844
    Quaternions: non-commutative algebra from the geometry of 3-space

    Hamilton's decade-long search for a multiplication on three-dimensional space ended in the four-dimensional non-commutative quaternions, the first prominent non-commutative number system, taken up in physics by Tait.

  20. 1846
    Group theory from the solution of polynomial equations

    Permutations of roots, first a tool for solving equations, become an object studied for its own structure and are then axiomatised independently of permutations.

  21. 1855
    Matrix algebra as an abstract calculus (Cayley)

    Eisenstein and Cayley treated arrays of coefficients as algebraic objects with their own addition, product and inverse; Cayley's memoir gave the first abstract definition and showed quaternions embed as matrices.

  22. 1878
    Geometric (Clifford) algebra

    Clifford fused Hamilton's quaternions and Grassmann's exterior algebra into a single geometric algebra.

  23. 1887
    Differential Galois theory

    Picard and Vessiot carried the Galois correspondence to linear differential equations, with Lie groups replacing finite groups.

  24. 1890
    Commutative algebra from invariant theory and ideals

    Non-constructive finiteness arguments for invariants and ideals of polynomials replace computation, and rings become the unit of study.

  25. 1893
    Abstract (axiomatic) rings and fields

    Dedekind, Weber, Steinitz and Fraenkel gave axiomatic definitions of fields and rings, culminating in van der Waerden's textbook.

  26. 1894
    Italian algebraic geometry

    Castelnuovo, Enriques and Severi classified algebraic surfaces up to birational equivalence with geometric methods lacking rigorous foundations.

  27. 1908
    Non-commutative ring theory: Wedderburn structure theory and chain conditions

    Quaternions and matrix algebras supplied examples; Wedderburn then classified finite-dimensional algebras into radical and semisimple parts and Artin extended this with chain conditions.

  28. 1908
    Noncommutative algebras, representation theory and number theory

    Noether, Brauer and Hasse linked hypercomplex systems to commutative algebra and arithmetic via modules and cohomological ideas.

  29. 1910
    Steinitz's abstract field theory

    Steinitz gave the first abstract definition of field and proved every field has an algebraic closure.

  30. 1927
    Group representations in quantum mechanics

    Symmetry groups of atoms and spectra are analysed by decomposing the state space into irreducible representations, which also drives the general theory.

  31. 1942
    Homological algebra

    Chain complexes, derived functors and exact sequences, first tools of algebraic topology, are turned into a general machinery for modules and then for sheaves.

  32. 1945
    Category theory

    Natural isomorphisms noticed in homology computations are formalised as functors and natural transformations, and the resulting structure becomes a general language.

  33. 1959
    Hyperfunctions and algebraic analysis

    Sato defines hyperfunctions as boundary values of holomorphic functions and develops microlocal methods.

  34. 1963
    Forcing: independence results enter algebra and analysis

    Cohen's method of forcing shows statements such as the continuum hypothesis are undecidable, and Shelah uses related techniques to show Whitehead's problem on abelian groups is independent.

  35. 1964
    Deformation theory of rings and algebras (Gerstenhaber)

    Kodaira-Spencer deformation ideas are carried to associative algebras, with Hochschild cohomology classifying infinitesimal deformations.

  36. 1974
    Buildings: geometry of algebraic groups

    Simplicial complexes attached to groups of Lie type encode their parabolic subgroups, giving a geometric language for finite simple groups and p-adic groups.

  37. 1979
    Monstrous moonshine

    Dimensions of representations of the Monster group match coefficients of the modular function j, and a proof requires a new algebraic structure from string theory.

  38. 1980
    D-modules and the Riemann-Hilbert correspondence

    Systems of linear PDE (D-modules) are matched with constructible sheaves, and localisation matches D-modules with representations of Lie algebras.

  39. 1981
    Geometric group theory

    Finitely generated groups are studied as metric spaces up to quasi-isometry, so large-scale geometry classifies algebraic properties.

  40. 1985
    Quantum groups from statistical mechanics

    Algebraic structures behind the Yang-Baxter equation of solvable lattice models are formalised as deformations of enveloping algebras, linking to knot invariants and representation theory.

  41. 1997
    Deformation quantization of Poisson manifolds

    The passage from classical to quantum observables is solved for every Poisson manifold by a formality theorem using string-theory-inspired graph integrals.

  42. 2005
    Boolean function analysis / invariance principle (Mossel-O'Donnell-Oleszkiewicz)

    An invariance principle transferred Gaussian results to low-influence Boolean functions, used to settle majority-is-stablest.

  43. 2008
    Additive combinatorics proves expansion in groups

    Sum-product and product-set growth estimates show that Cayley graphs of SL2(Fp) are expanders, a spectral gap statement about groups.

Algebraic geometry 37 events

  1. 1070
    Khayyam cubics via conics

    Cubics were classified and solved geometrically through intersections of conics, with a claim that ruler and compass are insufficient.

  2. 1827
    Homogeneous coordinates (Moebius, Pluecker)

    Algebraic coordinates made duality and line geometry computational.

  3. 1829
    Abel's theorem and abelian integrals

    A theorem on sums of integrals of algebraic functions introduced genus, lost until 1952.

  4. 1893
    Hilbert's Nullstellensatz and algebraic geometry as ideal theory

    Hilbert linked ideals of polynomials to geometric zero sets, starting the algebraisation of algebraic geometry.

  5. 1931
    Tarski's quantifier elimination for real algebra (decision method)

    Quantifier elimination showed the first-order theory of the reals is decidable.

  6. 1935
    Hodge theory of harmonic integrals

    Harmonic forms represent cohomology on Kaehler manifolds.

  7. 1940
    Rigorous foundations of algebraic geometry via commutative algebra

    Van der Waerden, Zariski and Weil used valuation and ideal theory to rebuild the Italian school's results rigorously.

  8. 1946
    Sheaf theory and sheaf cohomology

    Local-to-global problems from topology and several complex variables are packaged as sheaves with cohomology, then moved into algebraic geometry.

  9. 1955
    Coherent sheaves (FAC) in algebraic geometry

    Sheaf cohomology was imported into algebraic varieties over Zariski topology.

  10. 1955
    Serre GAGA

    Analytic and algebraic coherent sheaf cohomology on projective varieties were shown to agree.

  11. 1960
    Etale cohomology

    Grothendieck topologies gave cohomology theories for varieties over finite fields as required by Weil.

  12. 1960
    Scheme theory

    Algebraic geometry is rebuilt on arbitrary commutative rings so that geometry over the integers and over finite fields is treated uniformly with complex varieties.

  13. 1965
    Geometric invariant theory and moduli

    Nineteenth-century invariant theory is recast as a quotient construction for group actions on varieties, giving moduli spaces of curves and bundles.

  14. 1968
    Variation of Hodge structure and period domains (Griffiths)

    Periods of integrals on algebraic manifolds vary holomorphically with transversality, giving an analytic tool for moduli and cycle questions.

  15. 1971
    Mixed Hodge theory (Deligne)

    Cohomology of singular or open varieties carries a weight filtration, extending Hodge theory and mirroring weights in the Weil conjectures.

  16. 1974
    Deligne proof of Weil conjectures

    Etale cohomology plus Lefschetz pencils and Rankin methods proved the Riemann hypothesis for varieties over finite fields.

  17. 1982
    Teichmuller dynamics: homogeneous dynamics on moduli spaces

    Ratner-style measure rigidity is adapted to the SL(2,R) action on moduli of abelian differentials, proving the magic wand theorem.

  18. 1987
    Geometric Langlands and its physics

    The arithmetic correspondence is reformulated for curves over the complex numbers with sheaves in place of functions, and electric-magnetic duality in gauge theory is identified as its physical origin.

  19. 1990
    Mirror symmetry

    A duality between Calabi-Yau manifolds from string theory predicts numbers of rational curves, later proved mathematically and recast as an equivalence of categories.

  20. 1994
    Gromov-Witten / quantum cohomology

    Physical topological sigma models gave counts of curves; Ruan-Tian and Kontsevich-Manin made them rigorous.

  21. 1994
    Noncommutative projective geometry (Artin-Schelter-Zhang)

    Graded noncommutative rings are studied through the quasi-coherent sheaves on a 'projective scheme' built from their modules.

  22. 1996
    SYZ conjecture

    Mirror pairs were conjectured to be dual special Lagrangian torus fibrations, giving a geometric picture of mirror symmetry.

  23. 1998
    Donaldson-Thomas theory and wall-crossing

    Counting sheaves on Calabi-Yau threefolds gives invariants whose jumps across walls follow universal formulas.

  24. ≈ 1998
    Motivic integration

    Measures on arc spaces are valued in a Grothendieck ring of varieties, generalising p-adic integration with model-theoretic tools.

  25. 2002
    Derived algebraic geometry

    Toen-Vezzosi and Lurie combined algebraic geometry with homotopical algebra to build derived schemes.

  26. 2002
    Higher topos theory

    Quasicategory-based infinity-topoi and derived geometry reframed homotopy theory and moduli problems.

  27. 2005
    Gauge-theory heuristics for rational points (non-abelian Chabauty)

    Kim's programme uses spaces of path-collections on fundamental groups of curves, modelled on gauge-theory fields, in the hope of a least-action principle for finding rational solutions of Diophantine equations.

  28. ≈ 2006
    Mirzakhani moduli volumes

    Mirzakhani computed Weil-Petersson volumes, recovered Witten's conjecture, and established Teichmuller-dynamics rigidity (with Eskin).

  29. 2007
    Topological recursion and enumerative geometry

    Eynard-Orantin recursion recovers Gromov-Witten and Weil-Petersson volumes.

  30. 2007
    Tropical geometry transferred to Brill-Noether theory

    Degenerating curves to graphs and playing chip-firing games gave new proofs and extensions of classical statements about how many equations are needed to embed a curve.

  31. 2008
    Kontsevich-Soibelman wall crossing

    Wall-crossing formulas for DT invariants unify stability conditions and cluster transformations.

  32. ≈ 2012
    K-stability and Kahler-Einstein metrics on Fano manifolds

    An algebraic-geometric stability condition is shown to be equivalent to existence of Kahler-Einstein metrics.

  33. 2013
    Amplituhedron: scattering amplitudes as volumes in positive geometry

    Recursion relations for gluon amplitudes were shown to compute volumes of pieces of the positive Grassmannian, and amplitudes were then proposed to be the volume of a new geometric object.

  34. 2019
    Prismatic cohomology

    A single cohomology theory was constructed that specializes to etale, de Rham and crystalline p-adic theories.

  35. ≈ 2022
    Euler characteristic of Out(F_n) from Feynman-diagram counting

    Counting graphs weighted by symmetry was treated as a toy quantum field theory computation, showing the Euler characteristic of moduli spaces of graphs becomes hugely negative and hence that many cohomology classes exist.

  36. 2022
    Murmurations: machine learning and statistics reveal patterns in elliptic curves

    Classifiers trained on elliptic-curve data and averages of Frobenius traces exposed oscillating patterns that were then explained for Dirichlet characters and modular forms.

  37. 2025
    Non-rationality of cubic fourfolds attacked via mirror-symmetry-inspired Hodge atoms

    Curve counts on a fourfold, an idea inspired by mirror symmetry, split its Hodge structure into atoms and show that degree-3 five-variable equations cannot be parametrised.

Algebraic topology 16 events

  1. 1895
    Algebraic topology: Betti numbers to homology groups

    Numerical invariants (Betti numbers) of manifolds from the study of integrals and differential equations are replaced by groups, so algebra becomes the language of topology.

  2. 1945
    Axiomatic homology

    Homology theories were characterised by axioms such as excision, allowing comparison and later generalised theories.

  3. 1948
    Lie algebra cohomology

    Cohomology of compact Lie groups was computed algebraically via Lie algebras.

  4. 1951
    Serre's spectral sequence and homotopy groups

    Leray's spectral sequence applied to fibrations gave homotopy results for spheres.

  5. 1954
    Hirzebruch-Riemann-Roch theorem

    Characteristic classes expressed Euler characteristic of bundles.

  6. 1959
    Topological K-theory

    Vector bundle classes formed a generalised cohomology theory, used on Hopf invariant and index problems.

  7. 1970
    Bloch-Kato / norm residue

    Motivic Steenrod operations proved the norm residue isomorphism in all primes.

  8. 1973
    Higher algebraic K-theory

    Homotopy-theoretic constructions (plus construction, classifying spaces) define K-groups of rings in all degrees, importing topology into algebra and number theory.

  9. 1977
    Intersection cohomology

    Goresky and MacPherson extend Poincare duality to singular spaces.

  10. 1984
    Chromatic homotopy theory and the nilpotence theorem

    Formal groups organise stable homotopy into chromatic layers, and the nilpotence theorem proves the Ravenel conjectures.

  11. 1987
    Elliptic cohomology and topological modular forms

    Elliptic curves and modular forms are used to build cohomology theories that refine genera and are tied to string theory; Hopkins reported on them at the 2002 congress.

  12. 1987
    Floer homology

    Infinite-dimensional Morse theory proved Arnold's conjecture.

  13. 1988
    Topological and conformal field theory as axioms (Atiyah, Segal, Witten)

    Quantum field theory is recast as a functor from cobordisms to vector spaces, linking invariants of manifolds with physical models.

  14. ≈ 1993
    Floer theory and Fukaya categories (Lagrangian Floer)

    Lagrangian intersection Floer theory was organised into an A-infinity category; Fukaya-Oh-Ohta-Ono gave a rigorous foundation.

  15. 1995
    Cobordism hypothesis

    Fully extended TQFTs were classified by fully dualizable objects of a symmetric monoidal infinity-category.

  16. ≈ 1999
    A^1-homotopy theory and motivic cohomology

    Homotopy theory is rebuilt over schemes with the affine line as the interval, giving motivic cohomology and the proof of the Milnor conjecture.

Algorithms & complexity 15 events

  1. 1938
    Shannon switching circuits

    Boole's algebra was applied to analyse and simplify relay circuits.

  2. 1951
    Cellular automata and self-reproducing machines

    Von Neumann and Ulam model self-reproduction by lattice automata.

  3. 1956
    Formal language theory (Chomsky hierarchy and automata)

    Grammar types were classified by automata.

  4. 1956
    Kleene's theorem: regular events and finite automata

    Finite automata and regular events were shown equivalent.

  5. 1964
    Computational complexity

    Resource-bounded computation was classified into hierarchies.

  6. 1965
    Algorithmic information theory (randomness via program length)

    Randomness was defined through shortest program length.

  7. 1970
    Knuth-Bendix rewriting: universal algebra meets word problems

    Rewriting-system methods from universal algebra were applied to word problems, giving a computational route to normal forms in presented algebraic structures.

  8. 1971
    NP-completeness

    Satisfiability was shown universal for NP, with many combinatorial problems reduced to it.

  9. 1972
    Domain theory

    Continuous lattices gave models for the lambda calculus.

  10. 1994
    Natural proofs and algebrization barriers

    Meta-theorems explained why known techniques fail to separate P and NP.

  11. 2001
    Geometric complexity theory

    Mulmuley-Sohoni proposed attacking P versus NP via orbit closures and representation theory.

  12. 2002
    AKS primality

    Agrawal, Kayal and Saxena gave an unconditional deterministic polynomial-time primality test using polynomial congruences.

  13. 2002
    Unique games conjecture

    A conjecture about label cover instances yields tight hardness for many problems via Fourier-analytic dictatorship tests and SDP integrality gaps.

  14. ≈ 2019
    Sensitivity conjecture

    Huang's signed hypercube adjacency matrix and eigenvalue interlacing settled the sensitivity conjecture in a page.

  15. 2022
    AlphaTensor: reinforcement learning for matrix multiplication algorithms

    Matrix multiplication was cast as a single-player tensor decomposition game solved by AlphaGo-style training, and conventional search then refined its mod-2 algorithms within days.

Analysis 33 events

  1. 370 BCE
    Eudoxus: theory of proportion and method of exhaustion

    Eudoxus gave a theory of ratios for incommensurables and the exhaustion method, a precursor of integration.

  2. 870
    Ibn al-Haytham and Ibn Sinan: quadrature

    Sums of powers were used for areas and volumes, and Archimedean parabola quadrature was generalised.

  3. 1501
    Kerala series (Madhava-Gregory-Leibniz school)

    Madhava and followers derived series for sine, cosine and arctangent.

  4. 1614
    Napier's logarithms

    Napier linked arithmetic and geometric progressions to speed trigonometric calculation; Briggs made decimal tables.

  5. 1615
    Kepler's Nova stereometria doliorum vinariorum (infinitesimal volumes of solids of revolution)

    Volumes of revolved solids were found by summing infinitely many thin pieces.

  6. 1635
    Method of indivisibles (Cavalieri, Torricelli, Wallis)

    Cavalieri, Torricelli and Wallis computed areas by summing indivisible elements arithmetically.

  7. 1642
    Fermat's adequality (method of maxima, minima and tangents)

    An algebraic adequality rule found extrema and tangents of curves.

  8. 1647
    Saint-Vincent and Sarasa: hyperbolic area and the logarithm

    Area under the hyperbola was shown to behave as a logarithm; Leibniz tied log to the integral of dy/y.

  9. 1656
    Wallis: interpolation and infinite products

    Interpolating integer-power integrals gave an infinite product for pi.

  10. 1684
    The calculus (differential and integral calculus)

    Tangent, quadrature and series techniques are unified into an algorithm with a notation, with the inverse relation between differentiation and integration as its core.

  11. 1685
    Newton: infinite series and binomial theorem

    Expansion of non-integer powers and functions as series supplied tools for quadrature.

  12. 1694
    Elliptic integrals from lemniscate and elastica (Bernoulli)

    Arc length of ellipses and spirals and the equation of a compressed elastic rod led Jacob Bernoulli to the lemniscate integral, treated by analogy with the arcsine.

  13. 1704
    Trigonometry and the exponential: Cotes, De Moivre, Euler

    Bernoulli related inverse sine to logarithm, Cotes and De Moivre linked angle to complex logarithm, and Euler gave exp(ix) = cos x + i sin x.

  14. 1715
    Taylor: finite differences and Taylor's theorem

    A treatise created the calculus of finite differences and gave the series later called Taylor's.

  15. 1722
    De Moivre's formula: trigonometry and complex powers

    Powers of cos x + i sin x tie multiple-angle trigonometry to complex arithmetic.

  16. 1748
    Euler's Introductio in analysin infinitorum

    Functions replaced curves as the object of calculus, with e^{ix} relation, exponentials and logs.

  17. 1797
    Lagrange's algebraic foundation for the calculus

    A programme grounded calculus on power series without limits or infinitesimals.

  18. 1881
    Vector analysis

    Gibbs and Heaviside extracted vector methods from quaternions.

  19. 1892
    Heaviside operational calculus

    Heaviside treated differentiation as an algebraic operator to solve telegraph and circuit equations, later justified through Laplace transforms.

  20. 1895
    Borel sets and measure

    Borel introduced a hierarchy of sets generated from open sets and applied countable additivity.

  21. 1901
    Lebesgue measure and integral: set theory enters analysis

    Lebesgue defined measure in 1901 and the integral in 1902 using Cantor-style set concepts, showing analysis required set-theoretic methods.

  22. 1903
    Fredholm integral equations

    Fredholm treated integral equations as limits of linear algebraic systems and solved the Dirichlet problem.

  23. 1935
    Ahlfors' covering surfaces and value distribution

    Nevanlinna's value distribution theory is given a topological and geometric proof through covering surfaces and length-area arguments.

  24. 1958
    Entropy of dynamical systems (Kolmogorov-Sinai entropy)

    Shannon's entropy is turned into an isomorphism invariant of measure-preserving transformations, settling that Bernoulli shifts with different entropy are not isomorphic.

  25. 1961
    Nonstandard analysis (Robinson's infinitesimals via model theory)

    Robinson used model theory to build a field with true infinitesimals and reprove or extend results in analysis; the 1974 congress president called it a fresh direction.

  26. 1971
    Concentration of measure

    Isoperimetric inequalities in high dimension give sharp deviation bounds for Lipschitz functions, used for Banach space geometry and probability in product spaces.

  27. 1975
    Derivation of the Boltzmann equation from hard-sphere dynamics

    Hilbert's sixth-problem programme of deriving kinetic equations from Newtonian particle systems is carried to long times for hard spheres.

  28. 1976
    Pattern theory and variational image models (Grenander, Mumford)

    Statistical and variational models of images and signals are developed as a mathematical theory of perception and vision.

  29. 1983
    Sullivan's dictionary: rational maps and Kleinian groups

    Iteration of rational maps is matched term by term with the theory of Kleinian groups, so results and techniques of one side suggest and prove statements on the other.

  30. 1989
    Blum-Shub-Smale real computation

    A machine model over the reals combined Turing computability with numerical analysis.

  31. 1992
    Ramsey-theoretic dichotomies in Banach space theory

    Infinite Ramsey theory and descriptive set theory are used to prove dichotomies for Banach spaces and to construct spaces answering the unconditional basic sequence problem.

  32. ≈ 1998
    Higher-order Fourier analysis

    Gowers uniformity norms, matched with Host-Kra ergodic seminorms, produced a nonabelian Fourier theory for polynomial patterns.

  33. 2025
    Kakeya sets in three dimensions

    A volume estimate for unions of convex sets obtained from incidence geometry and multi-scale arguments settles the Kakeya set conjecture in R^3.

Applied mathematics 3 events

  1. ≈ 1586
    Archimedean statics and hydrostatics

    Archimedes treated equilibrium and floating bodies by geometric proof.

  2. 1920
    Mathematical biology

    Volterra and Lotka modeled predator-prey dynamics.

  3. 1950
    Operations research

    Wartime scientific analysis of radar and convoys becomes a quantitative decision discipline.

Approximation theory 1 events

  1. 1857
    Chebyshev’s theory of best uniform approximation

    Chebyshev studied how closely functions can be represented by simpler ones while controlling the largest error, establishing a central problem of approximation theory.

Astronomy & trigonometry 10 events

  1. 370 BCE
    Eudoxus' homocentric spheres

    Planetary motion was modelled by nested rotating spheres whose combined motion produces the observed paths.

  2. 147 BCE
    Babylonian astronomical parameters enter Greek astronomy

    Greek geometric lunar models were tested and calibrated against Babylonian period parameters and eclipse records.

  3. 140 BCE
    Trigonometry from astronomy: Hipparchus and chord tables

    Hipparchus compiled chord tables for astronomical computation, and later Menelaus, Indian astronomers and Pitiscus developed spherical trigonometry, sine tables and the name.

  4. 98
    Menelaus: Sphaerica and spherical trigonometry

    Menelaus' Sphaerica gave theorems on spherical triangles.

  5. 150
    Ptolemy's Almagest

    Ptolemy extended chord tables with sum, difference and half-angle formulas for astronomical computation.

  6. 499
    Indian sine (jya) and the birth of trigonometric functions

    Siddhantic astronomy replaced chords by half-chords, giving the sine; Aryabhata tabulated it.

  7. 970
    Islamic spherical trigonometry

    Islamic scholars used all six functions and proved spherical sine laws and identities.

  8. 1250
    Al-Tusi: trigonometry as an independent discipline

    Al-Tusi's Quadrilateral treated plane and spherical trigonometry separately from astronomy.

  9. 1533
    Regiomontanus: trigonometry separated from astronomy in Europe

    De triangulis omnimodis presented trigonometry as a branch in its own right.

  10. 1609
    Kepler's laws of planetary motion

    Planetary orbits became ellipses with area and period laws, replacing circle-based models.

Category & homological algebra 7 events

  1. 1955
    Abelian categories and derived functors (Tohoku)

    A common axiomatic framework unified derived functors for modules and sheaves.

  2. 1958
    Kodaira-Spencer deformation theory

    Deformations of complex structures were studied by cohomology.

  3. 1971
    Elementary topos theory

    Categories with subobject classifiers served as generalised universes of sets.

  4. ≈ 1994
    Categorification as programme

    Crane-Frenkel proposed categorifying quantum groups; Khovanov-Lauda and Rouquier gave concrete versions.

  5. 1999
    Khovanov homology

    The Jones polynomial was lifted to a bigraded homology theory, starting a categorification programme.

  6. 2014
    Higher-form and non-invertible symmetries in quantum field theory

    Symmetry in field theory was extended from groups to extended operators and then to non-invertible symmetries described by fusion categories, explaining pion decay rates and phases of matter.

  7. 2019
    Condensed mathematics

    Topological spaces are replaced by condensed sets so that real functional analysis can be done in a setting with abelian-category structure, a key vanishing result being proved in July 2019.

Combinatorics 36 events

  1. 300 BCE
    Jaina permutations and combinations (early Indian combinatorics)

    Jaina texts treated counting of permutations and combinations alongside multi-level infinities.

  2. 300 BCE
    Pingala: combinatorics of poetic metres

    Counting patterns of long and short syllables yielded binomial-type identities and a binary-like device; Halayudha later described the triangle.

  3. 1050
    Binomial coefficient triangle in China

    A table of binomial coefficients up to the eighth power appeared as the 'ancient method' of expanding powers.

  4. 1847
    Kirkman's schoolgirl problem and combinatorial designs

    Kirkman's triple-arrangement puzzle gave an early case of combinatorial designs.

  5. 1900
    Algebraic combinatorics: Young tableaux and symmetric functions

    Symmetric functions and tableaux are linked to representations (Schensted, Stanley).

  6. 1935
    Matroid theory and combinatorial geometry

    Whitney's abstraction of dependence unites linear algebra and graphs.

  7. 1935
    Mobius inversion on posets and incidence algebras (Rota)

    The Mobius function of the integers is generalised to partially ordered sets, giving a unified framework for inclusion-exclusion, counting and lattice theory.

  8. 1936
    Erdős discrepancy problem

    A combinatorial discrepancy question is reduced to correlations of multiplicative functions, then settled with entropy-decrement and short-interval results from analytic number theory.

  9. 1947
    The probabilistic method in combinatorics

  4. 1870
    Betti numbers and early combinatorial topology

    Riemann's connectivity of surfaces was extended by Betti to higher-dimensional spaces via numerical invariants.

  5. 1878
    Cardinality versus dimension: R^n equinumerous with R

    Cantor defined equivalence of sets by bijection and showed that Euclidean n-space has the same cardinality as the line, which forced dimension to be defined by something finer than counting points.

  6. 1905
    Metric spaces (Frechet)

    Frechet unified earlier function-space work by Cantor, Volterra, Arzela and Hadamard into abstract metric spaces.

  7. 1909
    Abstract topological spaces from point-set analysis

    Cantor's derived and open sets, Weierstrass's neighbourhoods and Frechet's metric spaces led Riesz and Hausdorff to topological spaces defined by neighbourhood axioms without distance.

  8. 1910
    Brouwer topology

    Brouwer used simplicial approximation and degree to prove invariance of dimension and the fixed-point theorem.

  9. 1910
    Knot problems become group-theoretic: knot groups (Dehn)

    Dehn took the fundamental group of a knot complement as the key invariant, treated knot questions as word problems, and used this to separate the two trefoils.

  10. 1935
    Fibre bundles and characteristic classes

    Stiefel-Whitney, Pontryagin and Chern classes are defined on bundles and computed by curvature, giving global invariants of manifolds.

  11. 1954
    Cobordism: homotopy computes manifold invariants

    Manifolds up to cobordism are classified by homotopy groups of Thom spaces, turning geometric questions into computations in stable homotopy theory.

  12. 1956
    Exotic spheres: algebraic topology reaches differential structures

    Characteristic classes and homotopy groups are used to show manifolds homeomorphic to the 7-sphere can be non-diffeomorphic, then to classify homotopy spheres.

  13. 1957
    Bott periodicity: Morse theory on loop spaces computes homotopy of Lie groups

    Morse theory applied to spaces of geodesics on loop spaces gives the periodicity of the stable homotopy groups of classical groups.

  14. 1958
    K-theory and Grothendieck-Riemann-Roch: algebraic geometry enters topology

    A construction made for a Riemann-Roch theorem in algebraic geometry (a Grothendieck group of bundles) is imported into topology as a generalised cohomology theory.

  15. 1960
    Handle decompositions and the high-dimensional Poincare (h-cobordism) theorem

    Gradient flows of Morse functions give handle decompositions, and cancellation of handles proves the generalised Poincare conjecture in dimensions at least five.

  16. 1960
    Kervaire invariant one problem (solved by equivariant homotopy)

    Equivariant stable homotopy and a slice spectral sequence show that framed manifolds with Kervaire invariant one exist only in dimensions up to 126.

  17. 1961
    Surgery theory (controlled modification of manifolds)

    Surgery classifies high-dimensional manifolds via algebraic obstruction groups.

  18. 1977
    Rational homotopy theory via differential forms

    Homotopy types are encoded by commutative differential graded algebras of polynomial forms, so rational homotopy becomes homological algebra.

  19. 1979
    Operator K-theory and KK-theory

    Kasparov links K-homology to elliptic operators.

  20. 1982
    Hyperbolic geometry in three-manifold topology (Thurston)

    Three-manifolds are decomposed into pieces carrying one of eight model geometries, hyperbolic being generic, so topology is studied with geometric tools.

  21. 1982
    Supersymmetry and Morse theory

    Supersymmetric quantum mechanics gives an analytic proof of the Morse inequalities, a template for later physics-inspired invariants.

  22. 1983
    Gauge theory transforms four-manifold topology

    Solutions of the Yang-Mills and later Seiberg-Witten equations give invariants distinguishing smooth structures on four-manifolds, results unreachable by classical topology.

  23. 1985
    Jones polynomial from operator algebras

    A representation of the braid groups arising from von Neumann algebras yields a new knot invariant, later interpreted through quantum field theory.

  24. 1989
    Contact topology and the h-principle (Eliashberg)

    Overtwisted contact structures are classified by homotopy theory, separating flexible from rigid phenomena.

  25. 1990
    Vassiliev invariants

    Knot invariants were filtered by order via singular knots, unifying Jones-type polynomials into a finite-type theory.

  26. 1994
    Heegaard Floer and Seiberg-Witten theory

    Seiberg-Witten monopole equations gave simpler four-manifold invariants than Donaldson's, and Ozsvath-Szabo later built a Floer theory of Heegaard diagrams.

  27. 1994
    Seiberg-Witten invariants

    Witten's physical dual to Donaldson theory gave monopole equations that quickly proved the Thom conjecture.

  28. 2001
    Heegaard Floer homology

    Holomorphic-disc counts in symmetric products of Heegaard surfaces give invariants of three-manifolds and knots with strong genus bounds.

  29. 2002
    Perelman's proof of the Poincare conjecture

    Ricci flow with surgery, controlled by an entropy functional and Alexandrov-space compactness, classifies three-manifolds and gives the Poincare and geometrisation conjectures.

  30. 2012
    Cubical geometry proves the virtual Haken conjecture

    Wise's special cube complexes and Agol's criterion show that hyperbolic 3-manifold groups are virtually special, giving virtual Haken and virtual fibring.

  31. 2013
    Triangulation conjecture disproved via Pin(2)-equivariant Seiberg-Witten Floer theory

    Equivariant Seiberg-Witten Floer homology of homology 3-spheres, via Galewski-Stern reduction, showed that manifolds of every dimension at least five need not be triangulable.
