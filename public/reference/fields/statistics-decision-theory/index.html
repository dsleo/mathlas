Statistics & decision theory

A curated selection of 13 recorded events in Statistics & decision theory. The dates describe these selected records, not the origin or size of the field.

Locate this field on the map ↗

Events in this field

  1. 1662
    Graunt: life tables

    Bills of mortality were analysed to build the first life table; Halley refined it.

  2. 1710
    Arbuthnot sex-ratio argument as an early significance test

    Arbuthnot used probability to argue birth ratios were not chance.

  3. 1764
    Bayes' inverse probability

    Bayes derived a posterior for the binomial.

  4. 1785
    Social choice theory: Condorcet's probability to Arrow's theorem

    Condorcet applied probability to majority decisions and found cycles, and Arrow proved no aggregation satisfies reasonable fairness conditions at once.

  5. 1805
    Least squares

    Fitting observations by minimising squared errors predicted Ceres.

  6. 1894
    Biometric mathematical statistics

    Pearson developed correlation and chi-square for biological data and founded Biometrika.

  7. 1921
    Fisher's statistical inference

    Fisher unified chi-squared, small-sample t and variance analysis into a framework tested on Rothamsted crop data.

  8. 1931
    Subjective probability and decision

    Keynes, Ramsey and de Finetti defined probability as degree of belief, reviving Bayesian ideas against frequentism.

  9. 1939
    Statistical decision theory

    Estimation and testing recast as games.

  10. 1953
    Markov chain Monte Carlo and the Bayesian revival

    Metropolis-type samplers, via Gibbs sampling, make posterior computation feasible.

  11. 1954
    Mathematical economics via fixed-point theorems

    Arrow-Debreu equilibrium existence by fixed-point theorem.

  12. 1970
    Global analysis and mathematical economics

    Topological and dynamical methods were applied to equilibrium existence and price adjustment.

  13. 2001
    Smirnov conformal invariance

    Smirnov proved conformal invariance of critical Ising and percolation using discrete holomorphicity.
