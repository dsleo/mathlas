Group & representation theory

A curated selection of 29 recorded events in Group & representation theory. The dates describe these selected records, not the origin or size of the field.

Events in this field

  1. 1350
    Oresme: graphing motion (configurations of qualities and motions)

    Uniform acceleration was studied and shown by area under a velocity diagram.

  2. 1854
    The abstract group concept

    Cayley, Kronecker, Weber and von Dyck gradually axiomatised groups independently of permutations, ending with the full modern definition.

  3. 1876
    Lie theory (continuous transformation groups)

    Lie combined contact transformations with integration theory of PDE to build continuous transformation groups and infinitesimal generators.

  4. 1882
    Combinatorial group theory

    Presentations by generators and relations arose from geometric studies of discrete groups, then Poincare's fundamental group, Tietze's transformations and the Dehn and Nielsen-Schreier line made group presentations a topological tool.

  5. 1888
    Killing-Cartan classification

    Killing and Cartan decomposed semisimple Lie algebras into simple ones and classified them into four families plus exceptional cases.

  6. 1896
    Burnside's use of character theory for finite group theory

    Burnside applied Frobenius characters to prove the p^a q^b solvability theorem.

  7. 1896
    Representation theory of finite groups

    Frobenius, Burnside and Schur studied groups through matrix representations, and character tables proved structural theorems such as Burnside's p^a q^b.

  8. 1901
    Schur: polynomial representations of GL(n) and invariant theory

    Schur's thesis used Frobenius methods to classify rational representations of the general linear group, tying them to symmetric functions.

  9. 1911
    Decision problems for groups (Dehn)

    Dehn posed word, conjugacy and isomorphism problems for finitely presented groups, motivated by topology.

  10. 1911
    Undecidability of the word problem for groups

    After Dehn posed decision problems for presented groups and Church proposed a logical route, Post, Markov, Boone and Novikov proved word problems insoluble and Higman's embedding theorem tied recursive presentation to finite presentation.

  11. 1930
    Von Neumann algebras

    Weakly closed self-adjoint operator algebras on Hilbert space became a theory with Murray's classification of factors.

  12. 1936
    Computational group theory: computers enter group theory

    From Todd-Coxeter coset enumeration to the 1967 Oxford conference, Sims's permutation methods and the Cayley and GAP systems, machine computation became a standard working method of group theory.

  13. 1937
    Lie-ring methods enter the Burnside problem (Magnus to Zelmanov)

    Magnus tied Baker-Hausdorff-type Lie machinery to exponent questions on groups, defining the restricted Burnside problem; this Lie-algebraic route was eventually completed by Zelmanov.

  14. 1940
    Restricted Burnside problem

    Zelmanov used Lie ring methods to show finitely many finite quotients exist for exponent n.

  15. 1964
    Golod-Shafarevich: nil algebras produce infinite periodic groups

    Using a construction on nil algebras, Golod and Shafarevich built a finitely generated infinite periodic group, answering the general Burnside question negatively by algebraic means.

  16. 1967
    Kac-Moody algebras

    Generalised Cartan matrices gave infinite-dimensional algebras.

  17. 1976
    Bass-Serre theory

    Group actions on trees decompose groups as amalgams and HNN extensions.

  18. 1979
    Canonical bases and character sheaves: geometry in representation theory

    Intersection cohomology and perverse sheaves give the Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials and canonical bases of quantum groups.

  19. 1980
    Permutation-group algorithms meet complexity theory

    Complexity-theoretic analysis was applied to permutation groups, giving polynomial-time versions of Sylow-type and quotient computations that fed back into practical systems.

  20. 1981
    Gromov polynomial growth via geometry

    Large-scale geometry shows polynomial growth implies virtual nilpotence.

  21. 1981
    KP hierarchy and the infinite Grassmannian (Sato)

    Soliton hierarchies are viewed as flows on an infinite-dimensional Grassmannian, tying integrable equations to representation theory.

  22. 1984
    Conformal field theory

    Belavin, Polyakov and Zamolodchikov classify critical 2D models via Virasoro.

  23. 1986
    Vertex operator algebras

    Borcherds and FLM axiomatise vertex operators to build the moonshine module.

  24. 1990
    Kashiwara crystal bases

    Crystal bases give combinatorial skeleton of quantum group representations.

  25. ≈ 1995
    CAT(0) cube complexes in geometric group theory

    Sageev and Haglund-Wise linked cube complexes with group actions, giving linearity and separability results.

  26. 2001
    Cluster algebras

    Mutation rules on seeds generate algebras whose combinatorics matches total positivity and canonical bases in Lie theory.

  27. 2012
    Umbral moonshine and pariah moonshine

    Mathieu moonshine and 23 further umbral moonshines link groups to mock modular forms, proved in 2015, and a pariah group (O'Nan) was tied to weight-3/2 modular forms in 2017.

  28. 2013
    Kadison-Singer via interlacing polynomials

    Marcus-Spielman-Srivastava solved Kadison-Singer using interlacing families of polynomials, a method arising from graph sparsification.

  29. 2020
    MIP*=RE: entangled provers, computability and the Connes embedding problem

    Verification with entangled provers was shown to capture exactly the halting-problem class, which refutes Tsirelson's problem and hence the Connes embedding conjecture.
