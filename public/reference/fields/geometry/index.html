Geometry

Events in this field

  1. 450 BCE
    Discovery of incommensurable magnitudes (irrationality of the diagonal)

    The failure of the hypotenuse of a right isosceles triangle to be a ratio of integers broke the identification of magnitude with number and forced a new foundation for geometry.

  2. 300 BCE
    Euclid's Elements: axiomatic geometry

    Results from earlier geometers were arranged as proofs from stated definitions, postulates and common notions in one treatise.

  3. 250 BCE
    Archimedes' mechanical method for areas and volumes

    Balancing figures on a lever (centres of gravity) is used to discover area and volume results later proved by exhaustion.

  4. 1435
    Projective geometry from Renaissance perspective

    Painters' perspective became a geometric theory in Alberti and Piero, del Monte proved vanishing points, and Desargues treated conics projectively with points at infinity.

  5. 1637
    Analytic geometry (Descartes, Fermat)

    Geometric problems were translated into algebraic equations, solved and classified by degree.

  6. 1639
    Desargues' projective geometry

    Properties invariant under projection, points at infinity and involution gave a unified theory of conics.

  7. 1799
    Monge's descriptive geometry

    Monge turned military engineering projections into a geometric discipline.

  8. 1822
    Poncelet and projective geometry

    Poncelet's treatise and the duality principle swapped points and lines.

  9. 1829
    Non-Euclidean geometry: the independence of the parallel postulate

    After Saccheri and Lambert explored denials of the fifth postulate, Gauss privately, then Bolyai and Lobachevsky openly, developed a consistent geometry with many parallels.

  10. 1839
    Pluecker: line geometry and algebraic curves

    Lines became elements of space and numerical characteristics of curves were related.

  11. 1868
    Beltrami and Klein models of non-Euclidean geometry

    Beltrami realised hyperbolic geometry on a surface in Euclidean space and Klein completed models using Cayley's distance, reducing consistency of non-Euclidean geometry to Euclidean.

  12. 1872
    Klein's Erlangen programme

    Each geometry is characterised by a transformation group and its invariants, giving a dictionary between geometric and algebraic statements and ordering the projective, affine, Euclidean and non-Euclidean geometries.

  13. 1882
    Transcendence of pi and the impossibility of squaring the circle

    Lindemann's proof that pi is transcendental implied no ruler-and-compass squaring of the circle, resolving a geometric problem by an algebraic argument.

  14. 1918
    Hausdorff dimension and fractal dimension

    Hausdorff allowed non-integer dimensions defined metrically, Besicovitch developed the theory, and Mandelbrot's fractal definition set it against topological dimension.

  15. 1952
    Mathematical general relativity: nonlinear PDE for Einstein's equations

    Well-posedness of the Einstein equations as a hyperbolic system is followed by global stability of Minkowski space and the analysis of shock formation.

  16. 1953
    Kodaira vanishing via harmonic integrals

    Hodge theory (harmonic forms) is applied to compact Kahler manifolds to prove vanishing theorems and characterise projective varieties.

  17. 1954
    Nash hard implicit function theorem and isometric embedding

    Nash solved the isometric embedding problem with a smoothing iteration that overcomes loss of derivatives, a method later extended by Moser.

  18. 1957
    Calabi conjecture (Calabi-Yau metrics)

    The complex Monge-Ampere equation is solved with a priori estimates, producing Ricci-flat Kahler metrics and showing a topological condition forces a metric.

  19. 1963
    Atiyah-Singer index theorem

    The analytic index of an elliptic operator is computed from topological data, connecting PDE, characteristic classes and algebraic geometry.

  20. 1967
    Fractal geometry: self-similarity, dimension and computer experiment (Mandelbrot)

    Mandelbrot combined self-similarity and fractional dimension to model coastlines and noise, defined fractals via Hausdorff dimension, and used computers to map Julia-set connectivity.

  21. 1974
    Lattice gauge theory and Yang-Mills instantons (geometry)

    Atiyah-Hitchin-Singer-Drinfeld-Manin construct instantons.

  22. 1980
    Noncommutative geometry

    Geometric invariants (cohomology, index theory) are extended to operator algebras viewed as spaces of noncommutative functions.

  23. 1987
    Higgs bundles and the Hitchin system

    Self-duality equations on a Riemann surface give an integrable system on the moduli of bundles, linking gauge theory and algebraic geometry.

  24. 1991
    Two-dimensional gravity and the moduli space of curves

    A prediction of topological gravity about intersection numbers on moduli spaces of curves is proved by combinatorial (ribbon graph) and hyperbolic-geometric routes.

  25. 1994
    Homological mirror symmetry and the Fukaya category

    Derived categories of coherent sheaves on one manifold are matched with Fukaya categories of Lagrangians on its mirror; the proposal was presented at the 1994 congress.

  26. ≈ 1999
    Random partitions, representation theory and Gromov-Witten theory

    Measures on partitions defined by Schur functions are analysed as determinantal point processes, and the same structures compute Gromov-Witten and Hurwitz invariants.

  27. 2004
    Optimal transport and synthetic Ricci curvature

    Lower Ricci curvature bounds are defined on non-smooth spaces by convexity of entropy along optimal transport geodesics, giving a stable notion under Gromov-Hausdorff limits.

  28. 2010
    Guth-Katz distinct distances

    Guth and Katz used polynomial partitioning and ruled surface results to nearly solve the distinct distances problem.

  29. 2013
    Virtual fundamental cycles via homotopical algebra

    Homotopy-coherent methods give a rigorous construction of virtual fundamental cycles on moduli spaces of pseudoholomorphic curves, underpinning Floer and Fukaya theory.

  30. 2016
    Sphere packing in dimensions 8 and 24

    A modular-form construction produces the magic function required by the Fourier linear programming bound, matching the E8 and Leech lattice packing densities.

  31. 2018
    Hodge theory via o-minimality

    Bakker-Klingler-Tsimerman used o-minimal definability for Ax-Schanuel and algebraicity of period maps.
