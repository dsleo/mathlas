Number theory

A curated selection of 60 recorded events in Number theory. The dates describe these selected records, not the origin or size of the field.

≈ marks an approximate map placement where a dated first statement is not established.

Events in this field

  1. 450 BCE
    Pythagorean number theory and musical ratios

    Whole-number ratios behind consonant string lengths supported a programme of studying even, odd, triangular and perfect numbers as objects in themselves.

  2. 300 BCE
    Euclid's Elements VII–IX: deductive arithmetic

    Divisibility, the Euclidean algorithm and geometric progressions of integers were developed as a self-contained deductive body.

  3. 300
    Chinese remainder theorem

    A single-remainder puzzle grew into a general method for systems of congruences, including non-coprime moduli, motivated by calendar calculation.

  4. 499
    Indian place-value notation and zero

    Brahmi digits developed into a positional decimal system whose schools around 500-650 CE worked out place value and zero.

  5. 628
    Pell's equation and the chakravala method

    Integer solutions of equations of the form N x^2 + 1 = y^2 were found by composition and cyclic methods, including the case 61 with a very large minimal solution.

  6. 825
    Transmission of Hindu-Arabic numerals to the Islamic world and Europe

    A treatise on Indian decimal calculation, later translated into Latin, spread the positional system and gave 'algorithm' its name.

  7. 1670
    Fermat's number theory (little theorem, sums of squares, Fermat correspondence)

    Fermat's correspondence introduced the little theorem, four-square statement and other results.

  8. 1744
    Analytic number theory I: Euler's product and Dirichlet's characters

    Infinite series and characters of finite abelian groups (a Fourier analysis on Z/nZ) are used to count primes in residue classes.

  9. 1801
    Gauss's Disquisitiones Arithmeticae

    Congruence notation, quadratic forms and cyclotomy were unified.

  10. 1829
    Jacobi: four squares via elliptic (theta) functions

    Jacobi derived the four-square theorem from theta functions.

  11. 1829
    Jacobi: theta functions and elliptic function theory

    Elliptic functions were rebuilt on theta functions in a systematic treatise.

  12. 1844
    Liouville and early transcendence theory

    Liouville gave explicit ways to prove numbers transcendental, opening a theory later extended to e by Hermite and pi by Lindemann.

  13. 1844
    Transcendental numbers from continued fractions (Liouville)

    Liouville used rational approximation by continued fractions to produce the first provably transcendental numbers, then gave an explicit example without them in 1851.

  14. 1846
    Ideals and algebraic number theory

    Failure of unique factorisation in rings of integers is repaired by introducing ideal elements, later recast as ideals, which become the central object.

  15. 1859
    Riemann's zeta function and the prime number theorem

    The zeta function is continued to the complex plane and its zeros are tied to the distribution of primes, so function-theoretic facts (no zeros on Re s = 1) give the asymptotic law.

  16. 1881
    Automorphic functions and uniformisation

    Poincare and Klein linked discrete groups of Mobius transformations, hyperbolic geometry and differential equations, and uniformised algebraic curves.

  17. 1896
    Geometry of numbers (Minkowski)

    Convex bodies and lattices in R^n are used to prove existence of integer points, with consequences for quadratic forms and algebraic number fields.

  18. 1897
    Class field theory

    Abelian extensions of number fields were matched to ideal-class-type groups, from Kronecker, Weber and Hilbert through Takagi's existence theorem and Artin's reciprocity law.

  19. 1899
    p-adic numbers

    Hensel treated integers like power series in a prime, giving p-adic numbers, and Kuerschak and Hasse later turned this into valuation theory and local-global principles.

  20. 1909
    Normal numbers: measure and probability applied to digit expansions (Borel)

    Borel defined normal numbers by digit frequencies, proved almost every real is normal, and Sierpinski, Champernowne, Mahler, and Copeland and Erdos later gave explicit examples.

  21. 1917
    Hardy-Littlewood circle method

    Contour integration of generating functions and exponential sums are used to count representations of integers (partitions, sums of primes), the method behind later Fourier-analytic density arguments.

  22. 1923
    Hasse local-global principle

    Hasse showed a quadratic form represents zero over the rationals when it does so over all completions.

  23. 1929
    Unipotent flows and the Oppenheim conjecture

    Values of indefinite quadratic forms at integer points are controlled by showing orbits of unipotent subgroups on homogeneous spaces are well-behaved.

  24. 1939
    Weil's dictionary: number fields, function fields, Riemann surfaces

    A three-way analogy between number fields, function fields over finite fields and compact Riemann surfaces transfers statements and methods between arithmetic and geometry.

  25. 1941
    The large sieve: harmonic analysis in analytic number theory

    Duality for trigonometric polynomials gives inequalities on sums over characters, producing the Bombieri-Vinogradov theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions.

  26. 1949
    Weil conjectures

    Counting points on varieties over finite fields is predicted to behave like Betti numbers, and a cohomology theory built for that purpose proves it.

  27. 1956
    Modularity of elliptic curves and Fermat's Last Theorem

    A counterexample to Fermat would give an elliptic curve contradicting modularity, and modularity of semistable curves is proved with Galois deformation theory and Hecke algebras.

  28. 1956
    Selberg trace formula links spectra of surfaces to number theory

    A formula equates sums over Laplace eigenvalues with sums over closed geodesics, mirroring the explicit formulas of prime number theory and later generalised by Arthur.

  29. 1959
    Iwasawa theory

    Class groups in infinite towers are studied with module theory over Iwasawa algebras.

  30. 1965
    Sato-Tate conjecture (Frobenius angle distribution of elliptic curves)

    Potential automorphy of symmetric powers gave equidistribution of Frobenius angles of elliptic curves.

  31. 1967
    Tate's thesis and adelic harmonic analysis

    Fourier analysis on idele groups gave a new proof of functional equations for Hecke L-functions.

  32. 1970
    Local Langlands for GL_n

    Harris-Taylor and Henniart proved the local Langlands correspondence for GL_n through the cohomology of Shimura varieties.

  33. 1970
    Logic solves Hilbert's tenth problem

    Recursively enumerable sets are shown to be Diophantine, so no algorithm decides solvability of Diophantine equations.

  34. 1970
    The Langlands programme: automorphic forms and Galois representations over number fields

    Galois representations are conjecturally matched with automorphic representations, linking arithmetic, representation theory and harmonic analysis.

  35. 1971
    Rigid analytic spaces (Tate; analytic geometry over non-archimedean fields)

    Complex-analytic ideas (coherent sheaves, analytic continuation) are made to work over complete non-archimedean fields, giving p-adic uniformisation of elliptic curves.

  36. 1973
    Random matrix statistics and the zeros of zeta

    The pair correlation of zeta zeros matches that of eigenvalues of random unitary matrices, suggesting a spectral interpretation of the zeros.

  37. 1974
    Function-field Langlands: Drinfeld and Lafforgue

    Moduli of Drinfeld modules and shtukas, objects of algebraic geometry over finite fields, are used to prove the Langlands correspondence for function fields.

  38. 1975
    Superrigidity and arithmeticity of lattices

    Ergodic-theoretic arguments on homogeneous spaces force lattices in higher rank semisimple groups to be arithmetic, tying Lie theory, ergodic theory and number theory.

  39. 1982
    Lenstra-Lenstra-Lovasz lattice basis reduction

    Geometry-of-numbers ideas give a polynomial-time algorithm that factors polynomials and attacks integer programming and knapsack cryptosystems.

  40. 1982
    p-adic Hodge theory and Fontaine’s period rings

    Fontaine’s period rings and filtered modules relate p-adic Galois representations to de Rham and crystalline invariants, creating a bridge between arithmetic and cohomology.

  41. 1983
    Faltings: Mordell's conjecture via moduli and heights

    Finiteness of rational points on curves of genus at least 2 is proved by combining the Tate conjecture for abelian varieties, moduli of abelian varieties and height theory.

  42. 1983
    Fundamental lemma (Langlands programme)

    Ngo proved the fundamental lemma by interpreting orbital integrals through the geometry of Hitchin fibration.

  43. 1986
    Hida theory: p-adic families of modular forms

    Ordinary cusp forms are organised into p-adic analytic families whose Galois representations vary in a Hecke algebra.

  44. 1986
    Ribet and modularity

    Ribet shows Frey curve would contradict modularity.

  45. ≈ 1995
    Modularity and FLT

    Wiles and Taylor-Wiles proved modularity of semistable elliptic curves via Hecke algebra patching, giving Fermat's Last Theorem.

  46. ≈ 1996
    Stability theory in diophantine geometry (Hrushovski)

    Stability-theoretic results about definable groups prove the Mordell-Lang conjecture for function fields and give a model-theoretic proof of Manin-Mumford.

  47. 2004
    Green-Tao: primes contain long arithmetic progressions

    Szemeredi's theorem is extended to a pseudorandom majorant via a transference principle, with sieve-theoretic input showing the primes sit inside one, using Gowers norms and ergodic-style structure theory.

  48. ≈ 2004
    Higher composition laws and arithmetic statistics

    Gauss's composition of quadratic forms is extended to cubes and higher spaces via prehomogeneous representations, and geometry of numbers counts the resulting number fields and ranks.

  49. 2006
    Kapustin-Witten: geometric Langlands as S-duality

    Twisted N=4 super Yang-Mills was used to derive Hecke eigensheaves from branes.

  50. 2006
    Measure rigidity and arithmetic quantum unique ergodicity

    Entropy and measure-rigidity methods for diagonal group actions show that Hecke eigenfunctions on arithmetic surfaces do not concentrate, a quantum chaos statement.

  51. ≈ 2006
    O-minimality in diophantine geometry (Pila-Wilkie, Pila-Zannier)

    Counting theorems for rational points on definable sets in o-minimal structures are combined with functional transcendence to prove Manin-Mumford and cases of Andre-Oort.

  52. 2006
    Periodic torus orbits, subconvexity and homogeneous dynamics

    Equidistribution of torus orbits on homogeneous spaces and subconvex bounds for L-functions are combined to prove equidistribution theorems for arithmetic objects.

  53. 2009
    Thin groups and the affine sieve (Bourgain-Gamburd-Sarnak)

    Expansion in Cayley graphs from sum-product theory powers a sieve on orbits of non-arithmetic subgroups of SL_n(Z).

  54. 2010
    Inverse theorem for Gowers norms and Mobius-nilsequences

    Green-Tao-Ziegler proved nilsequences control Gowers norms, settling linear equations in primes counts.

  55. 2011
    Perfectoid spaces and tilting (Scholze)

    Characteristic p and characteristic 0 p-adic fields are related by an equivalence of Galois groups extended to geometry, moving results from one side to the other.

  56. 2014
    Kakeya and Bourgain-Guth, decoupling for Vinogradov

    Bourgain-Demeter-Guth proved the main Vinogradov mean-value conjecture using l^2 decoupling from harmonic analysis.

  57. 2021
    Fargues-Scholze geometrisation of the local Langlands correspondence

    The Fargues-Fontaine curve and Scholze's diamonds were used to turn representations of Galois groups and of p-adic groups into sheaves, proving one direction of local Langlands.

  58. 2022
    Moment problem for class-group statistics (Sawin-Wood)

    A general theorem on when moments determine a distribution of group-valued random objects was used to recover class-group distributions and to correct earlier predictions.

  59. 2023
    Relative Langlands duality and electromagnetic duality

    Periods and L-functions were indexed by dual Hamiltonian spaces, guided by electromagnetic duality in physics, to extend the Langlands correspondence between periods and L-functions in both directions.

  60. 2024
    Apery-style irrationality via analytic continuation of power series

    Apery's 1978 method was generalised by extending convergence discs of the relevant power series, giving irrationality proofs for values such as L(2) and an infinite family of zeta-like sums.
