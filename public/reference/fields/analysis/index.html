Analysis

A curated selection of 33 recorded events in Analysis. The dates describe these selected records, not the origin or size of the field.

Events in this field

  1. 370 BCE
    Eudoxus: theory of proportion and method of exhaustion

    Eudoxus gave a theory of ratios for incommensurables and the exhaustion method, a precursor of integration.

  2. 870
    Ibn al-Haytham and Ibn Sinan: quadrature

    Sums of powers were used for areas and volumes, and Archimedean parabola quadrature was generalised.

  3. 1501
    Kerala series (Madhava-Gregory-Leibniz school)

    Madhava and followers derived series for sine, cosine and arctangent.

  4. 1614
    Napier's logarithms

    Napier linked arithmetic and geometric progressions to speed trigonometric calculation; Briggs made decimal tables.

  5. 1615
    Kepler's Nova stereometria doliorum vinariorum (infinitesimal volumes of solids of revolution)

    Volumes of revolved solids were found by summing infinitely many thin pieces.

  6. 1635
    Method of indivisibles (Cavalieri, Torricelli, Wallis)

    Cavalieri, Torricelli and Wallis computed areas by summing indivisible elements arithmetically.

  7. 1642
    Fermat's adequality (method of maxima, minima and tangents)

    An algebraic adequality rule found extrema and tangents of curves.

  8. 1647
    Saint-Vincent and Sarasa: hyperbolic area and the logarithm

    Area under the hyperbola was shown to behave as a logarithm; Leibniz tied log to the integral of dy/y.

  9. 1656
    Wallis: interpolation and infinite products

    Interpolating integer-power integrals gave an infinite product for pi.

  10. 1684
    The calculus (differential and integral calculus)

    Tangent, quadrature and series techniques are unified into an algorithm with a notation, with the inverse relation between differentiation and integration as its core.

  11. 1685
    Newton: infinite series and binomial theorem

    Expansion of non-integer powers and functions as series supplied tools for quadrature.

  12. 1694
    Elliptic integrals from lemniscate and elastica (Bernoulli)

    Arc length of ellipses and spirals and the equation of a compressed elastic rod led Jacob Bernoulli to the lemniscate integral, treated by analogy with the arcsine.

  13. 1704
    Trigonometry and the exponential: Cotes, De Moivre, Euler

    Bernoulli related inverse sine to logarithm, Cotes and De Moivre linked angle to complex logarithm, and Euler gave exp(ix) = cos x + i sin x.

  14. 1715
    Taylor: finite differences and Taylor's theorem

    A treatise created the calculus of finite differences and gave the series later called Taylor's.

  15. 1722
    De Moivre's formula: trigonometry and complex powers

    Powers of cos x + i sin x tie multiple-angle trigonometry to complex arithmetic.

  16. 1748
    Euler's Introductio in analysin infinitorum

    Functions replaced curves as the object of calculus, with e^{ix} relation, exponentials and logs.

  17. 1797
    Lagrange's algebraic foundation for the calculus

    A programme grounded calculus on power series without limits or infinitesimals.

  18. 1881
    Vector analysis

    Gibbs and Heaviside extracted vector methods from quaternions.

  19. 1892
    Heaviside operational calculus

    Heaviside treated differentiation as an algebraic operator to solve telegraph and circuit equations, later justified through Laplace transforms.

  20. 1895
    Borel sets and measure

    Borel introduced a hierarchy of sets generated from open sets and applied countable additivity.

  21. 1901
    Lebesgue measure and integral: set theory enters analysis

    Lebesgue defined measure in 1901 and the integral in 1902 using Cantor-style set concepts, showing analysis required set-theoretic methods.

  22. 1903
    Fredholm integral equations

    Fredholm treated integral equations as limits of linear algebraic systems and solved the Dirichlet problem.

  23. 1935
    Ahlfors' covering surfaces and value distribution

    Nevanlinna's value distribution theory is given a topological and geometric proof through covering surfaces and length-area arguments.

  24. 1958
    Entropy of dynamical systems (Kolmogorov-Sinai entropy)

    Shannon's entropy is turned into an isomorphism invariant of measure-preserving transformations, settling that Bernoulli shifts with different entropy are not isomorphic.

  25. 1961
    Nonstandard analysis (Robinson's infinitesimals via model theory)

    Robinson used model theory to build a field with true infinitesimals and reprove or extend results in analysis; the 1974 congress president called it a fresh direction.

  26. 1971
    Concentration of measure

    Isoperimetric inequalities in high dimension give sharp deviation bounds for Lipschitz functions, used for Banach space geometry and probability in product spaces.

  27. 1975
    Derivation of the Boltzmann equation from hard-sphere dynamics

    Hilbert's sixth-problem programme of deriving kinetic equations from Newtonian particle systems is carried to long times for hard spheres.

  28. 1976
    Pattern theory and variational image models (Grenander, Mumford)

    Statistical and variational models of images and signals are developed as a mathematical theory of perception and vision.

  29. 1983
    Sullivan's dictionary: rational maps and Kleinian groups

    Iteration of rational maps is matched term by term with the theory of Kleinian groups, so results and techniques of one side suggest and prove statements on the other.

  30. 1989
    Blum-Shub-Smale real computation

    A machine model over the reals combined Turing computability with numerical analysis.

  31. 1992
    Ramsey-theoretic dichotomies in Banach space theory

    Infinite Ramsey theory and descriptive set theory are used to prove dichotomies for Banach spaces and to construct spaces answering the unconditional basic sequence problem.

  32. ≈ 1998
    Higher-order Fourier analysis

    Gowers uniformity norms, matched with Host-Kra ergodic seminorms, produced a nonabelian Fourier theory for polynomial patterns.

  33. 2025
    Kakeya sets in three dimensions

    A volume estimate for unions of convex sets obtained from incidence geometry and multi-scale arguments settles the Kakeya set conjecture in R^3.
