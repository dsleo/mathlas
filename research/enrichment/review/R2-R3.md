# Review round 2, batch R3

The review was cut short by a session limit after four events. P122, P193, P065 and P191 were not reviewed and are unchanged.

**P358 Lie theory (changed, not promoted).** Crossref re-confirmed Lie 1880 (Math. Ann. 16, 441-528), Weyl 1925 (Math. Z. 23, 271-309) and Tresse (Acta Math. 18, 1894). EuDML 179698 confirms the 1874 Goettingen note (pp. 529-542). MacTutor Killing supports the Math. Annalen 1888-1890 four-part series and its significance, and MacTutor Weyl supports the 1924-26 representation theory (with Hurwitz, Schur and Cartan credited). Evidence and caveats were added; no year or role changed. Not promoted: the 1874 note, Killing and Weyl were not read (the GDZ scan could not be fetched), there is no first application, and the naming of "Lie algebra" (Weyl, 1930s, Encyclopedia of Mathematics only) was not verified against a primary text.

**P192 Leibniz: determinants (notes only, not promoted).** MacTutor's statements were re-read (1693 letter, Maclaurin 1748, Cramer 1750, Gauss 1801, Cauchy 1812, Seki 1683). Muenster's Leibniz bibliography lists no 1700 Acta Eruditorum article, and 1710 Miscellanea Berolinensia 1 has "Symbolismus memorabilis" (pp. 160-165), the probable 1710 item. The 1700 first statement is therefore still unidentified, and Knobloch's chapter is paywalled. Caveat added; data otherwise unchanged. Not promoted: the 1700 placeholder is unresolved, and Cauchy 1812 is not entered.

**P432 Complex numbers (changed, not promoted).** Numdam confirms Argand's Essai in Annales de Gergonne 4 (1813-14) 133-147 and Francais's article in 4, 61-71. MacTutor Argand says there is no proof that the 1806 printing was published. The 1806 Essai is now a precursor (private printing) and a new key_development is the public 1813-14 printing, so the `took` entry and `shown.methodological` moved from 1806 to 1813. MacTutor Wessel evidence was added (read 10 March 1797, printed 1799, unnoticed until 1895). Buee (Phil. Trans. 96, 1806, 23-88, Crossref) is recorded in the notes as a candidate; I could not confirm what it says geometrically, so it is not a milestone. Not promoted: there is no bibliographic record of the 1799 Wessel volume, and Gauss 1831, Wallis and Kuhn are unread.

**P187 Jaina combinatorics (notes and evidence only, not promoted).** MacTutor Jaina mathematics was re-read: Bhagabati about 300 BC, Sthananga from about the 2nd century BC, and Anuyogadvara cited for other numerical matters but with no factorial claim or date. Wikipedia's History of combinatorics supplies a second source for Pingala (2nd century BC, binomial counting) and Bhagavati (first combinatorics problem). Pingala's start-of-range year is flagged as the earliest bound, and his Sanskrit prosody is not Jaina. Not promoted: the dating is still low-confidence history-page evidence, and Kusuba, Plofker and Datta-Singh are unread.

**P122, P193, P065, P191 (not reviewed, unchanged, source-first).** Outstanding for all of them is the full review of every milestone.
