**P192 Leibniz: determinants (changed, stays source-first).** Read: Leibniz' letter to de l'Hopital of 28 April 1693 in Gerhardt's Math. Schriften II (1850, first print); the 1700 Acta Eruditorum reply to Fatio de Duillier with the "nova Artis Analyticae promotio" (Math. Schriften V, pp. 340-349, signed G.G.L.); the 1710 "Symbolismus memorabilis" (Miscellanea Berolinensia 1, Math. Schriften V, pp. 377-382, Latin text); Maclaurin's 1748 first edition (title page and Part I ch. XII, Theorems I-II, sections 86-87); Cramer's 1750 appendix; the Disquisitiones (the word "determinans" for binary forms); the RHM 2013 article for Cauchy. Finding: MacTutor's "two publications (1700 and 1710) contain results on coefficient systems" does not hold as determinant statements. The 1700 paper prints Leibniz' two-index coefficient notation (01, 02, 10, 11, 12 ...) for series reversion and wishes for canons of elimination; the 1710 paper compares powers of a multinomial with differentials of a product (coefficients as numbers of transpositions). Neither has a linear system or a determinant. Changes: the 1700 placeholder is gone; the 1693 letter and the 1700 print are one precursor (first public use of the index notation); Maclaurin 1748 is now the first_statement (map year 1700 -> 1748, distances updated for P147 and P429), Cramer 1750 the earliest key development; nine milestones reduced to eight by that merge; Cauchy's page range corrected to the Oeuvres pagination (the earlier 29-112 was unconfirmed). Open: Knobloch's manuscript dating (paywalled, not read), printed Seki/Takebe (P147) may precede Maclaurin in a separate tradition, the page range of the 1700 paper (bookseller pp. 198-208), Cauchy's memoir and Spottiswoode 1851 unread, Seki sourced only from MacTutor.

**P007 Fourier series (changed, promoted to direct).** Settled: the 1808 extract signed "P." is by Poisson. Darboux's note in Oeuvres de Fourier, t. 2 (1890), p. 215 (read in the Internet Archive scan) says the article is not by Fourier, that signed with the initial P it was written by Poisson, one of the editors of the Bulletin des Sciences for mathematics, and that it is the first writing to make Fourier's theory known; Fourier's own Discours preliminaire (t. 1) cites the extract (Bulletin des Sciences 1808, pp. 112-116). Confidence raised to high; the dealer description replaced. Also read: Darboux's Avant-propos (the 1807 memoir of 21 December 1807 known only through the extract; the manuscript was later edited by Grattan-Guinness and Ravetz 1972, not read), the 1811 prize (presented 28 September 1811, crowned 6 January 1812, printed 1824-26), Dirichlet 1829 in the arXiv transcription of the Crelle text, Riemann's history and habilitation volume, the 1822 title page. The undated vibrating-string precursor is dated 1753 (volume year of the Berlin memoirs, from Riemann's footnotes; print year not checked). Poisson added to the people. Open: the 1807 manuscript and the 1750-53 Berlin papers not read; Bessel 1819 unchecked; Dirichlet date is the printed volume year (MacTutor says 1828).

**P188 Potential theory (changed, promoted to direct).** Read: Lagrange 1773 (re-searched) and 1777 (Oeuvres t. 4, pp. 401-421), Laplace 1782 and the Saturn-ring memoir of 1787 (Oeuvres t. 11, pp. 275-293), Poisson's December 1813 note (the scan places it in the "Decembre 1813" issue, so Wangerin's 1812 is wrong), Green's title page and introduction, Gauss 1840 (original volume and Wangerin's annotated reprint), Todhunter's history (Arts. 789, 851, 866, 1007-1008).
