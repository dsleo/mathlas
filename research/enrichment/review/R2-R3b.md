# Review round 2, batch R3b

Direct reviews of five source-first events. Two are promoted; three are changed but stay source-first.

**P065 Geometry of numbers (promoted, grades B/B/B).** Read the 1910 edition of Minkowski's Geometrie der Zahlen (editors' preface, the Teubner announcement and the contents) and his 1896 Nouvelles annales paper (Numdam), which states the convex-body theorem and cites the book as 1896. The preface confirms that the first instalment, pp. 1-240, appeared in 1896; it already holds the theorem (section 30), the unit, discriminant and class-number applications (sections 42, 44, 51) and the title. So a first application (1896) was added and the methodological date moved from 1907 to 1896. The 1891 Crelle paper (Crossref) is now a precursor on Minkowski's own account of how he reached the principle. Cassels 1959 (Open Library) and the 1907 and 1910 records (Internet Archive) are confirmed. Open: the Teubner announcement, dated 1893 in the scan, states the principle and title without proof; the 1891 and 1896 texts beyond the contents were not read; Hurwitz has no works listed; MSC 11H was not checked.

**P193 Viete, trigonometry applied to algebra (promoted, grades B/C/B).** Read in the Internet Archive scans: Variorum responsorum liber VIII (1593), the reply to van Roomen as reprinted in the 1646 Opera, the 1615 Ad angularium sectionum (title page and text), and van Roomen's Ideae mathematicae (the degree-45 problem is printed there). The multiple-angle theorems for the double to quintuple angle were already printed, without proof, in Variorum VIII chapter X in 1593, so the first statement moves from 1595 to 1593 (map date 1593; rel distances to P198 and P460 updated) and the 1595 reply to van Roomen is the first application only; the two former entries were the same paper and are merged. The 1615 date, previously unresolved, is read from the title page (proofs by Anderson). Open: the 1595 print and the 1579 Canon mathematicus were not seen, the trigonometric reading of the equation rests on MacTutor, and lifespans are unsourced.

**P192 Leibniz: determinants (changed, not promoted).** MacTutor was re-read once more. Added Cauchy's memoir as a 1815 key development: it was read on 30 November 1812 but printed in 1815, and publication year governs. The 1700 first statement is still unidentified. The Wikisource index confirms "Symbolismus memorabilis" in Miscellanea Berolinensia 1 (1710), but the scan (digilib.bbaw.de) is images only and its subject, powers and differentials, is not clearly determinants. Knobloch's chapter is paywalled, and the 1693 letter was printed only in the 19th century, so a Leibniz "first public statement" stays doubtful. The list now has nine milestones, one over the schema cap. Not promoted for the unresolved 1700 item.

**P122 Al-Karaji polynomial algebra (changed, not promoted).** MacTutor (Al-Karaji, Al-Samawal, Abu Kamil, Al-Khwarizmi), Wikipedia, Britannica (through a search excerpt), Open Library and the Internet Archive were checked. No source dates al-Fakhri; Britannica puts his Baghdad period at about 1010-1015, so the milestone is now a range 1010-1015 (the map date stays 1010). The al-Bahir date (1149) is derived from age 19 and a birth about 1130, and the "polynomial rings" wording is a modern interpretation in MacTutor. Woepcke's 1853 extract is confirmed (Internet Archive, Open Library) but "first printed exposition" is no longer asserted. Not promoted: no manuscript or edition of al-Fakhri or al-Bahir was read, and the c. 820 and c. 900 dates for al-Khwarizmi and Abu Kamil are inferred from lifespans.

**P191 Homogeneous coordinates (changed, not promoted).** The 1827, 1835 and 1839 titles and years are confirmed by Internet Archive and Open Library records; MacTutor and Wikipedia were re-read. A duplicate entry for Plucker's 1835 book (key development and consolidation) was merged. The 1831 date of Plucker's second volume rests on MacTutor's "three years later". Not promoted: the Moebius book was not read (the scan has unusable OCR), and leads that were not checked remain, namely Plucker's Crelle 5 (1830) paper "Ueber ein neues Coordinatensystem" (Crossref), Feuerbach (1822) and Bobillier (1827-28). The first use of the phrase "homogeneous coordinates" was not found.
