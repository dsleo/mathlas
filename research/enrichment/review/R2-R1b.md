# Round 2 review, batch R1b

Continuation of R2-R1 for the six events it left unreviewed. Checked against Crossref, zbMATH Open, Numdam, Open Library, the Euler Archive, MacTutor, Wikipedia, Barr's survey of early algebraic cohomology and the primary texts named below. Two events are promoted to direct; four are changed but stay source-first with the outstanding work listed.

**P054 Homological algebra (promoted, grades C/B/B).** All six milestones checked in Crossref. The first statement (Eilenberg-Mac Lane 1942, Annals 43) is now supported beyond its title by Barr's history and MacTutor (Hom and Ext introduced in 1942); it stays a judgement because Hopf 1941/42 is contemporary and topological, hence conceptual grade C. Serre's FAC (1955) was read in translation: its introduction relates sheaf cohomology to the Cartan-Eilenberg Ext functors, and Grothendieck's 1958 lecture says homological methods entered algebraic geometry through that paper, which supports the first application. The 1956 naming date stays limited to the searched indexes (nLab and MacTutor credit the term to the book). Buchsbaum 1955 is real (Crossref) but not a milestone.

**P212 Etale cohomology (promoted, grades B/C/B).** Grothendieck's 1958 ICM lecture (printed 1960) was read: it proposes the idea of a Weil cohomology from unramified coverings and Galois cohomology without defining the etale topology, so the 1960 date is idea-level. Bourbaki exp. 279 was read: dated December 1964, printed 1966, it proves rationality of L-functions with etale cohomology and cites Artin's Spring 1962 Harvard notes (mimeographed) and the 1963/64 SGA 4 seminar. The undated precursor is replaced by Artin's 1962 notes (precursor, no bibliographic record); SGA 4 (Springer, 1972, Crossref) and Milne 1980 (Crossref) confirmed. The 1966 first-application date is a later bound because the seminar circulated earlier. Derived dates unchanged.

**P012 Commutative algebra (changed, not promoted).** The same papers were listed under two roles and the event had 15 milestones; it now has 8, with Hilbert 1890, Lasker 1905 and Noether 1921 once each (Gordan 1868, Macaulay and Krull 1935 moved to the notes). Crossref confirms Hilbert 1890 and 1893, Lasker, Noether and Krull; Wikipedia places the basis theorem in the 1890 memoir (MacTutor's 1893 is the later paper); Open Library confirms Zariski-Samuel 1958 and the cited Crossref DOI was a later reprint. Derived dates unchanged. Outstanding: no primary text read, Hilbert's 1888/89 notes, which volume of Moderne Algebra holds the ideal theory (1930 or 1931), earlier uses of 'commutative algebra', and the choice of Hilbert 1890 over Dedekind 1871 (a judgement, stated in the milestone).

**P005 Analytic number theory (changed, not promoted).** Euler E72 confirmed by the Euler Archive (written 1737, printed 1744; product over primes in Theorema 7 per Wikipedia). The Dirichlet puzzle is explained: Abhandlungen Berlin 1837 volume printed 1839, Crelle 18 (1838) the earliest print found (Crossref), Crelle 19 (1839) and 21 (1840) the class number papers. The undated Legendre precursor is dated by the Essai (1798). Outstanding: none of the texts was read, so whether the Crelle 1838 piece already defines the characters is unverified.

**P366 Automorphic functions (changed, not promoted).** The 14 February 1881 first note (C. R. 92, 333-335) is confirmed by two independent lists (the Numdam bibliography of Klein's notes and the Nantes Poincare-Klein correspondence), and Klein's transcribed notes on its content show it is a genuine first statement and that Poincare introduced the name. The same notes show the integration of linear differential equations by Fuchsian functions in the C. R. note of 21 February 1881, so the first application moves from the 1882 Acta memoir to 1881 (derived methodological 1882 to 1881; the memoir becomes a key development). Koebe 1907 is confirmed by zbMATH in the 1907 volume (the old '1908 volume' remark is withdrawn). Outstanding: C. R. scans, Acta memoirs, Klein 1882/83, Fricke-Klein unread; coinage of 'automorphic' not found.

**P040 Modularity (changed, not promoted).** The 1968 naming milestone was wrong: Giraud's note (Invent. Math. 5, Crossref) concerns the Shimura-Taniyama formula of complex multiplication, not the modularity conjecture, so it is removed and the derived institutional date is now null. Taniyama's statement was not in a 'proceedings 1956': Wikipedia cites Sugaku 7 (1956) 269 for Problem 12 and says the list was circulated in mimeograph at the 1955 symposium, so a 1955 precursor is added and the 1956 first statement kept. Weil 1967 (Crossref) added as the first serious evidence; Frey 1986 confirmed by zbMATH (Zbl 0586.10010); Serre, Ribet, Wiles, Taylor-Wiles confirmed by Crossref. Outstanding: the Sugaku print and the primary papers were not read; Diamond 1996, Conrad-Diamond-Taylor 1999 and Breuil-Conrad-Diamond-Taylor 2001 left out by the cap of 8; the name 'Taniyama-Shimura' is undated.
