# MSC2020 coverage audit for the Field Emergence Map

Authority: [Mathematics Subject Classification 2020](https://msc2020.org/MSC_2020.pdf), maintained by Mathematical Reviews and zbMATH. This compares all 63 top-level subject classes against **historical emergence events**, not every theorem or subtopic. One class can map to several lanes, and one event can span several classes.

Assessment: **53 represented**, **7 thin**, **3 scope categories**. The thin classes have at least one relevant event, but limited field-level history. There is no wholly absent top-level research class after the sourced additions below.

The 2020 classification has finer three- and five-character subjects; this is a top-level domain audit, not a claim of exhaustive subtopic coverage.

## Thin areas to research next

- **06:** Posets and domain theory appear, but the broader theory of ordered structures has no dedicated emergence event.
- **17:** Lie algebra cohomology appears; broader nonassociative algebra history is thin.
- **33:** Elliptic and theta functions appear, but the special-functions tradition is not represented as a field event.
- **44:** Fourier methods appear, while transform theory has no dedicated event.
- **78:** Hamiltonian optics appears; the mathematical theory of electromagnetism is thin.
- **80:** Heat flow motivates Fourier analysis, but thermodynamics and heat transfer have no dedicated event.
- **92:** Mathematical population ecology appears; the subject is represented by a single major event.
