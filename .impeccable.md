## Design Context

### Users

Mathlas serves a mixed audience: curious non-specialists, university students, and working mathematicians. They use it to orient themselves in mathematics, discover relations between fields and ideas, and move from a map-level overview into evidence-backed detail at their own pace.

### Brand Personality

Scholarly, exploratory, and humane. The atlas should feel like a carefully made historical object that rewards curiosity, while remaining welcoming to someone arriving without specialised vocabulary. It should create confidence and intellectual openness rather than perform expertise.

### Aesthetic Direction

An editorial, cartographic atlas: Computer Modern typography, restrained paper and ink palettes, and map-first composition. The experience should avoid dashboard conventions, generic cards, visual noise, and ornamental motion. Themes are alternative readings of the same atlas, not separate products.

### Design Principles

1. Start with orientation, then reveal depth: a reader should always know where they are and have a clear next intellectual move.
2. Keep the map quiet and legible; place controls and dense explanation in deliberate, accessible layers.
3. Make claims inspectable: dates, relations, sources, and uncertainty must be visible at the point where a reader needs them.
4. Treat genealogy and motivation as complementary lenses on ideas, not as decorative metadata or a single, universal story.
5. Support exploratory routes across fields so specialisation is connected to its problems, methods, and consequences.
